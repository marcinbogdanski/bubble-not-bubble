# Power and datacenter real estate

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Earlier cross-check: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [datacenter-pipeline.csv](/data/datacenter-pipeline.csv)*

## Summary

- Electricity, cooling and buildings turn chips into usable compute. PJM’s auction fell 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement, with only 525 MW of new generation/uprates cleared. This establishes regional adequacy pressure, not campus connection delays.
- Permitting risk now extends beyond Texas’s existing administrative halt: incoming governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia favor pauses or conditions. Texas tax repeal remains an intention requiring lawmakers. The local tally of 40 of 41 contests against development mixes binding restrictions and advisory votes, not canceled campuses.
- Vertiv closed down 14.02% at $176.73 on the vendor tape. CNN attributes the wider sell-off centrally to Texas policy uncertainty; this is a reported explanation, not a measured causal contribution. Equity and credit repricing do not establish lost orders or defaults.
- Equipment and established colocation remain profitable: Vertiv’s operating margin is 22.8%, GE Vernova Power’s EBITDA margin 21.4%, and Equinix’s adjusted EBITDA margin 51.4%. Their earnings and orders counter a blanket demand-collapse claim.
- AEP expects 84 GW of incremental contracted load by 2031; GE Vernova has 64 GW of firm gas-equipment backlog and 58 GW of reservations. None measures energised AI capacity. Nuclear finance is conditional and onsite fuel-cell shipments still lie ahead.
- Project debt, private credit, REIT balance sheets and utility rate bases fund construction. Selected announced campus budgets total at least $82B, not deployed capital. Proposed cost and tax changes could weaken marginal returns while scarce operating capacity gains pricing power.
- The central uncertainty is which projects face enforceable restrictions, when rent starts and who carries delay costs. Company assurances, conditional funding and market marks must be tested against permits, delivered capacity and collected cash.

## Analysis

The election increases the risk that projects take longer and cost more. Texas’s administrative audit halt predates the vote; the incoming governors’ positions do not themselves enact new restrictions. Advisory ballots require subsequent action, while prohibition measures require examination of scope and effective dates. Estimated capacity exposed to policy risk is not canceled capacity. Tax repeal, operator-funded grid works and labor conditions could raise project costs even where construction eventually proceeds.

[CNN’s closing account](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/05/investing/tech-heavy-nasdaq-plunges) links the sell-off centrally to Texas’s election and proposed restrictions. That is attribution, not identification of the cause of every price move. Vertiv’s fall reprices expectations without superseding reported earnings or establishing canceled orders. [Treasury yields fell while oil rose](/research/macro/rates-sovereign-geopolitics); lower benchmark rates can cushion financing, but borrower spreads can offset that relief. Oil is a possible inflation headwind, not a uniform proxy for campus electricity costs. Fed tightening comments and the BoE hike add macro context without an isolated causal contribution to this sector’s fall.

Scarcity can support incumbent rents while marginal developers lose money. Delays mismatch energisation, chip delivery, lease commencement and debt service. Dominion’s queue, Jupiter’s reported site status and [Microsoft’s delivery slips](/research/stack/microsoft) illustrate execution risk. PJM’s auction shortfall measures regional reliability, not a campus connection date. Buying chips early adds financing and obsolescence exposure; waiting can sacrifice supply slots. Annual electricity use, peak load and campus nameplate capacity are different measures and cannot be summed into usable supply.

Policy can also curb speculative overbuilding. [Ed Yardeni’s CNN counterargument](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/05/investing/tech-heavy-nasdaq-plunges) favors a slower build; Keith Lerner calls the response knee-jerk. These are opinions. Strong supplier profits, reservation conversion and Equinix bookings provide firmer opposing evidence. GE Vernova’s working-capital benefit qualifies its cash generation; Equinix and Digital Realty have non-recurring profit benefits. Orders, bookings and preleasing still need to convert into delivered equipment and recurring collected rent.

[CoreWeave’s CEO, reported by Dow Jones](https://www.itiger.com/news/2681008585), expects existing commitments and backlog to be unaffected by political changes; that is management’s assessment, not independent delivery assurance. [OpenAI’s compact](https://openai.com/index/setting-new-standard-responsible-ai-infrastructure/) promises energy/infrastructure cost coverage, financial assurances before major spending, disclosure and community participation at future campuses it owns and operates. It limits NDAs and accompanies proposed policy changes. It is neither national law nor a guarantee covering every partner-owned campus or existing compute contract.

[IREN’s post-close release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1878848/000187884826000065/irenreportsq1-fy27result.htm) discloses a later operating target due to customer design changes and supplier timing. Its funding description includes restricted cash, undrawn facilities and unreceived contractual prepayments, with additional planned sources subject to markets; see [neoclouds-other](/research/stack/neoclouds-other). This disclosure cannot explain the preceding cash-session decline without evidence of earlier dissemination. [Oracle’s completed financing](/research/stack/oracle) and AirTrunk’s loan also contradict a blanket funding-closure claim.

[Debt-market marks](/research/macro/debt-markets) show tenant-financing stress; they do not establish missed payments. Trepp’s current-loan finding remains dated to its own sample. Enforceable leases and [guarantees](/research/macro/circular-financing) redistribute losses without creating end-user demand. Empty shells still incur carrying costs; utility losses depend on customer collateral and rate allocation. AEP’s contracted load needs cancellation-protection checks, while PJM’s vetting can remove duplicate requests. Demand response, conditional nuclear finance and planned onsite power offer relief without proving commissioning.

The [forecast register](/forecasts/register) therefore faces upward pressure on power-layer impairment and F1/F4 risk, and on M1/M1b through repricing. C1/C2 lean upward if restrictions defer spending. F3/F3b lean upward on implementation risk, but supplier delays or policy proposals do not satisfy their binding-amendment tests. No qualifying credit event or commitment reduction is established here.

## Open questions

- Which state proposals become law or effective orders, and which existing permits, tax exemptions and projects are grandfathered? Texas’s audit-halt enforcement and expiry remain unclear. Local certification, effective dates and advisory follow-through require project-level checks.
- Does the federal permitting bill advance despite national-moratorium advocacy? Election calls and procedural scheduling do not settle either outcome.
- How much AEP load has take-or-pay protection rather than letters of agreement? Private-campus lease commencement conditions, utility collateral, metered load and the cost of meeting new protections remain incomplete.
- Jupiter’s hearing schedule does not establish the legal effect on its reported decision deadline; no intervening permit or motions outcome is verified. The FT project-bond versions still lack matched identifiers, timestamps and trade basis in the [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets).
- Does restricted supply strengthen operating-campus rents enough to offset delayed starts? Supplier earnings, colocation bookings and CoreWeave’s reassurance oppose immediate-collapse claims. Actual cancellations, cash collection and binding amendments would settle more than management expectations or distressed prices.
