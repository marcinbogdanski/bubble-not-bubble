# Power and datacenter real estate

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Earlier cross-check: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [datacenter-pipeline.csv](/data/datacenter-pipeline.csv)*

## Summary

- Electricity, cooling and buildings turn chips into usable compute. PJM’s auction fell 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement, with only 525 MW of new generation/uprates cleared. This establishes regional adequacy pressure, not campus connection delays.
- Equipment and established colocation are profitable: Vertiv’s operating margin is 22.8%, GE Vernova Power’s EBITDA margin 21.4%, and Equinix’s adjusted EBITDA margin 51.4%. Supplier earnings and bookings support demand; they do not establish returns on unfinished campuses.
- AEP expects 84 GW of incremental contracted load by 2031, backed by service agreements or letters of agreement. GE Vernova’s gas pipeline includes 64 GW of firm backlog and 58 GW of reservations. Neither is a measure of energised AI capacity.
- Project bonds, private credit, REIT balance sheets and utility rate bases fund construction. Selected campus budgets total at least $82B; AEP’s utility plan and AirTrunk’s development loan have different scopes and cannot be added as deployed AI capital.
- Nuclear uprates and onsite fuel cells offer supply responses, but DOE’s loan is conditional and NTT DATA/Bloom shipments begin in 2027. Texas’s proposed moratorium adds policy uncertainty without establishing an enacted statewide ban.
- Conversion into recurring, collected rent remains the central uncertainty. [Neocloud operating results and lease backstops](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) supply evidence of delivery and contractual protection; [project-credit marks](/research/macro/debt-markets) show repricing without establishing payment defaults.

## Analysis

Scarcity can support incumbent rents while marginal developers lose money. Annual electricity consumption, peak-load demand and campus nameplate capacity measure different things. The pipeline inventory mixes operating facilities, construction and announcements. Summing those ratings would overstate usable supply.

The practical risk is a timing mismatch between energisation, chip delivery, lease commencement and debt service. Dominion’s queue, Jupiter’s reported site status and Texas’s permit directive support local constraints. [Microsoft’s disclosed third-party delivery slips](/research/stack/microsoft) reinforce execution risk. PJM’s shortfall concerns regional reliability; it cannot establish a particular campus’s connection date. A national ranking of power against chip shortages remains unverified. Buying chips early creates financing and obsolescence exposure; waiting can sacrifice supply slots.

Demand forecasts remain conditional. AEP’s service agreements and letters of agreement provide more evidence than undifferentiated enquiries, but contractual strength, timing and cancellation protection vary. Its capital plan also serves reliability and wider customer demand. PJM’s contract and duplicate-request checks can reduce overstated load requests. Google/Voltus flexibility can ease peak pressure without adding a generating plant. DOE’s conditional finance and NTT DATA/Bloom’s shipment plan expand possible supply without proving commissioning. Onsite power still requires fuel, permits and site preparation.

Current supplier profits provide a buffer. GE Vernova reports conversion of reservations into firm orders as well as equipment shipments, strengthening the evidence beyond aggregate pipeline growth. Its working-capital benefit still makes free cash flow an imperfect measure of mature project economics. Vertiv’s sales, margins and orders support near-term demand, although customer cancellation protection is not quantified here. UIG’s completed acquisition and Trench’s agreed acquisition show suppliers investing in power integration and grid equipment; their purchase prices are not new campus construction spending.

Equinix’s bookings and adjusted EBITDA support established colocation economics. Its net income includes an atNorth transaction fee, so that measure alone cannot establish recurring earnings. Digital Realty’s cited results likewise include non-recurring benefits; its preleasing measure captures a different reporting period. Historical returns on stabilised properties and prospective development yields are not comparable investment spreads. No matched project cash-flow model establishes adequate returns across the pipeline.

Capital providers bear different risks. AirTrunk’s financing shows continued access to development credit; it does not establish the facility’s drawdown or future return. [River Bend’s amortisation and Google lease-payment backstop](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) reduce ordinary balloon-refinancing exposure while leaving construction, tenant and guarantor risk. The same note covers TeraWulf’s executed credit support, IREN’s closed equipment refinancing and operating evidence from Core Scientific, Hut and Cipher. [CoreWeave’s Denmark disclosure](/research/stack/coreweave) distinguishes partial delivery from total planned capacity. Microsoft’s declined [Nscale expansion option](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) is reported, unverified (grade C); it does not establish cancellation of the base contract.

[Meta’s residual-value support](/research/stack/meta), [Nvidia’s guarantees and lease commitments](/research/stack/nvidia) and [Broadcom’s contingent backstop](/research/stack/amd-broadcom-custom-silicon) shift who bears losses without creating end-user demand. Their amounts belong to the company notes and must not be summed with funded project principal. Trepp’s wider evaluated spreads coexist with current loan payments in its sample: market repricing and payment failure are different events.

A demand disappointment can appear in financing prices, land values, cancelled starts or tenant concessions. Empty shells still incur interest, taxes and maintenance. Enforceable leases initially shift underutilisation losses to tenants; defaults, concessions and renewals transmit them back to owners and lenders. Utilities’ losses depend on customer security and rate allocation. The [forecast register](/forecasts/register) overlaps this layer with Oracle-tenanted projects and neocloud landlords: these are shared exposures, not independent loss events.

## Open questions

- The FT bond-price versions disagree; identifiers, timestamps and trade basis are missing. Quotes remain reported, unverified in the [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets).
- PJM’s [earlier auction release](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2025-releases/20251217-pjm-auction-procures-134479-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf) and [annual report](https://services.pjm.com/annualreport2025/markets/) disagree on historical cleared capacity and shortfall. The latest delivery-year auction is shown here.
- Jupiter’s scheduling order does not establish the legal effect on the reported decision deadline. No intervening permit or motions outcome is verified. Texas’s proposed broader restrictions require legislation; the permit directive’s current enforcement needs confirmation.
- How much AEP load has take-or-pay protection rather than letters of agreement, and when will it energise? Private-campus lease rates, commencement conditions, utility collateral and metered load remain incomplete.
- Supplier earnings, reservation conversion and colocation bookings contradict a blanket collapse thesis. Collected recurring rent, nuclear-loan funding, actual peak reductions and completed connections would show whether the marginal build earns a return.
