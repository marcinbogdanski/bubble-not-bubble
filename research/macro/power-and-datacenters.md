# Power and datacenter real estate

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [datacenter-pipeline.csv](/data/datacenter-pipeline.csv)*

## Summary

- Electricity, cooling and buildings turn chips into usable compute. PJM’s auction fell 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement, with only 525 MW of new generation/uprates cleared. This establishes regional adequacy pressure, not campus connection delays (claims 2, 3, 4).
- Equipment and established colocation are profitable: Vertiv’s operating margin was 19.5%, GE Vernova Power’s EBITDA margin 18.8%, and Equinix’s adjusted EBITDA margin 53%. Those results do not establish returns on unfinished campuses (claims 10, 11, 14).
- Project bonds, private credit, REIT balance sheets and utility rate bases fund construction. Selected announced budgets total at least $82B. Meta and Nvidia retain conditional support obligations; budgets, guarantees and rent commitments cannot be added as deployed capital (claims 18, 19, 20).
- Loss timing depends on contracts. River Bend’s $3.25B debt fully amortises over 16.5 years with a Google rent backstop. Jupiter’s ~$18B construction loans were reported at 89–91¢ with its buildings subsequently reported unpowered (claims 8, 9, 21).
- Supply responses include proposed generation, flexible demand and faster installation. Their value depends on actual commissioning and site readiness; announced capacity is not delivered electricity (claims 25, 26, 27).
- Conversion of contracted capacity into recurring, collected rent remains a central uncertainty. Applied Digital delivered 62.5% of leased Polaris Forge capacity, a readiness measure that does not establish utilisation or rent collection (claim 22).

## Analysis

Scarcity can support incumbent rents while marginal developers lose money. Annual electricity consumption, peak-load demand and campus nameplate capacity measure different things. The pipeline inventory mixes operating facilities, construction and announced capacity. Summing those ratings would overstate usable supply.

The practical risk is a timing mismatch between energisation, chip delivery, lease commencement and debt service. Dominion’s queue, Jupiter’s site status and Texas’s permit halt support local constraints. PJM’s shortfall concerns regional reliability. A national ranking of power against chip shortages remains unverified; the power/commissioning dominance judgment stays at C (claim 30). Buying chips early creates financing and obsolescence exposure; waiting can sacrifice supply slots.

Demand forecasts remain conditional. PJM’s contract and duplicate-request checks can reduce overstated requests. Google/Voltus flexibility can ease peak pressure without adding a generating plant. Cumberland advances land-use approval; DSX addresses installation after preparation. None establishes that a particular campus has electricity. Nuclear procurement and onsite generation also retain connection, fuel, permit and reliability dependencies.

Current supplier profits and cash generation provide a buffer. GE Vernova’s working-capital benefit can precede firm-order conversion, while reservations remain distinct from backlog. Vertiv’s release leaves order conversion uncertain. Customer deposits and cancellation protection could delay supplier losses, but their coverage is not established here.

Equinix remains profitable under the illustrative fee adjustment, although the actual fee amount is undisclosed. Digital Realty’s realised profits include non-recurring benefits; its development yield assumes completion and lease-up. Historical returns on stabilised properties and prospective development yields are not comparable investment spreads. No matched project cash-flow model establishes adequate returns across the pipeline.

Capital providers bear different risks. Developers and construction lenders can incur delay costs before stabilised rent exists. River Bend’s amortisation and lease-payment backstop reduce ordinary balloon-refinancing exposure; they leave construction, tenant and guarantor risk. Blackstone’s description of long leases and investment-grade counterparties qualifies a blanket fragility claim without validating unleased projects.

Meta’s combined venture exposure covers both Louisiana and Texas, while the selected development-budget subtotal uses the Hyperion announcement. Conditional residual-value support is not an unconditional bond guarantee. Nvidia’s guarantees and lease commitments are separate categories; failed reassignment can leave it paying rent. Guarantees change who bears a shortfall without creating end-user demand.

A demand disappointment can appear in financing prices, land values, cancelled starts or tenant concessions. The order depends on funding and leases. Empty shells still incur interest, taxes and maintenance. Enforceable leases initially shift underutilisation losses to tenants; defaults, concessions and renewals transmit them back to owners and lenders. Equipment suppliers face weaker orders after cancellation protection expires. Utilities’ losses depend on customer security and rate allocation. Bond discounts establish repricing, not defaults or realised impairments.

The [forecast register](/forecasts/register) overlaps this layer with Oracle-tenanted projects and neocloud landlords. These are shared exposures, not independent loss events.

## Open questions

- The FT bond-price versions disagree; identifiers, timestamps and trade basis are missing. Both remain reported, unverified.
- PJM’s [earlier auction release](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2025-releases/20251217-pjm-auction-procures-134479-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf) and [annual report](https://services.pjm.com/annualreport2025/markets/) disagree on cleared capacity and shortfall. Their historical discrepancy remains unresolved; the latest delivery-year auction is shown here.
- Jupiter’s scheduling order does not establish the legal effect on the previously reported decision deadline. No permit or motions outcome is established at the note’s cutoff.
- Private-campus margins, lease rates, cancellation penalties, commencement conditions, utility collateral and complete guarantee coverage remain incomplete. Current metered load and commissioning certificates are also missing for much of the announced pipeline.
- Faster-deployment models and portfolio-quality claims require operating evidence. Collected recurring rent, actual peak reductions and completed connections would distinguish contracted demand from usable, paying capacity.
