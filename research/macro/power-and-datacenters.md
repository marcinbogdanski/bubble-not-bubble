## Analysis

Deliverable electricity, permits, cooling and a creditworthy customer make a shell useful. Annual TWh, peak-load MW, IT nameplate and redundant generator ratings differ. The [pipeline CSV](/data/datacenter-pipeline.csv) preserves these distinctions; do not sum it as operating supply.

LBNL models server deployment; EPRI's updated scenarios imply substantially more US load, while IEA's global central trajectory barely changes. EPRI's scenario construction, baselines and denominators differ across vintages, so ratios of the share endpoints are not like-for-like electricity-volume revisions. EIA forecasts commercial-sector growth, not separately measured AI demand. None is an order book. PJM reduced near-term demand after better vetting; queues require probability weights. [S1](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-new-report-evaluating-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers) [S2](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/oct26.pdf) [S3](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/epri-study-data-centers-could-consume-up-to-9-of-us-electricity-generation-by-2030-302157970.html) [S4](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/executive-summary.html) [S5](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai) [S6](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) [S61](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjms-updated-20-year-forecast-continues-to-see-significant-long-term-load-growth/) [S64](https://restservice.epri.com/publicattachment/96784)

PJM's prices and reserve shortfalls show scarce accredited reliability. A lower price cap does not improve adequacy.
