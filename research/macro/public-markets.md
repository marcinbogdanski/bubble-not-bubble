# Public markets: concentrated earnings, leveraged ownership and the price of growth

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [ai-basket-performance.csv](/data/ai-basket-performance.csv), [forecast-observations.csv](/data/forecast-observations.csv), [theory-shiller-cape.csv](/data/theory-shiller-cape.csv), [theory-finra-margin-debt.csv](/data/theory-finra-margin-debt.csv), [credit-spreads.csv](/data/credit-spreads.csv)*

## Summary

- Public equities concentrate exposure to the AI build. The top ten companies carry 40.6% of S&P 500 capitalization and 36.1% of trailing earnings. Their 20.9x forward multiple is near their own historical average. Concentration is principally an earnings and margin risk (claims 1–2).
- Consensus earnings growth is strong and broad, but Micron and Nvidia supply 36% of expected quarterly growth. Supplier profitability does not establish adequate returns for equipment purchasers (claims 6–8).
- Forward P/E of 19.0x and September CAPE of 40.58 use different earnings denominators. Neither establishes a bubble or safety; sustained earnings and cash generation are the central uncertainty (claims 3–5).
- Financing-sensitive equities have suffered larger losses. M1/M1a/M1b closed at 97.0201/99.0949/93.6265 against their common origin of 100. Falling Treasury yields alongside these losses fit a financing-channel interpretation, without identifying causality (claims 20–25).
- Margin debt of $1.480054T and daily leveraged funds can amplify losses. Passive capitalization weights drift without trading; options turnover does not establish directional appetite (claims 9–12).
- Credit marks show selective stress extending to Meta. They do not establish a credit event or measure contagion across private funding channels. Weak IPO aftermarkets and Chinese model-share losses reinforce the risk-appetite warning; prediction contracts remain different from this report’s forecasts (claims 15–19, 26, 28, 30).

## Analysis

The leaders’ forward multiples are near their own historical average, while their earnings carry disproportionate index exposure (claims 1–3). Broad earnings growth and profitable suppliers are substantial counterevidence to a pure speculation story (claims 6–8). But supplier cash generation can coexist with inadequate purchaser returns. If customers depend on renewed financing, today’s supplier revenue can weaken when that financing slows. Margin normalization belongs in valuation sensitivities.

Forward multiples depend on forecast earnings; CAPE smooths historical real earnings and may understate structural improvements. Their disagreement cannot settle valuation. The conditional stress in claim 29 illustrates separate price and earnings mechanisms. Its transfer of trailing earnings share to forward earnings, flat remainder and terminal multiple are assumptions. It is not a fair-value estimate or forecast.

The latest basket losses are greater in financing-sensitive names. That basket includes an equipment supplier, Vertiv, whose sensitivity does not establish borrower distress. Treasury yields fell during the selloffs in claim 24. This fits a financing-channel reading better than a contemporaneous rates shock, but co-movement does not identify causality. Inflation remains a macro risk. Forecast thresholds start from frozen origins; already-observed peak drawdowns do not resolve them (claims 20–25).

Equity retains residual upside while creditors need timely repayment. Dilution, collateral priority and refinancing affect them differently. CDS includes liquidity and risk premia; it is not an actuarial default frequency. Equity is contractually junior, but both markets can recognize losses concurrently. CoreWeave’s [convertible pricing](/research/stack/coreweave) demonstrated financing access at pricing; its conversion option prevents comparison with straight-debt coupons. It does not establish continuing access. Broad spreads and individual credit marks establish dispersion and selective stress, also observed in traded project paper. They cannot measure its extent across private credit, construction finance, collateral haircuts or overseas funding (claims 15–19).

Margin calls and daily leverage resets can force exposure reductions (claims 9–10). Capitalization-weighted portfolios instead drift with prices without trading. ETF redemptions can transfer securities in kind, so turnover does not fix underlying cash-stock selling. Options contracts also include hedges. Neither activity nor sampled holdings establishes aggregate bullish exposure (claims 11–12). A returning corporate bid could cushion selling; authorizations do not establish execution (claim 13).

Successful fundraising and poor aftermarket returns can coexist. Elice’s debut and Chinese model-share repricing show weaker risk appetite without establishing identical financing structures or a systemic freeze (claims 14, 28, 30). Prediction contracts differ from the register’s definitions and horizons (claim 26). Historical credit and equity warnings arrived in different orders; broad spreads alone cannot establish a systemic freeze.

## Open questions

- How much index EPS includes investment marks rather than recurring operating earnings? Comparable normalization and purchaser cash returns remain unverified.
- Matched-period Mag Seven earnings share, aggregate insider execution, industry leveraged-ETF assets and current net hedge-fund exposure remain unavailable.
- Credit quotes differ by vendor and time. Meta’s marks are separate observations; Oracle’s historical quotes are unsynchronized. Direct CDS histories and comparable bond transactions would settle the differences.
- Polymarket’s named card and FAQ disagree; the card is used. Full numerical resolution rules were not re-established in the latest fetch.
- Equity prices retain vendor limitations. CoreWeave’s latest sub-penny print may be a last sale rather than a closing cross. Exchange reconciliation remains incomplete.
- Meta’s record and payment dates remain open; apply the register’s corporate-action adjustment when effective.
- [Griffin, Sewing and Fink](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/822715) disagree on timing: Griffin warns of a bubble, Sewing rejects an imminent burst, and Fink argues faster construction reduces compute-cost inflation. Realized purchaser returns and financing would test those views.
