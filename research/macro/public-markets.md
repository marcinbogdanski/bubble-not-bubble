# Public markets: concentrated earnings, leveraged ownership and the price of growth

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Codex / GPT-6 · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- The largest ten companies were 40.6% of S&P 500 capitalization and 36.1% of trailing earnings. A later sum of the ten largest security lines is 38.63%; Alphabet occupies two lines. Nvidia alone was 7.88% (claim 1).
- Profits underpin much of the rally, but Micron and Nvidia account for 36% of expected quarterly index earnings growth. Ownership and earnings-growth concentration are different exposures (claims 1, 9).
- Forward multiples have compressed: S&P 500 19.0x, Nasdaq-100 21.36x, SOX 21.89x, on dated snapshots. September CAPE is 40.58. The unresolved question is earnings durability (claims 3–6).
- Basket performance is uneven: Micron and AMD retain exceptional annual gains; Oracle and CoreWeave have severe peak drawdowns. Nasdaq-100 lost 5.49% in the latest week (claims 11–21).
- Margin borrowing, options turnover and retail buying show risk appetite. Borrowing and leveraged products create forced-selling channels; falling prices alone do not force capitalization-weighted funds to sell (claims 22–26).
- CoreWeave combines real revenue with losses, heavy debt and distressed reported CDS. Equity bears losses first; broad credit contagion remains limited. Biggest uncertainty: whether projected profits become recurring cash after infrastructure costs (claim 37).

## Analysis

**What ownership concentrates.** Apollo's [January deck](https://www.apolloacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/011326-MarketConcentration.pdf) establishes the concern; the updated measurements extend it. Company aggregation, observation dates and Alphabet's share classes explain differences among concentration series. The Mag Seven is also an imperfect AI proxy: memory, networking, power equipment and foreign suppliers matter, while mega-caps earn substantial profits outside AI.

Profitable businesses underpin the prices. Nvidia's consolidated statements support its sales valuation, and Nasdaq's research supplies a serious fundamentals-based rebuttal. Supplier profits, however, do not establish adequate returns for equipment purchasers. Demand funded by renewed financing can support suppliers until customers cut orders. CoreWeave's interest expense and losses expose that distinction. [Nvidia](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm), [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/economic-institute/the-print/second-quarter-earnings-show-breadth), [CoreWeave](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000366/crwv-20260630.htm).

**What prices discount.** Forward multiples depend on expected earnings; consensus requires another substantial increase after an exceptional year. CAPE smooths past real earnings and may understate structural improvements. Their disagreement alone proves neither a bubble nor safety. [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-october-16-2026), [State Street](https://www.ssga.com/no/en_gb/institutional/insights/mind-on-the-market-12-october-2026).

Neither earnings-yield spread is an expected equity risk premium: equities grow, bond principal is contractual, and the CAPE/TIPS comparison mixes dates. Small spreads nevertheless offer limited cushioning against forecast cuts. A unique implied growth rate requires margins, reinvestment, discount rates and terminal valuation. RBC's explicit exercise illustrates persistence requirements using older prices; Nvidia's sales multiple alone cannot identify growth. [RBC model](https://www.rbcgam.com/documents/en/articles/market-outlook-new-year-2026.pdf).

**Performance and financing sensitivity.** Claims 11–21 summarize [the daily dataset](/data/ai-basket-performance.csv). Returns exclude dividends and currency conversion. TSM and ASML use US lines, SK Hynix domestic shares. Drawdowns compare the latest close with the highest close since the requested period began, or listing, rather than an intraday all-time high. Weekly bases use the prior Friday or preceding Korean session. October 10–17 contains a trading week, not weekend trading.

The cross-section shows financing-sensitive clouds and equipment suppliers suffering while memory profits lift other names. Index performance conceals those differences. The chip losing streak independently matches [Bloomberg's wrap](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/stocks-fall-as-coreweave-selloff-fuels-ai-jitters%3A-markets-wrap/92225060).

**Marginal demand and positioning.** FINRA captures customer margin, not all market leverage. Citadel reports its own retail platform; overlapping themes cannot be added into national flows. OCC counts contracts rather than directional exposure; zero-day trading includes hedging. TQQQ establishes sizeable leveraged exposure, not industry-wide assets. [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/margin-accounts/margin-statistics), [Citadel](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/news-and-insights/global-market-intelligence/october-update/?series=global-market-intelligence), [OCC](https://www.theocc.com/newsroom/views/2026/10-02-september-2026-monthly-volume-report), [ProShares](https://www.proshares.com/our-etfs/leveraged-and-inverse/tqqq).

Morgan Stanley's dated outlook and Citadel's newer commentary remain constructive, but do not establish strategist consensus or actual portfolio positioning. Sampled managers reduced Nvidia longs while Citadel increased Oracle. Holdings omit shorts, do not delta-adjust options, and precede the latest selloff. [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/Themes/outlooks), [Citadel filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1423053/000110465926097200/0001104659-26-097200.txt).

Insider sales deserve monitoring when expansion depends on investor confidence. The samples do not establish aggregate flight: trading plans, withholding and gifts have different interpretations. Oracle's cancellation directly contradicts an indiscriminate exit narrative. [Cancellation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389753/d20034d8k.htm).

**Issuance and returns.** Cerebras separates successful fundraising from aftermarket losses. CoreWeave remains above its offer despite its peak drawdown; Forgent offers a contrasting successful infrastructure listing, although electrical equipment is not exclusively AI. SK Hynix's US offering is an additional listing of an existing public issuer. Quarterly totals therefore do not count newly created AI companies. Anthropic confirms confidential submission, not a fixed launch; lab listing dates remain expectations. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec), [Renaissance](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/review/3Q26USReview_Public.pdf).

**Credit and unwind mechanics.** The CoreWeave equity/CDS divergence discussed in [the company note](/research/stack/coreweave) is possible without either market being irrational. Equity retains residual upside; creditors need timely repayment. Dilution, refinancing and collateral priority affect them differently. CDS includes liquidity and risk premia, rather than directly measuring actuarial default frequency. Losses hit equity and leveraged holders first, then creditors according to seniority.

Claim 39 separates price and earnings stress. Repeating the historical Nasdaq decline within today's largest-company block creates a substantial index loss even with the remainder flat. Profits need not fall with prices; if they do, multiple compression compounds the damage.

Passive ownership does not mechanically accelerate this: capitalization-weighted holdings drift with prices without trading. Actual redemptions transmit selling; daily leveraged funds may rebalance after losses. ETF secondary-market selling and underlying redemptions are separate transactions. ICI's denominators contradict claims that passive funds own most US stocks. [ICI](https://www.ici.org/system/files/2026-04/2026-factbook.pdf).

**Historical ordering.** Dot-com issuance and first-day returns warned about risk appetite; their collapse largely confirmed the turn. Broad quality spreads supplied weak early warning. In housing, deterioration in collateral and mortgage funding, then New Century's failure, preceded the equity peak. Today's comparable signals are cooling IPO aftermarkets, selective financing distress and narrow earnings dependence. Contained spreads do not invalidate warnings; selective distress does not establish a systemic freeze. [Precedents](/research/theory/historical-precedents), [Ritter](https://site.warrington.ufl.edu/ritter/files/IPO-Statistics.pdf), [Fed history](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/subprime-mortgage-crisis).

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Fundamentals rebuttal:** actual supplier earnings and compressed forward multiples are substantial counterevidence. Recurring cash after customers' financing and replacement costs remains unresolved.
- **Measurement:** current matched-period Mag Seven earnings share was not verified. Forward/trailing shares and growth contributions differ. Nvidia's fetched segment MD&A does not reconcile with consolidated revenue; the calculation uses consolidated statements.
- **Coverage gaps:** complete exchange reconciliation, industry leveraged-ETF assets, current net hedge-fund exposure and aggregate insider sales remain unavailable. Form 144 notices are not executions; even Form 4 sale lines can cover taxes.
- **Credit/issuance:** no direct ICE CDS history was fetched. TNW's financial context differs from SEC financials; only its attributed quote is used. Neither lab's launch date nor a current national IPO first-day mean was verified.
- **Timing:** Shiller uses an earlier October close and estimated CPI. Earnings, holdings and margin data have distinct lags.
