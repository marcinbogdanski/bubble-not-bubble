## Analysis

The latest move broadens the repricing from financing-sensitive owners of compute to suppliers. The chip basket fell more sharply on the day, while the Nasdaq-100 remains below its peak but above its forecast trigger. Peak drawdowns, losses since the frozen origin and one-day returns answer different questions. The corrected circular bootstrap was rerun from the 5 November levels with no drift and +10% annual drift, alongside volatility sensitivity and corrected origin benchmarks (claim 25). These are reference calculations, not the register's forecast probabilities.

[CNBC’s closing coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) quotes Orion’s Tim Holland attributing caution to the midterms’ implications for AI capex. [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/05/investing/tech-heavy-nasdaq-plunges) emphasizes Texas permitting and tax proposals. Incoming governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia favor restrictions of differing scope. Texas already had an administrative permit halt; a proposed broader moratorium and tax changes require separate implementation. Local ballots mix binding and advisory measures. Congressional calls concern incoming control. None of these establishes a uniform construction ban or an equivalent number of canceled projects.

The plausible transmission is longer delivery schedules, higher project costs and less certain supplier orders. This is an inference, not a measured causal allocation of the fall. [Reuters](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/wall-street-fear-gauge-jumps-most-since-liberation-day-as-ai-trade-stumbles-ce785fddd88af42c) also reports crowded-position unwinds and anticipated volatility-fund selling. Margin borrowing and daily leverage resets can amplify moves, but no named liquidation is verified. Passive weights drift without trading; options volumes include hedges. Falling Treasury yields argue against a contemporaneous nominal-yield shock as the sole explanation. Rising oil remains a possible headwind; Reuters attributes the rebound to unresolved Iran-war/shipping risk and tanker attacks. Fed tightening comments and the BoE’s hike add macro context without established causal weights ([rates note](/research/macro/rates-sovereign-geopolitics)).

Company news is heterogeneous. [Qualcomm and Arm](/research/stack/amd-broadcom-custom-silicon) released their outlooks before Thursday; Qualcomm retained its datacenter target. [Micron’s Taiwan construction halt](/research/stack/memory-hbm) is reported, unverified, and does not establish lost current production. [Nvidia’s H200 inventory bottleneck](/research/stack/nvidia) is anonymous-source reporting about import approval, not a new export rule or issuer guidance cut. [IREN’s target delay and conditional funding](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) were disclosed after the close and cannot explain the cash-session loss without evidence of earlier dissemination.

Credit prices sharpen the funding warning. CoreWeave’s CDS includes liquidity and risk premia; its bond low and touched spread are not synchronized closing yields. [Intrator’s response](https://www.itiger.com/news/2681008585) says existing commitments and backlog should withstand political changes; this is management’s expectation, not independent assurance. [Oracle’s financing completed](/research/stack/oracle) before the rout. No qualifying default, distressed exchange, named financing withdrawal or new downgrade was established in the checked disclosures. Broad OAS still measures the preceding session, so it cannot settle Thursday contagion. Private funding, collateral haircuts and project draw conditions remain less visible.

[Anthropic](/research/stack/anthropic) remains filed and unpriced; weaker listing expectations do not establish postponement. [OpenAI’s funding talks and community compact](/research/stack/openai) establish neither a newly failed round nor canceled compute commitments; the compact concerns future campuses it owns and operates. Equity prices are not substitutes for the register’s financing-event tests.

Strong earnings and Timmer’s comparison with the dot-com peak oppose a pure speculation interpretation, but supplier profits can coexist with inadequate purchaser returns. The valuation snapshots predate the rout and depend on earnings surviving it. CNN quotes Yardeni arguing that slower construction could curb speculative excess and Lerner seeing improving long-term risk/reward. Those counterarguments would gain support from stable orders, enforceable backlog, continuing funding access and better purchaser cash returns.

## Open questions

- Which policy proposals become enforceable rules, with what grandfathering, project coverage and effective dates? Which local results are binding rather than advisory?
- Do synchronized Thursday credit closes show broader contagion? CoreWeave bond identifiers, comparable transactions and Oracle marks remain missing.
- Will Nasdaq’s historical rows reconcile its inconsistent point-change displays, and will Cboe confirm the vendor VIX close? Constituent exchange-close reconciliation remains incomplete.
- Do weaker equities lead to canceled orders, disclosed commitment amendments or unavailable funding, or chiefly reduce speculative construction? Management assurances and policy intentions do not settle this.
- How much index EPS reflects investment marks rather than recurring operations? Fresh earnings estimates and purchaser cash returns are needed before calling the sell-off cheap.
- Tokyo and Seoul closed before the US rout; their next session is needed for matched reaction comparisons. Meta’s distribution record/payment dates remain open.
