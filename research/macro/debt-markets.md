# AI debt markets: where losses sit if the build disappoints

*Last updated: 2026-10-21 · Author: Codex / OpenAI · Status: reviewed · Reviewers: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check applied 2026-10-17; Claude (Opus 5.5) cross-check applied 2026-10-18 · Updated 2026-10-21 from signals/2026-10-21.md*

## Summary

- Credit risk depends on construction delivery, customer payments and equipment resale values. S&P’s **2026-08-27 forecast** projects capex above $1.3T by 2027 and expects all six covered hyperscalers to have negative free operating cash flow in 2026–2027, with recovery only in 2029; profitability alone does not establish capacity to self-fund the build. On **October 19**, S&P warned that interconnected financing obscures liabilities, with no quantified hidden-debt estimate and **no rating action**. The cash-flow thesis predates these remarks. [S&P][sp], [Reuters][sp19]
- Meta’s May 2026 bond issue raised $25B face and $24.91B net cash; its June debt fair value was $79.75B against $84B face. Alphabet raised $49.6B net common/preferred equity in June and authorized a further $40B ATM equity programme; preferred conversion is scheduled on/about May 15, 2029 unless earlier converted. Alphabet’s June notes fair value was $94.9B against a $101.085B broader debt perimeter. Equity adds loss-absorbing capital; fair-value discounts are not defaults. [Meta][meta], [Alphabet][goog]
- The note assembles approximately $572.5B of dated corporate principal and selected project securities, mainly from May–June 2026 snapshots. Construction packages, chip-platform capital, El Paso and subsequent issuance are displayed separately. CoreWeave, Hut 8 and WULF Compute debt (~$46B) also sits inside the [neocloud note’s](../stack/neoclouds-other.md) ~$80.7B; do not add the two totals. [Claims 12 and 40](#key-claims)
- AI-related IG issuance reached $330B in H1 2026, 10% of IG issuance against 2% in 2023 (KC Fed, A). Morgan Stanley data reported by the FT show global AI-related debt issuance falling to $23B in September from $113B in June (B), with US IG AI issuance reportedly halted in September (C). The measures have different coverage. FT’s **October 20** report puts AI-related issuance at **$466B YTD versus $101B** a year earlier and seven-name weekly CDS turnover at **>$9B in September versus <$2B in July**; turnover is not net protection purchased. [KC Fed][kcfed], [Axios/FT][axiosdebt], [relay][coin], [FT][ft20]
- Hyperion: Meta holds 20%; leases commence in 2029 with a ~$12.31B initial commitment over four-year terms. Residual-value guarantees have a declining ~$28B aggregate threshold: qualifying termination/non-renewal triggers payment of the shortfall between fair value and the threshold. Meta records no liability because payment is not probable. The threshold is close to $27.3B of Beignet securities, making Meta’s credit central without an unconditional bond guarantee. El Paso adds announced ~$12.5B financing and a conditional ~$13B RVG. [Meta 10-Q, Notes 5 and 13][meta], [El Paso announcement][elpaso]
- Wood/Jefferies, relayed **October 17**, reports Oracle CDS **186bp on September 11 → 288bp** and CoreWeave **749 → 1,118bp**, with endpoint fixing dates unspecified. New snapshots: Oracle **315/322bp on October 19** (CNBC/FT, unsynchronized); CoreWeave **~1,082bp on October 19**, **nearly 1,081bp on October 20**, and its **9.75% 2031 bond YTM 15.97% on October 20**. These are stress prices, not realized defaults or downgrade probabilities. [ET/ANI][wood], [CNBC 19][cn19], [CNBC 20][cn20], [FT][ft20]
- Holders include insurers and pensions, with Blackstone Credit & Insurance anchoring CoreWeave DDTL 4.0. Chicago Fed finds ~$450B AI-adjacent bank C&I commitments, $150B outstanding in late 2025; IMF finds liquidity shortfalls at 4 of 10 sampled BDCs representing ~$90B of $300B assets (not AI-specific). These establish channels and broader vulnerability, not holdings of individual AI instruments. [CoreWeave][crwv4], [Chicago Fed][chifed], [IMF][imf]
- Hyperscaler/utility issues average 16/17-year maturities; the full $330B pool has no published weighted maturity. Meta depreciates servers/network assets over 5–5.5 years; Microsoft uses 2–6 (accounting estimates). Equity takes first loss. Without defaults, long IG bonds can carry the largest dollar marks: a hypothetical 100/200bp widening on $330B costs ~$29–34B/~$55–63B under the duration assumptions below (arithmetic B; ranking C). CoreWeave’s five-year debt/three-year contracts add renewal risk. SpaceX’s **September 30 related-party financing aggregate is $12.897B**, down **$0.432B** from June’s **$13.329B** Valor balance; the new exhibit does not name the creditor. Its SI unit earned **$0.431B operating profit in Q3** against **$16.444B capex**. [KC Fed][kcfed], [Meta][metak], [Microsoft][msft], [CoreWeave][crwv55], [SpaceX Q3][spcx3], [June notes][spcx2]

## Analysis

**What is financed, and who can pay.** Advertising, software, retail and cloud cash flows support hyperscaler debt. Microsoft’s leased suppliers can borrow against its rent despite its absence of direct issuance. Corporate proceeds cannot all be assigned to AI; Alphabet’s mandatory preferred primarily creates dilution and dividends. S&P’s August forecast (claim 39) expects negative free operating cash flow across its six-company universe through 2027, with recovery only in 2029. The named providers above establish participation, not present beneficial ownership. An insurer may hold an asset directly or through a managed fund; a pension can be a fund investor rather than lender of record; a private-credit manager can arrange capital without retaining all the risk. A security-level bridge requires identifiers, current positions, fund investors, insurer general-account disclosures and the contractual waterfall. These institutional sources do not verify aggregate AI holdings or a quantified BBB−/BB GPU-mezzanine allocation. General corporate-bond purchase data cannot fill that gap.

**Maturity mismatch: long bonds, shorter-lived chips.** Claim 52’s 16–17-year maturities apply only to hyperscaler and utility issues, not the entire $330B issuance pool. Compare those subsets with Meta’s 5–5.5-year and Microsoft’s 2–6-year server/network accounting lives (claims 56–57). These server/network estimates are broader than GPUs and do not measure actual resale value or the date chips cease to earn revenue. Meta also reports much longer building lives. [Meta][metak], [Microsoft FY2026 10-K][msft]

Our inference is that long-duration creditors rely on cash generation across successive hardware generations after replacement capex. Rapid obsolescence, weaker rents or expensive replacement can erode that cushion before legal maturity. Long fixed-rate borrowing also reduces immediate rollover pressure, and diversified corporate issuers have durable assets and other revenues; their bonds are not automatically secured solely by the first chip cohort. For equipment/project debt, amortization, contracts, residual values and guarantees determine the mismatch security by security. Claim 23’s contract-renewal gap is separate from hardware useful life.

**Where the dollars of loss land without defaults.** Equity absorbs first loss by contract. Among creditors, without defaults the largest dollar losses can be mark-to-market losses on long-dated IG bonds: at assumed modified duration ~9.6–11.0, a 100bp spread widening marks a $330B pool down by about $29–34B, and 200bp by about $55–63B (arithmetic B; scenario/ranking C). That compares with ~$81B of listed neocloud debt on mixed bases, ~$14B for a +500bp no-default neocloud repricing, or ~$40B if every listed neocloud defaulted at 50% recovery. Realised credit losses are more likely first in undistributed construction loans and sub-IG GPU credit; this is a mechanism-based judgement, not a measured aggregate loss forecast. **Contagion and the default-cycle scenario.** BIS authors’ October 1 research finds that 46.4% by value of AI-to-AI investment deals in 2021–2025 also involved commercial supply-chain relationships (claim 44). This historical investment statistic is not a current debt share; it supports the inference that customer demand, financing and collateral support may fail together. Circularity can also secure scarce inputs and does not itself prove fictitious demand. [BIS Bulletin 137][bis137]

If returns disappoint, sponsor equity and subordinate capital generally absorb impairment first, followed by secured creditors to the extent collateral and guarantees fail; contractual priority controls the actual waterfall. Private-credit marks can lag traded comparables. Lower marks and redemptions can restrict new lending; warehouse and fund-finance lenders can tighten collateral terms or refuse renewals. Undistributed construction commitments then remain with underwriting banks.

ABS and CMBS route rents, mortgages or equipment receivables through vehicles to institutional investors. Subordination and reserves protect senior claims but cannot diversify a common tenant or unavailable power. Insurer losses initially hit investment income/capital; downgrades can increase capital pressure. Pension losses affect asset values and funding. PIMCO funds are not automatically Allianz general-account investments. The BIS explicitly identifies bank funding lines, guarantees and non-bank investors as transmission channels. [BIS][bis]

A telecom-style default cycle would start with delays and weak compute cash conversion, close refinancing access for marginal operators, then force restructurings, vendor concessions and collateral sales. Surviving buyers could acquire usable power-connected assets at lower cost. Unlike fiber, specialized chips can lose economic relevance during workouts. Systemic banking stress would require meaningful retained leverage, correlated guarantees or funding withdrawals; isolated project price declines do not establish it. This is a conditional mechanism, not a default forecast. [Richmond Fed][telecom], [BIS][bisjan]
