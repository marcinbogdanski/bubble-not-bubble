# AI debt markets: where losses sit if the build disappoints

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Earlier cross-check: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [ai-debt-stack.csv](/data/ai-debt-stack.csv), [credit-spreads.csv](/data/credit-spreads.csv)*

## Summary

- CoreWeave credit repriced sharply: CDS was reported at 1,164.52bp and its 2032 bonds as low as 74.75 cents, with a Treasury spread touching 1,200bp. These intraday marks establish financing stress, not a default (claims 25–26).
- Credit access remains selective. Microsoft priced $25B at 4.869–6.356%; Oracle closed $10B at 7.062–8.667%. Those issue yields are not Thursday trading marks. Oracle's Thursday CDS/bond marks and broad Thursday OAS remain unverified (claims 25, 30–31, 35).
- Proposed moratoria and tax changes raise completion and cash-conversion risks, but election positions are not enacted restrictions. CoreWeave management expects existing commitments and backlog to hold; implementation and customer payment timing remain the main uncertainty.
- Meta's negative outlook reaches Beignet and Sopaipilla, both still A+. Trepp's earlier datacenter CMBS widening came with all sampled loans current. These are transmission and repricing signals, not credit events (claims 14–15, 27, 33).
- The mapped principal subtotal is $654.497B, including legacy borrowing and mixed snapshot dates. Later issues and undrawn commitments remain separate; overlapping exposures cannot be added across layers (claims 9, 11).
- SPVs, guarantees and equipment finance extend funding access, but committed support is not cash. IREN's wider capex funding remains partly market-dependent (claims 18, 28, 34).
- Filed fair values sit $53.323B below matched face without establishing realized credit losses. First losses remain likelier in construction and weaker GPU credit; who retains the exposure is unresolved (claims 10, 19; judgement).

## Analysis

**Access and cash conversion.** Hyperscaler cash flows support borrowing, but capex increasingly requires external finance. Issuer notes own the company results; S&P's sector forecast is not a uniform quarterly observation (claim 7). Microsoft and Oracle demonstrate functioning IG issuance at different costs. Oracle's completed funding executes its announced bridge; it is not evidence of a failed Thursday raise (claims 30–31). Meta's prospectus establishes terms, not settlement; its stock forward supplies no initial cash ([Meta note](/research/stack/meta), [prospectus][meta-forward]).

IREN's closed refinance and TeraWulf's Google support show the value of collateral and counterparties. IREN's post-close release also delays its contracted ARR program and distinguishes conditional funding from sources still targeted in the market (claim 28). This disclosure cannot explain the preceding cash-session fall without evidence of earlier dissemination. The [OpenAI](/research/stack/openai) funding talks and [Anthropic](/research/stack/anthropic) IPO remain separate from completed transactions: no fresh withdrawal or issuer-announced postponement is established by the checked reports.

**Policy and completion risk.** Reporting attributes the sell-off to post-election uncertainty over the build. Incoming governors' pause positions in Texas, Ohio and Georgia, Texas tax proposals and widespread local opposition can threaten project schedules and returns. These positions are not new statewide laws; local ballots have differing binding and advisory effects. Texas's existing administrative permit directive is a separate, pre-election action ([power note](/research/macro/power-and-datacenters), [CNBC][ballots], [Axios][states]). Exposed capacity is not canceled capacity.

For construction lenders, delayed commissioning can postpone rent while interest and equipment obligations continue. Loss depends on permits, contractual relief, funding conditions and guarantees, not the election result alone. CoreWeave CEO Intrator expects no impact on existing commitments or backlog; that is management's counterargument, not delivery assurance ([Dow Jones][intrator]). OpenAI's compact promises financial assurances for future campuses it owns and operates, not every supplier project or an unconditional lender guarantee ([company statement][openai-policy]). Slower additions could also support existing permitted assets and reduce overbuild; policy risk does not imply uniform losses.

**Map and transmission.** The principal subtotal combines dated corporate and CoreWeave balances with selected project notes, includes legacy debt, and excludes November corporate flows and incomplete commitments (claims 9, 11). Beignet and Sopaipilla ratings explicitly depend on Meta, with weaker security than a direct asset pledge. Their negative outlooks transmit tenant risk without constituting downgrades or defaults (claims 14–15). Broadcom's conditional SPV support similarly connects supplier exposure to customer performance (claim 18). Named financiers establish participation, not retained ownership; bank commitments, nonbank liquidity mismatches and circular payments describe possible transmission channels rather than measured losses (claims 20–24).

**Marks before losses.** CoreWeave's reported bond spread widened materially over Treasuries (claim 26). Treasury par yields fell on Thursday ([rates note](/research/macro/rates-sovereign-geopolitics), [official curve][treasury]); this is not evidence of a general rise in risk-free discount rates. Oil's rebound and ongoing tightening pressures remain macro headwinds, not identified contributions to the sell-off. The missing synchronized Oracle and broad-credit closes prevent a claim of systemic Thursday widening (claims 25, 31, 35). Trepp's earlier sector widening likewise came with current loans (claim 33).

Long maturities expose investors to duration even without defaults (claims 2, 19). Filed fair-value shortfalls are a separate period-end measure, not Thursday losses; neither measure isolates rates from credit (claim 10). Fixed coupons do not reset, but new borrowing and refinancing become harder at distressed prices. No named financing withdrawal, payment default, distressed exchange or new downgrade was established by the checked sell-off disclosures. That is a verification limit, not proof none occurred. F1 and F4 face upward risk pressure; prices alone do not resolve either forecast.

## Open questions

- **Pricing and breadth.** Obtain synchronized CoreWeave marks with coupon/CUSIP, Oracle Thursday CDS and bond observations, and Thursday broad OAS/CDX. Wednesday OAS and vendor-specific CDS snapshots cannot establish market-wide Thursday widening.
- **Policy and contracts.** Which permits and incentives are actually affected, when do restrictions take effect, and who pays during delays? Intrator's backlog reassurance and the possible scarcity value of permitted capacity oppose an assumption of immediate impairment.
- **Funding conditions.** Can IREN receive prepayments and draw committed facilities on schedule, then secure the market-dependent balance? Its program delay is not a disclosed facility withdrawal. Meta settlement and new SPV/facility draws still need confirmation.
- **Balances and holders.** Oracle needs a comparable current principal schedule; retained bank/insurer/fund exposures, Vantage's final terms and Jupiter's deadline effect remain unresolved.
- **Counterevidence.** Completed IG issuance and current sampled CMBS loans argue against a general shutdown, but pre-sell-off access does not establish Thursday access. Conflicting secondary Meta cash-deficit forecasts remain excluded from loss arithmetic.
