# China: supply competitor, demand pool and policy risk

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Author: Codex/OpenAI · Status: reviewed · Reviewers: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check applied 2026-10-17; Claude (Opus 5.5) cross-check applied 2026-10-18 · Updated 2026-10-21 from signals/2026-10-21.md · Updated 2026-10-25 from signals/2026-10-25.md*

## Summary

- Alibaba's capex was RMB67.678bn in the June 2026 quarter, +75% y/y; it attributes the rise to procurement timing, CPU capacity for anticipated AI-agent adoption and higher chip-component prices. AI-related product revenue was RMB12.376bn, about 18% of that quarter's capex (B; scopes differ).[51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)
- In the June 2026 quarter, cash capex exceeded operating cash at Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu. Tencent's FCF was −RMB13.8bn; management's +RMB37.6bn excluding compute prepayments sets aside RMB51.4bn already paid, while net cash fell from RMB146.9bn to RMB58.2bn. Alibaba's cloud adjusted EBITA was +RMB5.628bn versus −RMB13.861bn in labs/apps; Tencent's non-IFRS operating profit was RMB75.6bn versus RMB86.1bn excluding new AI products (about RMB10.5bn drag, B).[51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm), [8](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Tencent-Announces-2026-Second-Quarter-Results.pdf), [10](https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-announces-second-quarter-2026-results)
- Alibaba announced a minimum RMB380bn three-year infrastructure programme in February 2025 and an unquantified increase that September. Group capex in April 2025–June 2026 totalled RMB193.741bn; the remaining RMB186.259bn equals 2.75 quarters at the June rate (B; group capex includes non-AI items, so this is a pace illustration). ByteDance's reported US$70bn annual 2026 scenario remains unverified and is not directly comparable.[1](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1830678592242057216), [2](https://home.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1911884625546838016), [71](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926060224/tm2614494d1_ex99-1.htm), [51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm), [12](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/805120)
- DeepSeek disclosed US$5.576m of V3 official-run compute in its February 2025 report revision and US$294,000 for specified R1 stages in September 2025; neither is an all-in development budget.[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2), [14](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09422-z/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9422_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- DeepSeek's September 30, 2026 Ascend releases target Ascend 950 and require CANN 9.2.0+ for TileKernels; FlashMLA claims 410/360 TFLOPS prefill/decode, about 95%/83% of peak. DeepInfer, released October 20, adds offline V4.1-Flash inference on Nvidia SM100 and Ascend 950; published benchmarks use Nvidia hardware. Software adaptation advances, while comparable deployment economics, qualified HBM volumes and yields remain unverified.[55](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/TileKernels), [56](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA/blob/main/docs/20260930-ascend-prefill-deep-dive.md), [74](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer)
- On October 17, 2026, Qwen3.7-Plus listed Singapore input/output prices of US$0.40/1.60 per million tokens for inputs up to 256K, before promotions. In mid-September, Chinese labs supplied 69% of OpenRouter tokens while closed-weight models retained 73% of spend, down from 89% in January (one channel, excluding resellers). MiniMax's H1 2026 revenue was US$116.6m, 60.8% outside mainland China, at 17.9% gross margin and US$293.0m adjusted net loss: volume competition coexists with equity-funded lab losses, without establishing a market-wide Western margin decline.[26](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/model-pricing), [63](https://openrouter.ai/data/newsletter/the-token-state-of-play), [64](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600680.pdf)
- Listed-lab equity repriced sharply while private funding continued. Bloomberg reported on October 23 that MiniMax and Zhipu had lost about US$150bn combined from their peaks: Zhipu was 81% below its June high, and MiniMax fell below its IPO price on October 22. Bloomberg attributed the falls to continued token-price cuts, the capability gap and possible DeepSeek/Moonshot listings. MiniMax then rose 16.74% to HK$187.60 on October 23. CSI 300 closed −1.8% on October 22, 20% below its June high. Meanwhile Reuters, relaying Bloomberg, reported DeepSeek near completing about RMB100bn (US$14.98bn) at a RMB500bn pre-money valuation (reported, unverified, C), and a CCTV-affiliated account said DeepSeek and MiniMax had won approval for global use of their models under the new registration regime. This limits that customer channel; it does not prohibit private use.[72](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-20/html/2026-21447.htm), [73](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-20/pdf/2026-21447.pdf)

## Analysis

**Demand and financing.** China expands infrastructure demand, but group capex includes general cloud, CPUs and facilities. Alibaba's fiscal year ends in March; Tencent and Baidu use calendar years. Private-company budgets are prospective. Adding these figures into “China AI capex” would manufacture comparability.

Alibaba's multi-year commitment is a floor, with an announced but unquantified increase. Group capex in April 2025–June 2026 totalled RMB193.741bn. Against the original RMB380bn floor, the illustrative remainder is RMB186.259bn, or 2.75 quarters at the June rate (B; claim 65). This pace could exceed the original floor around early 2027, but group capex includes non-AI items and does not verify spending within the announced programme. Alibaba attributes the June-quarter rise partly to procurement cycles, CPU capacity for anticipated AI-agent adoption and component inflation: expenditure is not clean evidence of proportionate AI-demand or useful-compute growth.[71](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926060224/tm2614494d1_ex99-1.htm), [51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm) Revenue growth is encouraging; negative FCF and the equity placement show shareholders funding expansion and bearing dilution and impairment risk.[1](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1830678592242057216)–[5](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-2029365886510432256), [50](https://home.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-2041268239081668608)

In the June 2026 quarter, Alibaba reported adjusted EBITA of +RMB5.628bn in AI Cloud and Compute Services and −RMB13.861bn in AI Labs and Applications. These segment results distinguish profitable infrastructure services from model/application investment losses; the cloud segment also includes non-AI services. AI-related product revenue was RMB12.376bn, about 18.3% of group capex in the quarter (B); that ratio compares different scopes and is not a return on invested capital. Standalone inference margins and investment-cohort returns remain undisclosed.[51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)

Alibaba's operating cash less group capex was −RMB44.733bn, distinct from reported FCF of −RMB44.670bn: its FCF bridge uses RMB67.660bn of eligible purchases and a +RMB0.045bn buyer-protection-deposit adjustment (claim 46). This is a definition difference, not an arithmetic error.[51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)

Tencent remains profitable, but its Q2 2026 FCF of −RMB13.8bn includes RMB51.4bn of compute prepayments (B); management's +RMB37.6bn counterfactual removes cash already paid for future compute. This shows timing, not reduced investment exposure. Cash capex plus these prepayments was about RMB110.7bn, versus RMB104.1bn of OCF before prepayments (B; group cash capex is not wholly AI). Net cash fell RMB88.669bn from March to June; the non-current prepayments/deposits/other-assets category rose RMB66.663bn over six months, without establishing that the whole category is compute. The full bridge from FCF to the net-cash fall is unverified. Non-IFRS operating profit of RMB75.6bn versus RMB86.1bn excluding new AI products implies about RMB10.5bn of quarterly drag (B), comparable in scale, but not accounting basis, with Alibaba's labs/apps loss. The FCF reconciliation subtracts cash capex, media content and lease payments; prepayments already depress operating cash and must not be subtracted again (claims 44–45). The offering extract's RMB96.3bn half-year cash payments and interim report's RMB84.720bn reported capex are distinct measures.[8](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Tencent-Announces-2026-Second-Quarter-Results.pdf), [62](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/E700_Launch-Announcement.pdf), [7](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/E700_IR.pdf) Management also describes a more favourable cloud pricing environment, evidence against a universal price war. Baidu similarly invests beyond current operating cash; its investment holdings provide liquidity, although “cash and investments” exceeds immediately available cash. These cash shortfalls concern the June quarter's consolidated groups, not normalized annual or AI-only operations.[8](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Tencent-Announces-2026-Second-Quarter-Results.pdf), [10](https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-announces-second-quarter-2026-results)

ByteDance's reported 2025 budget is contested: Reuters reports the figure and ByteDance's explicit rejection. Its May 2026 annual spending scenario and profit estimate remain reported, unverified, and cannot be directly compared with Alibaba's multiyear floor.[11](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusivebytedance-plans-20-billion-capex-in-2025-mostly-on-ai-sources-say-3826261?ampMode=1), [12](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/805120)

Financing already includes debt channels. On October 14, 2026, Tencent announced pricing of US$1.8bn and CNY26bn of notes for general corporate purposes, with issuance expected October 21; this does not allocate every dollar to AI.[52](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/E700_Pricing-Announcement.pdf) Reuters reported a US$29.6bn unsecured ByteDance facility on September 4, with an initial three-year term and extension options; general corporate purposes were communicated to lenders, while AI/offshore allocations came from anonymous sources. This is reported, unverified, not a verified drawdown.[53](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/bytedance-secures-296-billion-loan-in-ai-push-sources-say-4889439) On October 6, Reuters reported a prospective DeepSeek round exceeding RMB80bn, expected to close in October; closing is not established by the article. The July fundraising targeted a RMB500bn valuation after a reported ~US$7.4bn maiden round. Reuters relays Bloomberg's possible RMB100bn total and Tencent/CATL among the largest commitments; all remain reported, unverified, C. Tencent is both a builder and a reported leading DeepSeek investor, so its exposure to model economics extends beyond its own capex.[54](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/deepseek-set-to-net-over-12-billion-in-new-fundraising-source-says-4933887) These reports broaden the potential loss bearers beyond profit-funded expansion without establishing complete financing schedules.

MiniMax provides direct listed-lab evidence: H1 2026 unaudited revenue was US$116.573m (+283.1%), with 60.8% outside mainland China; gross margin was 17.9%, up from 12.1%. R&D of US$296.870m and adjusted net loss of US$293.031m were each about 2.5× revenue (B); cash/equivalents were US$930.905m. The company says financing activities mainly funded cash needs. This supports equity-funded expansion at a thin group gross margin; it does not measure a standalone token-serving margin. Pure-play lab shareholders bear the immediate dilution/loss risk, followed by platform shareholders through AI-product losses and cash commitments; lender exposure also depends on the profitable core businesses.[64](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600680.pdf)

Private labs depend on investors and infrastructure partners; government funding also supports semiconductor manufacturers.[25](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/chinas-memory-chip-makers-ride-ai-boom-to-new-power--and-us-scrutiny-4810472) Shareholders, strategic backers and asset owners bear dilution and impairment risk. Announced Tencent notes and ByteDance's reported unsecured facility make lender exposure concrete as a financing channel, subject to issuance/drawdown; landlords and public funders also bear risk where contracts or guarantees create exposure. The loss allocation remains incompletely disclosed.[52](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/E700_Pricing-Announcement.pdf), [53](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/bytedance-secures-296-billion-loan-in-ai-push-sources-say-4889439)

China is an evidenced margin competitor at the cheaper model tier and a growing domestic supplier, rather than a demonstrated substitute for US infrastructure demand. The June 2026 group-capex comparison is RMB131.852bn for Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu, or US$19.433bn at Alibaba's disclosed RMB6.7851/US$ convenience rate, against US$171.210bn for Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft: 11.35% (B; claim 66). These are differently defined group measures, not a national AI total; Meta includes lease principal and Microsoft includes accrual finance leases. Nvidia's H200 shipments were below 1% of its Data Center revenue in the July quarter, showing how narrow that export channel is, not the share of Chinese capex going to Nvidia. Domestic Ascend, Zhenwu and memory pathways limit the case for China as a backstop Western-chip buyer. **Prices and inference margins.** Open weights enable competing hosts and credible migration threats; cheap APIs can pressure scarcity pricing. The tariff samples establish offers, not a quality-adjusted price series or a causal estimate of global deflation. These A-grade platform disclosures, excluding resellers, show adoption and cheap-tier competitive pressure; extrapolation to the whole market is C. Chinese origin and open weights are overlapping categories, not identical partitions; closed weights are not synonymous with Western origin. Falling spend share in a growing market does not establish falling revenue or causally measure Western margins.[63](https://openrouter.ai/data/newsletter/the-token-state-of-play) MiniMax's 17.9% group gross margin and large R&D loss show the financial strain that can accompany this export strategy, without establishing near-cost pricing at every Chinese lab.[64](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0826/2026082600680.pdf)

Western margins collapse only if substitution works at comparable task success, latency, reliability and governance, and price reductions outrun cost reductions and usage growth. Cheaper tokens can expand agent usage and equipment demand while destroying model rents. Kimi's premium tier and Tencent's pricing commentary contradict universal commoditization.[8](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Tencent-Announces-2026-Second-Quarter-Results.pdf), [27](https://platform.kimi.com/docs/pricing/chat) Standalone inference margins after depreciation, power and unsuccessful outputs, and returns by investment cohort, remain undisclosed; Alibaba's segment EBITA is observable but does not resolve them.[51](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)

Its October 17 update claims shorter runtimes and outputs with unchanged API pricing. Lower task costs can therefore reflect efficiency alongside competitive rent pressure; these vendor claims do not establish realized returns.[57](https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/), [58](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/)

ASML's annual total-sales guide and quarterly system-sales share use different denominators; other makers report different periods. Exposure is not revenue certain to disappear.[45](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/a2026_10x14xpresentation.htm)–[49](https://www.tel.com/ir/irta3a00000006g5-att/fy27q1presentations-e.pdf)

**Taiwan and the bubble question.** TSMC acknowledges geographic concentration and interruption risks. Blockade, quarantine, shipping or energy disruption could stop deliveries without destroying fabs. Overseas expansion does not immediately recreate the manufacturing ecosystem.[42](https://investor.tsmc.com/sites/ir/sec-filings/2025_20F%20Report.pdf)

Taiwan's recent record equity close shows markets still capitalize substantial earnings.[43](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40442826) Growth, discount rates, recovery assumptions and geopolitical risk are jointly priced. No reliable isolated TSMC discount, executable war-insurance quote or market-implied conflict probability was verified. Military counts measure activity, not probabilities.[44](https://www.mnd.gov.tw/en/News/PLAAct/88006)

OpenRouter adoption and CXMT's estimated share gain establish concrete competitive channels; their effect on market-wide Western inference returns remains unmeasured. The June-quarter capex comparison does not support China replacing US infrastructure demand. Profitable platform cores finance rapidly rising commitments, while Alibaba/Tencent AI-product losses and MiniMax's thin margin and large R&D loss place shareholders first in line for impairment and dilution. Debt adds lender exposure, with core-business cash generation relevant to coverage. Taiwan remains a systemic supply tail despite healthy demand. These risk interpretations are C; utilization, incremental returns and security valuations remain inadequate to declare a China-wide AI bubble.
