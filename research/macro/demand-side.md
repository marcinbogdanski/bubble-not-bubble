# AI demand: can end-user revenue service the buildout?

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra (2026-11-06) · Data: [ai-revenue-estimates.csv](/data/ai-revenue-estimates.csv)*

## Summary

- Paid AI demand is large and fast-growing; payback is unproven. Our illustrative end-user revenue reconstruction is ~$160B for 2026 (judgment range $125–200B). It is 2.91× an assumed $55B 2025 base whose balancing remainder is not independently measured (claims 1, 3).
- Anthropic's S-1/A replaces leaked figures: $33.2B filed for January–September plus an assumed $20.0B Q4 gives $53.2B; filed revenue is a fifth of the total. OpenAI's $37B stays reported, unverified (C); the rest is allocation (D) (claims 1, 4–5).
- Anthropic's filing establishes gross marketplace revenue and supports roughly $5B of nine-month channel fees. Our $5B annual overlap deduction assumes those fees are partly included in the cloud allocations; that inclusion is unverified (claim 9).
- Q3 cloud growth was strong: Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103%, AWS +39%. These totals mix non-AI demand, enterprise AI, lab compute and, at Google, TPU systems. Their proportions are not disclosed; retaining the $44B non-lab allocation is a D assumption (claim 7).
- The conditional December exit pace is ~$250B. With 2027–28 endpoints held, base annual growth is 87.5% then 60.0%. The bear path grows annually but lies below that common central exit assumption (claims 30–32).
- The payback gap is not measured: a hypothetical $750B cohort needs $295–468B of annual owner revenue; base 2028 gives owners $192–288B (claims 29, 33).
- Adoption is broad but measured on different populations (Census 19.8%; Ramp 55.9% of its customers). Value evidence is mixed (claims 16–18, 25, 28).
- Biggest uncertainty: no filed OpenAI accounts, no invoice bridge across labs, clouds and apps, and no measured owner share of end-user spending.

## Analysis

**How the $160B is built.** Final spending comes from operating budgets, households and advertisers. App, lab and cloud sales overlap, so supplier invoices cannot be added. The reconstruction aims to count each final dollar once, by payer; deduplication is unverified. Lab receipts count on each lab's convention: OpenAI nets certain partner sales; Anthropic books marketplace sales gross (claims 4–5). Clouds count only AI sold to non-lab customers, because cloud AI figures include lab compute: Microsoft's revenue from OpenAI was $10.0B in the September quarter (claim 7). Marketplace fees on Anthropic's gross sales are the same end-customer dollar, so the part inside the cloud rows is deducted (claim 9). Apps count net of payments to labs and clouds already counted upstream (claim 8). The components sum to $159.2B, rounded to $160B (claim 2). It is an allocation, not an invoice census; overlaps remain D.

Anthropic's nine months are filed; its Q4 and OpenAI's H2 are assumed (claims 4–5). Q3 cloud growth was strong: Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103%, AWS +39%. These totals mix non-AI demand, enterprise AI, lab compute and, at Google, TPU systems. Their proportions are not disclosed; retaining the $44B non-lab allocation is a D assumption (claim 7).

The boundary leaves two things out: externally financed or subsidy-funded use is not excluded, and internal savings and ad gains fund owned infrastructure without appearing as sold AI products. The narrow product numerator cannot settle returns on broad corporate capex.

**Adoption and realized value.** Census measures employer businesses' reported use (19.8%); Ramp, payments among its customers (55.9%); the gap is not decomposed (claims 16–17). Ramp's September rebound and CloudZero's rising AI bill share (claims 18, 20) do not support a market-wide stall, though neither measures economy-wide dollars. CloudZero's 87% planning higher budgets but 10% net-new funding means expansion and substitution at once.

Value evidence is uneven. Task studies show gains in service and coding, METR a slowdown (claim 25). Consumer apps monetize better but retain worse, and selected app margins of ~25% challenge software-style economics (claims 27–28).

**Paths and payback.** The paths are stress scenarios, not forecasts (claim 30). Bear annual revenue grows from $125B to $170B to $210B, while remaining below the separate central $250B December 2026 exit assumption; contraction from a bear-specific exit is not established. The base path's +87.5% and +60.0% follow from chosen endpoints, not from the deployment narrative (claim 31). The lower 2026 base raises the growth needed to reach the report's $1.6–2.7T end-user hurdle to ~78–157% a year (claim 31).

A 40–60% steady-state owner capture is assumed, not observed (claim 33). Anthropic's compute expense and Microsoft's recognized OpenAI-related revenue have different scopes from final customer spending, and Microsoft's figure includes revenue share. External financing can fund upstream purchases, but these observations do not measure cash receipts or identify steady-state owner capture (claims 7, 11, 13). Base 2028 gives owners $192–288B, below the $536–848B a hypothetical $1.36T new cohort would need (claim 29). Populations, lives and timing are not matched, so this is not a measured gap. At 60% capture the bull case's $480B clears Goldman's $417B; at 40%, its $320B does not (claims 33–34).

Outside estimates use different revenue boundaries and time bases. IoT forecasts company AI revenue at a quarter-end pace; Gartner estimates cumulative revenue attributable to data centers; SemiAnalysis forecasts lab revenue. None directly calibrates our annual infrastructure-owner capture without a reconciliation. In particular, the IoT ratio does not establish capture above 60%, and numerical ordering against Gartner does not place its model between our base and bull paths.

## Open questions

- **Market total.** Neither a verified 2025 component backcast nor a complete 2026 allocation/elimination bridge exists. OpenAI's quarters remain reported, unverified, and both labs' Q4 sales are assumed.
- **Marketplace booking.** Anthropic's policy supports fee-only treatment of its collection-agent arrangements, but each cloud AI metric and our allocation need reconciliation. The full-gross-overlap stress is about $141B centrally; a single cloud's effect depends on its channel share. The $125–200B headline band is judgmental and does not cover every accounting stress.
- **Anthropic exit.** Year-end below $100B (Euronews) versus $110B (Reuters analysts); the filing's run-rate figure is blank (claim 12).
- **Capture.** What owners retain once external funding slows; the available ratios do not measure steady-state capture (claims 7, 13, 33).
- Would settle these: OpenAI filed accounts; AI-only cloud revenue net of lab purchases and fees; representative renewal data.
