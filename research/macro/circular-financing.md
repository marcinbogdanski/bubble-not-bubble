# Circular and vendor financing in the AI stack

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Author: Codex (GPT-6) · Status: reviewed · Reviewers: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check applied 2026-10-17; Claude (Opus 5.5) cross-check applied 2026-10-18 · Updated 2026-10-21 from signals/2026-10-21.md · Updated 2026-10-25 from signals/2026-10-25.md*

## Summary

- The IMF maps ~$300bn equity, $650bn debt and $1.4tn future orders. The financing channels match this register; exact deal overlap is unverified. Its “wrong-way risk” warning concerns simultaneous investment and customer-revenue impairment. [IMF]
- At July 26, 2026, NVIDIA disclosed $36bn of capacity-support commitments, typically lasting six years; these purchase obligations differ from supplier equity and guarantees. [NQ]
- NVIDIA’s August 17, 2026 PORTS guarantee has a $105bn initial cap. Broadcom separately disclosed a $29bn maximum backstop and up to $42bn of conditional convertible notes at August 2; these are not additive loss exposures. North Star remains reported, unverified. [NGUAR] [BQ] [NSTAR]
- Amazon’s July 30, 2026 filing confirms completion of its $50bn OpenAI investment after June-end. Microsoft disclosed approximately 25% ownership at June-end versus approximately 27% at recapitalization. The 10-K attributes the ownership decrease to recapitalization and other funding activity, without a complete capitalization bridge. [AMQ] [MOWN] [MAZ]
- Disclosed maximum supplier guarantees total about $193bn at the latest quarter-ends: NVIDIA $105bn (PORTS, effective from FY2029) plus $3.5bn, Alphabet $43.8bn of data-centre backstops plus $7.6bn, Broadcom $29bn, AMD $4.1bn. These are contingent caps payable on covered defaults, not loans or expected losses. [NGUAR] [NQ] [GOOGQ] [BQ] [AQ]
- Suppliers reported about $190bn of equity-investment gains across mixed 2026 reporting periods (Alphabet $113.6bn H1, Amazon $62.8bn H1, NVIDIA $7.5bn H1, Microsoft $6.5bn FY2026); a lab down round could reverse gains at the same time as orders fall. These are mixed-period portfolio/accounting measures, not gains all attributable to named labs (claim 60). [GOOGQ] [AMQ] [NQ] [MOWN]
- NVIDIA's CoreWeave capacity support can be terminated if CoreWeave enters insolvency, and Google's Fluidstack backstops take effect only at lease commencement: support is weakest in exactly the states where it would be needed, while effective investment-grade guarantees turn a covered lab default into a supplier loss rather than a project default. [CBACK] [WULFQ]
- SpaceX's SI segment turned from a $1.257bn Q2 operating loss to $431m Q3 operating profit, but Q3 SI capex was $16.444bn. September related-party financing totaled $12.897bn versus June's $13.329bn Valor balance; the September exhibit does not name the creditor (claims 64–65). [SPCX3] [SPCX2]
- S&P's 19 October warning highlights undisclosed interconnections, without quantifying hidden debt or announcing a rating action. OpenAI's 21 October Google Cloud private preview can consume existing cloud commitments; this distribution channel is not new financing (claims 66–67). [SPOPACITY] [GOAI]
- Meta’s September combined Louisiana/Texas ventures carry $68.404bn maximum exposure, including $41.493bn conditional residual-value guarantees. This is an exposure inventory, not funded principal; its broader perimeter supersedes the Hyperion-only view (claim 26). Meta also reported negative $2.120bn Q3 FCF and raised its 2026 capex floor (claim 71). [METAQ] [METAEX]
- Bank access remained selective: Nebius disclosed a $3.5bn secured facility and TeraWulf added about $150m committed revolver capacity. Blackstone describes predominantly long-term, investment-grade datacenter exposure; its portfolio view and Huang’s capital-scarcity comments do not establish new funded demand (claims 73–76). [NEBCITI] [WULFOCT] [BXCALL] [HUANG]

**What the IMF counts beyond this register.** Funding-side corporate borrowing and third-party bank/bond/project debt are not comprehensively recorded as separate links here. The register identifies support and selected facilities, but often omits the borrowing that support enables. The [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets) maps some of those instruments separately; its balances cannot be added to the IMF total. Which named debts make up the IMF-only remainder cannot be established from anonymous nodes. Conversely, this register includes later announcements, conditional instruments and boundary cases whose IMF inclusion is unknown. The sample counts cannot establish relative completeness.

Our inference is correlated impairment across investments, orders and support. Contract terms still determine recovery; neither inventory identifies demand that would vanish without financing.

**Attribution.** Claims 38, 39 and 63 are accounting-scale illustrations using selected historical announcements and assumed spending paths. They are not estimates or bounds of financing-dependent demand: the NVIDIA and AMD cases omit debt and other equity enabled by supplier support, while the backlog cases do not reconcile original contract values to remaining obligations. The small equity-basket percentages cannot dismiss financing dependence. Revenue flows and RPO stocks are different measures; annualizing a quarter does not produce trailing annual revenue. Mechanical arithmetic is B; scenario assumptions keep the overall claims at C.

Modern SPVs and guarantees extend this mechanism. Cisco’s inventory charge measures overordering, not vendor loans. Historical commitments and modern caps are neither realized losses nor comparable without adjusting for company scale. [LUC] [NORT] [CISCO]

**Single points of failure.** OpenAI/Anthropic concentrate upstream compute purchases and fundraising; final demand comes from their end users. Microsoft anchors multiple neoclouds; NVIDIA supplies hardware, equity and contingent purchases; Broadcom/Google concentrate TPU financing. Energization delays can block customer payments and funding while construction costs continue. Refinancing and equipment values transmit common shocks: SPVs cannot diversify a simultaneous fall in compute rents. Microsoft’s nearly 90% cloud-revenue share outside frontier-model companies and 25% RPO growth excluding OpenAI (claim 48) counter a two-lab explanation of the whole cloud business; neither isolates AI-only demand.
