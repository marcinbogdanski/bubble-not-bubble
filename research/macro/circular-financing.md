# Circular and vendor financing in the AI stack

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [circular-financing-deals.csv](/data/circular-financing-deals.csv)*

## Summary

- Investor capital funds lab spending that returns to cloud and chip suppliers. The IMF maps approximately $300B equity, $650B debt and $1.4T future orders; exact overlap with the local register is unverified (claims 1–3).
- Disclosed supplier guarantee caps total $193.0B across mixed dates and triggers. These are contingent maximums, not loans or expected losses (claim 4).
- Support can weaken during distress. NVIDIA can terminate CoreWeave capacity orders after persistent insolvency proceedings; Google’s Fluidstack backstops start at lease commencement. Broadcom’s reported customer facility may restrict access during defaults (claims 7, 11, 40).
- Suppliers recorded approximately $190B of mixed-period portfolio and dilution gains. Lab funding, orders and investment values can weaken together; not all gains are attributable to labs (claim 32).
- Profitability and financing needs coexist. SpaceX SI earned $431M operating profit against $16.444B capex. Broad Microsoft cloud demand and selective bank access counter a uniformly weak picture (claims 31, 33, 36–37).
- The biggest uncertainty is how much demand would disappear without supplier support. The selected $44B NVIDIA equity scenario gives 1.1–2.3% of annualized revenue, but excludes guarantees, leverage and unidentified investments and cannot measure that dependence (claim 28).

## Analysis

Investor capital flows to labs, then cloud providers and equipment suppliers. End-user subscriptions subsequently fund the labs. Adding these intermediate sales repeatedly would overstate final demand. These links establish neither fictitious accounting nor independent demand. The Cerebras loan credits and CoreWeave customer stock show why cash receipts, revenue and incentives must be separated (claims 24–25).

The IMF map and local deal register describe matching channels, but their membership and dollar totals cannot be reconciled. Corporate borrowing and third-party project debt extend beyond the local supplier-support inventory. The [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets) covers those instruments separately. No total here measures demand that would vanish without financing. The NVIDIA scenario excludes leverage, guarantees and unidentified investments; original contracts also cannot be equated with remaining backlog (claim 28).

Vendors collect cash earlier while cloud equity and lenders bear construction, utilization and refinancing risk. Guarantees return covered losses upstream when orders and collateral values may also weaken. PORTS exposure starts with applicable lease phases and declines with payments; assumption, reletting, sale and early termination affect recovery (claim 5). Broadcom’s backstop depends on rack recoveries (claim 8). NVIDIA’s CoreWeave termination right and Google’s lease-commencement condition leave important states uncovered (claims 7, 11). Meta’s conditional residual-value guarantees depend on nonrenewal or termination, residual shortfall and other conditions. Its venture inventory includes rent and equity, so it cannot be added as funded principal (claim 19).

Conditional liquidity is a separate vulnerability. Reported Anthropic lease acceleration can coincide with reduced facility access (claim 40). North Star’s proposed junior yield is not a borrowing rate for all labs, and its overlap with filed Broadcom facilities remains unresolved (claims 9–10). Investment marks can weaken alongside customer funding and purchases, but preferred rights and accounting methods determine whether prior gains reverse (claim 32).

Present profitability does not establish resilience to a financing shock; corporate losses do not establish lifetime project returns. SpaceX’s operating profit coexists with a larger build requirement (claim 31). Microsoft’s broad cloud customer base counters a lab-only account of demand, without isolating AI demand (claim 33). Nebius and TeraWulf demonstrate selective financing access; neither proves execution of NVIDIA’s financing-platform target (claims 35–37). Sustainable returns still require utilization, customer collections and refinancing to support the assets after supplier assistance.

Common customers connect otherwise separate obligors. OpenAI stress can reach Oracle and Oracle-tenanted vehicles. CoreWeave stress can reach Core Scientific and Applied Digital. Anthropic links TPU financing and several landlords. Effective guarantees can interrupt project-default paths while transferring losses to suppliers; unfinished phases remain exposed. Distinct obligors in the [forecast register](/forecasts/register) can therefore share a common cause. Meta and xAI/SpaceX add roots independent of the frontier-lab pair. The telecom precedent illustrates the financing channel, not a forecast of comparable losses (claim 34).

## Open questions

The IMF’s anonymous firms, deal membership and paid-versus-committed treatment remain unreconciled. Investee purchases, funding flows and remaining obligations are needed to estimate financing dependence.

Broadcom names no customer for its conditional notes; Reuters identifies Anthropic. North Star lacks executed terms and an overlap bridge. Alphabet’s unnamed equity commitment does not verify the reported investment headline. NVIDIA’s capacity-support overlap with CoreWeave remains unverified; funding completion rests on secondary reporting. Microsoft’s ownership change lacks a complete capitalization bridge. SpaceX’s September related-party creditor remains unnamed.

Guarantee collateral, fees, realized losses and construction-stage recourse remain incomplete. A comprehensive related-party revenue matrix and an audit of gross-versus-net treatment across the stack are unavailable.
