# Circular and vendor financing in the AI stack

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [circular-financing-deals.csv](/data/circular-financing-deals.csv)*

## Summary

- Investor capital funds lab spending that returns to cloud and chip suppliers. The IMF maps approximately $300B equity, $650B debt and $1.4T future orders; exact overlap with the local register is unverified (claims 1–3).
- Anthropic's XPU structure has $63B committed financing and a separate, contingent Broadcom backstop capped at approximately $43B. Principal, lease payments and guarantees describe overlapping exposures, not additive spending (claims 10, 41).
- Alphabet discloses $48B executed datacenter backstops and separate energy guarantees. Google has also executed support for TeraWulf's Anthropic-leased project, but its guarantee cap and funded debt are undisclosed (claims 11, 44).
- Conditional support can weaken during distress. Anthropic's filing verifies that rent acceleration can coincide with restricted access to Broadcom's note facility; NVIDIA can terminate CoreWeave orders after persistent insolvency proceedings (claims 7, 9, 40).
- Supplier equity support extends to Meta. The [NVIDIA](/research/stack/nvidia) and [custom-silicon](/research/stack/amd-broadcom-custom-silicon) notes own the investment amounts; Meta's forward structure separates investor purchases from cash received by the issuer (claims 42–43).
- Financing remains available selectively. The [Amazon](/research/stack/amazon), [other-labs](/research/stack/labs-other) and [neocloud](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) notes cover supplier-funded capex, SpaceX's conditional lease commitment and IREN's closed equipment refinance. The biggest uncertainty remains how much demand would disappear without supplier support.

## Analysis

Investor capital flows to labs, then cloud providers and equipment suppliers. End-user subscriptions subsequently fund the labs. Adding these intermediate sales repeatedly would overstate final demand. These links establish neither fictitious accounting nor independent demand. The Cerebras loan credits and CoreWeave customer stock show why cash receipts, revenue and incentives must be separated (claims 24–25).

The IMF map and local deal register describe matching channels, but their membership and dollar totals cannot be reconciled. Corporate borrowing and third-party project debt extend beyond the supplier-support inventory. The [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets) owns issuance and credit-market measures. No total here measures demand that would vanish without financing; original contracts also cannot be equated with remaining backlog.

The Anthropic XPU structure demonstrates that banks and private credit will finance equipment with supplier support. Broadcom retains residual-value risk while banks arrange first-lien financing and Blackstone provides the committed second-lien portion (claims 10, 41). The backstop increases with deployment and declines with lease payments and recoveries. It is neither funded equity nor the transaction's principal. The separate AI XPV arrangement remains in claim 8; the disclosures do not provide a complete overlap bridge across that arrangement, the new SPV, Anthropic's leases and its conditional notes. No combined guarantee total is defensible from these records alone.

Guarantees can return losses upstream when orders and collateral values weaken together. PORTS exposure starts with applicable lease phases; assumption, reletting, sale and termination affect recoveries (claim 5). NVIDIA's CoreWeave insolvency termination right leaves an important state uncovered (claim 7). Google's Justified support is executed, but project debt is anticipated and construction milestones remain ahead; the lease headline does not disclose the guarantee amount (claim 44). Meta's residual-value guarantees depend on nonrenewal or termination, residual shortfall and other conditions, while its venture inventory includes rent and equity (claim 19).

Conditional liquidity is a distinct vulnerability. Anthropic directly discloses that lease default can accelerate rent while preventing note issuance. PIK interest and less favorable conversion terms when revenue conditions fail increase the cost of using that facility; unused capacity is not unrestricted cash (claims 9, 40). Google and Amazon also release financing against conditions rather than supplying unconditional liquidity (claims 17–18). Anthropic's aggregate commitments and payment schedule belong in the [Anthropic note](/research/stack/anthropic), not a sum of the overlapping contracts here.

Supplier equity now reaches a profitable hyperscaler. Meta's prospectus expects no initial issuer proceeds from shares sold by forward sellers. Neither NVIDIA's announcement nor AMD's unnamed completed investment establishes cash received by Meta (claims 42–43). [Amazon's supplier facility](/research/stack/amazon) and [Oracle's planned vendor financing, customer hardware and prepayments](/research/stack/oracle) extend the same cash-timing problem beyond private labs. These arrangements can finance real demand while shifting repayment and residual-value risk.

The strongest counterevidence to a generalized funding freeze is actual access: the committed Anthropic structure, [IREN's completed refinancing](/research/stack/neoclouds-other), and the conditional [SpaceX lease commitment](/research/stack/labs-other). They do not establish utilization or lifetime returns. Broad cloud demand and operating results remain in the company notes. Investment marks can weaken alongside customer funding and purchases, but preferred rights and accounting methods govern how losses emerge.

Common customers connect otherwise separate obligors. OpenAI stress can reach Oracle and its tenanted vehicles; CoreWeave stress can reach its landlords; Anthropic links TPU financing and multiple campuses. Effective guarantees can interrupt project defaults while transferring losses to suppliers. The [Meta-linked project outlook changes](/research/macro/debt-markets) illustrate counterparty transmission without a payment default. Distinct obligors in the [forecast register](/forecasts/register) can share a common cause.

## Open questions

The IMF's anonymous firms, deal membership and paid-versus-committed treatment remain unreconciled. Investee purchases, funding flows and remaining obligations are needed to estimate financing dependence.

What portions of Broadcom's AI XPV, Anthropic SPV, leases and note facility overlap? The borrower now identifies its facility, but a complete allocation of retained exposure is unavailable. Google's Justified guarantee cap, triggers and relation to Alphabet's aggregate backstops remain undisclosed in the release.

NVIDIA's capacity-support overlap with CoreWeave remains unverified; OpenAI funding completion still rests on secondary reporting. Meta investment settlement and issuer cash receipt need separate evidence. [Nscale guarantee talks](/research/stack/nvidia) and [OpenAI fundraising targets](/research/stack/openai) remain reported, unverified, grade C; neither establishes a signed guarantee or closed round. SpaceX's September related-party creditor remains unnamed.

Guarantee collateral, fees, realized losses and construction-stage recourse remain incomplete. A comprehensive related-party revenue matrix and gross-versus-net audit across the stack are unavailable.
