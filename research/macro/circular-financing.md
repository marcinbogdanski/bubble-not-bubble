# Circular and vendor financing in the AI stack

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Author: Codex (GPT-6) · Status: reviewed · Reviewer: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check, applied 2026-10-17*

## Summary

- At July 26, 2026, NVIDIA disclosed $36bn of capacity-support commitments, typically lasting six years; these purchase obligations differ from supplier equity and guarantees. [NQ]
- NVIDIA’s August 17, 2026 PORTS guarantee has a $105bn initial cap. Broadcom separately disclosed a $29bn maximum backstop and up to $42bn of conditional convertible notes at August 2; these are not additive loss exposures. North Star remains reported, unverified. [NGUAR] [BQ] [NSTAR]
- Amazon’s July 30, 2026 filing confirms completion of its $50bn OpenAI investment after June-end. Microsoft disclosed approximately 25% ownership at June-end versus approximately 27% at recapitalization, without a complete capitalization bridge. [AMQ] [MOWN] [MAZ]
- AMD earned $1.990bn of GAAP operating income in Q2 2026; CoreWeave lost $626m on $2.575bn revenue and incurred $640m net interest expense. These corporate results do not establish lifetime returns on individual projects. [ARESULT] [CQ]
- CoreWeave’s March 20, 2025 prospectus treats its $350m stock issuance to OpenAI as an offset against related revenue. Equipment sales, cloud revenue and lab revenue cannot be summed as independent final demand. [CS1]
- The October 17, 2026 NVIDIA scenario uses a selected $44bn equity basket and assumed spending patterns. Its 1.1–2.3% result does not measure demand that would disappear without supplier financing (claim 38).

## Analysis

**Scope and accounting.** The CSV covers requested relationships; private side letters remain incomplete. Purchase contracts close financing loops but do not alone establish vendor financing. Intel and Groq are boundary cases: supplier integration and technology acquisition, respectively.

Microsoft disclosed $24.1bn of FY2026 revenue from OpenAI, including revenue sharing, and $6.0bn of receivables at June 30. These establish commercial exposure, not revenue causally attributable to Microsoft’s investment; a comprehensive cross-stack matrix remains unavailable. [MOWN]

Investor capital funds lab cloud spending and upstream servers; later subscriptions fund the lab. Adding these sales repeatedly counts intermediate transactions. This alone establishes neither fictitious accounting nor independent demand. Equity, guarantees and backlog are not current revenue. Cerebras’s H1 OpenAI-arrangement revenue of $74.4m is net of $3.3m of OpenAI-specific warrant amortization; its $86.3m of non-cash loan credits are a separate quantity, not cash receipts or an amount to add to revenue. The $46.3m warrant reduction in claim 24 covers all customers. CoreWeave likewise offsets associated revenue for the stock issued to OpenAI. These disclosures illustrate customer-incentive accounting without auditing gross-versus-net treatment across the stack. [CBQ] [CS1]

Claim 46 requires consistent revenue definitions. [ARR]

**Attribution.** Claims 38–39 are accounting-scale illustrations using selected historical announcements and assumed spending paths. They are not estimates or bounds of financing-dependent demand: the NVIDIA and AMD cases omit debt and other equity enabled by supplier support, while the backlog cases do not reconcile original contract values to remaining obligations. The small equity-basket percentages cannot dismiss financing dependence. Revenue flows and RPO stocks are different measures; annualizing a quarter does not produce trailing annual revenue. Mechanical arithmetic is B; scenario assumptions keep the overall claims at C.

NVIDIA’s August 10 financing-platform MOUs target more than $500bn of third-party capital, rather than committed or deployed financing. Its July 26 equity values and commitments (claim 43) also exceed the selected basket’s scope; they are not cash-investment totals or sales numerators. Together these disclosures make financing access a material issue beyond the equity scenario. [NPLATFORM] [NQ]

- **NVIDIA:** the basket adds OpenAI/Anthropic announced equity and incremental CoreWeave/Nebius investments. Assume half to all returns as NVIDIA revenue, evenly over the scenario window, freezing quarterly revenue. Unidentified investments, leverage and guarantees are excluded; supported sales could be much larger. Investee purchases and a complete paid-in reconciliation are undisclosed. Claim 45 updates reported funding status. [NPAID] Capacity support is potentially overlapping exposure, not another sales numerator. [NLOI] [ROUND] [NQ]
- **AMD:** assume none to all of the Anthropic equity commitment returns as hardware revenue over the same window. The resulting range excludes the potentially much larger purchase programmes incentivized by warrants: no customer-level revenue or warrant fair-value reconciliation permits a defensible percentage for those programmes. Unvested warrants do not prove the absence of supported orders. [AQ] [AANT]
- **Broadcom:** the gross transaction/backlog ratio assumes the entire financed equipment transaction remains in Broadcom RPO. The narrower content range is an explicit sensitivity, not disclosed silicon economics. Google equipment pass-through, already delivered racks and software backlog change the answer. Do not substitute the guarantee cap or note facility for sales, or add North Star without reconciling overlap. [BQ] [BXPV]
- **Oracle:** the $300bn scenario uses a rounded floor of the disclosed amount exceeding $300bn. The ratio assumes that amount is an OCI purchase commitment still included in current RPO. Company confirmation of partnership size does not provide that reconciliation. This is indirect exposure to supplier-funded OpenAI, not proof Oracle itself financed that fraction. [OSTAR] [ORESULT]
- **CoreWeave:** direct backstop and broader OpenAI agreement ratios are gross contract-equivalent sensitivities. Recognition to date, capacity resales, variable consideration and overlapping supported capacity reduce the retained backlog numerator. Adding them compares two selected historical contracts; it is neither a verified RPO split nor an upper bound on all financing-sensitive business. [CBACK] [OCORE] [CQ]

**Returns and loss allocation.** Current supplier profits and cash generation demonstrate present operating strength; they do not establish solvency under a financing shock. CoreWeave’s consolidated losses demonstrate a current financing burden, not the lifetime return of every contracted project (claim 37). Vendors receive cash earlier; cloud equity/lenders absorb utilization, execution and refinancing risks. Guarantees return tail losses upstream when hardware demand and collateral values may also fall.

Oracle reported negative $5bn Q1 FY2027 free cash flow despite operating profit, and completed a $20bn equity sale before commissions. Profitability therefore coexists with external funding needs. [ORESULT]

NVIDIA’s phased PORTS guarantee provides assumption/reletting/sale remedies after default or insolvency. Exposure grows as applicable phases enter service, declines with lease payments, and may terminate before twenty years, including when OpenAI achieves a satisfactory credit rating. The cap is maximum contingent exposure, not expected loss. OpenAI’s indemnity may be uncollectible during distress. Broadcom’s backstop also depends on collateral recovery. Lender protection does not remove system risk. [NGUAR] [BQ]

Reuters’ October 1 account identifies Anthropic as the note-facility borrower and reports $125.2bn of five-year TPU leases, restricted collateral and default-related acceleration that can coincide with restricted facility access (claim 44; C, reported, unverified). This adds liquidity risk: financing may be least accessible when urgently needed. It is separate evidence from North Star; the proposed junior-tranche yield is not a borrowing rate for all AI labs. [R42] [NSTAR]

**Historical comparison.** Lucent/Nortel financed customers as external capital constrained expansion. Nortel warned that retreating lenders could leave financing on its books longer. Modern SPVs and guarantees extend this mechanism. Cisco’s inventory charge measures overordering, not vendor loans. Historical commitments and modern caps are neither realized losses nor comparable without adjusting for company scale. [LUC] [NORT] [CISCO]

**Single points of failure.** OpenAI/Anthropic concentrate upstream compute purchases and fundraising; final demand comes from their end users. Microsoft anchors multiple neoclouds; NVIDIA supplies hardware, equity and contingent purchases; Broadcom/Google concentrate TPU financing. Energization delays can block customer payments and funding while construction costs continue. Refinancing and equipment values transmit common shocks: SPVs cannot diversify a simultaneous fall in compute rents. Microsoft’s nearly 90% cloud-revenue share outside frontier-model companies and 25% RPO growth excluding OpenAI (claim 48) counter a two-lab explanation of the whole cloud business; neither isolates AI-only demand. [MCALL]

## Conflicts and open questions

**Different assessments of financing dependence.** The original Summary said supplier support was “increasingly” prevalent; the reviewer finds dated examples but no comparable aggregate time series establishing that trend. The original note emphasized the narrow equity-basket scenarios and excluded guarantees, leverage and unidentified investments. The [independent Codex cross-check](/reviews/circular-financing-codex-xcheck.md) places more weight on financing-enabled expansion and conditional liquidity: neither the small scenario percentages nor the exclusions quantify demand that would disappear without support. Both views retain uncertainty about causal dependence; identifying it requires investee purchases, funding flows, overlap reconciliation and a counterfactual.

**Profitability and breadth of demand.** The original note judged supplier profitability to contradict a stack-wide insolvency thesis and described CoreWeave’s losses as demonstrating a customer-return problem. The reviewer’s narrower view is that present profits do not establish solvency under a financing shock, while corporate losses do not establish lifetime project returns. Oracle’s funding needs reinforce this distinction (claims 37, 47). The original note called profitable suppliers and growing usage the strongest counterevidence; the reviewer also emphasizes available commercial links, cloud breadth and guarantee remedies. Microsoft reported nearly 90% of FY2026 Microsoft Cloud revenue from customers outside frontier-model companies, while RPO excluding OpenAI grew 25%. These are meaningful counterevidence to a two-lab account of the whole cloud business, but neither isolates AI-only final demand. [ORESULT] [MCALL]

**Ownership and funding status.** The original note said Microsoft’s ownership “was subsequently diluted”; the reviewer finds the later disclosed percentage lower but no complete capitalization bridge establishing the cause or exact dilution. Its headline funding commitment also exceeds cash funded. Amazon’s June-end OpenAI figure excludes a disclosed subsequent completion; its Anthropic remaining facility ceiling is released against compute milestones, with subsequent equity participation reducing the ceiling. NVIDIA funding completion remains unverified (claim 45). [MOWN] [MAZ] [AMQ] [NPAID]

**Broadcom transaction linkage and source disagreement.** The original claim 14 placed the AI XPV transaction and filed backstop together under a single A grade. The reviewer verifies each separate disclosure at A but grades an assumed one-to-one linkage C; that linkage remains unresolved. The public note facility is A-grade but unnamed; Reuters’ October 1 report identifies Anthropic and adds lease/collateral/default restrictions at C (claim 44), rather than confirming the identity through the fetched primary filing. North Star’s reported senior guarantee lacks a fetched executed contract and must not be added without reconciling overlap. Its reported coupon and yield describe a proposed junior tranche. [BXPV] [BQ] [R42] [NSTAR]

**Accounting and demand interpretation.** The original note’s ordinary supply-chain accounting explanation remains a conceptual caution; the reviewer says it cannot serve as a blanket audit of gross-versus-net treatment. Customer incentives reduce recognized revenue in the disclosed examples (claims 25, 49), with no stack-wide accounting audit established. The original phrase “concentrate final demand” treated labs as end users; the reviewer identifies them as intermediaries concentrating upstream purchases and financing. The graph now distinguishes those roles. Groq’s reported headline differs from filed consideration; no customer-financing loop was established. [CBQ] [CS1] [NK]

Unresolved: investee-level NVIDIA sales; guarantee collateral/fees and realized losses; remaining lab contracts; a complete paid-equity reconciliation; programme overlap. Some SEC access remains indexed-only, but Microsoft’s full filing was retrieved directly over HTTP for this update. No comprehensive related-party revenue matrix was verified, despite the measured Microsoft–OpenAI link in claim 41; economic links do not automatically meet accounting definitions. The review’s preliminary Broadcom/OpenAI financing-talks lead supplies neither a fetched executed agreement nor an amount used here.
