## Summary

- On September 17, 2026, CoreWeave disclosed 3–6-month cluster contracts at about $40m/MW-year without naming a GPU generation; its North America H100 spot list was $2.464/GPU-hour on October 17. These different products support segmented pricing, without measuring a market-wide shortage or glut or establishing H100/A100 contract economics. ([row 20][s21], [row 9][s9])
- October 24, 2026 OCPI settlements were H100 $3.02, H200 $6.20, B200 $9.57 and A100 $1.08/GPU-hour. Recovered Silicon SDH100RT card/chart values reach $2.88 dated October 24; the stale $2.53 FAQ and weekend/freshness conflict remain explicit. Dated publications do not measure utilization or complete-month coverage. Posted-price aggregators remain separate instruments. ([rows 1, 3, 64, 66][s4], [Silicon][s51], [OCPI method][s43])
- OCPI H100/B200 October 1–24 daily-print means were $2.8921/$8.1338 versus September $2.8293/$7.2450 (+2.2%/+12.3%). B200 firming continues; its $9.58 October 23 high is bounded by the displayed July–October window. Historical H200 October-to-20 mean $5.860 and A100 October-to-16 $0.927 retain their dates; H200 representativeness remains D. These are partial-month daily-print averages. ([rows 5, 32, 56–59][s5], [B200][s55])
- CoreWeave's Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin was 59%, but its GAAP operating margin was −2% ($49m loss), with D&A at 54.1% of revenue ($1,393m), net interest expense of $640m and a $626m net loss. These figures expose thin operating coverage without establishing lifetime capital returns. Its covered-capacity NVIDIA backstop and NVIDIA's $36bn AI-cloud commitments can protect receipts without measuring independent end-user demand; their overlap is unresolved. ([rows 18, 51][s18], [row 44][s49], [row 45][s50])
- SemiAnalysis's one-year H100 midpoint rose from $1.70 in October 2025 to $2.35 in March 2026, while its composite stayed at $2.80–2.84 over those months, down from $6.62 in 2H 2023 (−57.7%). The recovery is in term prices within a C-grade survey; it does not validate company-wide useful lives. ([rows 16, 52–53][s17])
- American Compute's October 2026 appraisal, using data through August, reports a typical realized used H100 SXM sale at $14,250 and projects 2028 residuals of 5–7% of original list conservatively, or 7–14% moderately. This C-grade vendor appraisal needs independent sale records; Silicon Data's modeled residuals are not completed sales. ([row 54][s52], [row 30][s32])
- Paid GPU-hours and comparable renewal prices remain the main missing denominators: CoreWeave's 98% committed-revenue share in Q2 2026 and SF Compute's September 9, 2026 average 25% resale rebate measure neither productive utilization nor fleet occupancy. ([row 18][s19], [row 15][s15])

### Contract economics, utilization and losses

The [CoreWeave note](/research/stack/coreweave)'s revenue/MW quotient combines quarterly revenue with ending active power. Ramping and hardware mix distort it. The [Nebius note](/research/stack/neoclouds-other)'s $20–25m yield describes four landmark deals, with an overall Q2 average above $20m/MW-year; most associated capacity arrives late 2026 and contributes mainly in 2027. These numbers are not comparable tariffs. CoreWeave's subsequent short-term disclosure independently supports a premium for near-term clusters, but names no GPU generation; it cannot establish H100 or A100 contract economics. ([18][s18], [19][s19], [20][s20], [21][s21])

**Rate = annual revenue per MW × kW per GPU / (1,000 × 8,760)** at full-year paid availability. Retrieved DGX maximum powers produce row 21's conditional examples. Networking, storage, headroom, PUE, actual draw and billing occupancy alter them. These are dimensional checks, not discovered customer tariffs; there is no evidence these exact DGX configurations underpin Nebius's deals. IREN's $9.7m/MW-year is a contract-term average with phased delivery, not a 2026 run-rate; its release lacks a GPU count, preventing an exact GPU-hour calculation. ([specifications][s22], [B200 guide][s23], [IREN release][s24]) Ornn's [reserve listings](https://ornn.com/) are indicative offers, not executed contracts.

Contracted occupancy, billed occupancy, uptime and productive utilization differ. Take-or-pay lets idle chips earn revenue. SF Compute's resale demonstrates spare customer hours without measuring fleet occupancy. The undelivered MW in row 23 are scheduled future supply; those disclosures alone do not show overdue power or stranded purchased GPUs. ([15][s15], [19][s19], [26][s26])

CoreWeave's Q2 2026 GAAP operating loss was $49m (−2%) despite a 59% adjusted EBITDA margin. D&A absorbed 54.1% of revenue ($1,393m), before $640m of net interest expense (24.9% of revenue); adjusted operating income was $128m (5%). A hypothetical six-to-four-year life change applied to all that D&A would add $696.5m per quarter, an upper-bound sensitivity that assumes all D&A is affected GPU equipment and ignores cohort/residual differences (row 51). These figures show little operating room for faster depreciation, while lifetime capital returns still require cohort-level replacement spending, financing cash flows and lifetime revenue. Prepayments finance construction but create delivery obligations. Equity absorbs disappointing renewals first; secured lenders depend on contracted cash flows and recoverable hardware, with unsecured and vendor lenders exposed according to claim priority. Customers risk interrupted service and lost prepayments. Soluna is a primary counterexample to universal GPU-rental profitability. ([18][s18], [19][s19], [20][s20], [25][s25])

CoreWeave's September 18, 2026 convertible pricing adds unsecured financing exposure; planned capped calls and conversion rights mean its 2.875% coupon is not an all-in capital cost. The announcement alone does not verify closing (row 43). ([Financing release][s48])

NVIDIA's September 2025 agreement backstops residual unsold capacity covered by its CoreWeave contract through April 2032, subject to conditions (row 44). My inference is that this shifts some revenue risk to the supplier and weakens the link between paid capacity and independent final demand; it guarantees neither the entire fleet nor debt. NVIDIA separately discloses $36bn of AI-cloud commitments at July 26, 2026 (row 45); overlap is unresolved, so these amounts must not be added. ([CoreWeave 8-K][s49], [NVIDIA 10-Q][s50])

### Supply and a fiber-style glut

Epoch's shipment estimates include uncertainty fields. Cumulative shipments precede deductions for warehouses, retirements and unavailable power; they are not energized installed capacity. H100-equivalent peak operations differ from workload throughput; the aggregates sum component estimates/medians rather than estimate an aggregate median. September's Rubin deployment makes successor throughput a current effective-supply consideration (row 42). SemiAnalysis's customer-level shipment/install-base tables were not publicly retrievable. TrendForce's server forecast counts a different denominator from its GPU mix forecast. ([36][s36], [37][s37], [38][s38], [39][s39], [40][s40])

NVIDIA's $0.4bn H200 inventory/purchase-obligation charge in H1 FY2027 amid China restrictions shows why rising OCPI H200 rents do not establish healthy inventory economics everywhere (row 46). Restricted inventory is not necessarily available to these rental markets. ([NVIDIA 10-Q][s50])

A fiber-style glut would combine persistent price declines with immediately available connected clusters, failed renewals, distressed sales and unpaid debt despite growing compute consumption. Cheap isolated hours or efficiency-driven token-price declines alone are insufficient. Row 39's thresholds are monitoring judgments, not universal cost floors.
