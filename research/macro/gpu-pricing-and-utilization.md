*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [gpu-rental-prices.csv](/data/gpu-rental-prices.csv), [gpu-index-daily.csv](/data/gpu-index-daily.csv)*

## Summary

- GPU rents remain segmented. OCPI H100 is $3.52/GPU-hour and B200 $9.45 on 4 November; Silicon H100 is $2.97 on 5 November. None measures aggregate utilization (claims 1–4).
- Complete-October archived OCPI means are $2.9377 for H100 and $8.4965 for B200, each with 31 prints; up 3.8% and 17.3% against September. B200 nevertheless eased during the update window (claims 4, 6–7).
- Silicon's complete-October mean is unavailable. Its 25 available prints average $2.8580; missing early dates and unresolved freshness prevent treating that as a full-month forecast observation (claim 8).
- Lambda announces H100 $4.59 and B200 $7.69 per GPU-hour effective 16 November. Future list prices establish neither current realized rents nor paid occupancy (claim 13).
- Selected contract premiums and supplier support can sustain receipts without proving lifetime returns or independent end-user demand. Company financing and earnings belong in the [CoreWeave](/research/stack/coreweave) and [circular-financing](/research/macro/circular-financing) notes.
- Term-survey recovery supports H100 earning potential, but its flat composite and unverified used-sale records limit the conclusion. P1 remains substantially an aging test; read it with P1b and publication quality (claims 14–20, 24–29).

## Analysis

The evidence supports segmented pricing and selected cluster premiums. It does not measure a market-wide supply balance. Silicon standardizes rental characteristics across providers. OCPI uses volume-weighted active on-demand transactions and winsorization, excluding reserved contracts; contributor identities and the winsorization parameter are private. Provider rate cards record offers. These instruments cannot be spliced into a single rent series.

October firming survives the qualifications, especially B200's monthly comparison, while its retreat during the update window limits any claim of uninterrupted tightening (claims 4, 6–7). H200's cross-source gap still needs matched configurations, volumes and executable quotes (claim 9). Lambda's announced tariffs are future offers; current account pricing is not publicly established (claim 13). Historical provider-list ratios measure neither matched discounts nor current inventory. Spot interruptibility, connectivity and terms change the product being bought.

Contracted occupancy, billed occupancy, uptime and productive utilization differ. Take-or-pay can make idle chips earn revenue; customer resale discounts reveal spare hours without giving fleet occupancy (claim 16). [CoreWeave's earnings and Denmark delivery](/research/stack/coreweave) leave lifetime returns unproven: bringing capacity online establishes execution, not cash profitability. Cohort revenue, replacement spending, financing cash flows and residual recoveries are needed. [IREN's equipment refinancing](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) shows lending against deployed assets remains possible, but undisclosed pricing prevents a cost-of-capital comparison.

Equity absorbs disappointing renewals first. Secured lenders depend on contracted cash flows and recoverable hardware; unsecured and vendor lenders face losses according to priority. Customers risk interrupted service and lost prepayments. Supplier backstops transfer covered losses rather than establish market resale prices: [Broadcom's residual support](/research/macro/circular-financing) depends on rack recoveries after customer default. Company-wide accounting lives do not validate GPU economic lives; the [unit-economics note](/research/theory/ai-unit-economics) owns that comparison.

Term-survey recovery supports continued earning potential, but the flat composite limits its breadth (claims 14–15). Used asks, realized sales and modeled valuations remain different evidence. The opposing resale reports need matched configurations and sale ledgers (claims 17–20). Silicon's rental-based residual model cannot independently corroborate rental-driven hardware appreciation.

Efficiency gains increase effective supply and renewal risk. [Rubin availability on OCI](/research/stack/nvidia) makes generation substitution concrete without establishing deployed fleet scale. Benchmark ownership costs cannot replace rental tariffs: the apparent generation advantage narrows or reverses with the rental basis (claims 21–23). Neither calculation is a replicated service-price comparison.

A fiber-style glut would combine persistent price declines with available connected clusters, failed renewals, distressed sales and unpaid debt despite rising consumption. The [reported H200 import blockage](/research/stack/nvidia) and [Nscale expansion-option rejection](/research/stack/neoclouds-other) are grade C, reported, unverified; neither establishes a global glut or cancellation of a binding base contract.

P1 is substantially an aging test at its long horizon (claim 28). P1 Yes with P1b No favors aging; both Yes strengthens glut evidence. October OCPI averages lean against near-term price-collapse triggers, but monthly coverage is not fresh-publication certification. Silicon's available-print mean is not a complete-month mean. October also straddles forecast inception, so it cannot start the required sequence wholly inside the horizon. Apply the register's coverage and fallback rules; live-history discrepancies require publisher clarification before resolution. Preserve captured observations rather than silently replacing them.

## Open questions

Silicon's dated table takes precedence over its undated FAQ; its headline change and weekend dating remain unreconciled. Missing early-October observations prevent a complete-month Silicon mean. OCPI's live history differs from the archive and its headline changes disagree with its hub. Are these revisions or presentation errors? Publisher revision records and freshness flags are needed.

Comparable renewals, billed GPU-hours and fleet capacity remain missing. So do complete used-sale ledgers and effective prices for bilateral contracts. SemiAnalysis's “Sold Out” mapping and benchmark precision conflict need clarification. Lambda's current account tariffs must be distinguished from its announced effective-date schedule.

Supplier-support overlaps, actual collateral recoveries and matched H200 configurations remain unresolved. None of these price series supplies an independently verified market-wide utilization denominator.
