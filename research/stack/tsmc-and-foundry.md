# TSMC and the foundry layer

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [tsmc-monthly-revenue.csv](/data/tsmc-monthly-revenue.csv)*

## Summary

- TSMC manufactures and packages AI silicon. Gross margin of 66.4% and operating margin of 58.7% demonstrate comfortable supplier profitability, while downstream investment returns remain unproven (claim 2).
- Operating cash covered investment and dividends, leaving NT$671.293B in January–September. Net cash reserves and Moody's Aa2 stable rating support low current funding stress (claims 7, 8, 22).
- HPC supplied 68% of quarterly revenue; narrowly defined AI accelerators were high-teens percent in FY2025. Neither measures total current AI exposure (claims 3, 4).
- Annual capex guidance remains $60–64B. New spending mainly supplies 2028–29. The NT$38.8B AUO facility acquisition adds a site, with packaging use and output timing unconfirmed; sustained demand remains the biggest uncertainty (claims 6, 13, 28).
- Customers generally order without long advance notice. Capacity deposits fell 34.8% in 2025, leaving shareholders with the principal utilization risk in largely fixed-cost factories (claims 9, 18).
- Management expects mid-to-high-fifties percent AI-accelerator revenue CAGR through 2029, but calls consumer segments challenged and is prudent about 2027. Present scarcity can coexist with future overbuild (claims 11, 19).
- Overseas expansion dilutes margins and does not immediately remove Taiwan exposure. Intel Foundry remains loss-making; Samsung reports foundry earnings improvement without a standalone profit figure (claims 15, 18, 23, 24).

## Analysis

Current earnings and cash generation support low refinancing pressure. Operating cash covers gross investment and dividends, while net cash provides a cushion (claims 7, 8). Moody's upgrade reinforces that assessment (claim 22). The cash residual is not the total change in cash. Tax-credit-related investing inflows sit outside operating cash flow (claim 17).

Cash coverage still needs a forward spending check. Meeting annual guidance requires Q4 capex of $19.36–23.36B after Q3's $13.84B (claim 6). Depreciation and amortization are rising as the fixed-cost base expands (claim 27). Customer deposits offer limited protection: their decline and the short order horizon leave substantial capacity risk with TSMC. Contract terms do not establish universal take-or-pay protection (claim 9). Shareholders bear the first losses through weaker factory loading and returns; creditors become exposed if deterioration overwhelms the cash cushion.

The principal investment test is demand absorption in 2028–29 (claim 13). Earlier commissioning dates provide checkpoints (claim 14). Management's strong AI growth forecast supports expansion, but supplier sales alone cannot demonstrate profitable end-user demand. Merchant GPUs and custom accelerators can both use TSMC manufacturing and packaging; a broad AI retreat would affect both. Different chip designers may ultimately depend on overlapping cloud customers and funding sources. Anonymous customer shares do not establish named-customer exposure (claim 10).

HPC and the narrow accelerator category cover different products and periods. Neither provides a current total AI revenue measure (claims 3, 4). Consumer weakness can coexist with AI scarcity; consumer orders cannot automatically fill freed AI capacity because designs, qualification and packaging differ. Management checks financing, construction and power readiness, but these checks do not settle customer returns (claim 19). Reported wafer and CoWoS price increases remain unverified; if realized, revenue growth would also reflect price rather than chip volumes alone (claims 20, 29).

The AUO acquisition provides existing buildings and cleanrooms, but TSMC's filing specifies only operations and production. CoWoS conversion, usable capacity and commissioning cannot be inferred from floor area (claim 28). Renovation, equipment and qualification still determine output. The US interposer agreement adds another supply route without proving full take-or-pay protection (claim 26). Synchronized expansion can create overbuild, while tool costs and scarcity can limit the capacity each investment buys. CoWoS versions also use partly different equipment; wafer starts alone do not measure useful packaged output.

Overseas investment diversifies geography but dilutes margins. Arizona's multiyear envelope overlaps annual global capex and should not be added to it (claims 15, 16). Public assistance reduces capital costs without guaranteeing plant profitability. Taiwan remains a separate production and delivery risk even with strong demand; overseas capacity cannot immediately reproduce its manufacturing ecosystem (claim 18).

Historical downturns show that fixed-cost sensitivity can sharply compress margins without causing insolvency. They do not establish today's break-even utilization or a reusable margin floor. Management's through-cycle targets provide the opposing resilience case (claim 21). Returns require sustained loading, pricing, yields and productivity as investment comes online. Intel's losses and Samsung's incomplete disclosure prevent extending TSMC's profitability to the layer (claims 23, 24). Samsung's stated Taylor opening plan and the reported Fab 2 ramp concern different projects; anonymous booking claims establish neither binding backlog nor near-term HBM supply (claims 24, 30). This is a business-risk assessment, not a finding that current share prices already discount those risks.

## Open questions

- Does current AI demand justify the new capacity? Long-term outlook revisions, commissioning, utilization and customer funding would test the case. Plant-level returns and blanket cancellation protection remain undisclosed.
- What is total AI exposure? A reconciled bridge from HPC to accelerators, CPUs and networking is missing. Anonymous customer identities remain unestablished.
- How much usable CoWoS capacity exists by version? AUO conversion plans, equipment, qualification and timing are undisclosed; acquired floor area is not a capacity print.
- Will reported pricing increases occur, and offset overseas costs? Customer schedules, plant returns and exact public-support receipts would settle this.
- Moody's action is supported, but its research body was unavailable (claim 22).
- Samsung's earnings improvement is counterevidence to assuming rivals remain weak, but standalone foundry profit and Taylor Fab 2 binding bookings remain unknown. TSMC's break-even utilization and Taiwan disruption probability are unquantified.
