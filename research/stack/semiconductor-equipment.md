# Semiconductor equipment: ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, Tokyo Electron and the EUV sub-suppliers

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Claude (claude-fable-5-1 subagent) · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- The equipment layer sells the tools that turn AI capex into wafers. It is the most profitable part of the stack on reported numbers: ASML's Q3 2026 operating margin was 44.4% and net margin 42.5% [2]; KLA's June-2026 quarter gross margin was ~61% [27]; Lam's GAAP operating margin was 37.4% [24]; Applied's non-GAAP operating margin was 34.0% [20]; Tokyo Electron's was 28.9% [31]. Every one of them generates free cash flow well above capex.
- It is financed by its customers and its own cash, not by debt. ASML held €12.7bn of cash and short-term investments against ~€2.7bn of bonds at 27 Sept 2026 [3][11]; customers' down payments (non-current contract liabilities alone €9.3bn [3]) fund the capacity ramp. ASML's Q3 operating cash flow of €11.8bn was almost entirely prepayments: nine-month operating cash flow was €11.3bn, so H1 was roughly nil [2][3]. AMAT, Lam and KLA carry $3.7–6.0bn of bonds each against similar or larger cash piles; KLA is the only one with modest net debt [20][24][28]. All four run multi-billion buybacks (ASML €6.1bn in 9M 2026 [3]).
- Demand is now explicitly AI-led and running ahead of supply. ASML guides 2026 net sales of €44–45bn (+35–38% on 2025's €32.7bn) with memory system sales +~75%, advanced logic +>25%, and says planned 2027 and 2028 capacity (+30% a year) "will not fully meet the demand of our customers"; customers are asking to place orders for 2029–2030 [1][4]. TEL estimates AI data-centre applications are now more than half of the WFE market [31]. Lam, KLA and TEL put 2026 WFE at $150bn+, TEL puts 2027 at $190bn+ [26][29][31].
- Concentration is the structural risk. ASML's largest customer took 23.9% of 2025 sales and the top two 38.0% [11]; KLA's only >10% customer is TSMC [28]; AMAT's top two were 19% and 15% [22]. China was 29.1% of ASML's 2025 sales (36.1% in 2024) and still ~20% expected in 2026, 34% of Lam's FY2026 revenue, 30% of KLA's, 28% of AMAT's last quarter, 30.4% of TEL's [11][25][28][20][31]; a US bill (the MATCH Act) and reported pressure on the Netherlands for a near-total DUV ban are live threats to that revenue [36].
- History says this layer does not get a soft landing. In 2001 ASML's sales fell 40.5%, gross margin collapsed to 2.0% and it lost €479m; in 2009 sales fell 46.0% and it lost €151m; backlog in units fell 68% in 2001 and 54% in 2008; in 2002–03 it recorded 64 and 56 order cancellations or push-outs a year against ~200 systems sold [17][18]. In 2023 the backlog (~€39bn) cushioned ASML for about a year while bookings collapsed to €2.6bn in Q3 2023; 2024 sales were flat [15][16].
- The earliest signal has just been switched off: ASML stopped publishing quarterly bookings in 2026 and now discloses backlog only annually [9][10]. Replacements are contract liabilities on the balance sheet, KLA's and AMAT's backlog ($12.6bn and $15.0bn), Lam's deferred revenue ($2.4bn), and customers' capex guidance.
- Biggest uncertainty: whether 2027–2028 orders, placed with down payments by a handful of customers racing for AI capacity, hold if hyperscaler capex slows. Equity is priced for them to hold (ASML trailing P/E ~42, market cap ~$638bn [37]); the companies' balance sheets would survive a cut, their shareholders' valuations would not.

## Analysis

**What this layer is.** Wafer-fab equipment (WFE) is where chipmakers' capex becomes someone's revenue. ASML is the sole supplier of EUV lithography and the dominant supplier of DUV; Applied, Lam and TEL sell deposition, etch, clean and coat/develop tools; KLA sells process control (inspection and metrology). Behind ASML sit three critical sub-suppliers: Zeiss SMT (optical columns, ASML's "single supplier", 24.9% owned by ASML [11]), Trumpf (the CO2 drive laser for EUV light sources) and ASML's own Cymer unit (light sources, acquired 2013 [11]). Zeiss's output is the physical cap on EUV scanner output; ASML's capacity plan (+30% in 2027, more in 2028) is as much a Zeiss and Trumpf plan as an ASML one, and management said suppliers "must be able to grow about 30% a year" [5].

**Comfortably profitable: yes, by a wide margin.** The numbers are unusual even for a boom. ASML converted €11.7bn of Q3 sales into €5.0bn of net income (42.5%, flattered by the €0.5bn Mistral gain; ~38% without it) [1][3]. Nine-month 2026 net income was €10.6bn on €29.8bn of sales [3]. KLA's gross margin of ~61–62% and operating margin of ~43% are the highest in the group [27][29]; Lam's GAAP operating margin was 37.4% and is guided to 39.5% [24]; AMAT's 34.0% non-GAAP operating margin is the lowest of the US three and still far above the chipmakers it sells to, except TSMC [20]. TEL, with a lower-margin product mix, earns 28.9% [31]. All five convert profit to cash: FY2026 free cash flow was ~$3.8bn at KLA [27], ~$5bn at Lam (op cash flow $5.86bn less capex $0.97bn) [24], $5.7bn at AMAT in FY2025 [21] and €11.0bn at ASML in 2025 [11]. Installed-base service and upgrades (ASML IBM €3.3bn a quarter, Lam CSBG ~$2.5bn, KLA service $3.1bn a year) provide a recurring floor that did not exist in 2001 [1][26][27].

**How it is financed: by customers and retained cash, not by debt.** ASML's bonds total roughly €2.7bn after the €1bn July 2026 maturity (long-term €1,963m plus the €752m note due May 2027 now in current liabilities) against €12.7bn of cash and short-term investments [3][11]. The striking feature is the customer financing: contract liabilities, mostly down payments on systems to be shipped, were €18.2bn at end-2025 [11], and the non-current portion alone rose to €9.3bn by September 2026 [3]. Q3 operating cash flow of €11.8bn against net income of €5.0bn, after a first half with roughly zero operating cash flow, shows customers prepaying for 2027–2028 slots [2][3]. ASML also factored €3.5bn of "absolute, unconditional, irrevocable" receivables for down payments at end-2025 [11]. In other words, the buyers (TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Intel; ASML does not name them) carry part of the working-capital risk of the ramp. Lam's deferred revenue ($2.43bn, "mainly from customer down payments") shows the same mechanism at smaller scale [26]. The US names carry investment-grade bonds (AMAT $6.5bn, KLA $5.95bn, Lam $3.75bn) that are comfortably covered by cash in AMAT's and Lam's case; KLA has ~ $1bn of net debt, trivial against $4bn of annual operating cash flow [20][24][28]. Buybacks are the main use of cash: ASML spent €6.1bn on treasury shares in nine months under a €12bn 2026–2028 programme, AMAT $4.9bn in FY2025, Lam $3.85bn and KLA $2.29bn in FY2026 [3][21][24][27]. None of this layer is financed by private credit, vendor financing or SPVs; the financing fragility of the AI build sits with the buyers of chips and data centres, not the sellers of tools.

**How much is AI.** ASML does not disclose an AI share, but the mix shifts are unambiguous. Its 2026 guidance has memory system sales growing ~75% and advanced logic >25% while total logic grows only ~10%, i.e. mature logic (largely China DUV) is flat to down [4]. Nine-month 2026 memory sales of €10.3bn already exceed all of 2025 (€8.4bn) [2]. EUV units rise from 44 to ~65 in a year [4]. Management attributed customers' raised forecasts "primarily [to] their views on the size and duration of AI-related investments" [1]. TEL's estimate that AI data-centre applications exceed half of WFE [31], SEMI's +39% DRAM WFE for 2026 [33], Lam's NAND revenue doubling on enterprise SSD demand [26], KLA's advanced-packaging revenue of ~$1.1bn (+70%) [29] and AMAT's DRAM at 26% of systems sales [20] all point the same way. A rough decomposition for ASML's 2026 sales: ~20% China (mostly mature DUV, not AI) [4], ~27% installed-base service and upgrades (partly AI-driven, since upgrades raise EUV throughput) [1], and roughly half advanced logic and memory systems that are predominantly AI-driven. That decomposition is our estimate (grade C), not a company disclosure.

**Customer and country concentration.** One customer bought 23.9% of ASML's 2025 output and two bought 38.0% [11]. Taiwan (25.5%) and Korea (25.0%) match the geography of TSMC and Samsung/SK Hynix, but ASML does not name them. KLA names TSMC as its only >10% customer [28]; AMAT's two largest were 19% and 15% [22]. The China exposure is the second concentration: ASML's China share fell from 36.1% (2024) to 29.1% (2025) and to 14–23% of quarterly system sales in 2026, but is still guided at ~20% of 2026 net sales [11][2][4]; Lam's is 34%, KLA's 30%, AMAT's 28%, TEL's 30% [25][28][20][31]. That revenue is the most exposed to policy: the MATCH Act (introduced April 2026) would ban all immersion DUV sales to China and restrict servicing, and Dutch press reported in August that Washington is pushing the Netherlands toward a near-total ban [36]. ASML's January guidance range explicitly "accounts for possible outcomes of ongoing export control discussions" [9]. AMAT reportedly expects ~$600m off FY2026 revenue from controls already in place (grade C) [23].

**What a 2001-style capex cut does to this layer.** ASML's own filings give the template. In 2001, after a 76% sales rise in 2000, sales fell 40.5%, gross margin went to 2.0% (inventory write-downs and under-absorption), the company lost €479m including €400m of restructuring, backlog fell from 365 to 118 units, and 64 and 56 orders a year were cancelled or pushed out beyond twelve months in 2002 and 2003 against ~200 systems sold [17]. In 2008–09 sales fell 21.6% and then 46.0%, system sales fell from 260 to 70 units, gross margin fell to 28.7%, and ASML lost €151m [18]. The 2009 20-F describes the labour model (payroll plus temporary staff) that lets the company "quickly adapt its costs" [18]. Two things differ now. First, the installed-base business (about 27% of ASML's sales, a third of Lam's) is far larger and partly counter-cyclical, because customers upgrade rather than buy in a downturn. Second, gross margins start from 52–58% rather than 41%, so a 40% volume fall would compress margins toward the 30s rather than to zero. Against that, the capacity being added now (+30% a year, with Zeiss and Trumpf building to match) is fixed cost that would be stranded, and the 2027–2028 order book is concentrated in a few customers whose own capex depends on four or five hyperscalers. The 2023–24 episode shows the timing: ASML grew 30% in 2023 on a €38–39bn backlog while bookings fell to €2.6bn in Q3 2023; the flat year came in 2024, four to six quarters after orders turned [15][16][13]. A backlog of €38.8bn (about 14 months of 2025 sales, roughly 10 months of the 2026 run-rate) would delay but not avoid a cut [11].

**Lead times and cancellation structure.** ASML counts backlog only for orders "for which written authorizations have been accepted"; since 2008 it has restricted the headline backlog to systems with shipment dates within twelve months, and the 2009 20-F states plainly that "historically, orders have been subject to cancellation or delay by the customer" [18]. Most contracts require a down payment [11], which is the practical deterrent to cancellation: a customer that walks away forfeits cash it has already paid. AMAT's 10-K says customers "may delay delivery of products or cancel orders prior to shipment, subject to possible cancellation penalties", and that 31% of its $15.0bn backlog is not expected to ship within twelve months [22]. KLA's 10-K warns of "increased risk of order modifications, pushouts or cancellations" even while backlog rose from $7.9bn to $12.6bn [28]. Order lead times are not disclosed numerically in any of the fetched filings; ASML's Supervisory Board lists "order lead time" among its 2025 focus areas and the 20-F notes that component lead times have lengthened [11]. The practical reading: push-outs, not cancellations, are the first response in a downturn, and they show up in the twelve-month backlog before they show up in revenue.

**What would have to be true for current investment to earn a return.** For the equipment makers themselves, little: they are already earning it, and capacity capex is small relative to cash flow (ASML's 9M capex €1.1bn against €11.3bn of operating cash flow [3]). The question is for their shareholders. ASML at ~42x trailing earnings and $638bn of market value [37] is priced for the 2027–2028 order book to ship and for the 2030 range of €44–60bn [19] to be reached near the top, when 2026 guidance already sits at €44–45bn. A plateau in hyperscaler capex in 2027 would leave the companies profitable and the stocks de-rated; the 2001 and 2009 precedents show that equipment sales can halve within a year of the turn.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Supply-constrained vs over-ordered.** ASML says demand exceeds planned supply through 2028 and that it "believes the demand is real and not inflated by buying behavior" [5], yet it has declined to accept 2029–2030 orders and raised prices on critical tools [5][6]. Allocation shortages are exactly the conditions under which customers double-order (the 2000 and 2021 precedents). The down-payment regime limits this but does not eliminate it. The data that would resolve it (orders by customer, deposit sizes) are not disclosed.
- **China: resilient or about to be cut.** ASML guides China at ~20% of 2026 sales for the third quarter running [4][8], and Q3 China ship-to share rebounded to 23% from 14% [2]. Against that, the MATCH Act and the reported US push for a near-total DUV and servicing ban [36] could remove most of it. The Dutch government has reportedly lobbied against it; nothing is enacted as of this note. Lam and KLA carry even higher China shares [25][28].
- **WFE forecasts disagree by $50bn for 2027.** TEL's July view is $190bn+ for 2027 [31]; SEMI's mid-year forecast implies ~$175bn (2026 $143.9bn × 1.218) [33]; SEMI's separate fab-spending series showed only +9% for 2027 at the time of the December 2025 release. The forecasts have been revised up twice in 2026 and are now being made by the sellers.
- **Memory cycle inside the AI cycle.** ASML's 2026 growth leans on memory (+75%) [4] and Lam's on NAND conversions [26]. Memory capex has historically been the most volatile component of WFE; HBM demand is tied to GPU shipments. If HBM supply catches up with demand in 2027, memory tool orders could fall even while logic holds.
- **Unknowns.** ASML no longer reports quarterly bookings [9]; it has not reported a 2027 revenue figure and will update its long-term view only at the June 2027 Capital Markets Day [1]. The AI share of equipment demand is an inference, not a disclosure. Customer names behind the 23.9% and 38.0% concentration figures are not given [11]. Zeiss's and Trumpf's EUV-specific revenue and capacity are not disclosed [34][35].
