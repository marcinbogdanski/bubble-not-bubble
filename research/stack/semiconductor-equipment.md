# Semiconductor equipment: ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, Tokyo Electron and the EUV sub-suppliers

## Summary

- Equipment turns chipmakers' capex into tool revenue. Current operations are highly profitable: ASML's operating margin is 44.4%; KLA's gross margin is 61.9%. Periods and accounting bases differ across suppliers (claims 1–2).
- Operating cash and customer advances fund expansion alongside bonds, factoring and sales-type leases. Liquidity supports resilience, but deposits and backlog do not guarantee revenue (claims 7–12).
- Suppliers expect demand to exceed planned supply. ASML expects planned 2027–2028 output below customer demand; interest in 2029–2030 is not accepted orders. Lam's preliminary 2027 WFE view exceeds $200B, led by DRAM (claims 5–6, 17).
- Concentrated customers and China exposure transmit investment and policy shocks. Lam's China share reached 35%, attributed to mature-node investment timing and guided meaningfully lower. Down payments decreased despite rising deferred revenue (claims 13–15).
- KLA guides December revenue to $4.7B ±$0.2B. Applied’s agreement for ~20% of Besi strengthens its hybrid-bonding exposure, conditional on share delivery (claims 18, 22).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether commitments persist if downstream investment slows. A sustained slowdown could cut earnings and multiples. Historical losses establish downside, but a hard landing is not inevitable; current cash generation does not establish returns on new capacity (claims 21, 30).

## Analysis

ASML supplies lithography; Applied, Lam and TEL supply deposition, etch, clean and coat/develop tools; KLA supplies inspection and metrology. Critical EUV suppliers constrain expansion. ASML's capacity plan is specific to low-NA EUV and DUV immersion, with further expansion still under investigation (claims 5, 23).

Margins and cash generation show strong existing businesses. KLA’s quarter and December guide reinforce the near-term growth picture (claims 3, 18). Different fiscal periods and FCF definitions prevent a simple profitability ranking. Aftermarket revenue may cushion the cycle, but discretionary upgrades and mature-node equipment do not establish an invariant recurring floor (claims 1–2, 7, 24).

Cash and advances support resilience. ASML also factors receivables and provides sales-type leases. Its approximate net-cash figure mixes dates and debt measures. Customer advances do not universally guarantee orders: ASML has refunded down payments, AMAT permits cancellations subject to possible penalties, and KLA describes limited cancellation protection. RPO and backlog have different definitions and timing. Contract-liability changes also reflect recognition, refunds and payment terms, so they cannot directly replace bookings (claims 8–12).

Supplier forecasts and buyer capex support strong near-term demand. They do not establish final-user AI returns or the return on the next capacity increment. ASML's exact AI share is undisclosed; China geography, advanced-node applications and aftermarket revenue overlap and cannot be added into an AI/non-AI split. Memory demand can reverse independently of logic (claims 4, 14, 17–18, 25–26). Applied’s Besi investment targets hybrid bonding and advanced packaging; minority ownership and collaboration do not establish customer orders or investment returns (claim 22).

Lam's latest quarter supports the supply-constrained reading. Management expects delayed deliveries to ship later; checked call summaries reported no customer push-outs or cancellations, but that absence is not a management denial. China mature-node timing and falling down payments qualify the demand picture. The selloff is evidence of repricing; reporting attributes it to the supply-capped guide and profit-taking rather than lower orders (claims 14–16, 19).

Customer concentration can amplify a reversal. Geographic revenue is exposure, not an automatic prohibited-sales loss. Product eligibility, servicing rights, licensing and replacement demand determine the effect of policy changes (claims 13–14, 20).

Past downturns show severe downside, while the profitable plateau shows that a hard landing is not inevitable. Returns on expansion require durable demand, achievable throughput and pricing sufficient to cover new costs. Current liquidity does not guarantee future solvency or profits. A reconciled valuation model is still needed to assess shareholder downside (claims 21, 29–30).

## Open questions

- Do shortages reflect durable demand or over-ordering? Customer-level commitments and quantified down-payment changes would help distinguish them.
- WFE forecasts differ in vintage and scope. Comparable definitions and company-hosted call evidence are needed before treating the gap as disagreement; KLA’s release does not substantiate the attributed WFE or cleanroom-delay claims. The separate fab-spending series cannot be spliced into WFE.
- What are current export rules, affected products and servicing rights? Introduced texts and reported pressure do not certify enactment status.
- ASML's current backlog, AI revenue share and customer identities remain undisclosed in the checked material. Zeiss/Trumpf EUV-specific revenue and capacity are also unknown.
- What earnings and valuation outcome follows a slowdown? A cost model and reconciled valuation inputs are missing. The published KLA/TEL selloff range remains unverified here. Applied’s Besi delivery timing and transaction funding also need confirmation.
