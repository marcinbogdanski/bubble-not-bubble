# Nvidia

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Claude claude-fable-5-1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [nvidia-quarterly.csv](/data/nvidia-quarterly.csv)*

## Summary

- Nvidia supplies AI compute and is comfortably profitable. Quarterly revenue is $96.221B, gross margin 75.0%, and trailing FCF $126.9B (claims 1, 3, 5).
- Customer support ties its balance sheet to demand. Supply commitments are $279B, equity investments carry at $99B, and the conditional Ohio guarantee is capped at $105B (claims 11, 13–14).
- Receivables are $63.059B with DSO of 60 days. Extended terms support customers, but do not alone prove collection problems (claim 9).
- Supported labs are expected to supply roughly a quarter of FY2028 business. Their current loss-funded share is unquantified; customer concentration and funding constraints remain material (claims 8, 15, 29).
- Gross margin is guided lower, with recovery dependent on price increases. Zero China Data Center compute is a guidance assumption; China/Hong Kong headquarters still account for $7.9B of quarterly revenue (claims 3, 6, 28).
- Strong cash generation, AA/stable credit and discretionary buybacks argue against imminent distress. The rating predates Ohio (claims 5, 21–22).
- Valuation requires sustained future growth and margins. The biggest uncertainty is whether customer funding and project delivery can sustain demand as Nvidia's procurement and financing exposure grow (claims 11–19, 24–25, 29).

## Analysis

Nvidia's earnings and cash generation are strong, but customer overbuild can still impair future orders, margins and investment values. GAAP earnings include equity gains; adjusted net income removes those gains with associated tax adjustments. Pretax gains cannot simply be subtracted from after-tax earnings (claim 4). A comparable adjusted trailing earnings multiple remains unavailable because the reported series changes its treatment of stock-based compensation.

The financing exposure runs through distinct instruments. Receivables defer customer cash payments; equity absorbs investment losses; capacity agreements commit Nvidia to cloud purchases; guarantees cover contractual shortfalls after protections and recoveries. These cannot be summed as immediate losses. Customer advances run in the opposite direction, while equity lock-ups restrict liquidity (claims 9, 12–14, 17–20, 23).

The Ohio cap becomes effective only with readiness conditions. Recovery offsets, reimbursement and termination rights limit exposure, but do not ensure recovery during a correlated AI downturn. Compute's ability to serve other users does not establish that a particular site retains its financing value. SB Energy's unfinished gas-generation arrangements add execution risk. The Nscale convertible-note commitment also shows why the financing programme cannot be described solely as equity support (claims 13, 18–19).

Supply commitments create a separate risk if demand slips. Limited disclosed RPO cannot establish a matching firm chip backlog. Inventory can rise during a product ramp, but procurement and inventory become more dangerous if delivery or funding falters (claims 10–11, 23). DSO reflects extended terms to certain investment-grade buyers; early customer payments affected the preceding comparison. Aging and cash conversion are needed to distinguish normalization from deterioration (claim 9).

Direct customers include distribution and integration channels. Their shares do not identify ultimate users or how purchases are funded. The unnamed indirect lab may be OpenAI, but the filing does not identify it. Hyperscale and ACIE are business classifications, not funding categories. The expected supported-lab share concerns future total-company business, not today's loss-funded demand (claims 2, 8, 15). The report's accounting-scale scenario and narrow judgment do not establish that dependence (claim 30).

Meta's negative FCF illustrates buyer funding pressure; firmer GPU rental readings counter an immediate rental-price collapse. Neither allocates the funding of individual purchases or measures utilization. Huang's demand optimism coexists with working-capital and power constraints (claims 26–27, 29).

Input costs and product mix pressure margins, while competition remains an additional risk. Management's recovery path assumes realized price increases; disclosures cannot isolate each cause. China-related headquarters revenue measures something different from the absence of China compute in guidance (claims 3, 6, 28).

The valuation scenario requires roughly $604B of revenue at a sustained 55% net margin for a 10% annual price return over five years, assuming constant shares, no interim distributions and a 25x terminal P/E (claim 25). It is conditional arithmetic, not fair value. Lower selected multiples than Cisco's peak do not establish cheapness; Cisco's comparison used earnings reported after its peak. Nvidia's own provider and derived multiples also differ in basis (claim 24).

Equity repricing can precede defaults. Guarantees can protect lenders while moving losses to Nvidia. Contractual seniority, collateral, triggers and recoveries decide ultimate losses. Current FCF, discretionary repurchases and the credit assessment provide counterevidence to near-term insolvency, while correlated customer funding remains the central risk (claims 5, 13, 21–22).

## Open questions

- What share of demand depends on supplier support or loss-making labs? Customer-level cash allocation and ultimate-user data are missing.
- How do funded investments and commitments reconcile with later announcements? OpenAI's paid-in total, overlap among arrangements and the attribution of investment reclassifications remain unverified.
- Can Ohio be delivered and relet at values that protect Nvidia during a downturn? Project financing, readiness evidence and recovery terms matter.
- Will receivable aging and cash conversion support management's explanation of longer terms?
- How much margin pressure comes from memory costs, mix or competition? Realized pricing and product-level economics would help.
- Can adjusted trailing earnings be put on a consistent stock-compensation basis? The provider's forward multiple also needs a reconciled horizon and denominator.
- Rental trackers use different vendors and contracts. Comparable rents, utilization and realized collateral recoveries are still needed.
