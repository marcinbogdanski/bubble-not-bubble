# Alphabet: AI infrastructure, Gemini, Search and TPUs

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra (2026-11-06) · Data: [alphabet-capex.csv](/data/alphabet-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Alphabet combines Search, Gemini, Cloud and TPUs. Q3 Cloud grew 103% at a 36.2% operating margin; the $7.655B Alphabet-level operating loss, primarily shared AI R&D, sits outside the segments (claims 1–3, 13).
- Q3 capex exceeded operating cash; FCF was −$9.065B, including inventory outflow and the EC fine-and-interest payment. The $195–205B capex guide implies Q4 $63.0–73.0B; 2027 is guided "significantly" higher (claims 4–6, 13, 25).
- Nine-month funding included $61.9B equity and $76.3B net debt; €6B bonds followed in November. Liquidity net of funded debt was $48.6B before leases and other obligations (claims 7–9, 11).
- Alphabet is simultaneously an investor, supplier, guarantor and customer-financier. Its $128.169B measurement-alternative equity portfolio is concentrated primarily in one unnamed company reported to be Anthropic; total non-marketable securities are $135.230B. Anthropic's filed Google spending and funding arrangements are detailed in the [Anthropic note](/research/stack/anthropic); their exact contribution to Alphabet's Cloud backlog remains unverified (claims 15–17).
- Contingent support comprises $75.7B of executed and promised data-center/energy backstops, including $10B for Crux AI, plus $16.2B of energy-equipment and $6.6B of TPU repurchase guarantees. Crux's commercial agreement contributes $16.8B to backlog. These are distinct contractual categories with overlapping counterparties, not an expected-loss sum (claims 10, 18, 23).
- Q3 equity investment losses of $8.664B, largely driven by SpaceX marks, reduced net income by $6.7B. The nine-month equity gain of $127.282B already includes this loss. Investment marks make unadjusted net income a poor measure of operating AI returns (claims 14, 27).
- The test is whether $560.8B Cloud backlog produces cash fast enough to cover investment. Q3 depreciation rose 49.2% year on year; shares fell about 13% after results (claims 12, 21, 26).

## Analysis

Alphabet remains strongly profitable. Advertising can pay for AI through better matching, additional commercial queries and protection of Search distribution. Query growth supports engagement, but FX was management's largest explanation for sequential growth deceleration; lower AI Mode monetization also contributed, and incremental AI profit remains unisolated (claims 1–2). Regulatory remedies keep distribution and margin risk live (claim 25).

Free cash flow was negative in Q2 and Q3, and trailing FCF was $19.747B. At the $195–205B capex guide, Q4 operating cash must exceed $67.851–77.851B for full-year FCF to be positive (claims 4–6). Q3 OCF already includes a $3.220B inventory cash outflow and a $5.2B EC fine-and-interest payment; these explain much of that quarter's deficit but do not establish sustainable adjusted FCF (claims 13, 25). Q3 depreciation rose 49.2% year on year; $145.270B of assets were not yet in service. This points to further expense as depreciable assets become usable, without quantifying how much of the current build has yet to reach earnings (claim 21).

Funding has shifted from internal cash to capital markets. Nine-month equity and net debt together exceed $138B, the ATM is now in active use, and euro notes followed in November (claims 8–9). Buybacks are zero; acquisitions, private investments, stock-award taxes and dividends also consume funding (claim 11). Liquidity net of funded debt remains positive before leases and other obligations. Undrawn revolvers and management's stated liquidity outlook support near-term capacity, while restricted SpaceX shares cannot currently be freely sold (claims 7, 27). Parent creditors benefit from diversified earnings and high ratings, but the new euro notes are structurally junior to subsidiary liabilities and impose no leverage or liquidity maintenance tests. Common shareholders bear dilution and cash compression.

Cloud growth, a 36% segment margin and broad token usage contradict a thesis of wholly speculative demand (claims 3, 24). Yet the quality of that growth is harder to read. TPU hardware/software generally produces revenue on transfer of control, net of estimated hardware returns, while associated services follow their own recognition schedules; bridge-capacity usage is expected to pressure near-term margins (claims 3, 13, 20). Cloud margin also excludes the shared model research booked at Alphabet level (claim 1).

Overextension is clearest in correlated counterparties. Backlog includes $16.8B from Crux AI, a venture in which Google holds equity and has committed $10B of backstops; that portion is partly self-supported demand (claims 12, 23). Anthropic's filing reconciles Google's funding package and documents the investment-and-customer relationship, but does not establish its exact share of Alphabet's private portfolio or September Cloud backlog (claims 15–17). Data-center backstops, energy guarantees, purchase commitments and uncommenced leases overlap future spending; contractual maxima are not expected losses (claims 10, 18–19).

Equity investment marks affect reported earnings without measuring operating AI returns. The approximately 180× trailing-FCF proxy reflects depressed cash generation after current investment; its interpretation depends on future cash conversion. The 12.6% post-results share-price decline is observed, but the cited sources do not establish its cause (claims 14, 26). Adequate returns require durable Search economics, paid Cloud consumption, timely commissioning, collections from concentrated AI-lab customers and a 2027 capex plan that operating cash can eventually cover. Finnish permitting and model-delay allegations add unresolved execution signals (claims 28, 30).

## Open questions

- What share of backlog is TPU hardware, related-party (Crux AI) or Anthropic? Neither filing nor call quantifies concentration beyond "majority typical GCP contracts" (claims 12, 17, 23).
- What is Alphabet's exact Anthropic ownership and carrying value? Anthropic's filing reconciles Google's conditional funding package, but Alphabet does not allocate its measurement-alternative portfolio by investee (claims 15–16).
- How large are TPU system revenues and their margins, and how likely are repurchase rights to be exercised (claims 10, 13)?
- How large is 2027 capex, and can operating cash plus the remaining $27.6B ATM fund it without heavier debt issuance (claims 4, 8–9)?
- Did the Finnish suspension take effect, and what did OpenAI GA add to Cloud consumption (claims 28–29)?
