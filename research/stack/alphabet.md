# Alphabet: AI infrastructure, Gemini, Search and TPUs

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [alphabet-capex.csv](/data/alphabet-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Alphabet combines Search advertising, Gemini, Cloud and custom TPUs. Search remains a profitable return channel beyond selling tokens. Q3 Cloud revenue grew 103% with a 36.2% operating margin, helped by TPU system sales; shared AI research costs of $7.655B sit outside the segments (claims 1–3, 13).
- Capex is now running ahead of operating cash. Q3 capex was $51.355B against $42.290B of operating cash; free cash flow was negative for a second quarter and trailing FCF fell to $19.747B. The unchanged $195–205B guide implies Q4 capex of $63.0–73.0B, and 2027 is guided "significantly" higher, unquantified (claims 4–6).
- The build is increasingly externally financed: $61.9B of new equity (including $12.4B of at-the-market sales in Q3) and $76.3B of net debt in nine months, with a further €6B of bonds in November. Liquidity net of funded debt fell to about $48.6B (claims 7–9, 11).
- Alphabet is simultaneously an investor, supplier, guarantor and customer-financier. Its $128.169B private portfolio is concentrated in one unnamed company reported to be Anthropic; Anthropic's filed Google spending commitments are detailed in the [Anthropic note](/research/stack/anthropic); their inclusion in Cloud backlog is unverified (claims 15–17).
- Contingent support keeps widening: $48.012B of data-center credit backstops, $16.2B of energy guarantees, $6.6B of TPU repurchase guarantees and $10B of committed backstops for Crux AI, a joint venture whose own Google contract adds $16.8B to backlog (claims 10, 18, 23).
- Investment marks turned to an $8.664B Q3 loss, largely SpaceX after its IPO, cutting net income by $6.7B after a nine-month gain of $127.282B. Earnings remain a poor measure of AI returns (claims 14, 27).
- The central test is whether $560.8B of Cloud backlog converts to cash fast enough to cover a capital base whose depreciation already rises about 49% a year, while the stock fell about 13% the day after results (claims 12, 21, 26).

## Analysis

Alphabet remains strongly profitable. Advertising can pay for AI through better matching, additional commercial queries and protection of Search distribution. Query growth at a five-year high supports that, but management also says lower AI Mode monetization weighed on Search growth, so incremental profit after inference costs and substitution remains unisolated (claims 1–2). Regulatory remedies keep distribution and margin risk live (claim 25).

The cash picture has tightened. Free cash flow was negative in Q2 and Q3, trailing FCF fell to under $20B, and the unchanged guide implies a Q4 capex step-up that would need operating cash well above any quarter so far to keep the full year positive (claims 4–6). Inventory built ahead of TPU deliveries explains part of the gap and is already in operating cash (claim 13). Depreciation growing about 49% a year with $145B still awaiting service means the income statement has not yet absorbed most of the build (claim 21).

Funding has shifted from internal cash to capital markets. Nine-month equity and net debt together exceed $138B, the ATM is now in active use, and euro notes followed in November (claims 8–9). Buybacks are zero; acquisitions, private investments, stock-award taxes and dividends also consume funding (claim 11). Liquidity net of debt is still positive but thinner, and restricted SpaceX shares are not spendable liquidity (claims 7, 27). Senior creditors retain a diversified, highly rated parent; common shareholders bear dilution and cash compression.

Cloud growth, a 36% segment margin and broad token usage contradict a thesis of wholly speculative demand (claims 3, 24). Yet the quality of that growth is harder to read. TPU hardware recognizes revenue on delivery rather than through consumption, some contracts carry repurchase options backed by $6.6B of guarantees, and management guides margin headwinds as bridge capacity ramps (claims 10, 13, 20). Cloud margin also excludes the shared model research booked at Alphabet level (claim 1).

Overextension is clearest in correlated counterparties. Backlog includes $16.8B from Crux AI, a venture in which Google holds equity and has committed $10B of backstops; that portion is partly self-supported demand (claims 12, 23). The private portfolio, unnamed $20B conditional funding and filed Anthropic spending connect investment marks to commercial collections without establishing that all exposures are the same recipient or can be added (claims 15–17). Data-center backstops, energy guarantees, purchase commitments and uncommenced leases overlap future spending; contractual maxima are not expected losses (claims 10, 18–19).

Equity marks now cut both ways: the Q3 SpaceX decline reversed part of the first-half gain, so unadjusted earnings and the stock's FCF multiple both mislead in opposite directions (claims 14, 26). The post-results fall suggests the market is pricing the cash gap rather than the revenue beat. Adequate returns require durable Search economics, paid Cloud consumption, timely commissioning, collections from concentrated AI-lab customers and a 2027 capex plan that operating cash can eventually cover. Finnish permitting and model-delay allegations add unresolved execution signals (claims 28, 30).

## Open questions

- What share of backlog is TPU hardware, related-party (Crux AI) or Anthropic? Neither filing nor call quantifies concentration beyond "majority typical GCP contracts" (claims 12, 17, 23).
- How does the reported Anthropic investment package reconcile with the unnamed conditional commitment? Exact ownership and funded consideration remain unverified (claims 15–16).
- How large are TPU system revenues and their margins, and how likely are repurchase rights to be exercised (claims 10, 13)?
- How large is 2027 capex, and can operating cash plus the remaining $27.6B ATM fund it without heavier debt issuance (claims 4, 8–9)?
- Did the Finnish suspension take effect, and what did OpenAI GA add to Cloud consumption (claims 28–29)?
