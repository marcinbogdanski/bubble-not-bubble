# Microsoft

*Last updated: 2026-10-21 · Cross-checked by: Claude claude-fable-5-1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [microsoft-capex.csv](/data/microsoft-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Microsoft sells Azure capacity, enterprise AI services and software features, and remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner. FY2026 operating income was $155.2B; operating cash flow was $182.9B (claims 1–2, 22).
- Cash PP&E consumed 63% of operating cash flow; finance-inclusive capex was $145.3B, or 79%. Conventional free cash flow remained $67.0B, but the illustrative residual after lease principal, shareholder distributions and other investing was −$4.691B (claims 3–5, 11).
- Obligations extend beyond current spending: $329.1B of uncommenced leases sits within $443.506B of lease payments and $743.821B of total contractual obligations. These overlapping measures cannot be added together (claims 9, 12).
- Demand is broad: Azure quarterly growth was 43%, and nearly 90% of Cloud revenue came from outside frontier labs. Backlog is more concentrated: OpenAI represented about 45% of December commercial RPO; its June balance remains undisclosed (claims 13, 16–18).
- Calendar-2026 capex guidance of about $175B reflects lease reclassification. The building-life change does not establish lower physical investment, and management still guides positive FY2027 FCF (claims 6–7, 28).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether OpenAI collections, replacement customers and contract recoveries can support the infrastructure obligations. Software earnings and liquidity provide buffers; neither persistent shortages nor inevitable overbuild is established.

## Analysis

Microsoft's software earnings and enterprise distribution support the build. Azure growth, the AI-business run-rate and paid Copilot seats show demand beyond a single customer (claims 13–15). Nearly 90% of Cloud revenue outside frontier labs measures realized customer mix, not the non-AI share or the ability to redeploy spare GPUs profitably. Falling Cloud margins show infrastructure costs already reaching earnings (claim 16). Capacity constraints can affect reported growth; they do not demonstrate profitable demand at every future price.

Cash generation remains substantial, but conventional FCF understates the broader funding demands. The $67.0B figure deducts cash PP&E only. After lease principal and shareholder distributions, the residual is $15.170B; deducting other investing makes it −$4.691B (claims 4, 11, 29). The latter is an illustration, not evidence every investing dollar is AI capex. Unpaid PP&E adds payment-timing exposure (claim 30). Reserves and changes to repurchases provide alternatives to borrowing, so a corporate financing deficit is not established.

The obligations schedule makes the longer-term risk concrete. Uncommenced leases cannot be assumed cancellable. Lease payments include both commenced and uncommenced contracts; adding those commitments to recognized lease liabilities mixes measurement bases. Purchase and construction commitments overlap future operating and investing cash uses, and supplier announcements cannot simply be added to the filing totals (claims 8–12, 23–25).

The building-life change shifts future leases toward operating classification. It reduces comparability of finance-inclusive capex without establishing a physical-spending cut. Cash payments and operating-lease liabilities remain relevant. Calendar guidance is a different period from the fiscal-year growth outlook, and neither finance-inclusive capex nor its reclassification directly determines cash PP&E or FCF (claims 6–7, 28).

OpenAI is simultaneously a customer, investment and distribution partner. December's derived backlog balance is not a current exposure estimate: the June growth disclosure does not identify its bookings, runoff or renewals (claims 17–18). The same-period revenue comparison highlights supplier dependence, but incorporates a reported, unverified denominator and Microsoft's revenue share (claims 19, 26). It is not proof of fictitious revenue.

A payment failure could impair receivables and investment value, reduce future revenue and leave idle capacity. Historical recognized revenue, outstanding receivables and backlog cannot be added as a face-value loss; lost revenue is not lost profit. HLBV accounting, funded capital and ownership alone do not establish investment carrying value or remaining loss-recognition capacity (claims 19–20). Software profits and broad cloud demand provide buffers, but recoveries and replacement-customer economics remain unknown.

Microsoft shareholders bear lower cash generation and impaired returns. Suppliers and financiers may suffer earlier liquidity or market-price stress, while Microsoft remains liable under valid accepted contracts. Delivery, acceptance, termination and recourse determine the order; IREN's changing guarantee illustrates this (claim 24).

Broader OpenAI distribution increases competition. The partnership also gives Microsoft benefits through ended outgoing payments and AGI-independent capped incoming payments. Google Cloud commitment drawdown does not establish fresh financing, lost Azure revenue or cancellation of existing commitments (claims 21–22).

The 58.7x FCF multiple contrasts earnings with cash generation but does not identify the market's assumed repayment probability or capex path. A valuation model would be needed to establish those expectations; the forward multiple's denominator remains unspecified (claim 27).

## Open questions

What are OpenAI's current Azure consumption, RPO, unrestricted liquidity and receivable ageing? How much of recognized revenue is consumption versus revenue share? The reported rate remains unverified, and the payment cap is undisclosed.

What are Microsoft's investment carrying value and remaining loss-recognition capacity? Net investment impacts do not supply a carrying-value roll-forward.

Which leases and purchase commitments can be cancelled, and on what terms? The filing does not allocate obligations by named supplier. Replacement demand and contract recoveries would determine losses on unused capacity.

Finance-inclusive capex, stated quarterly lease amounts and cash PP&E do not reconcile by simple addition. A consistent bridge across payment timing and lease classification is needed. Company guidance uses calendar capex despite secondary coverage labelling it fiscal; the company basis is retained.

The valuation interpretation remains unproven without a reverse valuation and a dated consensus earnings denominator.
