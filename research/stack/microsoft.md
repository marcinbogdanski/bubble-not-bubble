# Microsoft

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra (2026-11-06) · Data: [microsoft-capex.csv](/data/microsoft-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Microsoft sells Azure capacity, enterprise AI services and software features, and remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner. September-quarter revenue was $92.3B (+19%) and operating income $45.9B (+21%); Azure revenue was $33.2B, up 48% (claims 1, 13).
- Capex is outrunning cash generation's growth. Finance-inclusive capex was $45.2B in the quarter; cash PP&E took 68% of operating cash flow, and free cash flow fell 27% year over year to $18.6B. After lease principal, distributions and other investing, the illustrative residual was −$6.452B (claims 2–5, 11).
- Microsoft issued $25.0B of general-purpose senior unsecured notes on 2026-11-04, supplementing liquidity as investment rises. Fixed-rate tranches totaling $23B priced at yields of 4.869–6.356%; another $2B floats with SOFR. Cash and short-term investments were $64.5B before the issue; no proceeds are specifically earmarked for AI capex (claims 10, 30).
- Obligations keep growing: uncommenced leases reached $364.6B, starting through FY2034, and leases are shifting to operating classification, which finance-inclusive capex does not capture (claims 7–9, 12).
- OpenAI concentration is now visible in revenue: $10.0B in the quarter, 10.8% of the total, with the receivable up to $7.2B. Its share of the flat $677B commercial RPO remains undisclosed (claims 17–19).
- Calendar-2026 capex guidance is about $170B, trimmed from $175B because third-party (neocloud) capacity deliveries slipped to the second half. Demand still exceeds supply, and management guides positive FY2027 FCF (claims 6, 14, 28).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether OpenAI collections, replacement customers and contract recoveries can support the infrastructure obligations. Software earnings, an Aaa rating and market access provide buffers; neither persistent shortages nor inevitable overbuild is established.

## Analysis

Software earnings and enterprise distribution support the build. Cloud gross margin was 66%, guided to about 65%; management attributes the year-over-year declines to mix shifting toward Azure, partly offset by improving product-level margins, chiefly Azure. These aggregate margins do not isolate AI infrastructure unit returns.

After finance-lease principal and the cash-flow statement’s dividends and repurchases, the residual was $6.497B; subtracting the $12.949B Other, net investing line gives −$6.452B. Management links the increase in that line mainly to facilitating component purchases, not necessarily its entire balance (claims 11, 29). The prospectus’s September pro forma debt is $60.358B carrying value, or an illustrative $66.269B principal after adding the issue, before intervening movements. Both exclude $70.973B finance and $27.477B operating lease liabilities. Equity of $470.509B and TTM operating cash of $195.878B provide substantial buffers. Management’s liquidity assessment also relies on capital-market access. The issue demonstrates that access; its proceeds are not earmarked solely for the build.

Depreciation rose 56% to $11.1B, while about three quarters of capex went to assets management calls short-lived, mainly CPUs and GPUs (claims 5, 26). Continued investment can raise depreciation, but that classification does not establish economic obsolescence or future cohort returns. Company operating margin increased from 48.9% to 49.8%; management credits revenue growth, platform efficiencies and investment discipline, partly offset by the mix shift toward Azure. The longer building-life estimate was expected to provide only a minimal FY2027 operating-income benefit (claims 1, 7).

Uncommenced leases are conditional in part. Lease payments include commenced and uncommenced contracts; adding them to recognized liabilities mixes measurement bases. Supplier announcements cannot be added to filing totals (claims 8–9, 12, 23–25).

The 10-Q also reports that datacenter rules are already restricting and delaying development and increasing costs. Its broader warnings cover investment ahead of fully developed revenue, reliance on financing, scarce suppliers, customer workload shifts and potential underutilization or impairment. Most of those warnings were already in the annual filing; the separate datacenter-regulation paragraph is new. These are disclosed risks and constraints, not proof of a quantified cancellation or loss ([10-Q, risk factors][Q127]).

Operating-lease additions sit outside finance-inclusive capex, complicating comparisons. The calendar-2026 guidance trim reflects third-party delivery delays; termination rights depend on contract conditions (claims 6–7, 23–25).

Commercial RPO was nearly flat sequentially at $677B, helped by a new large Azure commitment; commercial bookings fell 12% against a prior-year large-contract comparison. With no current OpenAI share disclosed, the December-based scenario is not a current exposure estimate (claims 17–18).

A payment failure could impair receivables and investment value, reduce future revenue and leave idle capacity. Recognized revenue, receivables and backlog cannot be added as a face-value loss; lost revenue is not lost profit. HLBV accounting, funded capital and ownership do not establish carrying value or remaining loss-recognition capacity (claims 19–20). Recoveries and replacement-customer economics remain unknown.

Shareholders bear lower cash generation and impaired returns. The new unsecured notes rank ahead of equity, but their indenture imposes no financial covenants or meaningful restrictions on additional leverage or distributions. Bondholders can incur price losses from rates, rating changes, spreads or liquidity even without default; principal impairment depends on a more severe credit deterioration. Microsoft remains liable under valid accepted supplier contracts (claims 23–25).

The historical FCF multiple does not identify the market's assumed capex path (claim 27).

## Open questions

What are OpenAI's current Azure consumption, share of RPO, unrestricted liquidity and receivable ageing? How much of the $10.0B is consumption versus revenue share? The reported rate remains unverified and the payment cap is undisclosed.

What are Microsoft's OpenAI carrying value and remaining loss-recognition capacity?

Which leases and purchase commitments can be cancelled, and on what terms? Which third-party contracts slipped, and do late-delivery termination rights apply? The filing does not allocate obligations by supplier.

Finance-inclusive capex, stated lease amounts ($7.4B on the call versus $5.192B of finance right-of-use additions) and cash PP&E do not reconcile by simple addition, and operating-lease additions now sit outside the capex measure. A consistent bridge is needed. The call's "calendar year" is read as 2026; no calendar-2027 figure was given.

The valuation interpretation remains unproven without a reverse valuation and a dated consensus denominator.
