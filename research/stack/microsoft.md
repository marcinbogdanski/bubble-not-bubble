# Microsoft

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Claude claude-fable-5-1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [microsoft-capex.csv](/data/microsoft-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Microsoft sells Azure capacity, enterprise AI services and software features, and remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner. September-quarter revenue was $92.3B (+19%) and operating income $45.9B (+21%); Azure revenue was $33.2B, up 48% (claims 1, 13).
- Capex is outrunning cash generation's growth. Finance-inclusive capex was $45.2B in the quarter; cash PP&E took 68% of operating cash flow, and free cash flow fell 27% year over year to $18.6B. After lease principal, distributions and other investing, the illustrative residual was −$6.452B (claims 2–5, 11).
- Microsoft has started funding the build with bonds: $25.0B of notes from 2028 to 2066 settled on 2026-11-04, at yields up to 6.356%. Cash and short-term investments fell to $64.5B before the issue (claims 10, 30).
- Obligations keep growing: uncommenced leases reached $364.6B, starting through FY2034, and leases are shifting to operating classification, which finance-inclusive capex does not capture (claims 7–9, 12).
- OpenAI concentration is now visible in revenue: $10.0B in the quarter, 10.8% of the total, with the receivable up to $7.2B. Its share of the flat $677B commercial RPO remains undisclosed (claims 17–19).
- Calendar-2026 capex guidance is about $170B, trimmed from $175B because third-party (neocloud) capacity deliveries slipped to the second half. Demand still exceeds supply, and management guides positive FY2027 FCF (claims 6, 14, 28).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether OpenAI collections, replacement customers and contract recoveries can support the infrastructure obligations. Software earnings, an Aaa rating and market access provide buffers; neither persistent shortages nor inevitable overbuild is established.

## Analysis

Microsoft's software earnings and enterprise distribution support the build. Azure growth accelerated to 48% on the recast definition, Microsoft Cloud grew 30%, and management again says demand exceeds supply (claims 13, 14, 16). Nearly 90% of FY2026 Cloud revenue outside frontier labs measures realized customer mix, not the non-AI share or the ability to redeploy spare GPUs profitably. Cloud margins of 66%, guided to 65%, show infrastructure costs reaching earnings. Capacity constraints can affect reported growth; they do not demonstrate profitable demand at every future price. Higher-than-expected token pricing is a single-quarter observation.

Cash generation is still large, but capex now grows faster than it. Q1 FCF fell 27% to $18.6B as cash PP&E doubled (claims 3–4). After lease principal and distributions the residual was $6.497B; deducting other investing, mostly component purchases, makes it −$6.452B (claims 11, 29). Cash fell $12.3B in the quarter, and a $25.0B bond issue followed (claims 10, 30). Leverage remains low: as-adjusted debt of $60.4B is small against $470.509B of equity and TTM operating cash flow near $196B. Spreads of +25 to +105bp show the market treats Microsoft as near-sovereign credit. The point is direction, not distress: the build now draws on reserves and debt markets as well as operating cash.

Depreciation rose 56% to $11.1B in the quarter (claim 26), and about three quarters of capex buys short-lived CPUs and GPUs (claim 5). Earnings will carry a fast-rising depreciation charge on assets with short economic lives; operating margin held only because revenue grew faster and opex rose 6%.

The obligations schedule makes the longer-term risk concrete. Uncommenced leases reached $364.6B, almost four times the June 2025 level, some conditional on contractual milestones (claim 9). Lease payments include commenced and uncommenced contracts; adding them to recognized lease liabilities mixes measurement bases. Purchase and construction commitments overlap future operating and investing cash uses, and supplier announcements cannot be added to filing totals (claims 8, 12, 23–25).

The building-life change shifts new leases toward operating classification. In Q1 operating right-of-use additions exceeded finance-lease additions, and operating lease liabilities rose 25% (claims 7–8). Finance-inclusive capex therefore understates the build relative to earlier quarters. The cut in calendar-2026 guidance from $175B to $170B is attributed to third-party delivery delays shifted into the second half, not to lower demand (claim 6). Delivery slippage at neoclouds is a supplier-execution signal; whether contract terms let Microsoft walk from late tranches is set in those notes (claims 23–25).

OpenAI is simultaneously a customer, investment and distribution partner. Its $10.0B of Q1 revenue, 10.8% of Microsoft's total, compares with $24.1B for all of FY2026, though the filing does not separate consumption from revenue-share payments (claim 19). The receivable grew 20% in the quarter. Commercial RPO was flat sequentially at $677B; with no OpenAI share disclosed, the December-based scenario is not a current exposure estimate (claims 17–18). Nadella's remark that "four customers who buy a lot of capacity" is "not a business" signals intent to diversify, not evidence of it.

A payment failure could impair receivables and investment value, reduce future revenue and leave idle capacity. Recognized revenue, receivables and backlog cannot be added as a face-value loss; lost revenue is not lost profit. HLBV accounting, funded capital and ownership do not establish carrying value or remaining loss-recognition capacity (claims 19–20). Software profits and broad cloud demand provide buffers; recoveries and replacement-customer economics remain unknown.

Shareholders bear lower cash generation and impaired returns; new bondholders sit senior to them but are exposed only in a far worse case. Suppliers and financiers may suffer earlier liquidity stress, while Microsoft remains liable under valid accepted contracts. Delivery, acceptance, termination and recourse determine the order (claim 24).

The 58.7x FCF multiple predates the results and the lower Q1 FCF; it contrasts earnings with cash generation but does not identify the market's assumed capex path (claim 27).

## Open questions

What are OpenAI's current Azure consumption, share of RPO, unrestricted liquidity and receivable ageing? How much of the $10.0B is consumption versus revenue share? The reported rate remains unverified and the payment cap is undisclosed.

What are Microsoft's OpenAI carrying value and remaining loss-recognition capacity?

Which leases and purchase commitments can be cancelled, and on what terms? Which third-party contracts slipped, and do late-delivery termination rights apply? The filing does not allocate obligations by supplier.

Finance-inclusive capex, stated lease amounts ($7.4B on the call versus $5.192B of finance right-of-use additions) and cash PP&E do not reconcile by simple addition, and operating-lease additions now sit outside the capex measure. A consistent bridge is needed. The call's "calendar year" is read as 2026; no calendar-2027 figure was given.

The valuation interpretation remains unproven without a reverse valuation and a dated consensus denominator.
