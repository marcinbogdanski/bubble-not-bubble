# Amazon: profitable cloud, increasingly externally funded expansion

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra (2026-11-06) · Data: [amazon-capex.csv](/data/amazon-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- AWS supplies cloud infrastructure, model access and Trainium accelerators. Q3 revenue grew 39% with a 38.7% operating margin, and AWS earned 63% of consolidated operating income. Management projects AWS growth above 40% in each of 2027 and 2028. These establish operating strength, not returns on new AI infrastructure (claims 1–3).
- Amazon's consolidated build far exceeds internal cash generation. Trailing FCF was −$42.357B, or −$44.371B after lease and financing-obligation principal, with a further $65.156B of trailing investment and acquisition outflows (claims 4, 6).
- The approximately $220B 2026 cash-capex guide holds, and 2027 is to be meaningfully higher. Nine-month operating cash funded 66.1% of net cash capex; investment outflows widened the funding gap. Long-term-debt proceeds were $115.100B, including the $17.5B term loan. Up to approximately $80B of secured supplier borrowing capacity starts in 2027. Liquid assets fell to $114.2B; long-term debt reached $180.9B face (claims 5–11).
- Amazon finances major customers. It completed its $50B OpenAI investment and has $15B of delivery-contingent Anthropic capacity outstanding. Anthropic's $238.7B carrying value equals 38.9% of Amazon's book equity; most of the Q3 increase went through other comprehensive income rather than earnings (claims 13–16).
- Backlog reached $578B and management reported triple-digit year-on-year growth. Announced lab commitments are large relative to net backlog growth, but inclusion, consumption and customer shares are undisclosed. Management's diversification description does not establish the concentration (claims 19–20).
- The central uncertainty is whether contracted demand produces the cash paybacks management claims while capital intensity rises further in 2027. Strong AWS earnings, outside lab financing and long-dated unsecured notes argue against near-term distress; rapid asset growth, correlated customer financing and secured vendor financing increase execution risk. Overbuild, insolvency and mispricing are not demonstrated (claims 21–30).

## Analysis

AWS growth and margins establish operating strength, but not utilization or returns by infrastructure vintage. Management attributes margin pressure to lower-margin AI work and component prices (claims 1–2, 22).

The capital bill is predominantly in AWS: about three quarters of net PP&E additions. That measure includes noncash assets and ordinary cloud spending, so it is not AWS cash capex or AI-only investment (claim 21). AWS net PP&E grew 64.7% in nine months, and AWS depreciation and amortization 63.4% year-on-year against 39% revenue growth. The annualized operating-income/net-PP&E proxies fell from their H1 readings as assets outran earnings; they still show substantial profitability against deployed assets, but omit other invested capital and annualize a growing business (claim 22).

Management's return case is attributed: under-three-year payback, chips used over five to six years, and a claim that payback is accelerating (claim 23). The 2025 shortening of a server subset's life cuts against inflated-earnings claims while showing that obsolescence matters; Q3 talk of extending lives should be watched in the next 10-K (claim 24). Profitable utilization, durable pricing and manageable replacement costs must validate the payback.

Amazon's FCF deducts cash property purchases net of sales and incentives. The adjusted −$44.371B also deducts lease and financing-obligation principal, but excludes $65.156B of trailing investment outflows (claims 4, 6). Unpaid PP&E increased $26.779B over the same trailing period; the nine-month increase was $21.473B, including purchases on extended terms. Supplier-loan principal will be classified as debt repayments and therefore fall outside these FCF measures (claim 9). The approximately $220B guide implies about $47.8B of Q4 cash capex, versus $75.9B in Q3; lower spending and delivery/payment timing remain possible explanations, not established facts (claim 5). Negative FCF during expansion does not itself demonstrate poor project returns.

External borrowing finances a substantial shortfall. Nine-month OCF of $113.841B covered 66.1% of $172.188B net cash capex; after $63.753B of investment/acquisition outflows, the deficit was $122.100B before financing principal. Long-term-debt proceeds were $115.100B, including the $17.5B term loan; net of debt repayments they were $112.341B. Liquid assets fell $8.803B (claims 6–11). Management's twelve-month operating-liquidity assessment includes borrowing and other financing. Parent notes are mostly long-dated and have no financial covenants, but lack subsidiary guarantees and rank behind subsidiary creditors; bank facilities retain covenants/default terms, including a good-faith-dispute exception for specified vendor-financing acceleration (claim 29). Interest remains covered by operating income, though trailing cash interest understates the current accrual pace and excludes capitalized interest (claim 30). Contractual commitments create substantial future payment demands (claim 12). The conditional Globalstar acquisition expected in 2027 is a further non-AI funding claim: announcement-date value was approximately $10.9B including debt, with mixed cash/stock consideration rather than an all-cash obligation (claim 12).

Backlog has a 6.1-year weighted remaining contract life, not a disclosed collection horizon. The labs Amazon finances have made large commitments, but their actual share of backlog growth is not disclosed (claims 19–20). OpenAI broadens the customer base while keeping correlated exposure to frontier-lab funding. Filed payment obligations protect AWS contractually but pressure Anthropic's ability to pay across suppliers (claims 17, 28). Outside financing and filed positive operating cash flow support payment capacity, though interim accounts are unaudited and management expects significant future cash use (claims 26–27).

Anthropic's carrying value is neither cash nor an ownership percentage. Q3 pre-tax income was below operating income because net non-operating activity was negative. Anthropic note value rose by approximately $48.3B before tax; the $35.974B after-tax unrealized OCI line covers all available-for-sale debt securities. Notes currently put unrealized valuation changes through OCI, with reclassification on conversion or realization; preferred-stock observable-price adjustments and impairments already affect earnings. IPO conversion produces common stock subject to lock-up and securities-law restrictions. The $238.7B carrying value, 38.9% of equity, therefore creates both earnings and book-equity risk (claims 14–15).

Shareholders bear investment losses and impairments first; unsecured parent-note holders rank behind subsidiary creditors on subsidiary assets and behind secured creditors on collateral. A favorable equity case requires sustained AWS growth near the projected 40%, retail and advertising cash generation, and eventual normalization of capital intensity after a larger 2027. The evidence supports demanding execution requirements and growing capital-return and counterparty risk, without establishing overbuild, insolvency or mispricing.

## Open questions

Headline AWS growth (39%) and growth calculated from disclosed sales (39.3%) differ by rounding. The exact AWS allocation of the energy gain is undisclosed.

Whether the ~$220B guide reflects lower Q4 spending or delivery/payment timing remains unresolved; Q4 disclosures should clarify it. The size of the 2027 increase is unquantified. Amazon disclosed no gigawatts of power added in the quarter or year.

Anthropic's filing describes fixed payments, true-up exceptions and cancellation rights; complete contracts would clarify collateral, prepayments and enforcement. Amazon's recognized Anthropic and OpenAI revenue, customer margins, exact stakes and customer-level backlog remain unknown.

Segment cash flows, AI versus ordinary-cloud capital allocation, Trainium utilization and returns by vintage would test management's payback case. Audited lab accounts and public valuations would test customer payment capacity and investment marks.
