# Amazon: profitable cloud, increasingly externally funded expansion

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [amazon-capex.csv](/data/amazon-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- AWS supplies cloud infrastructure, model access and Trainium accelerators. Q3 revenue grew 39% with a 38.7% operating margin, and AWS earned 63% of consolidated operating income. Management projects AWS growth above 40% in each of 2027 and 2028. These establish operating strength, not returns on new AI infrastructure (claims 1–3).
- Amazon's consolidated build far exceeds internal cash generation. Trailing FCF was −$42.357B, or −$44.371B after lease and financing-obligation principal, with a further $65.156B of trailing investment and acquisition outflows (claims 4, 6).
- The approximately $220B 2026 cash-capex guide holds, and 2027 is to be "meaningfully" higher. Funding is now mostly external: $115.100B of long-term-debt proceeds in nine months, the $17.5B term loan drawn, and a new secured supplier loan facility of up to about $80B from 2027. Liquid assets fell to $114.2B while long-term debt rose to $180.9B face (claims 5, 7–11).
- Amazon finances major customers. It completed its $50B OpenAI investment and has $15B of delivery-contingent Anthropic capacity outstanding. Anthropic's $238.7B carrying value equals 38.9% of Amazon's book equity; most of the Q3 increase went through other comprehensive income rather than earnings (claims 13–16).
- Backlog of $578B grew by triple digits year-on-year. Much of the growth plausibly comes from the labs Amazon funds, which management describes as "well-diversified" without disclosing shares (claims 19–20).
- The central uncertainty is whether contracted demand produces the cash paybacks management claims while capital intensity rises further in 2027. Strong AWS earnings, outside lab financing and long-dated unsecured notes argue against near-term distress; rapid asset growth, correlated customer financing and secured vendor financing increase execution risk. Overbuild, insolvency and mispricing are not demonstrated (claims 21–30).

## Analysis

AWS is a substantial operating business. Revenue growth, margins, backlog and contracted chip commitments establish sales, contracts and deployment. They do not reveal utilization, maintenance spending or cash returns by infrastructure vintage. The Q3 margin of 38.7% eased from Q2 as depreciation rose faster than revenue; management attributes the pressure to lower-margin AI work and component prices (claims 1–2, 22).

The capital bill is predominantly in AWS: about three quarters of net PP&E additions. That measure includes noncash assets and ordinary cloud spending, so it is not AWS cash capex or AI-only investment (claim 21). AWS net PP&E grew 64.7% in nine months, and AWS depreciation 63.4% year-on-year against 39% revenue growth. The operating-income/net-PP&E proxies fell as assets outran earnings; they still show substantial profitability against deployed assets, but omit other invested capital and annualize a growing business (claim 22).

Management's return case is attributed: under-three-year payback, chips used over five to six years, and a claim that payback is accelerating (claim 23). The 2025 shortening of a server subset's life cuts against inflated-earnings claims while showing that obsolescence matters; Q3 talk of extending lives should be watched in the next 10-K (claim 24). Profitable utilization, durable pricing and manageable replacement costs must validate the payback.

Amazon's FCF deducts cash property purchases net of sales and incentives. The adjusted −$44.371B also deducts lease and financing-obligation principal, and excludes $65.156B of trailing investment outflows, mostly lab equity (claims 4, 6). Two items flatter the cash measure: $21.5B of capex received but not yet paid, and the implied Q4 capex of about $47.8B, far below Q3's $75.9B (claims 5, 9). Either the guide is conservative or spending is being shifted into payables and 2027. Negative FCF during expansion does not itself demonstrate poor project returns.

Funding has moved from internal cash to markets. Nine-month long-term borrowing of $115.1B exceeded the $63.8B of lab and acquisition outflows and covered most of the cash deficit; liquid assets still fell $8.8B from year-end (claims 7, 10). The debt is mostly long-dated, unsecured and covenant-free, and interest remains small against operating income (claims 29–30). The new supplier facility of up to $80B is different in kind: secured on purchased equipment and maturing in five years, it resembles vendor financing and ties debt service to asset lives (claim 9). Commitments of $883.6B, including $159.6B of equipment purchase obligations, would persist if demand weakened (claim 12).

Backlog's collection horizon is 6.1 years. Most growth plausibly comes from the labs Amazon also finances; management calls it diversified but gives no shares (claims 19–20). OpenAI broadens the customer base while keeping correlated exposure to frontier-lab funding. Stronger reported payment obligations protect AWS contractually but pressure Anthropic's ability to pay across suppliers (claims 17, 28). Outside financing and reported adjusted profitability support payment capacity; neither establishes audited cash generation (claims 26–27).

Anthropic's carrying value is neither cash nor an ownership percentage. Q3 earnings were operating; the $36B Q3 revaluation of the notes went to equity through other comprehensive income, so a reversal would hit book equity first and earnings only on conversion or impairment (claims 14–15). With $238.7B in one private company equal to 38.9% of equity, shareholders bear valuation risk alongside operating risk.

Shareholders bear lower distributions, impairments and investment losses first; unsecured creditors retain claims on the diversified enterprise, though the secured facility would rank ahead on its collateral. A favorable equity case requires sustained AWS growth near the projected 40%, retail and advertising cash generation, and eventual normalization of capital intensity after a larger 2027. The evidence supports demanding execution requirements and growing capital-return and counterparty risk, without establishing overbuild, insolvency or mispricing.

## Open questions

Headline AWS growth (39%) and growth calculated from disclosed sales (39.3%) differ by rounding. The exact AWS allocation of the energy gain is undisclosed.

Whether the ~$220B guide implies a real Q4 slowdown or spending shifted into payables and supplier financing; the Q4 cash-flow statement will settle it. The size of the 2027 increase is unquantified. Amazon disclosed no gigawatts of power added in the quarter or year.

Public contracts would settle collateral, prepayments, cancellation rights and the reported spending-shortfall terms. Amazon's recognized Anthropic and OpenAI revenue, customer margins, exact stakes and customer-level backlog remain unknown.

Segment cash flows, AI versus ordinary-cloud capital allocation, Trainium utilization and returns by vintage would test management's payback case. Audited lab accounts and public valuations would test customer payment capacity and investment marks.
