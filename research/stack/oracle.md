# Oracle: profitable software, a leveraged AI infrastructure build

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Codex (OpenAI) · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- Oracle combines a profitable software franchise with rapidly expanding OCI infrastructure. Accounting profit remains strong; the infrastructure build does not fund itself from operating cash flow.
- FY2026 free cash flow was **−$23.686bn**; Q1 FY2027 added **−$5.396bn**, despite substantial customer advances. Those advances are financing economically, even when classified as operating cash flow. [S2]
- Borrowings total **$125.337bn**, or **$88.260bn net** of cash and securities, before leases. Future datacenter leases create substantial additional exposure. [S1]
- OpenAI confirms a partnership exceeding **$300bn over five years**. The precise enforceable minimum, cancellation rights and share of Oracle's RPO remain undisclosed. Calling the entire headline amount guaranteed cash would be unjustified. [S6]
- Equity, bonds, customer-funded hardware and developer financing materially improve feasibility. They redistribute risk; they do not establish that the completed assets will earn adequate returns.
- Credit stress is observable: Reuters reported **288bp five-year CDS**; S&P's downgrade to **BBB−** is corroborated by reputable reporting. Neither establishes imminent default. [S18], [S19]
- The largest uncertainty is whether OpenAI's cash payments arrive on the schedule needed to service Oracle's equipment, leases and debt. Oracle is overextended where it assumes long obligations before power, customer liquidity and durable rental margins are secured.

## Analysis

**Economic position (analytical inference, C).** Oracle's database, support and applications franchise provides earnings and customer relationships. Consolidated profitability does not establish GPU-rental returns: there is no standalone audited income statement. The leaked margin and management's mature-project illustration need reconciliation for utilization, depreciation, equipment vintages and overhead. [S2], [S25], [S26]

**Guidance chronology and exact language.** Catz's September 9, 2025 statement said OCI would “increase to $32 billion, $73 billion, $114 billion, and $144 billion over the subsequent four years.” October reporting subsequently raised these targets. [S7], [S9], [S10] Kehring's October 16 slides label the table “Updated Long-term Financial Outlook for Revenue and Earnings”; row 15 reproduces its annual values. [S8] Oracle's September 10, 2026 release states: “For fiscal year 2027, we now expect total revenue to be at least $90 billion, and non-GAAP EPS to be at $8.10.” [S2] Maxson's same-day call retained gross capex guidance, “with not more than $70 billion in net cash CapEx.” [S11] AI World 2026 is still ahead. Later-year targets are dated, not newly reconfirmed promises.

**RPO is contracted activity, not funded cash.** Oracle's accounting definition includes deferred revenue, uncollected invoices and future billing; certain variable consideration is omitted. [S0] OpenAI's announcement corroborates the partnership's scale, upgrading the headline beyond an anonymous press leak. It does not publish the underlying order forms. Row 3's ratios assume an unchanged original amount and comparable scope; they ignore delivery, subsequent orders and amendments. Neither is a measured customer concentration. Nor does evidence establish Oracle as OpenAI's largest supplier by *currently delivered* compute; that superlative needs a comparable supplier register.

A signed performance obligation can still depend on Oracle commissioning usable capacity. Customer insolvency can impair recoverability despite contractual liability. Undisclosed acceptance milestones, power conditions, minimum purchases, advances, guarantees and termination rights matter more than backlog. Current revenue diversification says little about future customer concentration.

**Corporate versus project financing.** The bond deals are Oracle unsecured corporate obligations; issue yields in the table are historical pricing, not October executable yields. The bank revolver is a liquidity backstop with rating-sensitive pricing, not permanent equity. The legacy term loan predates the current build and should not be described as a newly raised Stargate loan. Calendar-year and fiscal-year financing plans overlap; the completed ATM is already included. [S12], [S13], [S14], [S15], [S0]

The ownership chain matters:

| Site | Buildings / development capital | Compute relationship and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Abilene, Texas | Crusoe develops; JV with Blue Owl-managed funds and Primary Digital Infrastructure funds real estate | Oracle operates OCI and delivered Nvidia racks for OpenAI. Delivery does not prove legal title to every GPU. [S6], [S21] |
| Shackelford County, Texas; Port Washington, Wisconsin | Vantage develops Oracle-linked campuses; construction lending reported separately from Oracle bonds | Oracle supplies OpenAI compute and leases infrastructure. Public announcements do not disclose every SPV, lien or equipment owner. [S6], [S23] |
| Doña Ana County, New Mexico, Project Jupiter | STACK develops with Blue Owl-managed investment; project lenders fund construction | Oracle is the intended tenant/operator serving OpenAI. GPU title and exact financing recourse remain undisclosed. [S24], [S30], [S31] |
| Lordstown, Ohio; Milam County, Texas | SoftBank/OpenAI development, with SB Energy involved in Milam | Stargate branding alone does not make these Oracle liabilities. [S6] |

Customer-supplied GPUs contradict blanket Oracle ownership. Leasing buildings leaves economic exposure even where developer SPVs borrow. Adding project loans, Oracle debt and lease commitments without mapping overlapping claims would double count.

**Can the build be funded? (Conditional analysis, C.)** The CSV reconciles actual OCF less cash capex. For planning, use either gross capex against OCF including advances, or net capex outlay against OCF excluding the corresponding advances. Subtracting net capex from unadjusted OCF counts customer funding twice. Net outlay also includes manufacturing-finance flows; it is not ordinary FCF. [S2]

The following deliberately transparent illustration uses row 37's annualized operating cash, management's net spending ceiling, the disclosed principal schedule and unchanged FY26 dividends:

- FY27: **70−46.960+7.210+5.787 = 36.037** external funding required. The announced **40** could cover this and leave **35.857** from opening cash/securities of **31.894**.
- FY28, repeating those operating and net-spending assumptions: **70−46.960+10.145+5.787 = 38.972** required. Without another raise, even exhausting that illustrative opening cash leaves **3.115** uncovered.
- FY28 stress, gross spending at the prior guidance midpoint and no customer/manufacturer offsets: **92.5−46.960+10.145+5.787 = 61.472**, leaving **25.615** uncovered against the same cash balance.

These are C-grade scenarios, not predictions. Annualizing a seasonally strong quarter retains ordinary deferred-revenue inflows; it is not recurring earnings. Later revenue growth could improve cash generation; higher capex, interest, dividends or lease cash payments could worsen it. Financing fees, acquisitions and a necessary liquidity reserve are excluded. Existing operating interest and rent are already in OCF; adding them wholesale again would double count. Finance-lease principal and future increases need a fuller cash model. No verified FY28 capex guidance was found. The defensible conclusion is conditional fundability with continuing capital access, not self-financing or inevitable insolvency.

**Delay, nonpayment and loss allocation (conditional analysis, C).** A construction delay can leave interest and carrying expenses accruing before compute is billable. In September, Bloomberg reported Oracle's force-majeure notice while carrying both Oracle's statement that the notice did not establish a delay and Blue Owl's assertion that commitments were unchanged. Its report says interim costs could continue even if rent is deferred. That is an unresolved contractual risk, not proof Oracle can walk away. [S30]

If OpenAI cannot pay, Oracle first loses collections and utilization while retaining obligations unless contracts provide relief. Shareholders absorb lower profit, impairment and dilution; corporate creditors are exposed if cash resources and refinancing fail. Project equity is economically junior to its secured lenders, but recoveries depend on guarantees, completion support and tenant claims. Unsold construction loans remain with underwriting banks; distributed loans move exposure to buying funds, insurers or other lenders. Blue Owl-managed fund investors should not automatically be conflated with Blue Owl's corporate balance sheet. Paid chip vendors may have transferred much of the demand risk, while equipment owners retain obsolescence and resale risk. The actual waterfall cannot be resolved without private loan and lease documents.

**What securities discount (interpretation, C).** The verified equity price produces a moderate near-term adjusted earnings multiple, not evidence that the old growth narrative is fully priced. Its much lower multiple of the distant target illustrates how much execution remains disputed; time value, dilution and non-GAAP adjustments make that comparison unsuitable as a price target. [S20] CDS and the disclosed debt fair-value discount indicate materially more demanding financing conditions. The latter also reflects interest rates and duration, not solely expected credit losses. Neither measure directly gives default probability. Current executable bond yields were not verified.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Fundability versus leverage:** completed equity issuance, actual customer advances and supplied hardware contradict a debt-only funding narrative. They also transfer obligations to future service periods; continued funding availability remains uncertain.
- **Margins versus obsolescence:** management's example and reported stronger renewal pricing challenge the low-margin extrapolation. Neither discloses a complete, audited return after financing and replacement investment.
- **Insider profit-taking versus conviction:** the verified Catz example accompanied option exercise under a prearranged plan. It proves a sale, not advance knowledge of failure. Ellison's cancelled plan is counterevidence. A complete, deduplicated insider-sale total was not verified. [S27], [S28]
- **Dating and source limits:** the Jupiter stress article retrieved was September reporting, not a newly verified mid-October FT investigation. S&P's original page was located but its substantive text was inaccessible; the downgrade is secondary grade B. Moody's and Fitch observations are explicitly dated, not certified unchanged through today. The private contract, current bond yields and exact GPU ownership remain unresolved. Search snippets, accessible republications and a retrieved SEC filing were used where direct pages failed; no paywall was bypassed.
- **Ranking:** “most leveraged large AI builder” is plausible within a narrowly defined hyperscaler peer group, but this note does not establish that ranking using uniform lease-adjusted leverage. It does establish substantial financing and counterparty exposure without that superlative.
