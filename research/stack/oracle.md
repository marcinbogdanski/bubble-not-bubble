# Oracle: profitable software, a leveraged AI infrastructure build

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [oracle-capex.csv](/data/oracle-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Oracle sells OCI capacity, much of it to OpenAI, funded by debt, leases, equity and customer money. RPO is $664B, ~90% infrastructure; 13% converts within a year (claims 1–2, 4).
- FY2026 FCF was −$23.686B; Q1 FY2027 −$5.396B even with $11.363B of customer advances in operating cash (claims 7–9).
- Investor Day (2026-10-28) set positive FCF in H2 FY2028, FY2029 FCF of at least $30B, FY2030 revenue of at least $225B, and net debt below 3.5× adjusted EBITDA in FY2029. No capex or FCF by year (claim 19).
- The plan sizes ~$220B of remaining build (about 6GW). Vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware, prepayments and operating cash cover all but $20B. Oracle capped new capital-markets money through FY2030 at that $20B, excluding refinancing (claims 17, 20).
- It raised the whole $20B five days later: $10B of stock at $116, 11.2% below the prior close, and $10B of notes at 7.06–8.67%, closed 2026-11-04 (claims 22–23). Those yields sit above the BB index and, at the long end, the single-B index (claim 24).
- Borrowings are $135.3B pro forma; leases add $43.8B, with $288B more not yet commenced (claims 11–12).
- S&P BBB−/A-3 stable, Moody's Baa2 negative and Fitch BBB stable all rated the new notes (claims 25–27).
- **Judgement (C): Oracle is overextended relative to its rating and its own cash generation. The raise removed the near-term liquidity question but not the dependence.** If vendor and customer funding fall short, Oracle must break its cap, slow the build or draw the revolver. The biggest uncertainty is whether the ~$110B of offsets the bridge implies arrives (claims 20, 39–40).

## Analysis

**Profitability.** Software earns the profit: Q1 GAAP operating income was $6.728B (claim 6). There is no audited GPU income statement. Management's 1GW example, a cost-plus contract at ~35% gross margin and ~28% ROI (claim 21), conflicts with the leaked 14% GPU rental margin (claim 36). Utilization of 97.9% and new contracts (claim 37) show demand, not realized returns.

**RPO is contracted activity, not cash.** About 90% of the $664B is infrastructure, billable only once capacity is commissioned. Oracle claims rising diversification but gives no customer share; S&P's "about half OpenAI" is the only estimate (claims 1, 4). The gap between ~$50B and ~$70B of reported OpenAI revenue (claim 38) weakens any reading of headline revenue as available customer funding. It shows no missed payment.

**The Investor Day plan.** Oracle replaced open-ended funding with a bounded plan (claims 17, 19). The bridge carries the weight. About $220B of self-funded-equivalent build becomes a $20B gap only after vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware, prepayments and operating cash (claim 20). Oracle gave no amounts for any of them. Reading the "~50% reduction" label as the three vendor and customer sources implies roughly $110B of offsets over seven quarters (C). September guidance implied $20–25B for all of FY2027 (claim 18). The bridge also implies FY2028 build of about 220−(90–95−28.5) ≈ $154–159B on a self-funded basis, against no FY2028 capex guidance (C).

**What "FCF positive" will measure.** Under vendor financing, principal repayments go to financing cash flow (claim 21), and customer hardware never enters capex. Reported FCF can turn positive while vendor-financing liabilities and lease commencements ($288B, claim 12) still grow. Oracle's leverage target counts both (claim 19); that is the measure to watch.

**Execution of the raise.** Oracle raised the full $20B within five days, earlier than the January–May 2027 window it had signalled in September (claim 17). The equity priced 11.2% below the prior close and 19.7% below the Investor Day close, for 2.85% dilution (claim 23). The notes priced at 240–335bp, 34–129bp wider than the BB index and, at the long end, above the single-B yield (claim 24). That is an investment-grade rating priced as high yield. The issue adds ≈$0.777B of annual interest and has no step-up or change-of-control protection (claims 14, 22).

**Can FY2027 be funded? (C.)** Yes, on central assumptions: ≈$28.3B of liquidity at May 2027 after the raise, plus $19.7B undrawn (claim 39). FY2027 raises of $40B cover the central $39.6B need (claim 40). The weak point is customer funding. Without the offsets, FY2027 alone is $17–29B short, and the cap leaves no planned capital-market buffer. Oracle would have to draw the revolver, lean harder on vendor financing (debt-like), slow the build, or break a commitment made publicly a week before.

**Ratings.** The raise is credit-supportive in the agencies' own terms. Moody's treats equity as key to Baa2, and Fitch's triggers punish a shift away from the balanced mix (claim 27). S&P's tests (leverage above 4.5×, FOCF path by FY2029, claim 26) now meet a company guiding to positive FCF in FY2028, though S&P's adjusted FOCF need not match Oracle's FCF. The FY2027 leverage forecast in the mid-4× range is still the binding constraint. A miss on Q2 offsets or a lease-driven rise in adjusted debt would use that headroom.

**Who bears the losses (C).** Site financing sits off balance sheet but not outside Oracle's economics (claims 30–32). Shareholders take first losses; project lenders carry delay risk. Parent noteholders are structurally junior to $107.2B of subsidiary liabilities and have no financial covenants (claims 11, 22).

**Delay.** The Jupiter permit survived the dismissal motion; the hearing is 2026-12-07 (claim 33). The pipeline delay and force majeure remain reported (claim 35). A slip pushes FCF past H2 FY2028 while leases and interest accrue.

**What must be true for a return.** The 1GW economics must hold at scale (claim 21), leases must commence only as capacity bills, and customers must prepay or supply hardware at the bridge's scale.

## Open questions

- **Bridge components:** Oracle gives no amounts for vendor financing, customer hardware, prepayments or other cash flows (claim 20). Q2 and Q3 cash flow statements, and the 10-Q's vendor-financing balance, would settle it.
- **Agency texts:** the Moody's and Fitch triggers come from one secondary report (claim 27). No S&P comment on the plan was found. The agencies' own releases, and their treatment of vendor financing as debt, are needed.
- **S&P FOCF test year:** PPC Land reports FY2029; Fortune reads FY2028 (claim 26).
- **Overextended or conditionally fragile?** An alternative reading: with $40B raised and positive FCF guided for FY2028, Oracle is funded and only conditionally fragile. Realized offsets and project returns would settle it.
- **Margins:** the 35% example against the 14% leak (claims 21, 36). Audited OCI returns after depreciation would settle it.
- **Ownership:** GPU title, SPV liens and recourse at each site are undisclosed.
