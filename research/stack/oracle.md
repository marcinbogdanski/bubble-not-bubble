# Oracle: profitable software, a leveraged AI infrastructure build

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra (2026-11-06) · Data: [oracle-capex.csv](/data/oracle-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Oracle sells OCI capacity, with substantial future OpenAI commitments, financed by debt, leases, equity and customers (claims 1, 4).
- FCF remains negative despite customer advances (claims 7–9).
- Targets: positive FCF in H2 FY2028; FY2029 FCF ≥$30B. No complete annual capex/FCF dollar schedule (claim 19).
- The ~$220B bridge relies on vendor financing, customer hardware, prepayments and operating cash; further market funding is capped at $20B through FY2030, excluding refinancing (claims 17, 20).
- November’s $20B gross raise closed seven days after Investor Day: equal equity and notes, with expensive debt pricing (claims 22–24).
- Pro forma borrowings $135.3B; leases, purchase commitments and a historical lessor guarantee add exposure (claims 11–12).
- Reported ratings remain investment grade (claims 25–27).
- **Judgement (C): Oracle remains dependent on customer and vendor funding. November’s raise materially strengthens liquidity, but does not establish that funding risk has disappeared.** The corrected central scenario leaves about $29.3B at May 2027, below May 2026 liquidity; borrowing capacity and future offsets remain conditional.

## Analysis

**Profitability.** Oracle is profitable at group level: consolidated profit does not isolate software or GPUs (claim 6). The illustrative future contract economics and the historical reported GPU fleet margin differ in period, scope and accounting basis; their gap is a question for reconciliation, not a demonstrated contradiction. The filing generally depreciates servers and networking equipment over six years; actual asset lives and the conversion of construction in progress into operating assets affect future returns.

**RPO is contracted activity, not cash.** About 90% of the $664B is infrastructure, recognised as revenue as the applicable performance obligations are satisfied; billing and prepayments can occur earlier. Customer concentration is unquantified (claim 4).

**The Investor Day plan.** The bridge infers ~$110B vendor/customer offsets (C), with operating cash also required (claim 20). The annual gross-to-net difference is not necessarily customer cash (claim 18). The sources do not reconcile the self-funded-equivalent bridge to annual Oracle capex, so they do not establish a FY2028 capex figure.

**What "FCF positive" measures.** Vendor principal is financing cash flow; customer hardware bypasses capex. Positive FCF can coexist with rising vendor and lease liabilities, both counted in the leverage target (claims 12, 19, 21).

**Execution of the raise.** Oracle priced the full $20B on November 2, five days after Investor Day, and closed it on November 4, seven days after the plan announcement. The equity sale is increasing the share count by 2.85% and diluting existing ownership by about 2.77% (claim 23). The pricing indicates expensive funding for an investment-grade issuer; different maturities and spread conventions prevent a matched high-yield comparison.

**Can FY2027 be funded? (C.)** The corrected central scenario leaves about $29.3B of cash and securities at May 2027, around $2.6B below the opening balance (claims 39–40). This supports near-term funding under the stated assumptions, not preservation of May liquidity or a guarantee against quarterly shortfalls. The $10B revolver is conditional and backs commercial paper; the combined $19.7B capacity is not an independent committed cash buffer. Management already stated in the Q1 filing that operations and available financing should cover at least twelve months, with discretion over some spending. Lower collections, funding delays, collateral demands or additional cash uses could still require spending changes or further financing.

**Ratings.** Equity supports ratings, but S&P's adjusted leverage/FOCF tests remain constraints (claims 26–27). Moody’s reportedly put adjusted debt/EBITDA above 4× at August 31 and quarterly FCF near −$7B, and retained uncertainty about equity funding beyond 2026. Its metrics differ from Oracle’s. The original agency texts were not retrieved.

**Who bears the losses (C).** Project borrowings are not automatically Oracle borrowings, but Oracle bears contractual lease and purchase exposure, and the annual filing also disclosed a lessor-debt guarantee whose subsequent disposition remains unverified (claim 12). Parent notes rank behind subsidiary liabilities (claim 11).

**Delay.** Jupiter’s revised permit application survived a dismissal motion; the public hearing is scheduled for December 7 (claim 33). Permit approval and power delivery remain dependencies. Management describes phased delivery and backup options, so a site slip could threaten the group FCF target but does not prove it must move. Reported force-majeure relief would require agreement; other costs and the full lease-term rent obligation could remain (claims 34–35).

**What must be true for a return.** The 1GW economics must hold at scale (claim 21); customers must pay enough, soon enough, to cover lease costs that can begin before service delivery, and customers must prepay or supply hardware at the bridge's scale.

## Open questions

- **Bridge components:** Future cash-flow statements and any separate disclosure of vendor balances, repayments and customer receipts may test the bridge; no complete component reconciliation is currently available.
- **Agency texts:** obtain original actions and vendor-financing treatment (claim 27).
- **S&P FOCF test year:** PPC Land reports FY2029; Fortune reads FY2028 (claim 26).
- **Conditional liquidity:** realized offsets, quarterly cash needs and project returns test the positive central scenario.
- **Margins:** reconcile illustrative non-GAAP returns and historical GPU fleet margins (claims 21, 36).
- **Ownership:** GPU title, SPV liens and recourse at each site are undisclosed.
