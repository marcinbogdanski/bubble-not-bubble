# Oracle: profitable software, a leveraged AI infrastructure build

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [oracle-capex.csv](/data/oracle-capex.csv)*

## Summary

- Oracle sells OCI capacity, much of it to OpenAI, funded by debt, leases, equity and customer money. RPO is $664B; 13% converts within twelve months (claims 1–2).
- FY2026 operating cash was $31.977B against $55.663B of capex: a $23.686B consolidated deficit. Q1 FY2027 FCF was −$5.396B even with $11.363B of customer advances inside operating cash (claims 7–9). The software franchise is profitable, but its separate cash contribution is not measured.
- At 2026-08-31 borrowings were $125.337B ($88.260B net of cash and securities). Lease liabilities add $43.806B, and $288B of uncommenced leases start in FY27 Q2–FY29 (claims 11–12).
- OpenAI's partnership exceeds $300B over five years. Oracle discloses no customer share; S&P estimates OpenAI at roughly half of May's $638B RPO (claims 3–4).
- Oracle completed its $20B ATM inside a ~$40B FY2027 plan. It expects no more debt in calendar 2026, so the remaining debt falls in January–May 2027 (claim 17).
- S&P rates Oracle BBB−/A-3 stable since 2026-07-09. Its FY2027 forecasts (mid-4× leverage, FOCF nearly −$42B) leave little headroom against its triggers (leverage above 4.5×, no path to positive FOCF by FY2029). Moody's (Baa2 negative) and Fitch (BBB stable) sit two notches above junk. Five-year CDS was 308.25bp on 2026-10-21 after 315–322bp on 2026-10-19 (claims 23–26).
- Jupiter's air permit is pending, with a permit hearing on 2026-12-07; management says New Mexico and Wisconsin construction is on track (claims 31, 33).
- **Judgement (C): Oracle is overextended relative to its rating and its own cash generation, while current liquidity supports conditional fundability.** FY2027 needs about $40B. The plan covers that if customers fund $20–25B of capex; losing those offsets raises the need to roughly $60–65B (claims 18, 38–39). The single biggest uncertainty is whether customer funding and capital-market access hold through the 2027 debt window.

## Analysis

**Profitability.** The database, support and applications franchise earns the profit: Q1 GAAP operating income was $6.728B (claim 6). OCI is more than GPU rental, and there is no audited GPU income statement. Management's 30–40% AI-infrastructure margin and 35% project example (claim 21) conflict with the leaked 14% GPU rental margin (claim 22). Utilization of 97.9% and >$30B of new AI contracts (claim 20) show demand, not realized returns.

**RPO is contracted activity, not cash.** It includes future billings that depend on Oracle commissioning usable capacity. OpenAI's >$300B partnership corroborates the scale, but order forms, minimum purchases and termination rights are private (claim 3). S&P's estimate that OpenAI is about half of RPO is an agency estimate (claim 4). The reported gap between ~$50B and ~$70B of OpenAI annualized revenue (claim 37) weakens any reading of headline revenue as available customer funding; it shows no missed payment.

**Cash generation.** The build runs ahead of cash: FCF was −$23.686B in FY2026 and −$5.396B in Q1 FY2027 (claims 7–8). Planning must match gross capex with operating cash including advances, or net capex with operating cash excluding them. Subtracting net capex from unadjusted operating cash counts customer funding twice. In Q1 the two measures differ by the $0.830B manufacturer-financing outflow (claim 9).

**Financing and leverage.** Borrowings are $125.337B, lease liabilities $43.806B, and $288B of uncommenced leases will commence through FY2029 (claims 11–12). Interest expense rose 55% (claim 14). Bond issue yields are history, not current yields. The revolver is a backstop, not permanent funding (claim 16). The preferred carries a cash or dilution cost (claim 17).

**Can FY2027 be funded? (C.)** Rolling forward from $37.077B of August liquidity with central assumptions leaves ≈$8.4B without new money and ≈$28.4B with the remaining ≈$20B (claim 38). That excludes the $2.6B of restricted cash already reclassified, finance-lease principal and other financing flows. On a full-year basis, preserving May liquidity needs a central $39.610B, which is why a ~$40B plan broadly covers it (claim 39). Customer funding is the swing factor. Gross guidance of $90–95B against ≤$70B net implies $20–25B of offsets (claim 18). Without them the need rises to roughly $60–65B; losing only the offsets still to come adds ≈$10–15B (claim 39). The remaining debt tests market access in January–May 2027. The A-3 short-term rating suggests a narrower commercial-paper market (C inference). No FY2028 capex guidance was found.

**Who bears the losses (C).** The site financing sits off Oracle's balance sheet but not outside its economics (claims 28–30). Developers' SPVs borrow; Oracle leases the buildings and serves OpenAI. Project debt, Oracle debt and leases cannot be added without double counting. Customer-supplied GPUs mean Oracle does not own all the hardware. If OpenAI cannot pay, Oracle loses collections while keeping its obligations unless contracts give relief. Shareholders take first losses through lower profit, impairment and dilution. Project lenders and equity carry construction and delay risk; unsold loans stay with underwriting banks. Unsecured creditors lose only if cash and refinancing fail. The real waterfall is in private loan and lease documents.

**Delay.** A delay accrues interest and carrying costs before compute is billable. Management says construction is on track (claim 33). Against that: the Jupiter pipeline is reportedly delayed, a force-majeure notice is reported, and the air permit is pending with a 2026-12-07 hearing (claims 31, 34). Construction, power and billable-service clocks must be tracked separately. The emissions cut and the Point Beach cost absorption (claims 32, 35) ease permitting and power but add cost.

**Rating headroom.** S&P's own forecasts (mid-4× leverage, FOCF nearly −$42B) sit just inside its triggers (claim 24). A commissioning or customer-funding miss would use that headroom. Moody's negative outlook is a separate warning (claim 25).

**What markets price (C).** The equity trades at 15.8× near-term EPS and 6.1× the FY2030 target (claim 27). CDS around 308–322bp and debt fair value at 84.6% of carrying amount (claims 15, 26) show tighter financing conditions; the fair-value gap also reflects rates and duration. Neither gives a default probability.

**What must be true for a return.** OCI must reach the outlook path (claim 19), leases must commence only as capacity becomes billable, and OpenAI and other customers must keep paying and prefunding.

## Open questions

- **Overextended or conditionally fragile?** The Summary's C-grade judgement rests on rating headroom and dependence on customer and market funding. An alternative reading calls Oracle conditionally fragile with unresolved project returns, since no inability to refinance or expected project loss is quantified. Contract waterfalls and realized returns would settle it.
- **S&P FOCF test year:** PPC Land reports FY2029; Fortune reads FY2028. Fitch's −$18B FY2027 forecast differs from S&P's −$42B on different dates and definitions. The primary S&P text would settle it.
- **Jupiter deadline:** the 2026-12-07 hearing falls after the reported 2026-11-23 deadline. A ruling on extension is needed.
- **Margins:** management's 30–40% range against the 14% leak. Audited OCI segment returns after depreciation would settle it.
- **Insiders:** selected sales and purchases; no complete insider total or motive is established.
- **Ownership:** GPU title, SPV liens and financing recourse at each site are undisclosed.
