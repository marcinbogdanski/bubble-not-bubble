## Summary

- Anthropic sells Claude through API, apps and cloud marketplaces. Annual accounts are audited; 2026 interim results are unaudited.
- Revenue: $4.6B in 2025, $33.2B in 9M 2026, $17.0B in Q3. Usage supplies 79%, marketplaces 44% booked gross, top five customers ~25% of 9M sales. Policy enforcement may reduce revenue and growth (claims 1–3, 25).
- Q2's $974M operating profit became a $567M Q3 loss, reflecting expensive compute and donations. Adjusted Q3 profit $2.463B excludes $1.070B stock pay/$1.960B donations. Noncash valuation effects dominate the $104.0B nine-month net loss; dilution and cash-repayment risk remain (claims 5–8, 11).
- OCF was $6.569B; cash/investments $131.845B; net preferred proceeds $110.432B. Management expects significant operating/investing cash use even if performance improves, while expecting funding for at least 12 months (claims 9–10).
- Commitments $895.4B include $69.8B expected next year and $447.5B before end-2030; $213.8B has identified exits/conditions. Variable $150–175B excluded; rent may precede usable compute (claims 12–17).
- Suppliers fund the build: $77B conditional facilities plus $5B AMD equity; access may be delayed/lost. Broadcom residual-value support and Google support for anticipated TeraWulf financing are contingent (claims 18–24). IPO remains filed/unpriced, with an unconfirmed November target (claim 26).
- Central risk: volatile customer spending versus long-lived obligations, upfront investment and losses, compounded by legal restrictions, conditional finance and limited shareholder control.

## Analysis

**Filed revenue establishes scale, not durability.** Q3 revenue was $16.956B versus $1.382B a year earlier. Customer concentration persists despite rapid expansion; large customers can stop spending. Enforcement can remove violating revenue, not merely improve its mix. Gross marketplace sales do not prove circular funding. RPO is contracted revenue, not total demand or cash for compute (claims 1–3, 25, 29).

**Profitability depends on included costs.** Functional gross margin excludes much compute and distribution spending. Both short-term compute and donations contributed to Q3's GAAP loss. Adjusted profit excludes real shareholder costs. Note remeasurement is noncash, but conversion dilutes holders and unconverted notes can require cash repayment. Neither adjusted profit nor headline net loss measures cash generation (claims 5–11).

**Current liquidity does not establish a sustainable funding model.** Ending cash is not interchangeable with financing inflows. Comparing cash with next-year contractual payments omits payroll, other expenses, upfront investments, contingencies and financing restrictions. Management expects significant net cash use for the foreseeable future even with improved performance, while expecting existing liquidity, sales and revolver availability to cover at least 12 months. Longer-term funding needs operating cash and additional capital (claims 9–13).

**Commitments create timing and flexibility risks.** The aggregate spans years, not current debt; annualized quarterly revenue is neither guidance nor cash flow. Counterparty financing/facility failure can permit termination while removing expected supply; satisfying conditions can make commitments firm. Remedies vary, and some rent precedes usable capacity. Financing preferences can lock in inferior hardware (claims 12–18, 24). Incoming governors' moratorium/tax positions are intentions, with differing legal effects from existing directives or local measures; [power and permitting](/research/macro/power-and-datacenters) tracks implementation. No qualifying Anthropic binding amendment is established by the sell-off reports.

**Supplier finance connects conflicts and cash risks.** Partners are also customers/competitors, affecting pricing, capacity and distribution. Google may defer funding; Broadcom may terminate facility access while rent remains. TeraWulf financing is anticipated, not established as funded. Separate entities, collateral, guarantees and remedies do not establish a complete loss waterfall. Cross-defaults can transmit commercial failure into financing/infrastructure disruption (claims 11, 17–24). [Market credit marks](/research/macro/debt-markets) alone establish no credit event.

**Governance and legal exposure affect returns.** Class A votes confer limited influence under founder/Trust control; discretionary public-benefit contributions and donation matching add dilution/expense. Related-person disclosures identify potential conflicts, not proven improper pricing; the issuer's arm's-length assessment is not independently established. Legal restrictions can remove revenue or require retraining while compute payments continue (claims 27–30).

**The IPO is still unpriced.** Weaker market expectations are not issuer postponement or a completed down round. F5 needs a comparable fully diluted valuation, not preferred-share prices. Management expects growth to slow; much usage revenue lacks long-term contracts. Returns depend on growth surviving pricing, legal and financing pressure, future cash needs, dilution and entry valuation. The Opus cost/task improvement is one release, not a recurring forecast (claims 3, 26).

## Open questions

- No complete product/channel matrix or named partner customer-revenue/historical-payment breakdown: how much demand depends on investor/supplier relationships? Gross marketplace revenue alone proves no circularity.
- Run-rate is defined but blank in the filing; the July reported figure remains unverified (claim 4). How much will enforcement and concentrated-customer spending affect growth? RPO does not measure all future demand.
- How do unstarted leases overlap the vehicle and TeraWulf contracts? Vehicle documents must establish tranche pricing and remedies.
- What binding changes follow permitting restrictions, and which payments precede usable capacity?
- F5 needs IPO price, share count and comparable dilution assumptions against the $965B private mark. Actual IPO-date stock charge remains unspecified; Q4 will show its effect alongside short-term compute (claim 26).
