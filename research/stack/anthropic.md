## Summary

- Anthropic sells Claude through its API, its apps and the three big clouds. Its S-1 (26 Oct) and S-1/A (2 Nov) are the first filed frontier-lab accounts: annual figures audited, 2026 interim unaudited (claims 1–2).
- Revenue: $4.6B in 2025, $33.2B in the first nine months of 2026, and $17.0B in Q3 alone. About 79% is usage-based. Cloud-marketplace sales (44%) are booked gross, and the cloud providers' fees are counted as sales expense (claims 1–4).
- It does not make money yet on GAAP. Operating income was $974M in Q2, then a $567M loss in Q3. Adjusted operating income was $2.5B (15% margin) because it leaves out stock pay and donation charges. The nine-month net loss was $104B, almost all from revaluing convertible notes and partner financing contracts, which costs no cash (claims 8–10).
- Cash is now positive: $6.6B of operating cash flow in nine months, and $131.8B of cash and short-term investments on 30 September. Nearly all of it came from $110.7B of preferred stock sold in 2026, not from operations (claims 11–12).
- The overextension is in commitments: $895.4B, nearly all compute. $69.8B falls due in the next 12 months, above Q3 revenue × 4 ($67.8B), and $447.5B before the end of 2030. About $213.8B can be terminated or is conditional. A further $150–175B of variable obligations are not in the total (claims 14–20).
- Suppliers are also funders and guarantors. Amazon, Google and Broadcom provide up to $77B of conditional financing, paid in shares or convertible notes. Broadcom backs the $63B lease vehicle with up to ~$43B, and Google backs TeraWulf's debt (claims 21–26).
- Biggest uncertainty: whether revenue outgrows compute committed 12–18 months ahead that cannot be cancelled; the company expects margins to shrink as capacity arrives (claims 7, 15).

## Analysis

**Demand is real and verified.** Revenue grew from $386M in 2024 to $17.0B in Q3 2026. The customer base is broad: no customer above 10%, and more than 1,900 customers above $1M (claims 1–2, 27). Two qualifications come from the filing itself. About 5–7% of revenue came from customers who were breaking the usage policy, and that share is meant to shrink. A ~$600M price cut in one quarter shows revenue per task falling quickly (claim 5). Gross booking of cloud-marketplace sales makes revenue not directly comparable with net-reporting peers (claim 4).

**Profitability depends on the measure.** The 85.5% gross margin counts only serving compute. All compute is 57% of revenue, and total operating expense was above revenue in Q3 (claims 7–8). The positive adjusted operating income leaves out $5.3B of stock and donation charges in nine months. Those are real costs to shareholders even though no cash leaves (claim 9). The $104B net loss mostly reflects the rising valuation pushing up the value of convertible notes. It costs no cash, but it shows how much of the equity value early note-holders own (claim 10). Q3 shows the pattern the company warns about: $5.0B of expensive short-term compute turned a Q2 operating profit into a loss. Management expects more short-term compute, and expects margins to shrink as committed capacity arrives ahead of revenue (claims 7, 9).

**Cash is ample for now, and it came from equity.** $131.8B of cash and investments against $69.8B due in the next twelve months covers next year's payments almost twice over, before counting any revenue. Operating cash flow is positive (claims 11–12, 15). But $110.7B of the 2026 inflow was preferred stock. Operating cash flow was $6.6B over nine months, against a ~$45B a year payment schedule in 2028–2030 from the fixed table alone. Leases that have not started add more (claims 14–15).

**The commitments are the overextension.** $895.4B is about 25 times trailing revenue. Next-year payments ($69.8B) already exceed Q3 revenue × 4. About three quarters of the total is not cancellable; the hyperscaler contracts must be paid "regardless of actual usage" (claims 14–17). Most of the flexibility sits with weaker counterparties. SpaceXAI can be cancelled after early 2027. Lambda and Nscale depend on those firms raising money (claim 18). In a downturn, that flexibility could vanish just when the counterparties' own financing dries up. A further $150–175B of variable obligations sits outside the total (claim 16).

**Suppliers fund the customer that buys from them.** Google, Amazon and AMD fund Anthropic in shares, and those draws are tied to Anthropic hitting compute milestones on their own hardware (claims 23–26). Broadcom is supplier, landlord, possible lender at 9–13% interest paid in kind, and the guarantor of up to ~$43B for the $63B vehicle's lenders (claims 19–21). Google also guarantees debt for TeraWulf's Anthropic data centre (claim 22). Losses would be layered. Anthropic equity takes the first loss, and early note-holders now own a large claim on it. Then come second-lien funds such as Blackstone. Then Broadcom's backstop (72.5% of lease amounts still owed, minus rack value). The senior lenders sit last. Cross-default clauses connect lease, revolver and note defaults (claim 13). A missed payment to one supplier could therefore spread to all of them.

**Governance limits outside shareholders.** Founders' 50.1% vote and Trust board control leave Class A holders no say on capital decisions; the public-benefit allocation and donation matching are continuing dilution and cost (claims 29–30).

**For the investment to pay off**, revenue must keep compounding while price per task falls ~40% a model generation (claim 31). It must also pass the scheduled step-ups in fixed payments (2027–2030) without depending on short-term compute or supplier equity. The company itself says growth will decline and much usage revenue is not under long-term contract (claim 27).

## Open questions

- The filing gives no gross figure for consumption or subscriptions by channel, and no revenue from Amazon, Google or Microsoft as customers. How much of the revenue is circular is still unknown.
- Run-rate revenue is defined but blank. The July and October leak figures cannot be checked (claim 6).
- The overlap of the $451.2B unstarted leases with the $63B vehicle and TeraWulf leases is not shown (claims 14, 19, 21).
- The $63B tranche split and pricing need the vehicle's documents or Broadcom's 10-K.
- The IPO price set against the $965B Series H mark decides F5 (claim 28).
- Q4 2026 will show whether short-term compute and the IPO stock charge deepen the GAAP operating loss.
