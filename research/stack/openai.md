# OpenAI

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 (prior version)*

## Summary

- OpenAI is a major buyer at the top of the stack. It reports 1.2B weekly users (claim 32). September annualised revenue was ~$50B on its own net basis and Q2 revenue $6.7B, both reported, unverified (claims 1, 3).
- Public evidence does not establish profitability. Leaked accounts show a $20.92B operating loss on $13.07B revenue; adjusted gross margin was 33%, all reported, unverified (claims 4–5). Latest reported quarterly cash burn was $3.7B (claim 6).
- It relies on equity. March's $122B committed round was priced at $852B post-money; at least $80B is confirmed funded (claims 12–13). The proposed round seeks about $20B at a desired $1.25T valuation, with the first close expected below the target: reported, unverified, not cash received (claim 15).
- Funding needs remain large. On the February plan, ~$162B of resources falls short of $167B of burn during 2028; the September plan is disputed (claims 8, 10).
- Four quantified cloud contracts total ~$710.4B nominal, ~14.2× September run-rate, with different maturities beyond 2030. An additional AWS agreement has no disclosed value; chip and Stargate plans overlap the total (claims 23, 25, 31).
- Suppliers finance part of the build. [Microsoft's customer credit](/research/stack/microsoft), [Amazon's funded investment](/research/stack/amazon) and [Nvidia's lease support](/research/stack/nvidia) transmit OpenAI exposure across the stack. The biggest uncertainty is when payments fall due against collections and unrestricted cash; no consolidated schedule establishes that bridge.

## Analysis

**Profitability.** Run-rates are not annual sales. H1 revenue was $12.4B; the ≥$70B year-end target is an exit rate (claims 2–3). No public figure shows profit after training, research, free-tier inference and corporate costs. Paid-user compute margin is narrow; adjusted gross margin still precedes research spend. The leaked accounts imply a different cost-of-revenue margin, with no bridge reconciling the definitions (claims 4–5). On the reported training plan, the revenue needed to cover training alone depends heavily on assumed margins (claim 26). Users, seats and throughput show breadth, not retention or profit (claims 32–33). [Anthropic's filed financials](/research/stack/anthropic) improve visibility into a competitor but do not reconcile OpenAI's accounting basis (claim 35).

**Financing.** The company runs on equity. March liquidity includes some round receipts and cannot simply be added to committed capital (claims 11–13). The February funding illustration still needs additional resources during 2028 (claim 10). The reported round now seeks about $20B, and even that is not the expected first-close amount. Its desired $1.25T valuation does not establish a priced transaction or comparable fully diluted terms (claim 15). Smaller investor allocations indicate financing friction; they establish neither a closed down round nor imminent insolvency. Solvency through the burn period depends on further capital or a better cash path.

**Dependence on the stack.** OpenAI buys delivered services; suppliers buy chips, lease buildings and finance construction. The obligations differ. The Azure purchase commitment is contracted; Oracle's dollar amount is reported; CoreWeave's ceiling is delivery-conditioned (claims 18, 22, 24). Amazon's latest release adds an unquantified agreement, which cannot increase the numerical total without terms (claims 23, 25). [AMD and Broadcom's deployment roadmaps and financing structures](/research/stack/amd-broadcom-custom-silicon) are distinct from cash paid by OpenAI. Stargate, Oracle capacity and hardware overlap, so their headlines cannot be summed into one liability (claims 25, 31).

Supplier financing is concrete. Amazon invests while selling AWS. Microsoft's filing shows substantial customer revenue and receivables, but does not separate consumption from revenue-share payments or disclose receivable ageing (claim 20). Its [capacity-delivery delays](/research/stack/microsoft) do not establish an OpenAI cancellation. Nvidia's conditional support helps finance leased facilities (claim 27). OpenAI itself lends to Cerebras to create supply (claim 30). These arrangements finance real services; the supplier-financed share and ultimate collection risk remain uncertain.

**Where it is overextended.** Contracted spending extends far beyond current revenue. Shareholders absorb dilution first if growth disappoints, while suppliers face collection risk and financed capacity without an equally strong replacement customer. [SoftBank's financing exposure](/research/macro/circular-financing) and supplier guarantees connect lab equity risk to debt. The CFO warning and management rebuttal remain conflicting evidence about affordability (claim 36). The reduced fundraising target increases the importance of actual cash receipts, but the reviewed sources disclose no qualifying binding-commitment amendment.

**What has to be true.** Revenue must keep growing while margins rise toward forecast levels. Capital must arrive until cash flow turns positive, which the February plan projects for 2030 (claim 7). Distribution across clouds can broaden paid demand (claim 38); public evidence does not yet show that it covers the associated research and compute obligations.

## Open questions

- **Funding terms.** What closes, when is cash received, and is the quoted valuation pre- or post-money on comparable fully diluted terms? The latest report does not settle these points (claim 15).
- **Run-rate and margins.** A gross-to-net revenue bridge and reconciliation of cost-of-revenue versus adjusted margins would make comparisons meaningful (claims 1, 4–5).
- **Liquidity.** Opening cash and cash-plus-securities reports differ; current unrestricted liquidity is unknown (claim 11).
- **The September plan.** Its cash-burn forecast is disputed and its date unverified (claim 8).
- **Commitments and collections.** Minimum payments, termination penalties, overlaps and supplier-financed share are undisclosed. Receivable ageing or signed payment extensions would test strain; reported equity funding and continuing new supplier agreements are counterevidence to an imminent inability to pay (claims 13, 20, 23, 25).
