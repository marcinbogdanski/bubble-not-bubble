# OpenAI

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5*

## Summary

- OpenAI is the largest single buyer at the top of the stack. It reported 1.2B weekly users on 2026-09-29 (claim 32). September annualised revenue was ~$50B on its own net basis, and Q2 revenue $6.7B, both reported, unverified (claims 1, 3).
- Public evidence does not establish profitability. Leaked 2025 accounts show a $20.92B operating loss on $13.07B revenue, reported, unverified (claim 4). The reported adjusted gross margin was 33% (claim 5). Q1 2026 cash burn was $3.7B (claim 6).
- It is financed by equity. In March it announced $122B committed at $852B post-money; Amazon and SoftBank confirm at least $80B funded by 2026-10-01 (claims 12–13). A further round of up to $30B at $1.25T pre-money is reported, not closed (claim 15).
- Its cash-flow forecasts have worsened. Cumulative 2026–30 cash flow moved from +$14B to −$69B to −$179B across three plans, with a disputed fourth at −$278B (claim 9). On the February plan, ~$162B of resources falls short of $167B of burn during 2028 (claim 10).
- It is overextended on commitments. Four cloud contracts total ~$710.4B nominal, ~14.2× the September run-rate, over periods beyond 2030 (claim 25). Chip and Stargate plans overlap them; Altman's ~$1.4T aggregate has no public schedule (claim 31).
- Suppliers carry much of the exposure. Microsoft booked $24.1B of FY2026 revenue from OpenAI, ~114% of OpenAI's reconstructed $21.2B for the same months (claims 20–21). Nvidia guarantees leases up to $105B; AMD granted warrants; Amazon invested $50B (claims 13, 27–28).
- The biggest uncertainty is timing: when contracted purchases fall due, against cash collections and unrestricted cash. Neither is disclosed (claims 11, 18).

## Analysis

**Profitability.** Run-rates are not annual sales. H1 2026 revenue was $12.4B, so the ≥$70B year-end target is an exit rate, not a 2026 sales base (claims 2, 3, 39). No public figure shows profit after training, research, free-tier inference and corporate costs. That absence does not prove every segment loses money. Paid-user compute margin of ~70% is encouraging but narrow. The 33% adjusted gross margin is broader and still precedes research spend. The leaked accounts imply 42.62% on cost of revenue; no bridge reconciles the two (claims 4, 5). On a reported $32B training plan, a 33% margin needs ~$97B of revenue before other overhead; at the forecast 52–67% margins, $61.54B–$47.76B (claim 26). Users, seats and throughput show breadth, not retention or profit (claims 32, 33). Lower API prices and the $500 Pro tier may move revenue per customer either way; realised ARPU is undisclosed (claim 34).

**Financing.** The company runs on equity. $122B was committed in March and at least $80B of it is confirmed funded (claims 12, 13). March liquidity of $73B already includes some receipts, so it cannot be added to the round (claim 11). On the February plan, ~$162B of resources falls short of $167B of burn during 2028. The peak need through 2029 leaves a ~$56B gap; the disputed September plan implies ~$116B (claim 10). Each successive plan has been worse than the last (claim 9). The reported $30B round at $1.25T pre-money would sit above March's $852B post-money, but it is a proposal (claim 15). March's price is ~17.0× the September run-rate (claim 39). Imminent insolvency is not supported. Solvency through 2028 depends on further capital or a better cash path.

**Dependence on the stack.** OpenAI buys delivered services; suppliers buy chips, lease buildings and finance construction. The obligations differ. Microsoft's $250B is a contracted Azure purchase (claim 18). Oracle's $300B is reported, not filed (claim 22). CoreWeave's ceiling is delivery-conditioned (claim 24). AWS has grown well beyond the first headline (claim 23). Chip deals are roadmaps: AMD's warrant aligns purchases with dilution, not cash (claim 28); Broadcom's ramp does not prove the programme was cut (claim 29); Nvidia's $100B LOI shrank to $30B of equity (claim 16). Stargate, Oracle capacity and Nvidia hardware overlap, so headlines cannot be summed into one liability (claims 25, 31).

Supplier financing is concrete. Amazon invests while selling AWS. Microsoft is investor, supplier and revenue-share recipient; its $24.1B of OpenAI revenue roughly equals OpenAI's own ~$21.2B for the same months, and its $6B receivable is ~91 days of that (claims 20, 21). Nvidia's guarantee lets SB Energy finance facilities hosting Nvidia systems; it phases in as leases commence and recoveries come first (claim 27). OpenAI in turn lends to Cerebras to create supply (claim 30). This circulation is real financing, not evidence that revenue is fictitious. The supplier-financed share is undisclosed.

**Where it is overextended.** Four cloud contracts total ~14.2× current run-rate (claim 25). If OpenAI falls short, shareholders absorb dilution first. Oracle and CoreWeave then risk receivables and financed capacity; Nvidia its triggered guarantees; SoftBank its equity, a $10B loan and its landlord role (claims 14, 27). Microsoft is cushioned by diversification (claim 19). The CFO warning and its rebuttal show the position is contested inside the company (claim 36). Safety controls may delay revenue from research spend (claim 37).

**What has to be true.** Revenue must reach roughly the plan's $350B by 2030, which needs 71.0–76.6% annual growth (claim 39). Margins must rise toward the forecast range. Funding must keep arriving at rising valuations until cash flow turns positive, which the February plan put in 2030 (claim 7).

## Open questions

- **Run-rate basis.** Reuters attributes the $68–70B figure to an Anthropic-comparable gross basis; whether that is a basis difference or a correction is unsettled. An audited gross-to-net bridge would settle it (claim 1).
- **Liquidity.** $40B of end-2025 cash and securities versus ~$25B of cash; securities may explain it. A balance sheet would settle it (claim 11).
- **Margins.** 42.62% on cost of revenue versus 33% adjusted; definitions differ and no bridge exists (claims 4, 5).
- **The September plan.** Its −$278B is disputed by a finance employee and its date is unverified (claim 8).
- **Commitments.** Minimum payments, termination penalties, overlaps and the supplier-financed share are undisclosed. Signed amendments or payment extensions linked to inability to pay would be the strongest evidence of strain (claims 22, 25).
