# OpenAI

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Codex (GPT-6) · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- OpenAI sells ChatGPT, enterprise products and APIs while anchoring compute construction. Demand is substantial; comfortable profitability is unsupported.
- September run-rate reportedly reached ~$50bn, versus $68–70bn including partner-gross revenue. Growth remained strong. Annualisations are unaudited (claims 1–4).
- Reported inference margins do not finance frontier research and expansion; later burn forecasts are larger and contested (claims 5–9).
- The $122bn round at $852bn post-money is company-confirmed. Commitments, receipts, secondary sales and supplier incentives differ (claims 10–13, 28, 36).
- Overlapping cloud contracts, chip roadmaps, guarantees and Stargate ambitions cannot simply be summed. Nvidia's $105bn guarantee cap is filing-confirmed; its $100bn LOI is not available cash (claims 20–31).
- The nonprofit retains control of the PBC. Key-person, execution and financing risks intersect: supplier credit can sustain construction but concentrates losses if demand disappoints.
- Biggest uncertainty: the dated, enforceable cash-payment schedule, net of delivery conditions and overlapping projects, relative to cash generation and funded capital.

## Analysis

Run-rate snapshots are not annual sales. The CFO retrospectively rounds the December figure; March monthly sales also differ from later quarterly revenue. September's gross/net correction does not establish lost customers: CNBC reports continued growth (claims 1–3).

Subscriber, seat, throughput and advertising figures establish breadth, not retention or profit. Workplace seats include more than Enterprise-plan seats; throughput is not API share. Menlo's modelled estimate comes from an Anthropic investor. Consumer leadership can coexist with enterprise competition (claims 32–35).

No public evidence establishes a comfortably profitable OpenAI segment after allocating training, research, free-tier inference and corporate costs. Paid-user compute margin is encouraging, but excludes costs outside that customer group. The reported adjusted gross margin is broader and still precedes major research expense. Neither is operating margin. Attributable net loss also includes financing/restructuring accounting: it must not be called cash burn. The reported full-year operating loss and cash outflow demonstrate the distinction (claims 5–9).

Secondary proceeds went to sellers. The SoftBank-led round arrived in stages and enlarged; the later announcement explicitly says committed capital. Amazon and SoftBank independently confirm disbursements, which belong within the rounds, not on top. Current unrestricted cash remains unreconciled (claims 10–13, 28, 36).

OpenAI pays cloud providers for delivered services; those providers buy chips, lease buildings and finance construction. Oracle's reported dollar purchase obligation is stronger evidence than a capacity aspiration, but its full contract is unavailable. Microsoft's announcement explicitly says contracted Azure purchases. CoreWeave's initial order depends on delivery and availability; its aggregate ceiling is not cash presently due. AWS has expanded materially beyond the original headline, and its filing identifies chip-performance obligations. Cerebras distinguishes committed capacity from options and receives working capital from OpenAI. These are economically different obligations (claims 16, 20, 26–29).

Chip roadmaps require additional care. AMD's signed purchase agreement and warrant align purchases with dilution incentives; the warrant is not cash financing. Broadcom announced a deployment term sheet alongside supply agreements, not a disclosed fixed-dollar cloud bill. Its later ramp does not prove that the whole programme was cut or cancelled: starting deployment and reaching scale are different milestones. The Nvidia LOI likewise cannot fund a solvency calculation at face value after Huang's revision (claims 21, 23–25).

Stargate encompasses overlapping sites, hardware and cloud partnerships. Counting its umbrella ambition, Oracle capacity and Nvidia hardware again counts parts of the same build repeatedly. Altman's aggregate is a management description without an auditable contract inventory (claims 30–31). An enforceable liability above a trillion dollars cannot be certified.

Supplier financing is nevertheless concrete. Amazon contributes equity while selling AWS services; Microsoft is investor, supplier and revenue-share recipient. CoreWeave gives customer equity; AMD offers a purchase-linked warrant. Nvidia supplies credit support that enables a landlord to finance facilities hosting Nvidia systems. Conversely, OpenAI lends to Cerebras to create supply. This circulation is real financing, not evidence that revenue is fictitious. The supplier-financed fraction is undisclosed.

The Nvidia guarantee deserves particular precision. It covers specified land, power and shell obligations, phases in when qualifying leases commence, declines with lease payments and can terminate on specified events. It is neither an immediate disbursement nor all tenant liabilities. OpenAI says it starts paying for completed leased capacity; the landlord owns the facility. Nvidia's indemnification claim against OpenAI is weak protection in the very insolvency scenario that triggers support. The historical-precedents note's cap is verified, but the contingent structure matters (claim 22; [project statement][s53]).

Microsoft's RPO measures future supplier sales, not OpenAI customer revenue. The Azure commitment is already in that backlog. The Microsoft note's June concentration estimate assumes an unchanged OpenAI balance: growth outside frontier labs does not establish no consumption or renewal. Its related-party sales include revenue share and cover a different period from OpenAI's annual accounts; they are not a pure inference bill. Investment gains include recapitalisation effects, not just operations (claims 16–19).

Oracle's average annual bill exceeds current annualised sales, although payments ramp later. Reported blended margins cannot finance the training plan before overhead. Research compute already runs on supplier infrastructure: adding training to all cloud purchases would double-count. Claim 40 optimistically assumes full-year sales equal the year-end run-rate target. Rapid growth could reach the long-term ambition; slower growth requires more financing. Better margins and staggered deployment are essential (claims 39–40).

If OpenAI falls short, its shareholders absorb impairment or dilution. Oracle and CoreWeave risk receivables and financed capacity; lenders and landlords bear losses according to collateral and guarantees. SoftBank must service its own debt. Microsoft risks investment value, receivables and capacity, cushioned by diversification. Nvidia risks equity, orders and triggered guarantees; AMD risks orders and incentives. Broadcom risks custom-chip demand, inventory and any financing support actually signed. Oracle's customer hardware funding and replacement demand are meaningful offsets (claim 37).

The Foundation retains control through the PBC structure and overlapping boards. Conventional equity financing does not eliminate mission/commercial conflicts. The board crisis demonstrates dependence on Altman's relationships. OpenAI's review attributed the crisis to lost trust and endorsed his return, without attributing it to financial misconduct (claims 14–15; [review][s21]).

Altman's funding case combines growth, new products, compute resale and further capital. Friar described guarantees lowering financing costs, then clarified her language; Altman rejected taxpayer support for private datacentres while acknowledging chip-factory guarantee discussions. Neither remarks nor Stargate's launch establish an effective taxpayer guarantee (claim 31; [Reuters][s45], [clarification][s54]).

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Demand/accounting:** September's basis correction coexists with reported growth. Missing: audited partner bridge, annual sales and collections.
- **Burn versus liquidity:** the later deficit forecast is larger, but a finance employee publicly disputed the figures. The Information's account also describes substantial financing receipts. The underlying deck, unrestricted cash and a cash-flow bridge remain unavailable; imminent insolvency is unsupported (claims 8, 13, 28, 36).
- **Unit economics:** paid-user margins contradict universal incremental losses; blended margins and research contradict company-wide profitability. Comparable cost allocations would resolve this.
- **Deferral versus cancellation:** Abilene's additional expansion reportedly ended while the broader Oracle agreement remained on track. Broadcom's ramp and Nvidia's revised equity plan are also insufficient to establish wholesale demand destruction. Signed amendments and payment changes would be stronger evidence (claims 21, 25, 38).
- **Unverified:** consolidated minimum purchase payments, termination penalties, contract overlaps, supplier-financed percentage, current API share, latest comparable global seat/user totals, realised ad revenue, IPO price/date and any binding government backstop. Reported financing talks are not committed liquidity.

The first persuasive warning would be payment extensions, credit reserves or cancellations explicitly linked to inability to pay, especially alongside failed funding. A headline correction or relocation is insufficient; the fetched sources establish no OpenAI-wide default.
