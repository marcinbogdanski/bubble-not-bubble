# Memory and HBM: SK hynix, Samsung Memory and Micron

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Author: Codex / GPT-6 · Status: reviewed · Reviewer: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check, applied 2026-10-17*

## Summary

- Memory is comfortably profitable: Micron's GAAP gross/operating margins were 86.8%/80.7% in FYQ4 ended September 3, 2026; SK hynix reported a 76% operating margin in calendar Q2 2026. Strong current pricing supports these earnings; lasting through-cycle returns remain unproved, although Micron expects protected SCA floor margins above previous cycle peaks. [M4][H2][MR]
- As of October 17, 2026, the latest located releases are Micron FYQ4 results (September 30), Samsung consolidated Q3 guidance (October 8), and SK hynix Q2 results (July 29). Samsung's guidance provides no Memory segment result. [M4][S3][HI]
- On September 30, 2026, Micron reported 26 take-or-pay SCAs covering over 35% of estimated revenue across the period through 2030; three-quarters of that covered revenue has a pricing framework. Inference: these transfer some demand risk to customers, while market-linked pricing, execution and enforceability remain exposures. [MD][MK]
- Counterpoint's estimated HBM revenue shares changed from 58%/21%/21% for SK hynix/Samsung/Micron in Q1 2026 to 50%/33%/18% in Q2 2026 (grade C; rounded Q2 total exceeds 100%). Revenue share differs from shipment share and producer HBM revenue mix; these estimates alone do not establish durable technological leadership. [CP]
- Nvidia disclosed $279bn of multi-year supply/capacity commitments, up $160bn, on August 26, 2026; Amazon raised 2026 cash capex from about $200bn to $220bn on July 30, citing memory cost; ASML forecast 75% growth in 2026 memory net system sales on October 14. These measure linked procurement and investment responses, not independent end-user returns. [NC][AC][AR]
- Micron's September 30, 2026 plan targets ID1 initial wafer output in mid-2027 and ID2 in late-2028; SK hynix's August 7 plan targets M17 and Y2 cleanrooms for December 2028 and June 2029. These staggered milestones do not determine qualified capacity, yields or the date of a price reversal. [MD][HN]
- Micron's FY2026 OCF of $89.675bn exceeded gross PP&E spending of $30.712bn; SK hynix's July 2026 ADS offering adds approximately $26.2bn of estimated net proceeds. These support expansion funding, but neither establishes future project returns or fair equity valuation. Return impairment after price normalization remains a scenario, not a measured conclusion. [M4][HF4]

## Analysis

Data: [Micron](/data/memory-micron-quarterly.csv), [SK hynix](/data/memory-skhynix-quarterly.csv), [Samsung](/data/memory-samsung-quarterly.csv), [HBM revenue shares](/data/memory-hbm-market-share-quarterly.csv). Blanks mean unavailable or uncollected; Samsung preliminary guidance is explicitly separated from actuals.

**What is earning the money.** HBM serves accelerators, conventional DRAM supplies system memory and NAND persistent storage. Shared AI demand meets different manufacturing constraints. Micron's margins establish current profitability; Cloud Memory includes other products. Samsung reports Memory sales but broader DS profits. Neither permits a clean comparison of HBM-only margins. [M4][S2]

Use SK hynix's operating profit rather than net profit distorted by nonoperating gains. Its estimated HBM mix can fall while HBM shipments grow if conventional-memory prices rise faster; this is a mathematical possibility, not evidence that those shipment dynamics occurred. The absolute HBM estimate remains tentative. [SP][HG]

**HBM changes the cycle's transmission.** Customization and qualification impede substitution. SK hynix links supply to Nvidia's roadmap; Samsung identifies HBM4 sales for Vera Rubin. Micron previously confirmed price/volume agreements for its entire calendar-2026 HBM supply; Samsung said its production-ready HBM4 capacity was sold out. These statements concern planned capacity, not unlimited future supply. Micron's latest update contracts most next-year bits; Samsung confirms next-year agreements without specifying complete coverage. [HP][S1][MT1][ST1][MD][ST]

Micron files binding volumes, take-or-pay terms and deposit retention rights. Deposits secure capacity and enter financing cash flow, outside operating FCF. [MK] Its September 30 update reports $32bn of customer financial commitments, mostly deposits; this is not all cash already received. Management expects SCA floor margins above prior cycle peaks. The 10-K also warns that compulsory purchases can build customer inventories and that breaches alongside weaker demand can expose excess industry supply. [MD][MR][MK] Our inference: contracts improve liquidity and protect selected margins, but can delay the inventory cycle; enforcement, counterparty credit and recovery remain material.

Samsung's July 30 Q&A describes five finalized major data-center accounts and five further negotiations, five-year rolling terms and required advances. Its envisaged 60–70% coverage of planned DRAM/NAND capacity depends on pending deals closing; it is neither achieved coverage nor a disclosed price floor. This broadens the evidence for customer risk-sharing beyond Micron, without establishing uniform protection across producers. [ST]

**Pricing power cuts across products.** TrendForce's contract forecast still points upward, while its wafer-economics work says conventional server memory became more attractive than annually priced HBM. Micron likewise says next-year HBM price increases narrow its margin gap with conventional DRAM. HBM is therefore not automatically the industry's highest-margin product at every moment. Annual pricing can initially restrain upside and later defer downside. [TP][TW][MR]

Nvidia's final HBM4 prices and terms are undisclosed. TrendForce's blended ASP forecast includes generation and stack mix, rather than a uniform like-for-like price increase. Its reported consideration of less memory per device shows buyers responding to cost. [TH]

Micron's September 30 outlook allows for double-digit PC/mobile unit declines despite industry revenue growth and lowers expected server memory-content growth. Samsung's Q2 DX division lost KRW0.8tn, with component costs identified as a pressure. Supplier pricing power can therefore coexist with weaker downstream economics; this does not establish an AI spending collapse. [MD][S2]

SK hynix's October 16 OCP report describes HBF, rack-level memory pooling and KV-cache reuse through demonstrations and internal simulations. These identify ways to redistribute workload demand across HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND, without quantifying commercial displacement. A fixed HBM-per-accelerator assumption therefore needs mix and efficiency sensitivities; the demonstrations do not establish falling HBM demand. [OCP]

**Reconciling the adjacent notes.** Nvidia's commitment increase buys multi-year supply assurance; it should not be booked as memory manufacturers' current revenue or read as wholly HBM. Its margin guidance places some scarcity cost at the accelerator vendor before price increases flow through. Amazon places another portion in buyers' capital budgets. ASML's memory growth and SEMI's DRAM equipment forecast describe the supplier response, including process transitions rather than solely additional wafer starts. [NC][NT][AC][AR][SE]

Inference: nominal capex can rise without more physical capacity; forward purchases can obscure final demand elasticity. Linked commitments do not independently verify end-user returns.

**When supply actually arrives.** M15X and Yongin's first phase are the near-term SK hynix additions. Its newly approved Yongin Y2 and Cheongju M17 have later cleanroom dates. Micron's schedule separates Idaho initial wafer output from Taiwan Tongluo meaningful product shipments, Singapore packaging, Japan output supporting node transitions, later Singapore NAND output, and a still later New York start. First wafers require ramping; packaging adds no DRAM wafer capacity. Samsung confirms Pyeongtaek investment but the reviewed disclosures do not establish a precise fab-by-fab qualified-output calendar. [H2][HN][MD][ST]

ASML said on October 14 that planned equipment-capacity growth of 30% in 2027 and a further 30% aspiration in 2028 would still fall short of customer demand. Installed-base upgrades also raise customer output. Tool availability and upgrades must therefore accompany fab dates in a supply model; construction dates alone can overstate or understate supply growth. [AV]

Overlapping capacity ramps, yield improvements and weaker demand could reverse prices without every fab opening together. Investment returns require paid demand for each vintage and disciplined equipment installation. Construction schedules alone establish neither a glut nor its absence. [HN][MD]

**Financing and loss bearers.** Micron's current operating cash generation covers gross physical investment before incentives; customer security and public subsidies add protection. SK hynix has substantial net cash and also accessed public equity through the ADS offering. Samsung's consolidated liquidity and OCF comfortably exceed disclosed cash PP&E spending, although neither is attributable entirely to Memory. Management capex and cash purchases have different recognition bases and must remain separate series. [M4][H2][CITI][S2][ST]

Samsung Memory draws on Samsung Electronics' consolidated balance sheet. SK hynix is a listed issuer within SK, with investment holding company SK Square a shareholder. Parent investment income is not additional subsidiary cash; affiliation establishes no blanket creditor guarantee. [SQ] Our loss-allocation inference: equity absorbs impaired returns first, contracted customers may lose deposits, and lenders retain residual default risk.

Micron's 129-day DIO, up nine days in FYQ4, has a disclosed explanation: management cites node-end-of-life build-ahead and capitalized incentive compensation and expects DIO to decline. This explains management's view of the increase, rather than independently proving its completeness; subsequent declines are the test. [MR]

**History remains relevant.** Micron's reversal from high gross profitability to negative gross margin, and SK hynix's annual operating loss, show that technical leadership does not eliminate fixed-cost exposure. Historical server contract-price falls were fast enough to meet a P2-sized decline within a quarter for some products. LTAs may protect selected revenue while uncontracted conventional memory sets the marginal market price. HBM contracts can dampen or delay the cycle and transfer losses to customers; their floors mean the historical trough should not be assumed to apply uniformly to protected revenue. They cannot by themselves make unwanted capacity economically productive. [MH][HH][T22][T23][MR][MK]

**Adopted P2 definition.** Use the fixed TrendForce **DDR5 8GB SO-DIMM contract-price Session Average**, in dollars per module, at the final assessment in each calendar month. Define the baseline as the maximum of those observations during calendar 2026. Resolve yes if any later monthly observation published by the relevant register horizon is at or below the threshold in claim 39. Freeze product, currency and contract basis; do not substitute the site's more recent spot table. The adopted rule tests each monthly final assessment; an intramonth dip is outside this observation convention. [PX][FR]

This fixed-product proxy excludes server DRAM and HBM. The public contract table lags newer spot data and a member-only contract update. The unverified baseline makes the example conditional. Archive assessments; adjudicate discontinuation explicitly. The register adopted this definition in vintage 0.2a on October 17, 2026, with probabilities unchanged. [PX][FR]

This note establishes current profitability and financing capacity, but does not estimate fair equity values or a probability of capital impairment. Such a conclusion requires a dated valuation and a stress case separating protected revenue, market-priced output and committed expansion spending. A fall in this consumer-module proxy alone cannot resolve impairment of contracted HBM capacity. [XR]

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Protected versus cyclical — note versus cross-check:** The original note interpreted current margins as scarcity earnings and emphasized normalization, disputes and delivery liability. The cross-check gives greater weight to Micron's expected floor margins above prior cycle peaks and staged equipment spending as defenses against the old cycle. Both views retain enforcement risk; the 10-K's customer-inventory and breach warnings provide a concrete route to a delayed reversal. The evidence establishes management's protection claims, not uniform through-cycle outcomes. [MD][MR][MK][XR]
- **A synchronized supply glut versus continued shortages — weighting differs:** The original note emphasized a reversal from overlapping ramps, better yields and customer retrenchment. The cross-check judges near-term scarcity more strongly supported than near-term overbuild, citing staggered equipment installation, ASML bottlenecks and supplier shortage forecasts. Samsung expects tight supply through 2028 and TrendForce forecasts next-year HBM scarcity. Neither view has a qualified-capacity/yield model; shared demand optimism could still fail, while downstream affordability, customer inventory and architecture changes provide specific later-reversal mechanisms. [ST][HN][TH][AV][MD][OCP][XR]
- **Market-share disagreement and grading:** IDC and Counterpoint differ in claim 27. Neither estimate is audited company revenue; comparisons require consistent periods and denominators. The original note graded these estimates B; the cross-check grades them C under the methodology's estimate rule, adopted here. The final prospectus verifies that SK hynix quoted IDC, not IDC's measurement. [HF4][CP][XR]
- **Investment conclusion — ranking versus measurement:** The original Summary called inadequate returns after normalization the “main vulnerability.” The cross-check accepts it as a scenario but finds no valuation or impairment bridge that ranks it against equity valuation risk or measures loss probabilities. The revised Summary preserves the scenario and leaves that ranking open pending valuation and project stress cases; the review does not claim shares are expensive. [XR]
- **Backlog date trap:** claim 8 compares year-end RPO with a later call update. No reconciliation was verified. RPO excludes certain variable-price arrangements and is not all expected sales. [MK][MR]
- **Unverified scope:** current HBM revenue and total-revenue mix for Micron and Samsung; company-confirmed SK hynix HBM dollars; a universal industry sellout through 2027; Nvidia's supplier-specific prices and cancellation rights; precise Samsung fab output dates; numerical 2027 capex budgets for both Korean vendors. Original contracts, segment disclosures and qualified-output data would resolve these gaps.
