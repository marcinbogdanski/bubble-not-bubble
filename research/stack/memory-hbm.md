# Memory and HBM: SK hynix, Samsung Memory and Micron

## Summary

- HBM serves accelerators, conventional DRAM supplies system memory and NAND persistent storage. Memory is comfortably profitable: Micron's GAAP gross/operating margins are 86.8%/80.7%; SK hynix's are 83.2%/76% (claims 1, 8).
- Operating cash funds current investment. Micron's annual OCF of $89.675B exceeds gross PP&E spending of $30.712B; SK hynix also raised approximately $26.2B net through public equity (claims 2, 12). The layer has little debt (claims 3, 11, 20).
- Micron's 26 take-or-pay agreements cover over 35% of estimated revenue through 2030. Pricing frameworks and expected floor margins offer protection, but enforcement and customer inventories remain risks (claim 4). SK hynix's backlog and LTA disclosures remain unreconciled (claim 13).
- Near-term scarcity is better supported than near-term overbuild. Micron expects supply constraints through 2027–28, while capacity arrives in stages (claims 6, 7, 14). SK hynix's plan extends to 2033; CXMT's conventional DRAM revenue share reached 10% (claims 15, 23).
- Shareholders already bear losses: SK hynix Korean shares fell 56.4% from their peak; its ADS trades below its offer (claims 16, 17). These observations establish neither fair value nor project-return impairment. The biggest uncertainty is whether protected revenue and paid demand will sustain returns after prices normalise.

## Analysis

Current margins establish profitability, not durable through-cycle returns. HBM-only margins cannot be compared cleanly: Micron's Cloud Memory includes other products, Samsung reports broader DS profits, and SK hynix's HBM revenue estimate is not company-confirmed. Conventional-memory inflation can reduce HBM's revenue mix even while HBM shipments grow. Market-share estimates do not establish durable technological leadership (claims 1, 8, 19, 22).

Micron's contracts improve liquidity and protect selected margins. Its filing describes binding volumes, take-or-pay terms and deposit retention rights. Deposits enter financing cash flow, outside operating FCF. Protection still depends on enforcement, counterparty credit and recovery. Compulsory purchases can shift inventories to customers and delay a reversal; breaches alongside weaker demand can expose excess supply (claim 4). Samsung's pending coverage is conditional. SK hynix's disclosures do not establish equivalent protection (claims 13, 21).

Supplier pricing power raises downstream costs. Nvidia's commitments buy multi-year assurance; Amazon's capex revision attributes more spending to memory. Neither independently measures end-user returns. Nominal capex can rise without more physical capacity. Blended HBM price forecasts also incorporate product mix (claims 24, 27, 28).

Supply must be measured at qualified shipments. Cleanroom openings, initial wafers and meaningful output are different milestones; packaging adds no DRAM wafer capacity. Equipment constraints can delay ramps, while yield improvements and upgrades can raise output without new fabs. Staged installation and shortage forecasts support near-term scarcity. Overlapping ramps, customer retrenchment and CXMT's conventional-DRAM expansion remain routes to a later reversal. No qualified-capacity/yield model establishes its date (claims 6, 7, 14, 15, 23, 29).

Current cash generation covers physical investment. SK hynix's operating cash includes investment dividends, while Samsung's liquidity is consolidated. Public equity and subsidies add funding. Equipment installation must remain disciplined as prices change; cash available today does not establish returns on later capacity (claims 2, 5, 10, 12, 20).

Our inference is that equity absorbs impaired returns first, contracted customers may lose deposits, and lenders retain residual default risk. Observed share losses can precede project impairment. Korean and ADS peak drawdowns use different windows; neither establishes fair value. This note does not estimate fair equity values or a probability of capital impairment (claims 16, 17).

P2 fixes the product, currency and contract basis. It tests final monthly assessments against the full-year peak, not intramonth dips or spot prices. The consumer-module proxy excludes server DRAM and HBM; a fall alone cannot resolve impairment of contracted HBM capacity. Archive public refreshes and adjudicate discontinuation explicitly. The final baseline remains unverified (claim 25).

## Open questions

- SK hynix reports no long-term-contract backlog and later reports LTA customers. Contract terms and timing need reconciliation; Micron's protections cannot be assumed industry-wide.
- IDC and Counterpoint disagree on the same-period HBM share. Neither is audited producer revenue; consistent denominators are needed.
- Micron's later call RPO has no verified reconciliation to its year-end filing. Variable-price arrangements are partly excluded; RPO is not all expected sales.
- HBM-only revenue/margins, supplier-specific customer prices and cancellation rights remain incomplete. A universal industry sellout is unverified.
- Samsung's precise qualified-output calendar, Korean vendors' future capex budgets, CXMT qualification and architectural substitution remain insufficiently measured.
- No valuation or project cash-flow stress case bridges current scarcity profits to normalised returns. Contract enforcement, paid demand and equipment deployment would settle more than fab announcements alone.
