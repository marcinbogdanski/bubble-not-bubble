# Memory and HBM: SK hynix, Samsung Memory and Micron

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [memory-micron-quarterly.csv](/data/memory-micron-quarterly.csv), [memory-skhynix-quarterly.csv](/data/memory-skhynix-quarterly.csv), [memory-samsung-quarterly.csv](/data/memory-samsung-quarterly.csv), [memory-hbm-market-share-quarterly.csv](/data/memory-hbm-market-share-quarterly.csv), [forecast-observations.csv](/data/forecast-observations.csv)*

## Summary

- HBM serves accelerators, conventional DRAM supplies system memory and NAND persistent storage. Memory is comfortably profitable: Micron's GAAP gross/operating margins are 86.8%/80.7%; SK hynix's operating margin is 80%; Samsung's broader DS margin is 72%, not Memory-only (claims 1, 8, 19).
- Cash generation funds investment. Micron's annual OCF exceeds gross PP&E spending; Samsung's quarterly OCF covers cash PP&E. SK hynix has KRW98.7T net cash, but large Korean shareholder-return plans compete with investment for funds (claims 2, 11, 17, 20, 27, 28).
- Micron's 26 take-or-pay agreements cover over 35% of estimated revenue through 2030. SK hynix now discloses contracted capacity and price bands; Samsung has signed 10 major accounts. Their protections cannot be assumed equivalent, and negotiations are not contracted output (claims 4, 13, 21).
- Scarcity remains better supported than near-term overbuild. Suppliers plan staged capacity additions and expect persistent constraints. Micron's reported Taiwan construction pause is unverified; CXMT's conventional DRAM share does not establish qualified HBM supply (claims 6, 7, 14, 23, 26).
- Shareholders already bear losses: SK hynix Korean shares fell 56.4% from their peak at the recorded observation. That establishes neither fair value nor project-return impairment. The biggest uncertainty is whether protected revenue and paid demand sustain returns after prices normalise (claim 16).

## Analysis

Current margins establish profitability, not durable through-cycle returns. HBM-only margins cannot be compared cleanly: Micron's Cloud Memory includes other products, Samsung reports broader DS profits, and SK hynix's HBM revenue estimate is not company-confirmed. Conventional-memory inflation can reduce HBM's revenue mix even while HBM shipments grow. Market-share estimates do not establish durable technological leadership (claims 1, 8, 19, 22).

Micron's contracts improve liquidity and protect selected margins. Its filing describes binding volumes, take-or-pay terms and deposit retention rights. Deposits enter financing cash flow, outside operating FCF. Protection depends on enforcement, counterparty credit and recovery. Compulsory purchases can shift inventories to customers and delay a reversal; breaches alongside weaker demand can expose excess supply (claim 4).

The Korean contract evidence supports firmer demand visibility. SK hynix's agreed price bands matter, but its broader sellout language includes negotiations. Samsung reports a HBM sellout while its capacity-wide LTA target remains conditional. Neither disclosure establishes Micron-equivalent deposit or take-or-pay protection. Cancellation terms and collections matter more than the word “sold out” (claims 13, 21).

Supplier pricing power raises downstream costs; see the [Nvidia](/research/stack/nvidia) and [Amazon](/research/stack/amazon) notes for buyer commitments and spending. Neither independently measures end-user returns. Nominal capex can rise without more physical capacity. Blended HBM price forecasts also incorporate product mix (claim 24).

Supply must be measured at qualified shipments. Cleanroom openings, initial wafers and meaningful output differ; packaging adds no DRAM wafer capacity. Equipment constraints can delay ramps, while yield improvements and upgrades can raise output without new fabs. Staged installation and shortage forecasts support scarcity. Overlapping ramps, customer retrenchment and CXMT's conventional-DRAM expansion remain routes to a later reversal. No qualified-capacity/yield model establishes its date. The Taiwan report is an execution-risk lead, not evidence of a confirmed capex cancellation or a revised Micron shipment timetable (claims 6, 7, 14, 15, 23, 26, 29).

Available operating cash-flow disclosures cover physical investment, though SK hynix's cash-flow comparison remains half-year and includes investment dividends. Samsung's liquidity is consolidated, and its company capex measure differs from cash PP&E. Public equity and subsidies add funding. The Korean shareholder-return plans reduce retained funding if executed; they are neither all paid nor wholly incremental to announced programs. Equipment installation must remain disciplined as prices change; cash available today does not establish returns on later capacity (claims 2, 5, 10, 12, 17, 20, 27, 28).

Our inference is that equity absorbs impaired returns first, contracted customers may lose deposits, and lenders retain residual default risk. Observed share losses can precede project impairment. This note does not estimate fair equity values or a probability of capital impairment (claim 16).

P2 fixes the product, currency and contract basis. It tests final monthly assessments against the full-year peak, not intramonth dips or spot prices. The consumer-module proxy excludes server DRAM and HBM; a fall alone cannot resolve impairment of contracted HBM capacity. No new qualifying observation comes from these earnings; the final baseline remains unverified (claim 25).

## Open questions

- SK hynix's LTA price bands improve visibility, but deposit collection, cancellation rights and legal purchase obligations remain insufficiently disclosed. Samsung's sellout and target coverage need the same contractual tests.
- IDC and Counterpoint disagree on same-period HBM share. Neither is audited producer revenue; consistent denominators are needed.
- Micron's call RPO has no verified reconciliation to its year-end filing. Variable-price arrangements are partly excluded; RPO is not all expected sales.
- The Micron Taiwan pause needs issuer confirmation and a project-specific shipment impact. [Taylor base-die bookings](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/04/news-samsungs-2nm-taylor-fab-2-reportedly-fully-booked-before-2030-output-with-amd-leading-hbm-base-die-orders/) remain grade C, reported, unverified; base dies do not establish DRAM wafer capacity.
- CXMT HBM yields/qualification and Huawei sourcing routes remain grade C, reported, unverified ([China note](/research/macro/china)). They establish neither commercial HBM volumes nor direct Korean-vendor supply to Huawei.
- HBM-only margins, qualified-output calendars and normalised project returns remain incomplete. Customer inventories, contract enforcement and paid demand would settle more than fab announcements alone.
