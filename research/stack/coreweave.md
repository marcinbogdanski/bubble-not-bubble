# CoreWeave (CRWV)

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [coreweave-quarterly.csv](/data/coreweave-quarterly.csv)*

## Summary

- CoreWeave rents Nvidia GPU compute under committed contracts to Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Nvidia and Jane Street. Q2 2026 revenue was $2.575B (+112% y/y). 2026 guidance is $12.4–13.2B. June backlog was $104.2B; the top three customers supplied 72% of Q2 revenue (claims 1, 5, 7, 9).
- Growth runs on external finance. June debt principal of $35.551B becomes an illustrative $42.151B with later borrowings, before repayments. 2026 capex guidance is $35–39B, and $35.5B of leases have not yet commenced (claims 6, 15, 16, 22).
- Not GAAP-profitable: Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $1.510B (59% margin) becomes a $626M net loss after D&A and net interest of $640M (24.9% of revenue). Growth front-loads costs but does not make profit impossible (claims 2–4).
- Cash does not cover debt service: H1 operating cash flow of $3.663B against $14.117B of equipment purchases and $5.219B of principal repaid. The 30 June schedule lists $4.4B more for the rest of 2026 and $6.2B for 2027. EBITDA covers net interest 2.36×, not principal (claims 4, 19, 20).
- Markets price distress: five-year CDS was 1,065.84bp on 21 October after a 1,194.5bp intraday record on 15 October. The 2031 notes yielded 15.97% on 20 October. Shares closed at $61.39 on 16 October (week −25.2%). Reports show sector financing delays, not a closed CoreWeave channel or a default (claims 32–34, 39).
- Equity is first loss; then losses run by borrower. Most DDTLs carry parent guarantees; $20.817B of unsecured notes and converts rank behind secured claims. Nvidia's $6.3B capacity purchase is conditional, not a debt guarantee (claims 13, 18, 23).
- The biggest uncertainty is renewals: DDTL 5.5 lends five years against three-year contracts. Management's claims of initial-term recovery and pricier renewals are unaudited, and its disclosure controls were not effective at 30 June (claims 25, 28, 31).

## Analysis

**Profitability.** EBITDA is real; profit is not. In Q2, D&A and net interest consumed nearly all of the $1.510B of adjusted EBITDA (claim 2). Net interest takes about a quarter of revenue, and Q3 guidance implies 25.5% (claim 3). The six-year depreciation life (claim 26) is an estimate that quarterly D&A cannot validate. Growth front-loads depreciation and interest before contracts ramp. Current losses prove the model is not profitable at today's financing cost, not that it cannot be.

**Cash and debt service.** H1 operating cash fell $10.454B short of equipment purchases, before $5.219B of principal repayments (claim 20). Debt and equity issuance closed the gap. Prepayments helped operating cash: deferred revenue rose $1.507B (claim 30). But prepayments are future delivery obligations, not free cash. RPO of $103.7B is a recognition schedule, not a collections schedule (claim 8). Scenarios that pair two-year RPO with assumed margins against 2027 principal give thin coverage, and they mix perimeters and dates. Neither they nor the 2.36× interest cover (claim 4) prove the company can repay principal from operations.

**Financing and leverage.** The stack has four layers: GPU-backed DDTLs in SPVs, unsecured notes and converts, vendor financing and a revolver (claims 15–18). DDTL pricing shows the credit gradient. 4.0, largely non-recourse and serving one unnamed large customer, is A3 at SOFR+2.25%. 5.5 is Ba2/BB+ at SOFR+5.5% (claim 25). The September convert raised $3,570.8M net of capped calls (claim 38). Its conversion price now sits far above the stock (claim 18). The ATM is an authorization, not proceeds (claim 37). Liquidity at June looked adequate on management's own assertion (claim 23). That assertion predates the October selloff.

The $35–39B capex guidance is 2.89× revenue guidance at midpoints (claims 5–6). It does not translate one-for-one into new external funding: prepayments, committed draws, vendor finance and operating cash all offset it. But the company depends on continued access to capital. High secondary yields (claims 33–34) raise the cost of new unsecured money. They do not reset existing fixed coupons. They also do not prove that issuance is impossible. The October reporting (claim 39) establishes sector-wide pressure and some delayed deals. It does not establish a closed CoreWeave channel or a missed payment.

**Covenants.** DDTL 3.0's December 2025 amendment deferred its first DSCR test to October 2027 and allowed unlimited equity cures until 28 October 2026 (claim 24). After that date cures are limited, not abolished. Relief of this kind is a concrete warning sign. It is not a default.

**Dependence on the stack.** CoreWeave depends on Nvidia for every GPU and on a few customers for most revenue (claims 9, 13). Each period's largest customer is not named. OpenAI's commitment is large and its credit risk is flagged in the 10-K (claim 10). Nvidia is supplier, shareholder and conditional capacity buyer (claims 13–14). None of those roles is a guarantee of CoreWeave debt.

**Renewal risk.** DDTL 5.5 lenders explicitly underwrite renewals: five-year debt against three-year contracts (claim 25). Management counters with A100 and H200 renewals at or above original prices, prepayments, and selected Q3 contracts at ~$40M/MW (claims 28–29). Current index rents are firm, with H100 above $3 (claim 40). That supports present demand. It says nothing direct about CoreWeave's realized prices or the rent an older GPU commands at renewal. Management's return claims also carry less weight because the 10-Q found disclosure controls ineffective (claim 31).

**Who bears losses.** Equity is first loss and exposed to dilution. Creditors rank by borrower and collateral. Most DDTLs have parent guarantees, so a parent failure reaches SPV lenders too. DDTL 4.0 is largely non-recourse. Unsecured noteholders sit behind secured claims on pledged GPUs and contracts (claims 17, 23). Market prices already reflect this. CDS implies a risk-neutral five-year default probability near 56.5–63.0% (claim 35). The equity still has value because shareholders keep the upside in survival states.

**What would have to be true.** For the investment to earn a return, initial contracts must recover most of each GPU cohort's cost, as management claims. Renewals must hold price. The company must refinance $6.2B of 2027 principal (claim 19) while the market prices it as distressed. Cohort-level cash flows would settle the first two points. None has been published.

## Open questions

- **Funding access.** IFR reports a sector freeze. The CEO and Huang describe options and a working-capital squeeze. Only an actual post-selloff financing, or a failed one, will settle this.
- **Initial-term returns.** Management says initial contracts repay asset debt. No cohort returns are disclosed or audited. Contract expiry dates are also not disclosed, so the timing of the first large renewal cohort is unverified.
- **Market marks.** CDS and bond quotes are secondary snapshots at different times, with no primary ICE or TRACE series. Quotes for the 2032 notes conflict across tertiary sources.
- **Customer identity.** The Q2 largest-customer share may or may not be Microsoft. Falling Microsoft dependence is an inference.
- **Meta total.** Reuters treats the April ~$21B as additional to September's $14.2B; the 8-K language supports this but does not state a cumulative remaining value.
- **What triggered the 14 October fall.** It coincided with a hot CPI print and with credit stress. Causality is not established.
