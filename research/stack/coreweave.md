# CoreWeave (CRWV)

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Claude (claude-fable-5-1 subagent) · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- CoreWeave is the largest listed "neocloud": it buys Nvidia GPUs, installs them in leased data centers and rents them under multi-year take-or-pay contracts to Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Nvidia itself, Jane Street and a long tail of labs and enterprises. Revenue was $2.58bn in Q2 2026 (+112% y/y), guided to $12.4–13.2bn for 2026, with a $104bn revenue backlog at 30 June 2026 plus "more than $25bn" signed in early Q3.
- It is the clearest case of debt-financed AI compute. Total debt principal rose from $8.8bn (Mar 2025) to $35.6bn (Jun 2026), and roughly $6.6bn more was added in Q3 2026 (a $4.2bn convert, $1.2bn drawn on a new $2.6bn GPU-backed loan, $1.2bn on the revolver). Operating lease liabilities add $16.3bn, so debt-plus-leases was about $52bn at end-June. 2026 capex guidance is $35–39bn against $12.4–13.2bn of revenue.
- It is not profitable on a GAAP basis and cannot be while it grows: Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $1.51bn (59% margin) but depreciation ($1.39bn) and net interest ($640m) turned that into a $626m net loss. Interest is 25% of revenue and guided to $860–940m in Q3. The gap between adjusted EBITDA and net loss is almost entirely the cost of the GPUs (depreciation) and the cost of the money that bought them (interest).
- The contracted cash flows do cover interest comfortably (roughly 2.4x at Q2 run-rate) and the amortising GPU-backed loans (DDTLs) are structured to be repaid out of the specific contracts they finance. What the contracts do not cover is the capex needed to deliver the backlog: every dollar of RPO requires GPUs to be bought first, so the model only works while capital markets keep funding $35bn+ a year at tolerable rates.
- The market stopped believing that in mid-October 2026: 5-year CDS traded above 1,000bp (intraday record 1,194.5bp on 15 Oct, up from ~452bp in June), the 9.75% 2031 notes yielded 15.8% and the 9.625% 2032 notes traded near 78 cents, and the shares fell 15.5% on 14 Oct (the day of a hotter-than-expected September CPI print) and 11.9% on 16 Oct, closing at $61.39, 67% below the June 2025 peak but still 53% above the $40 IPO price.
- Who loses first: equity (market cap ~$34bn against $42bn of debt), then the ~$21bn of unsecured notes and converts, which sit structurally behind ~$16bn of SPV-level GPU-backed loans held by Blackstone, Magnetar, Morgan Stanley/MUFG/JPMorgan syndicates and, for the investment-grade DDTL 4.0, insurance-style buyers. Nvidia (≈11% shareholder, $6.3bn purchaser of unsold capacity through April 2032, and sole GPU supplier) is both the backstop and the main beneficiary of the build.
- Biggest uncertainty: what GPU rental prices look like when the first large contracts roll off in 2027–2029. Equipment is depreciated over six years while contracts run three to five, so one to three years of each GPU's accounting life is uncontracted. The newest loan (DDTL 5.5) explicitly lends five years against three-year contracts.

## Analysis

### What the business is and where it sits in the stack

CoreWeave converts Nvidia GPUs into contracted rental revenue. All GPUs in its fleet are Nvidia's (the 10-Q says this is a contractual obligation to customers), and three unnamed suppliers accounted for 60% of 2025 purchases. It owns the servers (gross technology equipment $33.8bn at June 2026, plus $11.9bn construction in progress) but leases the buildings ($16.3bn of operating lease liabilities) and increasingly co-invests in data-center joint ventures (up to $1.7bn committed in H1 2026). Active power went from 420 MW (Mar 2025) to 1.5 GW (Jun 2026) with 3.7 GW contracted; management targets more than 1.85 GW by end-2026 and "more than 5 GW of AI factories by 2030" under the January 2026 Nvidia framework.

Ninety-eight per cent of revenue comes from committed contracts. Demand concentration is extreme but falling: Microsoft was 67% of 2025 revenue; the largest customer was 36% in Q2 2026, with a second at 26% and a third at 10%. The filings warn that the A–D labels may not map to the same company across quarters, so the "Microsoft share" trend is an inference. The named contract book is roughly: OpenAI up to $22.4bn through 2030–31, Meta $14.2bn through 2031 and "approximately $21bn through December 2032" (whether incremental is not stated), Jane Street ~$6bn, Nvidia $6.3bn, and the original Microsoft MSA (February 2023, value undisclosed). OpenAI and Jane Street are private; the 10-K explicitly flags OpenAI's credit risk.

### Profitability: EBITDA is real, profit is not

Adjusted EBITDA margins of 57–62% are consistent and large: $1.51bn in Q2 2026. But the two costs that EBITDA excludes are precisely the costs of this business model. Depreciation ($1.39bn in Q2, i.e. $5.6bn annualised on $33.8bn of gross equipment, consistent with the six-year straight-line policy) is the GPU; net interest ($640m) is the money that bought it. Adjusted operating income, which keeps depreciation but drops SBC, was $128m (5% margin), down from 16% a year earlier. Net loss was $626m, and the accumulated deficit is $4.0bn. Interest alone absorbs 25% of revenue and is guided to rise to $860–940m in Q3 on $3.45–3.6bn of revenue. The Q3 guidance implies interest about four times adjusted operating income.

Operating cash flow looks healthier ($3.7bn in H1 2026) because customers prepay: deferred revenue was $9.7bn ($2.7bn current, $7.0bn non-current) and rose $1.4bn in the half. Prepayments are a genuine credit mitigant but they also mean reported CFO runs ahead of earned revenue.

### Financing: the loan stack

Debt principal was $35.6bn at 30 June 2026, up from $21.6bn at year-end and $8.8bn at the IPO. Adding the September $4.2bn convert, the $1.2bn DDTL 5.5 draw and the $1.2bn revolver draw gives roughly $42bn before any further Q3 DDTL draws or amortisation. The stack has three layers:

1. **GPU-backed delayed-draw term loans (~$16bn drawn, up to ~$25bn committed).** Each DDTL sits in a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary that owns specific GPUs and the specific customer contracts, with advance rates tied to the depreciable cost of the servers and projected debt-service coverage; loans amortise as contract cash flows arrive. DDTL 1.0 ($2.3bn, July 2023, 15% effective) and DDTL 2.0 ($7.6bn, May 2024, 11%) were led by Blackstone with Magnetar as co-lead and Coatue; Magnetar still held $438m of DDTL 1.0 at end-2024 and is also owed a $189m "loan" (a reclassified capacity deposit). DDTL 3.0 ($2.6bn, Morgan Stanley/MUFG) funds OpenAI deliveries. DDTL 4.0 ($8.5bn, MUFG agent) is the first investment-grade-rated GPU loan (A3/A(low)), non-recourse, priced at SOFR+2.25% or ~5.9% fixed, against an investment-grade customer. DDTL 5.0 ($3.1bn) was the first publicly syndicated one, Ba2/BB+, SOFR+4.5%, against two non-investment-grade customers. DDTL 5.5 ($2.6bn, JPMorgan/MUFG) is Ba2/BB+ at SOFR+5.5% and, for the first time, lends five years against contracts averaging three years: the press release says lenders are "underwriting renewal risk". Pledged collateral (ex DDTL 4.0) was $18.2bn of non-current and $2.6bn of current assets.
2. **Unsecured notes and converts (~$21bn after September).** Five senior notes totalling $10.0bn at 8.5–9.75% coupons (2030–2032 bullets), $6.6bn of 1.75% converts (2031, 2032; conversion prices $119.60 for the 2032s) and $4.2bn of 2.875% converts due April 2033 at a $97.85 conversion price, 22.5% above the 17 September share price and now 37% above it. The converts were sold with capped calls that cost $492m (April) plus an undisclosed amount in September.
3. **Vendor and bank lines.** $5.1bn of OEM and software-licence financing at 9–11%, mostly short-dated; a $2.2bn revolver, $1.2bn drawn in August; letters of credit of $533m backing leases.

The company's own April 2026 noteholder presentation shows debt at 7.0x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA and, on a fully drawn $53.5bn stack, 3.3x a "LTM run-rate" EBITDA of $16.1bn. That run-rate is about 2.7x the actual Q2 annualised EBITDA of $6.0bn; the leverage story depends on growth that has not yet been delivered. The December 2025 amendment to DDTL 3.0, which cut the minimum-liquidity covenant to $100m, pushed the debt-service-coverage test out to October 2027 and allowed unlimited equity cures until 28 October 2026, shows that at least one facility had to be re-cut around delivery delays.

### Can contracted revenue service the debt? The arithmetic

Take the balance sheet at 30 June 2026 and the RPO schedule. $103.7bn of RPO, 41% within 24 months, is about $21bn a year of contracted revenue through mid-2028, roughly double the Q2 run-rate of $10.3bn. At a 55–60% adjusted EBITDA margin that is $11.5–12.7bn a year of contracted EBITDA.

Against that: interest on ~$42bn of debt at a blended ~8% (Q2 contractual interest of $592m on roughly $30bn average debt annualises to 7.8%) is $3.4bn, so the Q3 guide of $860–940m a quarter is already there; scheduled principal is $4.4bn in H2 2026 and $6.2bn in 2027. Debt service of roughly $9–10bn a year is covered about 1.2–1.3x by contracted EBITDA alone, and more comfortably if the backlog added in Q3 converts. Interest cover on the Q2 run-rate is 2.4x. On that narrow question, the answer is yes: if customers pay and nothing else is bought, CoreWeave can service its debt.

But "nothing else is bought" is not available. The contracted revenue only exists once the GPUs are installed, and 2026 capex of $35–39bn is three times revenue. H1 2026 shows the actual flow: CFO $3.7bn, capex $14.1bn, debt service $6.2bn, funded by $16.7bn of net new debt and $3.0bn of equity (Nvidia and Jane Street). Capital markets provided about $20bn in six months, then another ~$7bn in Q3. The model's solvency is therefore a function of the price at which the next $30–40bn can be raised. With unsecured yields at 15–16%, the unsecured market is closed at acceptable cost; the secured DDTL market priced at SOFR+5.5% in August and has not been tested since the sell-off.

Per MW, from the filings: in-service PP&E (net PP&E less CIP) of about $35bn supports 1.5 GW, so roughly $23m per MW. Annualised Q2 revenue is $6.9m per MW on quarter-end capacity (higher on average capacity, which was below 1.5 GW for most of the quarter). At a 59% EBITDA margin that is about $4.1m per MW-year, a 5.6-year pre-interest payback on a six-year depreciation life. Charge 9% interest on fully debt-funded capital and the payback stretches past the depreciation life. The same contract-period arithmetic is why the equity return depends on the second life of the GPU: the three to five contracted years recover most but not all of the hardware cost, and the uncontracted tail (one to three years) has to be re-let at a price that is still unknown.

### Renewal risk, 2027–2029

The 2025 fleet is H100/H200 and GB200; the first large contracts (DDTL 1.0 matures March 2028; RPO recognised 2026–2028) roll off from 2027. Current pricing is supportive: the H100 on-demand median was $4.20/GPU-hr in October 2026, the one-year contract index rose 38% between October 2025 and March 2026, and management said it raised prices about 25% across SKUs in July 2026. That is also the bull case for the equity. The bear case is mechanical: each Nvidia generation roughly doubles performance per dollar, Blackwell and Rubin supply is arriving at the hyperscalers as well, and the Nvidia residual-capacity purchase ($6.3bn through April 2032) is a floor for unsold capacity, not a price guarantee for renewals. If H100-class rents fall by half at renewal, the uncontracted tail of depreciation is largely unrecovered, the DDTL 5.5 structure (five-year debt on three-year contracts) is tested first, and the unsecured notes, which rely on enterprise value rather than collateral, absorb the loss.

### Who holds the debt and who loses first

Equity first: a ~$34bn market cap at $61.39 against ~$42bn of debt, with Nvidia (~11%), Magnetar, Coatue, Jane Street and insiders (94m Class B shares) as the large holders. Next the unsecured layer: ~$21bn of high-yield and convertible bondholders, whose paper already trades at 78–80 cents. Then the SPV lenders: Blackstone, Magnetar and Coatue in DDTL 1.0/2.0; bank syndicates led by Morgan Stanley, MUFG and JPMorgan in 3.0, 5.0 and 5.5; and the investment-grade buyers of the non-recourse DDTL 4.0, who are exposed only to one SPV and one investment-grade customer. Vendor financiers ($5.1bn) are senior-ish by being short-dated. Nvidia sits on both sides: supplier, 11% shareholder, buyer of unsold capacity, and the party whose CEO said on 15 October that CoreWeave "doesn't need a backstop". Customers' prepayments ($9.7bn deferred revenue) are an unsecured claim too, which is why the large contracts are structured through SPVs.

### What the securities discount

At $61.39 the equity is 2.6x the midpoint of 2026 revenue guidance and about 11–12x annualised Q2 EBITDA on an enterprise value of ~$70bn excluding leases. That is not a distressed equity multiple; it prices continued growth. The credit market disagrees: 5-year CDS above 1,000bp and unsecured yields of 15–16% imply, on standard recovery assumptions, a majority probability of a credit event within five years. The two can be reconciled only if one assumes the equity would be heavily diluted (new equity, converts) rather than wiped out, or if the credit market is pricing illiquidity and forced selling rather than default. Analysts split on exactly this line (JPMorgan, BofA and Cantor: funding scare, demand intact; Bernstein, HSBC and D.A. Davidson: economically challenged model, "the subprime tranche of the AI trade").

## Conflicts and open questions

- **What triggered the 14 October fall.** The hot September CPI (3.7% headline, core 0.33% m/m, October hike odds doubling to 47%) landed the same morning as a 15.5% drop, and the coordinator's lead frames the move as CPI-driven. CNBC and MT Newswires frame it as a bond-market event (yields near 15%, CDS at records) in which inflation raised funding costs. Both are consistent; neither source cites a company-specific catalyst. Reuters pieces mentioned as leads (loan-stack explainer, CEO stock sales) could not be fetched: reuters.com and ft.com block the crawler, and cnbc.com returned 403 on fetch, so CNBC figures here rest on search snippets.
- **CDS level.** Reported peaks range from "above 1,000bp" (CNBC) through 1,140bp (CNBC Daily Open) to 1,194.5bp intraday (Bloomberg via TNW and TheStreet). Quotes differ by provider and intraday timing; the direction and order of magnitude are not in dispute.
- **Bond price.** The lead said 76 cents / ~16% for the 9.625% 2032s; the best fetched figure is ~78 cents / just over 16% (TradingNews, tertiary) and CNBC's 14.8% on 14 October. Treat the price as C-grade.
- **Daily moves.** One aggregator (roic.ai) reported an 8.3% fall on 15 October; Yahoo closes and CNBC show +5.9%. The Yahoo close series is used.
- **Capex.** Cash purchases of PP&E ($6.4bn in Q2) differ from company-reported capex ($9.4bn) because financed and accrued additions ($9.8bn of liabilities for PP&E additions at June) are excluded from the cash-flow line. The CSV uses the cash figure.
- **Meta $21bn.** The April 2026 release says "approximately $21 billion through December 2032"; it does not say whether this replaces or adds to the $14.2bn order form of September 2025. Backlog went from $66.8bn to $99.4bn in Q1 2026, consistent with a large net addition, but not decisive.
- **Microsoft trend.** "Customer A" fell from 72% to 36% of revenue, but the filings say the labels may represent different customers in different periods. Microsoft's own 2026 commitments are undisclosed.
- **Run-rate vs actual.** The company's leverage slide uses a $16.1bn "LTM run-rate" EBITDA (3.3x) against an actual annualised $6.0bn (7x). Which is the right denominator depends on delivery in H2 2026 and 2027.
- **Insider selling.** Form 144 volume (100 notices in three months) is primary; the dollar totals circulate only through aggregators. The 10b5-1 plans were adopted in November 2025 and March 2026, so the sales are pre-planned but not therefore uninformative.
- **Not verified.** The FT interview with Brannin McBee (14 Oct, "$52bn of debt and lease obligations", ">$35bn raised this year"); both figures are reproducible from the 10-Q ($35.6bn + $16.3bn) and the financing 8-Ks, so they are used as derived, not as quotes. Nvidia's exact stake (13F-based, secondary). Corporate ratings (tertiary).
- **Core Scientific.** CoreWeave remains Core Scientific's largest hosting tenant after the failed merger; megawatt energisation in 2026 was not verified.
