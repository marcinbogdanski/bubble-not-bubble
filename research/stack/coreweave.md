# CoreWeave (CRWV)

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [coreweave-quarterly.csv](/data/coreweave-quarterly.csv)*

## Summary

- CoreWeave rents Nvidia GPU compute under committed contracts. Q2 revenue was $2.575B (+112% y/y); 2026 guidance is $12.4–13.2B. June backlog was $104.2B; three customers supplied 72% of Q2 revenue. Q3 results are due 10 November (claims 1, 5, 7, 9).
- Growth runs on external finance. June debt principal of $35.551B becomes an illustrative $42.151B with identified later borrowings, before repayments. Capex guidance is $35–39B; $35.5B of leases have not commenced (claims 6, 15, 16, 22).
- Not GAAP-profitable: Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $1.510B becomes a $626M net loss; net interest consumed 24.9% of revenue (claims 2–4).
- Operating cash does not cover principal repayments: H1 CFO was $3.663B against $5.219B repaid and $14.117B of equipment purchases. The June schedule lists $6.2B due in 2027 (claims 19–20).
- Credit stress deepened: reported intraday CDS was 1,164.52bp; 2032 bonds traded at 74.75 cents and their Treasury spread touched 1,200bp. Shares closed at $43.66, down 9.83% (vendor quote). These prices establish no default or impairment. Restricted DDTL equity cures remain available (claims 24, 33–34, 39).
- Political risk threatens delivery timing; Intrator expects existing commitments and backlog to be unaffected. Most of DEN01's planned 22.5MW is online. Management's assessment and delivery progress counter collapse fears without proving future cash returns (claims 7, 27).
- Renewals remain the central uncertainty: DDTL 5.5 lends five years against three-year contracts. Management's initial-term recovery claims are unaudited; disclosure controls were ineffective at June. Equity bears losses first, then creditors by borrower and collateral; Nvidia's capacity purchase is no debt guarantee (claims 13, 23, 25, 28, 31).

## Analysis

**Profitability.** D&A and net interest exceed adjusted EBITDA (claim 2). Net interest takes about a quarter of revenue, and Q3 guidance implies 25.5% (claim 3). The six-year equipment life is an estimate that quarterly D&A cannot validate (claim 26). Growth front-loads depreciation and interest before contracts ramp. Current losses show the model is not profitable at today's financing cost; they do not establish that it cannot become profitable.

**Cash and debt service.** H1 operating cash fell $10.454B short of equipment purchases, before $5.219B of principal repayments (claim 20). Debt and equity issuance closed the gap. Deferred revenue rose $1.507B, but prepayments create delivery obligations (claim 30). RPO is a revenue-recognition schedule, not a collections schedule (claim 8). Neither backlog nor 2.36× EBITDA interest cover proves that principal can be repaid from operations (claim 4).

**Financing and leverage.** Funding combines GPU-backed DDTLs, unsecured notes and converts, vendor financing and a revolver (claims 15–17). DDTL 4.0 is largely non-recourse, A3 and priced at SOFR+2.25%; 5.5 is Ba2/BB+ at SOFR+5.5% (claim 25). The September convert provided $3,570.8M net of capped calls (claim 38). The ATM is an authorization, not proceeds (claim 37). Management's June liquidity assessment predates the credit selloff (claim 23).

Capex guidance is 2.89× revenue guidance at midpoints (claims 5–6). Prepayments, committed draws, vendor finance and operating cash offset some funding needs, but continued capital access matters. CDS of 1,164.52bp and the reported bond low show severe funding stress (claims 33–34). A Treasury spread is not an all-in yield; secondary prices neither reset fixed coupons nor prove every financing channel closed. The equity fall increases potential ATM dilution per dollar raised (claim 39). No financing withdrawal or qualifying credit event is established by the checked reports; prices alone do not establish impairment or resolve F1/F4.

**Covenants.** DDTL 3.0's unrestricted cure window has ended. Equity cures remain available within the three-consecutive-month limit; the first DSCR test remains in October 2027 (claim 24). The calendar transition alone establishes neither a covenant breach nor use of a cure. Actual compliance and any fresh amendment require disclosure.

**Dependence and delivery.** A few customers supply most revenue (claim 9). Nvidia conditionally purchases residual capacity but does not guarantee debt (claim 13). Policy risk spans [Texas's existing permit directive and proposed tax changes](/research/macro/power-and-datacenters), incoming governors' moratorium positions in [Texas and Ohio][BALLOTS] and [Georgia][STATES], and local votes with differing legal effects. Intentions are not enacted statewide bans; opposition is not a count of canceled CoreWeave projects. The risk is delayed revenue collection while debt service continues. Intrator expects existing commitments and backlog to be unaffected (claim 7). DEN01 delivery and operating capacity support that counterargument, but neither establish future commissioning dates or cash returns (claim 27). Customer funding risk belongs in the [OpenAI note](/research/stack/openai); rental benchmarks in the [GPU-pricing note](/research/macro/gpu-pricing-and-utilization).

**Renewal risk.** DDTL 5.5 lenders underwrite renewals: five-year debt against three-year contracts (claim 25). Management cites A100 and H200 renewals, prepayments and selected short contracts at attractive prices (claims 28–29). Those disclosures do not establish fleet-wide returns or future renewal rents. Ineffective disclosure controls add reason to seek independently checkable cohort cash flows (claim 31).

**Who bears losses and what must work.** Equity is first loss and exposed to dilution. Most DDTLs have parent guarantees; DDTL 4.0 is largely non-recourse. Unsecured noteholders rank behind secured claims on pledged assets (claims 17, 23). Initial contracts must recover most GPU costs, renewals must sustain returns, and operations or refinancing must cover the $6.2B of 2027 principal (claim 19). No published cohort-level cash flows settle those questions.

## Open questions

- **Funding access.** What post-selloff financing has actually closed, on what terms, and how much ATM equity has been issued? Distressed secondary prices do not answer this.
- **Covenant compliance.** Has DDTL 3.0 used a cure, required another amendment or breached a test? The expiry of unrestricted cures establishes none of these.
- **Initial-term returns.** Management says initial contracts repay asset debt. Cohort returns and the expiry schedule needed to test that claim are undisclosed.
- **Market marks.** No synchronized CDS close or identified bond trade supports a closing spread comparison. Coupons, CUSIPs, exact trade times and primary TRACE confirmation remain missing.
- **Delivery and policy.** Intrator expects commitments and backlog to be unaffected. Which sites already hold necessary permits, and what revenue timing or costs would enacted restrictions change? Project-level schedules, customer amendments and DEN01 cash contribution would test management's assessment; campaign positions alone cannot.
