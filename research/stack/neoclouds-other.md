# Neoclouds beyond CoreWeave: Nebius, IREN, the miners, Lambda, Crusoe and Nscale

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude claude-fable-5-1 · Data: [neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv](/data/neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv)*

## Summary

- GPU clouds sell compute; converted miners increasingly lease powered shells. The financing footprint is large, but the ~$80.7B dated selected-company subtotal mixes debt measures and is not a current layer balance (claim 1).
- Operating delivery is becoming measurable: Core Scientific bills 485MW and earns $215.432M quarterly colocation revenue; Cipher has begun recognizing HPC rent. Hut's large AI leases still require construction (claims 16, 27–28).
- Selective funding remains available. IREN closed $363M of equipment refinancing; TeraWulf signed Google support for financing its Anthropic development. Neither a lease value nor a credit-support agreement is funded debt (claims 11, 21).
- Returns after capital costs remain unproven. Core Scientific's $133.6M adjusted EBITDA sits beside $985.1M quarterly capex; Nebius's AI-cloud margin does not overcome its cash-investment gap (claims 2–3, 27).
- Microsoft reportedly declined Nscale's Texas expansion option without cancelling the base contract. This weakens incremental demand visibility, not the disclosed backlog by the option's nominal size (claim 30).
- The biggest uncertainty remains who bears delivery, construction, refinancing and customer-payment risk. Google support is contractual at Justified; Nvidia support for Nscale remains talks, reported, unverified (claims 17, 21, 25).

## Analysis

The dated debt subtotal establishes scale, not a consolidated balance sheet. September balances and financing events in the CSV must not be added to earlier issuer observations. IREN's completed equipment refinancing shows lenders will release capital against deployed assets and customer contracts; its undisclosed pricing prevents a cost-of-capital comparison. Nebius's bridge has a short maturity and rising spread. Lambda draws against commissioning, while Nscale's expected Nvidia convertible tranche remains distinct from reported guarantee discussions (claims 1, 5, 11, 23, 25).

Core Scientific's billed capacity and colocation revenue are stronger evidence than an uncommissioned contract ceiling. Its positive adjusted EBITDA does not cover the scale of construction and acquisition spending, and warrant revaluation dominates GAAP profit. Cipher now recognizes HPC rent but still has negative adjusted EBITDA; most cash remains restricted. Hut's adjusted EBITDA excludes its own digital-asset marks, yet its AI lease portfolio is still a future delivery obligation. These results do not establish a common profitability ranking across landlords, GPU operators and miners (claims 13–16, 27–28).

Nebius's cash flow benefits from deferred revenue and released receivables, yet falls well short of capex. Adjusted EBITDA excludes hardware replacement costs. IREN's contracted versus operating ARR exposes a commissioning gap, but debt divided by ARR is not debt-service coverage. Management's contract pricing and payback estimates support sampled demand without establishing realised returns after financing, replacement capex and dilution (claims 2–3, 7–11).

Large buyers recur across contracts without a measured concentration denominator. IREN's termination, remarketing, parent-recourse and refund terms determine cash exposure. Nscale's reported option rejection concerns incremental capacity, with the underlying decision predating the report; it is not evidence of a newly cancelled binding base contract. The disputed option size should not be deducted from contracted value (claims 4, 11–12, 25, 30).

Construction creates risk before rent begins. Cipher must fund overruns before reimbursement through rent. TeraWulf's signed Google support improves the financing framework, but the release supplies neither a support cap nor completed financing; Fluor's guaranteed maximum pricing is still being established. Existing Google support at other projects does not define Justified's coverage. Hut's nonrecourse project notes and parent revolver remain distinct. Guarantee calls may precede creditor losses, so there is no universal loss order (claims 17, 21–22, 28).

The [GPU-pricing note](/research/macro/gpu-pricing-and-utilization) compares transaction indices with advertised offers; neither measures fleet utilisation or collected revenue. The [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets) tracks bond marks, which are not default events. Gartner's consolidation forecast includes acquisitions, not just failures (claim 29). Investment returns still require timely commissioning, collections, durable pricing and sufficient cash after capital costs.

## Open questions

- What are outstanding balances after draws, repayments and exchanges? IREN's refinancing terms and net debt effect remain undisclosed.
- What are realised cohort returns after replacement capex, interest, fees, taxes and dilution? Comparable utilisation and collection data are missing.
- What cap, activation conditions, duration and recourse attach to Justified's Google support, and when will financing close? Does Fluor's final guaranteed maximum price adequately limit overruns?
- Who will take Nscale's optional Texas capacity, and what is its definitive scope? Anonymous reporting disagrees on size; Nvidia guarantee discussions remain unverified.
- Can [Lambda's future list prices](https://lambda.com/pricing) be reconciled across retrievals on matching configurations? Posted offers cannot settle realised pricing; see the [pricing note](/research/macro/gpu-pricing-and-utilization).
