# Neoclouds beyond CoreWeave: Nebius, IREN, the miners, Lambda, Crusoe and Nscale

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude claude-fable-5-1 · Data: [neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv](/data/neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv)*

## Summary

- GPU clouds sell compute; converted miners increasingly lease powered shells. Their financing footprint is large, but the ~$80.7B selected listed-company subtotal mixes dates and debt measures (claim 1).
- Major technology buyers anchor contracts, while their aggregate customer share remains unmeasured. Delivery conditions and tenant collections connect borrowers across the stack (claims 4, 12, 25).
- Selective funding access remains open through bank facilities, project debt and equity. Commitments and delayed draws are not cash received (claims 5, 20, 22–25).
- Profitability after capital costs remains unproven. Nebius's 49.7% AI-cloud adjusted EBITDA margin coexists with approximately −$3.4B operating cash less capex; landlord margins omit different costs (claims 2–3, 13).
- Sampled contracts and GPU indices support continuing demand. Estimated payback and prepayments do not establish realised fleet returns or a growing prepayment share (claims 7, 11, 28).
- Equity and bond repricing coexist with financing access. Convertible repayment exposure remains; consolidation forecasts do not imply equivalent default rates. The biggest uncertainty is how delivery, financing, construction overruns and backstop terms allocate losses (claims 6, 17, 21, 27, 29–30).

## Analysis

The selected debt subtotal establishes scale, not a consolidated balance sheet. Private facilities, gross issuance, repayments and exchanges need separate reconciliation. Bank access is selective: Nebius's bridge carries a short maturity, rising spread and minimum-liquidity covenant; TeraWulf's larger revolver does not establish a cash draw. Lambda draws against commissioning, while Nscale's expected Nvidia tranche remained future funding at announcement (claims 1, 5, 20, 23, 25).

Operating profitability does not settle the return on the build. Nebius's cash flow benefits from deferred revenue and released receivables, yet falls well short of capex. Adjusted EBITDA excludes hardware replacement costs. IREN's contracted versus operating ARR exposes a commissioning gap, but debt divided by ARR is not debt-service coverage. Landlord gross margin and NOI exclude different costs and cannot establish a common comfort ranking. Cipher and TeraWulf warrant charges are largely noncash; their negative adjusted EBITDA still matters (claims 2–3, 8–11, 13, 16, 19).

Large buyers recur across contracts without a measured concentration denominator. AMD also anchors Core Scientific commitments. The conditions disclosed by IREN and Nscale make blanket contract opacity an inaccurate description: delivery, termination, remarketing, parent recourse and refund obligations determine cash exposure. Nscale's prospectus financing status cannot be projected unchanged beyond its date (claims 4, 11–12, 25).

Construction creates risk before rent begins. Cipher's extended lease requires overrun funding before reimbursement through rent. Google support must be assessed by project, amount, activation and duration; initial coverage does not establish protection throughout an extended lease. Hut's nonrecourse project notes and secured parent revolver are distinct obligations. Guarantee calls may precede creditor losses, so there is no universal loss order (claims 17, 21–22).

Demand evidence and financing stress can coexist. Management's contract pricing and estimated payback support sampled demand, while transaction indices are separate from advertised offers. Neither establishes universal sell-outs, fleet utilisation or returns after financing, replacement capex and dilution. Sharp repricing, the weak Elice debut and Firmus talks show selective risk appetite, not common insolvency. FT coverage and Gartner's forecast do not establish the cause of equity moves. Investment returns require timely commissioning, collections, durable pricing and sufficient cash after capital costs (claims 6–7, 27–30).

## Open questions

- What are outstanding balances after issuance, draws, repayments and exchanges? Private facility announcements do not settle this.
- What do cohort returns look like after replacement capex, interest, fees, taxes and dilution? Comparable realised utilisation and collection data are missing.
- Which unnamed tenants and ultimate users sit behind contracts? Full financing conditions, parent recourse and extended-term backstop coverage require agreement-level disclosure.
- Silicon Data's dated display conflicts with its undated FAQ; aggregator retrievals also disagree. Publication timestamps, comparable configurations and complete histories would settle the differences (claim 28).
- FT bond-mark versions remain unresolved without security identifiers and transaction timestamps (claim 30). Crusoe's joint-venture debt and Firmus investment completion remain unverified.
