# Neoclouds beyond CoreWeave: Nebius, IREN, the miners, Lambda, Crusoe, Nscale and the rest

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: draft · Author: Claude (claude-fable-5-1 subagent) · Reviewer: none*

## Summary

- The "other neocloud" layer is now large: at 30 June 2026 the seven listed names covered here (Nebius, IREN, Core Scientific, Applied Digital, Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8) carried about **$45bn of debt** between them, and with CoreWeave's $35.1bn the listed layer is **~$81bn**, rising to **~$91bn** after the August–October 2026 convertible issues and to **>$110bn** once private-company facilities (Nscale ~$9.5bn committed, Lambda ~$2.9bn, Crusoe's Abilene JV) are added. The debt sits in three forms: GPU/contract-backed term loans and project bonds, convertible notes, and single-tenant lease bonds.
- Almost all of it is written against **take-or-pay contracts with four counterparties**: Microsoft (reported >$60bn of neocloud commitments: Nscale ~$23bn, Nebius $17.4–19.4bn, IREN $9.7bn, Lambda undisclosed), Meta (Nebius up to $27bn plus ~$3bn), OpenAI (via Oracle at Crusoe's Abilene campus; CoreWeave), and Anthropic (via Fluidstack, with Google backstopping the miners' leases: TeraWulf ~$19bn, Hut 8 $7.0bn, Cipher >$9bn at Barber Lake). Landlords of landlords (Core Scientific, Applied Digital) are exposed to the same names one step removed.
- **Pricing is not yet showing stress.** On-demand H100 medians are flat to +18% year on year, B200 +19%, Lambda and Crusoe show H100s sold out, and Nebius reports Q2 2026 deals at $20–25m per MW per year with payback under two years and says it "could sell [its] entire 2027 capacity on these terms today". Prepayments (IREN ~45–55% of GPU capex; Nebius 50–60%) are financing a growing share of the build.
- **Profitability is split.** Nebius (41% adjusted EBITDA margin on AI cloud, $2.2bn Q2 operating cash flow dominated by prepayments) and the lease landlords with delivered capacity (Core Scientific 59% colocation gross margin, Applied Digital 89% HPC NOI margin, Hut 8 with non-recourse project bonds and no parent debt) are the comfortable end. IREN (net loss $703m on $639m of mining impairments, $7.6bn debt against ~$1bn operating ARR), Cipher (negative adjusted EBITDA, $6.0bn debt, rent only starting) and TeraWulf (negative adjusted EBITDA, $5.2bn debt, Anthropic lease not before 2H 2027) are overextended relative to delivered revenue.
- **Equity has repriced hard.** Every listed name fell 7–12% on 16 October 2026 alone; Nebius is down ~18–21% on the week and 45% from its 52-week high, IREN and Cipher sit at 52-week lows, and all four recent convertibles are far out of the money (IREN $73.07 vs $26.99; Nebius $313 vs $165; Cipher $15.31 vs $8.98; CoreWeave $97.85 vs $61.39). FT-reported marks on single-tenant data-centre bonds (Hut 8 ~92–94c, QTS "Excalibur" <88c, Meta "Hyperion" <92c, CoreWeave <80c) and Gartner's 14 October forecast that over 80% of neoclouds cease independent operation by 2030 are the proximate causes.
- The single biggest uncertainty is **whether the contracts are as firm as the bonds assume**: what the Microsoft/Meta/Anthropic agreements allow in deferral or non-renewal, and whether the Google and investment-grade "backstops" cover full lease terms or only initial tranches. None of the four counterparties' contract terms are public.

## Analysis

**What the layer is.** Three business models now share the "neocloud" label. (1) GPU clouds that own the servers and sell compute by the GPU-hour under multi-year contracts: Nebius, IREN, Lambda, Crusoe, Nscale, Together, Voltage Park, Vultr. (2) Bitcoin miners converted into powered-shell landlords that lease critical IT megawatts under 10–20-year take-or-pay leases and never touch a GPU: Core Scientific, Applied Digital, Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8. (3) Sovereign vehicles (Humain, G42/Core42) whose economics are state-backed and opaque. The second group's tenants are the first group and the hyperscalers, so the same end-customer cash flow often services two layers of debt: Core Scientific's $3.3bn notes amortise from CoreWeave rent in a lockbox (row 17), and CoreWeave's own loans amortise from Microsoft, OpenAI and Meta payments.

**Profitability and cash.** Nebius is the only GPU cloud here with a clean operating picture: 41% adjusted EBITDA margin on its AI cloud in Q2, $3.0bn ARR against $8.55bn of debt, and $2.25bn of quarterly operating cash flow — though $1.2bn of that was customer prepayment, not earned revenue (row 2). Its capex ($5.7bn in a quarter) is roughly ten times quarterly revenue, so the equity story rests on contracts converting at the $20–25m/MW/yr it reports (row 8). IREN is the inverse: FY26 adjusted EBITDA of $246m came mostly from mining; AI cloud revenue was $129m for the year against $7.6bn of debt, and the gap between $4bn "contracted ARR" (an 8,760-hour multiplication of GPU/hour prices on capacity not yet commissioned) and ~$1bn "operating ARR" is the layer's clearest case of a company priced on bookings rather than revenue (rows 10, 12). The landlords with delivered capacity earn landlord margins: Core Scientific 59% colocation gross margin, Applied Digital 89% HPC NOI (though $183m of its $263m HPC revenue was pass-through tenant fit-out), Hut 8 positive adjusted EBITDA with no parent debt. Cipher and TeraWulf are pre-revenue on their HPC businesses, with negative adjusted EBITDA, $6.0bn and $5.2bn of debt, and headline net losses dominated by warrant revaluations (rows 20–23). The private names disclose bookings and valuations but not margins: Crusoe $140bn TCV at $30.9bn equity value, Nscale $103bn TCV at a $14.6bn Series C, Together $1.15bn bookings at $8.3bn.

**How it is financed.** The instrument mix has shifted in 2026 from converts and ATMs toward secured, amortising, investment-grade-rated paper against named contracts: Lambda's 6.78% fixed DDTL rated Baa1 (row 26), IREN's $3.6bn Microsoft facility at 6.0% (row 13), Nscale's ~$3bn IG-rated DDTLs (row 29), Nebius's first secured facility at SOFR+250 (row 7), Hut 8's $7.5bn of 2042 project bonds at ~6.1–6.2% sold to insurers (row 24), and Cipher's Ba-rated project notes at 6.0–7.125% (row 21). This is cheaper than the 11–14% private-credit GPU loans of 2023–24 and the miners' 7.75% unsecured-equivalent notes, but it depends on two things: the tenant's credit (Microsoft, Meta, an "AA- or higher" tenant, or Google standing behind Fluidstack) and the loan amortising inside the contract term. Convertibles remain the equity-like cushion, and every 2026 issue is now deeply out of the money (IREN conversion $73 vs $27 spot; Nebius $313 vs $165, accreting to 110–125% of principal; Cipher $15.31 vs $8.98; CoreWeave $97.85 vs $61). If prices do not recover, these are debt: Cipher's holders can put in November 2030, Nebius's 2030 notes mature at 110% in February 2030. Customer prepayments are the quiet third leg: IREN says 45–55% of GPU capex on recent deals, Nebius 50–60%, and Nebius expects >$9bn of prepayments in 2026 alone (rows 4, 13). Prepayments transfer financing risk to the customer but also make the customer's balance sheet the real lender.

**Dependence on three (four) customers.** Microsoft alone reportedly committed >$60bn to neoclouds by November 2025 (row 33), spread across Nscale (~$23bn), Nebius ($17.4–19.4bn), IREN ($9.7bn) and Lambda, in addition to its share of CoreWeave (67% of CoreWeave's 2025 revenue per the CoreWeave note). Meta has up to $30bn with Nebius (row 5) and is CoreWeave's second anchor. OpenAI sits behind Oracle's 15-year lease at Crusoe's Abilene campus, behind Nscale's Stargate Norway, and in CoreWeave's backlog. Anthropic has become the fourth anchor through Fluidstack: TeraWulf's $19bn Justified lease is signed directly with Anthropic, and Hut 8's River Bend and Cipher's Barber Lake are Fluidstack leases backstopped by Google (rows 21, 23, 24). Applied Digital's non-CoreWeave campuses are all with one unnamed IG hyperscaler. The practical consequence is that the layer's credit is a derivative of four buyers' capex plans. The March 2026 Abilene episode shows both sides: OpenAI and Oracle declined the expansion, and Microsoft took the 900 MW instead within weeks (row 28). Substitution worked because demand is currently excess; it would not in a downturn.

**The 2001-style chain.** If model-training demand paused, the sequence would run: on-demand and short-term prices fall first (the merchant tail: Together, Voltage Park, Vultr, Lambda's on-demand fleet, IREN's non-Microsoft $2.8bn of 3–4-year contracts). Second, hyperscalers stop signing new tranches and use whatever deferral rights the undisclosed contracts contain; "contracted ARR" and "TCV" shrink before revenue does, and converts stop being equity. Third, the GPU-backed DDTLs, which amortise over four to seven years against fleets whose resale value tracks rental rates, breach coverage covenants; the lenders here are banks (JPM, GS, MUFG, MS) and private credit (Blackstone, Magnetar, PIMCO, Blue Owl, Apollo), with insurers now holding the IG tranches (row 35 for the bear framing, graded D because its $2.99/hr figure contradicts the price boards in row 34). Fourth, the landlords are the last to feel it because their leases are 10–20 years take-or-pay and often backstopped, but their bonds already trade at 88–94 cents on single-tenant and refinancing fear (row 36), and their equity holds 90–96m CoreWeave-era warrants (Core Scientific) and Google warrants (TeraWulf) whose value tracks the sector. Who bears the loss, in order: convert holders and equity (already down 50–75% from highs), then GPU-loan lenders, then project-bond holders, then the backstop providers (Google's disclosed $600m at TeraWulf; reported $1.8bn in total), then the hyperscalers through take-or-pay. The circularity is real but partial: Nvidia holds equity in Nebius ($2bn), Nscale ($1bn convert), CoreWeave, Crusoe, Lambda and Together, and is reported by the FT to be the tenant of a Hut 8 campus.

**Pricing pressure or sold-out?** The evidence as of this week says sold-out: H100 medians have risen year on year, B200 medians are up 19%, Lambda and Crusoe list H100 as unavailable, Nebius reports rising prices on old GPUs and $40–50m/MW short-term deals, IREN reports ~$25m/MW in current discussions (rows 8, 12, 34). The bear reading is that these are spot indicators in a supply-constrained year, that rate cards for Blackwell are rising because Hopper is being retired, and that underwriting on 2024 peak rates has already been invalidated once (row 35). Gartner's view (row 37) is about the layer's aggregate revenue, not current prices: it argues the layer cannot earn $8.5tn by 2030. Both can be true.

**CoreWeave** is covered in [research/stack/coreweave.md](/research/stack/coreweave.md). For this note it matters as the layer's price-setter: $35.1bn of debt, $103.7bn RPO, a B+ rating, unsecured notes at ~15% yields and five-year CDS reported above 1,000 bp during the week of 14 October, and as the tenant whose rent services Core Scientific's and Applied Digital's bonds (rows 17, 19, 38).

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Are prices rising or falling?** Aggregator medians (row 34) show H100 flat to +18% YoY; Compute Forecast (row 35) says H100 fell 64–75% to $2.99 in 14 months. Both are partly right (long-run decline from 2024, rebound in 2026), but the underwriting question — what rate the loans assume — is not disclosed by any lender. Data that would resolve it: covenant rate assumptions in the DDTL credit agreements (CoreWeave and Lambda file them as exhibits; Nscale does not).
- **How firm is "contracted"?** Core Scientific calls its $24bn "potential" revenue; Applied Digital counts $86bn "if renewals are exercised"; IREN's $4bn is ARR on uncommissioned GPUs; Nscale's $103bn and Crusoe's $140bn are unaudited company claims. The Nebius Meta #2 deal is explicit that $15bn of the $27bn is capacity Nebius intends to resell. None of the Microsoft/Meta/Anthropic agreements' termination or deferral terms are public.
- **Backstop size.** TeraWulf discloses $600m of Google credit support; reporting puts Google's total Fluidstack backstop at $1.8bn (row 31); Cipher's $1.733bn notes amortise against "Fluidstack rent and the Google backstop" without stating the amount. Whether Google covers full 10–25-year lease terms or an initial tranche determines whether the miners' bonds are Google credit or Fluidstack credit.
- **Who are the unnamed tenants?** Applied Digital's four non-CoreWeave campuses, Hut 8's "AA- or higher" Beacon Point tenant (FT says Nvidia), Nebius's $775m-facility counterparty, Lambda's two IG offtakers. Concentration cannot be measured while they are unnamed.
- **Gartner's 80%.** The release's heading is "market to consolidate"; secondary coverage reads it as failure. Gartner's own June 2026 release projected neoclouds capturing 20% of a $267bn AI cloud market by 2030. The two are consistent only if a few neoclouds survive large.
- **Crusoe Abilene financing.** Search summaries cite $11.6bn of debt and equity, $9.6bn from JPMorgan; the sources (DCD) could not be fetched. Treated as unverified.
- **Lambda IPO and valuation.** Bloomberg-sourced reports range from $3bn at $12bn (Aug) to $4bn at $14.5bn (Oct); no filing exists. Treated as reported, unverified.
- **Why 16 October?** All eight listed names fell 7–12% on one day. Secondary coverage attributes the week to CoreWeave's bond and CDS moves, Gartner, and a hot CPI print; no company-specific disclosure was found.
