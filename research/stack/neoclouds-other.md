# Neoclouds beyond CoreWeave: Nebius, IREN, the miners, Lambda, Crusoe and Nscale

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Earlier cross-check: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude claude-fable-5-1 · Data: [neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv](/data/neoclouds-other-debt-contracted.csv)*

## Summary

- GPU clouds sell compute; converted miners increasingly lease powered shells. The ~$80.7B dated selected-company debt subtotal mixes measures and is not a current layer balance (claim 1).
- Neocloud equities sold off sharply, leaving sampled converts far below their initial strikes. These prices indicate financing pressure, not missed payments (claim 6).
- IREN targets >$4B operating ARR for January, potentially end-February. Its after-close disclosure cites customer design changes and supplier timing; it cannot explain the cash-session decline without evidence of earlier dissemination (claim 8).
- IREN's remaining FY27 capex plan requires $6B of market-dependent funding beyond $9B of cash and committed sources. The smaller ARR program's “fully funded” claim includes conditional facilities and unpaid contractual prepayments (claim 29).
- Operating delivery is real: IREN generated $200M quarterly AI Cloud revenue, Core Scientific bills 485MW, and Cipher recognizes HPC rent. Capital costs still overwhelm evidence of sustainable returns (claims 10, 16, 27).
- Selective finance remains available: IREN closed equipment refinancing and TeraWulf signed Google support. Nscale's Texas expansion option was reportedly declined without cancelling its base contract (claims 11, 21, 30).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether commissioning and collections arrive before funding conditions or policy restrictions bind. Incoming governors' proposals and adverse local votes increase uncertainty; they do not establish a uniform construction ban or cancelled backlog. See the [power note](/research/macro/power-and-datacenters).

## Analysis

The dated debt subtotal establishes scale, not a consolidated balance sheet. September balances and financing events in the CSV must not be added to earlier issuer observations. IREN's completed equipment refinancing demonstrates access against deployed assets, but its undisclosed pricing prevents a cost-of-capital comparison. Nebius's bridge has a short maturity and rising spread. Lambda draws against commissioning; Nscale's expected Nvidia convertible tranche is separate from reported guarantee discussions (claims 1, 5, 11, 23, 25).

IREN now has a disclosed commissioning delay and a conditional funding plan. The contracted ARR target remains intact, but operating revenue depends on testing and customer acceptance. Management attributes timing to a customer design change and supplier availability, not the election. The results were filed after the regular session; treating them as the cause of that session's fall would reverse the known chronology. The cash, facilities and prepayments supporting the ARR program are neither unrestricted cash nor full funding for the broader pipeline. Delays can push collections beyond expenditure and facility availability periods (claims 8–10, 29).

There is counterevidence to a demand-collapse interpretation: IREN reports delivered capacity, rising AI Cloud revenue and positive adjusted EBITDA. Core Scientific's billed capacity and Cipher's first HPC rent likewise demonstrate operating demand. Nevertheless, IREN remains loss-making after depreciation and financing; Core Scientific's construction spending dwarfs adjusted EBITDA; Cipher's adjusted EBITDA remains negative and most cash restricted. Nebius's cash flow benefits from working-capital movements yet falls short of capex. These business models do not support a common profitability ranking (claims 2–3, 10, 13–16, 27–28).

Policy adds completion and cost risk. Texas's existing administrative permitting/audit directive is distinct from the governor-elect's proposed moratorium and tax changes, which require implementation. Ohio's conditional moratorium position and Georgia's proposed construction pause are also intentions; local ballot results mix binding restrictions with advisory votes. The [power note](/research/macro/power-and-datacenters) owns legal status and ballot evidence. A longer approval process or reduced incentives could increase carrying costs before rent, but no uniform ban or quantified cancellation of these issuers' capacity is established. Constraints could also protect utilization and pricing at already operating sites; the net effect depends on each project's permits and customer contracts.

Large buyers recur across contracts without a measured concentration denominator. IREN's termination, remarketing, parent-recourse and refund terms govern cash exposure. Core Scientific's lockbox depends on CoreWeave rent. The [CoreWeave note](/research/stack/coreweave) tracks counterparty credit stress; [Dow Jones reports management's expectation](https://www.itiger.com/news/2681008585) that political changes will not affect existing commitments or backlog. That is counterevidence, not independent assurance. Nscale's reported option rejection concerns incremental capacity and cannot be deducted from binding base contracts (claims 4, 11–12, 25, 30).

Construction creates risk before rent begins. Cipher bears overruns before reimbursement; TeraWulf's signed Google support lacks a disclosed cap or completed financing, and guaranteed maximum construction pricing remains in development. Hut's project debt and parent revolver are distinct (claims 17, 21–22, 28). The [debt-markets note](/research/macro/debt-markets) owns intraday credit marks and the gap in same-day broad spreads; distressed prices do not establish a qualifying credit event. Lower Treasury yields do not ensure cheaper issuer financing when credit spreads widen; [rates and energy](/research/macro/rates-sovereign-geopolitics) remain separate cost channels. The [GPU-pricing note](/research/macro/gpu-pricing-and-utilization) tracks prices, not collected revenue. Returns require timely commissioning, durable pricing and cash after capital costs.

## Open questions

- Can IREN commission and obtain customer acceptance within its target window and facility availability periods? How much of its committed financing and contractual prepayments is drawable or received, and on what terms can targeted funding close?
- Which campuses face binding permit restrictions, grid delays or prospective tax changes? Project-level evidence is needed before turning political positions into cancelled capacity or higher costs.
- Do rising AI Cloud revenue and existing customer commitments outweigh slower commissioning and financing pressure? Realised cohort returns after replacement capex, interest, fees, taxes and dilution remain missing.
- What cap, activation conditions and recourse attach to Justified's Google support, and when will financing close? Does Fluor's final guaranteed maximum price adequately limit overruns?
- Who will take Nscale's optional Texas capacity, and what is its definitive scope? Anonymous reporting disagrees on size; Nvidia guarantee discussions remain unverified.
