# Meta: advertising-funded AI infrastructure

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra, Claude Opus 5.5 · Data: [meta-capex.csv](/data/meta-capex.csv), [ai-debt-stack.csv](/data/ai-debt-stack.csv), [credit-spreads.csv](/data/credit-spreads.csv), [datacenter-pipeline.csv](/data/datacenter-pipeline.csv), [circular-financing-deals.csv](/data/circular-financing-deals.csv), [ai-unit-economics-capex-by-year.csv](/data/ai-unit-economics-capex-by-year.csv), [ai-unit-economics-useful-life.csv](/data/ai-unit-economics-useful-life.csv)*

## Summary

- Advertising funds Meta's AI build. Quarterly revenue grew 26%; operating profit excluding the settlement charge rose 11.4%. The core franchise remains profitable (claims 1–2).
- Quarterly FCF turned negative at −$2.120B. The $135–145B capex guide leaves annual FCF between −$15.341B and +$1.451B across three sensitivities, all below approximately $22.3B of annualized dividends and stock-tax withholding (claims 3–6).
- In the two weeks after its Q3 results Meta moved from cash flow plus bonds to a mix weighted towards equity. It raised $14.375B of 6.50% mandatory convertible preferred (cash received), $15B of US-dollar notes and €9.0B of euro notes. It also sold $25B of Class A stock forward, but that cash arrives only when Meta settles, on or before 2027-11-19, and it plans a $60B at-the-market program once Class C trades (claims 7, 14, 21–24).
- On a pro forma basis, cash and securities are about $111.8B against about $99.0B of US-dollar debt at face value. At 30 September, before the raises, cash was $82.449B against $83.669B of debt (claim 7).
- New money cost more: the 2036 note priced at Treasuries +160bp, against +90bp in April. S&P kept Meta at AA− but moved its outlook to negative. It forecasts 2027 capex above $225B and leverage above its 1.0x threshold. Reports of its 2026 FOCF forecast conflict: −$22B or −$39B (claims 23, 25–26).
- The Hyperion and El Paso project notes moved to a negative outlook with Meta. S&P rates them one notch below Meta and lowers them in step with Meta (claim 27).
- Uncommenced leases of $355.73B, $121.41B of 2027 commitments and $68.404B of maximum venture exposure limit how far Meta could retrench (claims 8–11).
- The biggest uncertainty is whether AI returns justify a 2027 build that S&P puts about 60% above 2026. Utilization, investment allocation and contribution profit are undisclosed (claims 5, 15–17, 26).

## Analysis

Advertising supplies a profitable operating base. GEM gives model-level evidence that better predictions raise advertiser conversions, but conversion gains are not incremental Meta revenue, and auction demand and mix also move prices. These gains do not establish the return on the whole build (claims 1–2, 15–16).

Cash generation is the binding constraint, and Meta has now financed around it. Implied Q4 capex exceeds Q3 operating cash flow by roughly $16–26B, before about $5.6B of quarterly owner payments. Cash that has arrived or settles this month covers that gap: ~$14.4B of preferred, $15B of dollar notes and €9.0B of euro notes. It does not cover a 2027 like the one S&P projects. The $24.9B forward is the bridge into 2027 and the $60B ATM the next step, but neither is cash today. The forward's proceeds depend on Meta choosing to settle, and its price drifts with funding rates and dividends. Chip suppliers' purchases flow to the forward sellers, not to Meta, so they are vendor support for Meta's share price rather than funding (claims 3–7, 14, 21–24, 29).

The mix matters for credit. Equity and mandatory convertibles add cash without adding debt, which is why pro forma liquidity now exceeds face debt by about $13B, the opposite of September. Debt still rose by $15B plus €9B, and the market charged for it: the 2036 note priced 70bp wider over Treasuries than in April, with long tranches above 7.3%. The preferred adds ~$0.93B a year of dividends ranking ahead of common, and conversion at up to 1.6 shares per $1,000 dilutes owners in 2029. Equity at $625 also cost owners: it is 12% below the 2026-10-16 close used in the forecast baselines (claims 7, 22–23).

S&P's action is an outlook change, not a downgrade, and its reasoning is about duration: 2027 capex above $225B, FOCF negative for three years, and leverage rising above 1.0x mainly from lease commencements. The 2026 FOCF figure is reported as either −$22B or −$39B. Reuters' −$39B matches the 2028 figure in the other report, which suggests a transposition, but S&P's research update would settle it. Either figure is worse than Meta's own sensitivities because S&P's FOCF measure and capex path differ. The ATM and forward are the plausible offsets to S&P's leverage path, and S&P's treatment of them is unpublished (claims 6, 25–26).

The project vehicles show how Meta's credit reaches off-balance-sheet debt. S&P rates Hyperion's and El Paso's ~$39.9B of notes by credit substitution: Meta's rating minus one notch, moved in step with Meta. Their investors are exposed to Meta's corporate credit more than to the campuses. A Meta downgrade would therefore cut ratings across the vehicles at once without any project default. A distressed event would need Meta to fail on rent or guarantees, which its liquidity makes remote. The 91-cent Beignet mark reflects market pricing of that linkage and duration, not payment stress (claims 10–13, 27).

Commitments make retrenchment hard, but they do not prove excess capacity. Uncommenced leases, 2027 commitments and minimum-rent clauses lock in cost whether or not workloads arrive. Accrued equipment shows cash spending lags deliveries, and depreciation is accelerating. Returns require sustained ad gains or new product profit, useful infrastructure and eventual capex normalization. Workload utilization, investment allocation and contribution profit remain the decisive missing evidence (claims 8–9, 17–19, 30).

## Open questions

- Reconcile S&P's 2026 FOCF forecast (−$22B or −$39B) from the research update, and learn how S&P treats the preferred, forward and ATM in leverage (claims 25–26).
- When and how far Meta settles the forward, whether the 6M-share option was exercised, and whether the suppliers completed their purchases (claims 21, 29).
- Quantify 2027 capex with Q4 results and compare it with S&P's >$225B (claims 5, 26).
- Reconcile Meta's settlement total with Illinois's account (claim 20).
- Obtain primary El Paso pricing and actual Beignet trades (claims 12–13).
- Disclose campus commissioning, workload utilization, frontier investment allocation and contribution profit (claims 15–17, 30).
