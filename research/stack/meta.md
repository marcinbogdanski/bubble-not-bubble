## Summary

- Advertising funds Meta's AI build. Quarterly revenue grew 26%; operating profit excluding the settlement charge rose 11.4%. The core franchise remains profitable (claims 1–2).
- Quarterly FCF turned negative at −$2.120B. The $135–145B capex guide leaves annual FCF between −$15.341B and +$1.451B across three sensitivities, all below approximately $22.3B of annualized dividends and stock-tax withholding (claims 3–6).
- Cash and securities of $82.449B sit below $83.669B of debt carrying value. Bonds and project capital supplement operations; Class C adds a prospective equity channel that preserves voting control (claims 7, 14, 26).
- Uncommenced leases of $355.73B and $121.41B of commitments due next year constrain spending flexibility. Maximum venture exposure is $68.404B, with overlapping leases and conditional guarantees (claims 8–11).
- GEM's measured ad gains support monetization, but neither those experiments nor management's incremental-revenue statement establishes returns on frontier-model investment (claims 15–16).
- Strong advertising, financing access and reusable infrastructure support the bull case. Weak residual cash and contractual rigidity support caution. Undisclosed workload utilization, investment allocation and contribution profit prevent a finding of proven overbuild or equity mispricing (claims 6, 8–10, 15–18, 22, 27).

## Analysis

Advertising supplies a profitable operating base. Better recommendations can increase engagement and inventory; better predictions can improve advertiser conversion economics and bids. GEM supplies model-level evidence, but conversion improvements are not incremental Meta revenue. Auction demand and mix also affect prices. Model gains cannot be summed, and their compute requirements do not establish the return on the whole build (claims 1–2, 15–16).

Cash generation is the immediate constraint. All current funding sensitivities fall short of annualized dividends and withholding. Taxes, working capital, legal payments and capex timing can change the result. The seasonal case is unadjusted and should not be treated as a forecast. The settlement charge is largely unpaid, so cash outflows lag its earnings effect. Stock compensation is added back in operating cash flow but dilutes owners; withholding is a separate financing outflow. Cash fungibility prevents tracing particular owner payments to bonds (claims 3–7, 20, 23).

Meta retains financing access and a profitable franchise. Class C creates another funding route without changing voting control. It does not establish distress. Compute resale offers could hedge unused capacity, but neither offers nor reported talks establish contracted demand. Product launches strengthen the commercial case while leaving paid AI adoption and contribution margins unknown (claims 14, 17–18, 26).

Venture accounting does not remove economic exposure. Meta retains rent, construction and conditional residual-value obligations. External investors still bear uncovered structural and extreme-event risks. Maximum venture exposure is neither expected loss nor additional corporate debt. Do not sum it with its guarantee and lease components or Beignet principal; only the lease component can overlap uncommenced leases. Short initial leases therefore offer less economic freedom than their term suggests (claims 8–13).

Commitments make spending cuts harder, but do not prove excess capacity. The build depends on power, cooling, construction and software portability as well as chips. Accrued equipment shows cash spending lags deliveries; depreciation is arriving. Longer useful lives improve accounting earnings without creating cash. Company-wide compensation and disputed recruitment offers cannot establish an MSL hiring budget or waste (claims 9, 19–21, 25, 29–30).

The valuation illustration uses stale market inputs. Its trailing multiple cannot identify mispricing without normalized cash flow and a discount-rate/growth model. Returns require sustained ad gains or new product profit, useful infrastructure and eventual investment normalization. Workload-level utilization, investment allocation and contribution profit remain the decisive missing evidence (claims 15–18, 22, 27).

## Open questions

- Reconcile Meta's settlement total and behavioral terms with Illinois's account using the executed agreement; quantify engagement and inventory effects (claim 23).
- Reconcile Scale's investment and deal amounts and verify ownership. Resolve the disputed recruitment-package terms (claims 24–25).
- Obtain primary El Paso closing/pricing documents and actual Beignet trades. Match spreads by date and duration before interpreting a structural credit premium (claims 12–13).
- Disclose campus commissioning, workload utilization, frontier investment allocation and contribution profit. Establish signed resale demand and paid AI adoption (claims 15–18, 30).
- Quantify next year's capex and identify the financing mix for the remaining build (claims 6–7, 14, 27).
