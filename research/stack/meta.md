# Meta: advertising-funded AI infrastructure

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Author: Codex / OpenAI · Status: reviewed · Reviewers: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check applied 2026-10-17; Claude (Opus 5.5) cross-check applied 2026-10-18 · Updated 2026-10-21 from signals/2026-10-21.md · Updated 2026-10-25 with Q3 2026 results*

## Summary

- In Q3 2026 revenue was $64.60bn, up 26% (also 26% in constant currency) and above the $61–64bn July guide. Advertising supplied 97.6% of it. Operating income fell 37% to $12.99bn, a 20% margin, after a $9.89bn charge for the attorneys-general settlement. Excluding that charge it was $22.88bn: up 11% year on year, a 35% margin. Family of Apps operating income fell 31% to $17.12bn. Ad impressions grew 10% and price per ad 14% (Q2: 14% / 12%). Net income rose 264% to $9.86bn, mainly because Q3 2025 carried an $18.95bn tax provision. [Q3 release][S44], [10-Q][S45]
- Q3 free cash flow was −$2.12bn: operating cash flow $32.79bn against capex, including finance-lease principal, of $34.91bn. It is the first negative quarter in this note's 2024–26 series. The common "first since the IPO" label is not stated in Meta's documents, and earlier quarters were not re-checked (claim 76, C). Nine-month FCF is $11.05bn. The capex guide, narrowed to $135–145bn, implies Q4 capex of $49.2–59.2bn, 41–70% above Q3. Q4 operating cash flow equal to Q3 gives FY FCF of −$5.3bn to −$15.3bn. The $73–76bn Q4 revenue guide at Q3's 50.8% cash margin gives +$0.4bn to −$11.1bn. Breaking even needs Q4 operating cash flow of $38.1–48.1bn. These figures replace the July-based sensitivities in claims 13, 41 and 50. Every case falls short of dividends plus stock-tax withholding, $16.8bn in nine months and about $22.3bn a year at that run-rate. They are not forecasts (claims 59, 64, 69). [Q3 release][S44], [10-Q][S45]
- On September 30 cash and marketable securities were $82.45bn, down $7.81bn in the quarter with no bonds issued. That is below the $83.67bn carrying value of corporate debt; in June it was $6.60bn above. The $10.80bn of restricted escrow is excluded. Between October 19 and 21 Meta created a non-voting, Nasdaq-listed Class C stock, effective January 19, 2027 and distributed one share per existing share (in effect a two-for-one split). The 10-Q says Meta expects to use Class C "to conduct equity offerings from time to time". This adds an equity route to fund the build that leaves Zuckerberg's voting control intact (claims 70, 73–74). [8-K][S47], [10-Q][S45]
- On October 13, 2026, Meta reported GEM conversion and CTR improvements of 18.9% and 12% over the past year, plus conversion uplifts of 5.8% / 4.1% / 1.6% on Facebook / Instagram / off-site for targeted users. GEM trains on several thousand latest-generation GPUs. On October 21 management said its AI investments "are already delivering substantial incremental advertising revenue". It added that the MSL investments "will take longer to generate returns than those in our core business". Neither statement comes with a baseline or a revenue bridge. They support ad-ranking gains but say little about returns on the frontier-model share of the $135–145bn 2026 build (claim 68). [Engineering update][S13], [Q3 release][S44]
- On September 30 Meta disclosed $68.40bn of maximum exposure to its two unconsolidated data-center ventures: Louisiana/Hyperion and Texas/El Paso, 20% of each. In December, with Hyperion alone, it was $45.95bn. The total comprises residual-value guarantees of $41.49bn, leases of $18.48bn, equity of $4.73bn and future funding of $3.70bn. The Texas additions, about $13.4bn of guarantee and $6.2bn of leases, match the July El Paso terms. Only the lease component can overlap with uncommenced leases. Beignet's $27.3bn debt is still supported by Meta's rent, construction and conditional residual-value obligations, not by a direct debt guarantee (claim 72). [10-Q][S45], [S&P][S16], [El Paso][S21]
- Uncommenced leases reached $355.73bn on September 30 ($278.99bn in June). Non-cancelable commitments reached $454.52bn ($349.31bn), of which $121.41bn falls due in 2027 ($81.65bn). Meta gave no 2027 capex figure. It said it is "still developing our budget and capacity plans for next year" but expects "to continue investing aggressively". It expects Reality Labs losses to narrow to about $15bn as investment shifts to AI. A 2027 spending cut would now meet more contractual friction; these obligations do not by themselves show excess capacity (claims 67, 71). [10-Q][S45], [Q3 release][S44]
- Trailing-twelve-month FCF through September fell to $25.13bn from $37.87bn through June. Priced at the pre-results October 16 close with the July share count, that is roughly 72× (an illustration only: a post-results price and the September share count are needed). Judging that price still needs normalized cash flow and the undisclosed split of investment between core recommendations and frontier products (claim 77). ## Analysis

**Scope and cash economics.** The latest financial statements used here now cover September. Q3 results were released October 21 after the close and the 10-Q was filed October 22 (claims 59–77). The June-based paragraphs below are kept as the record of what was expected before the print. [Q3 release][S44], [10-Q][S45] Meta reports no cloud segment, but commercialization has moved beyond the prospective categories in its July call. Meta announced Enterprise Platform on September 28, 2026, Muse for Small Business on September 29, and Muse Wonder API/code availability on October 12, including Wonder Full for Muse Code subscribers. These launches establish commercial offerings; the announcements do not disclose revenue or margins sufficient to demonstrate infrastructure payback. On September 15, Meta One reported 15 million subscriptions and trials combined, with individual bundles at $7.99/$19.99 per month. These include non-AI features: the mixed figure is not a count of paying AI users or a basis for calculating AI ARR (claims 39–40). [Enterprise][S34], [Small Business][S35], [Wonder][S36], [Meta One][S37]

**Q3 2026 outcome (October 21).** Revenue beat the top of the July guide by 0.9%, yet operating cash flow, at 32.786, barely exceeded H1's quarterly average of 32.044. The cash margin fell to 50.8%, against the 54.7% H1 margin the guidance-based sensitivity assumed. Even so, Q3 operating cash was flattered relative to profit. The 9.89 legal charge was largely accrued rather than paid (other liabilities +7.439; legal-related non-current accruals 8.304), and the 12.7 of fixed payments falls over ten years. Capex of 34.906 exceeded OCF, so FCF was −2.120 (claims 59, 64). With dividends and withholding of 5.425 on top, liquidity fell 7.811 with no new debt. It now sits below corporate debt carrying value (claim 70). Narrowing the guide to 135–145 raises the low end and implies a Q4 capex step-up of 41–70%. Q4 operating cash would have to reach 38.1–48.1 for the year's company FCF just to break even (claim 69). [Q3 release][S44], [10-Q][S45]

The Q3 call transcript was not retrieved: q4cdn paths following the Q2 naming returned 404, and the IR listing is script-rendered. Management statements here are therefore taken from the CFO outlook commentary and CEO quote in the release, which are Meta's own written remarks (claims 65–68). The October capex wording is "narrowed", not "raised": the low end rose by 5, the top is unchanged, and the midpoint rose by 2.5. Secondary descriptions of a raise are a reading of that change, not Meta's word. The Class C structure adds a non-voting equity channel; the 10-Q expects equity offerings and warns Class C may trade at a discount (claim 73). Together with no repurchases since 2025, a negative quarter of FCF and liquidity below debt, this marks a move from funding the build out of cash flow towards funding it with external capital (debt, SPVs, and prospectively equity). It is not evidence of distress: Meta has no financial covenants, and FoA still earned 17.117 in the quarter (claim 62). [8-K][S47], [10-Q][S45]

On the July 29 call, management included direct compute sales among enterprise opportunities and said it had multiple offers at a premium to its acquisition cost, while preferring the expected higher margins from selling intelligence (claim 52, A for the statement). NYT reporting summarized by Engadget on July 17 described early talks to lease Anthropic up to $10bn of compute over two years (claim 53, C, reported/unverified); no signed deal was found in searches through October 18. Resale offers provide a possible hedge against stranded capacity and may indicate capacity available beyond Meta’s immediate internal needs, but do not prove excess capacity or contracted demand. [Call][S11], [talks report][S39]

This note does not establish a peer ranking for “most AI spending relative to business”: companies do not report comparable AI-only capex.

The table and [quarterly CSV](/data/meta-capex.csv) separate capital purchases from finance-lease principal. Earlier quarterly releases label property purchases net of proceeds; later releases use purchases. These are calendar-quarter cash expenditures, excluding unpaid deliveries and broader commitments. Acquisitions, equity investments, operating leases, and construction held for sale are not all captured in company capex.

Before Q3, funding sensitivities gave different answers; claim 69 now supersedes them for the current outlook. Flat-H2 OCF gives FY FCF of −16.824 to −1.824. The earlier Codex cross-check added the unadjusted FY2025 H2/H1 ratio of 1.335289×, implying +4.664 to +19.664, but Claude identified an important cash-tax distortion: H1’25 cash taxes were 5.544 and FY taxes 7.578, leaving H2 taxes of only 2.034 after the July 2025 OBBBA. The 10-K describes a significant effect of that law on tax obligations; the adjustment is a sensitivity, not proof that the full tax fall was a nonrecurring benefit. H1’26 taxes were already 1.999. Removing cash taxes before computing seasonality gives (66.213 + 2.034)/(49.587 + 5.544) = 1.237906×. Applying it to H1’26 pre-tax cash generation and assuming H2 taxes also equal 1.999 gives H2 OCF 79.810 and FY FCF −1.102 to +13.898 (claims 13 and 41). The unadjusted case remains recorded for comparison rather than serving as the headline upside. [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8], [cash taxes][S10], [S23]

A separate guidance-based illustration derives Q3’25 revenue of 51.243 from FY revenue 200.966 less H1 89.830 and Q4 59.893. July’s Q3’26 guide of 61–64 implies growth of 19.04%–24.90%. Extending that growth to Q4 and applying H1’26’s 54.724% OCF margin gives H2 revenue 132.297–138.803, H2 OCF 72.398–75.959 and FY FCF −8.514 to +10.047 across revenue/capex endpoints (claim 50). This assumes a constant cash margin and matched growth across quarters; it is not additional company guidance. None of these scenarios is a forecast: taxes, legal payments, working capital and payment timing can change the result. [FY2025][S6], [guidance/H1][S8], [10-Q][S10]

Cash expenditure also lags the build. P&E in payables and accruals rose from 9.331 in December 2025 to 19.502 in June 2026, a 10.171 increase (A inputs, B difference). This indicates roughly 10 of investment accrued ahead of payment; it is not a full accrual-capex reconciliation and does not change cash-basis guidance. H1 D&A rose 49.9% to 12.355 from 8.242 (claim 55), showing that the earnings drag is already arriving even as unpaid investment grows. [10-K][S23], [10-Q][S10]

The coverage tests also differ. Breaking even on FY company FCF requires H2 OCF of 65.912–80.912 (annual capex less H1 OCF), or 2.8%–26.3% above H1. Covering H2 capex entirely within H2, without using H1 FCF, requires 79.082–94.082, or 23.4%–46.8% above H1 (claims 42–43). Positive company FCF would still precede dividends and equity-award withholding. [Inputs][S8]

First-half FCF also nearly disappears after dividends and cash withholding on employee equity. Annualizing those H1 outflows gives 22.806, exceeding FY FCF in all three current sensitivities: the residual is −39.630 to −8.908 (claim 51). The gap requires financing or existing liquidity under these assumptions. Meta received 24.91 of net May note proceeds, but cash is fungible: this does not trace particular dividends or withholding payments to those bonds. [Cash flows][S8], [10-Q][S10] The residual in claim 15 is retained cash after selected financing outflows, not a replacement FCF definition; dividends distribute owner cash rather than incur operating expense. Stock compensation is added back in OCF but dilutes owners; withholding is a separate financing outflow. Neither should be casually treated as free economic funding. Meta has already conserved cash by stopping repurchases. Debt and project capital supplement ads. The liquidity-minus-debt figure in claim 14 excludes restricted escrow and does not net all liabilities.

April’s capex revision cited component prices and additional data-center costs. Higher spending therefore does not necessarily buy proportionately more compute. July narrowed the range upward. In its July 2026 call, management declined a numerical 2027 capex outlook while describing plans to maximize 2026 and 2027 capacity; this dates the absence of guidance to the statement checked. Of June’s 349.31 of non-cancelable commitments, 53.52 is due in 2026 and 81.65 in 2027; July’s additional 68 of leases begins in 2027–28 for 18–20 years (claim 48). This creates contractual friction for a 2027 spending cut, without making every item capex or proving the entire budget fixed. July also guided Q3 revenue to 61–64, FY expenses to 165–169 and FY operating income above 2025 (claim 49). August reaffirmed capex but added the legal charge: July’s expense guidance is consequently not a complete current expense outlook. The settlement’s instalments and conditional component must not be treated as an immediate cash payment of its entire headline amount.

The August 26 settlement also creates a separate engagement and ad-inventory risk. Illinois describes teen daily time limits, nighttime feed blocks and school-time notification limits; their revenue impact is undisclosed (claim 47). Meta’s own account also lets teens choose a non-algorithmic feed as their default, with parents able to require that setting, in participating states; this bears directly on recommendation-driven engagement. Its account differs on some other behavioral terms. The payment totals of approximately 18 from Meta and up to 17.1 from Illinois remain unreconciled; neither a known scope explanation nor the exact reach of the restrictions should be assumed without checking the executed agreement. [Illinois AG][S32], [Meta][S27]

Q2 2026 profitability also needs a legal/severance bridge. Adding only legal charges of 2.40 and severance of 1.18 to reported operating income of 18.775 gives 22.355 and a 36.77% margin, versus 30.88% calculated from reported amounts (claims 44–45). This is a limited grade-B adjustment, not normalized earning power or cash savings. Reported operating income fell 8% year on year; the limited add-back is +9.4% versus Q2’25 operating income of 20.441, which management rounds to +9%. Approximately 8,000 affected employees were still included in June headcount, and management identified third-party AI token costs among expense-growth drivers (claim 46). Layoffs show adjustment capacity, not proven adequate savings; neither all margin deterioration nor all expense growth can be attributed to owned infrastructure or researchers. [Results][S8], [call][S11]

**Does AI pay through advertising?** The mechanism is credible: better recommendations increase engagement and inventory; better predictions improve advertiser conversion economics and willingness to bid. Growing impressions alongside growing prices is stronger evidence than volume alone. GEM engineering disclosures provide a plausible connection between additional compute and measured advertiser outcomes.

The October 13 engineering post attributes 18.9% conversion and 12% CTR improvements to GEM over the past year, with additional targeted-user conversion uplifts of 5.8% on Facebook, 4.1% on Instagram and 1.6% off-site. It describes training on several thousand latest-generation GPUs (claim 18). That is evidence about an ad-ranking model, with no disclosed allocation to the frontier/MSL programme or bridge to the return on the 130–145 annual build. It does not measure total ad-AI compute needs or prove GEM’s share of capex. The July call separately claims ranking improvements of 8.3% in Facebook ad clicks and 15.7% in conversions (claim 19); these gains must not be added to the October measurements. [Engineering][S13], [call][S11], [guidance][S8]

But company experiments establish narrower propositions than the investment thesis. Conversion improvements are not identical to incremental Meta revenue, and the October GEM claims do not provide a complete counterfactual, cost allocation, or audited profit bridge. Model-release gains may overlap; do not sum them. Price also reflects auction demand, currency, geography, placements, and advertiser mix. Impression growth can reflect increased ad load as well as better engagement.

Advantage+ run-rate measures revenue using the tools; some advertisers would have spent without them. It is neither a separate AI subscription business nor wholly incremental sales. Similarly, assistant monthly actives and post-rebuild daily growth use different denominators. Neither establishes paying demand or assistant margins. The strongest conclusion is that AI is improving an already monetizable ad system; the evidence does not prove that every frontier-training investment earns a satisfactory return.

**Financing and loss allocation.** Hyperion’s ownership sits in a venture whose external investor interest is financed through Beignet Investor. Meta does not consolidate the VIE because it says it lacks control over the activities most consequential to economic performance, notably remarketing. Beignet’s debt is therefore absent from Meta’s corporate debt total. That accounting judgment is not evidence that Meta has transferred all economic risk.

Meta leases the facilities, guarantees tenant obligations, and bears substantial construction risk. S&P describes minimum rent even without utilization, cost-overrun protection, and a residual-value mechanism designed to repay debt when leases end early. Its DSCR is a rating-case modeled coverage profile, not observed operating coverage or a guaranteed floor under every stress. Shareholders absorb rent, impaired equity, and applicable guarantee payments before the transaction resembles a simple abandoned building left to creditors. External equity and bondholders nevertheless retain uncovered risks, including structural and extreme-event exposures. The rating agency specifically highlights the missing direct asset pledge. The disclosed maximum venture exposure is neither expected loss nor additional corporate debt.

Hyperion’s maximum exposure was nearly unchanged at 46.03 versus 45.95 in December 2025. It comprises roughly 28 of RVG threshold, 12.31 of leases, 2.92 of equity and approximately 2.8 of future funding implied as a residual; that last estimate inherits the rounded RVG input (claim 56). Only the lease component can overlap with the 278.99 of uncommenced leases: the RVG, equity and future-funding components are not lease commitments. The RVG covers a qualifying fair-value shortfall below its declining threshold, subject to other conditions, rather than guaranteeing Beignet’s principal. [10-Q][S10]

Do not add the maximum venture exposure, RVG threshold, lease commitment, and Beignet principal together: these overlap. Undiscounted future obligations are not present corporate debt; reconcile overlaps before summing lease and purchase commitments. They still reduce management’s ability to cut expenditure when returns disappoint. The newer El Paso partnership shows that this is becoming a repeatable financing strategy. July 24 reporting described roughly 12.3 of Sopaipilla notes being marketed; a fetched July 28 placement report gives 12.547 of 7.534% amortizing notes due November 30, 2048, placed July 27 at T+287.5bp, while another report gives approximately 7.5% yield. Proposed ratings were reported A+ / AA−; Fitch’s July 22 page identifies its rating as AA−(EXP). Pricing, final size and closing remain reported/unverified (C) pending a primary pricing record (claim 54). Meta’s own April 2036 notes priced at 5.25%. The higher reported SPV cost is a signal to investigate, not a clean measure of structural credit premium: issuance dates, duration, amortization, liquidity and Treasury yields differ. [Capacity][S40], [placement report][S41], [yield report][S42], [Fitch][S43], [corporate pricing][S20]

The FT price report is verified as reporting through its syndicated article. It is not a verified transaction record. Falling long-duration bonds can reflect Treasury yields, liquidity, or credit spreads; price alone cannot demonstrate insolvency or project failure. Compare same-date spreads and duration with Meta corporate debt, and obtain actual Beignet trading evidence.

**Assets, people, and accounting.** Prometheus, Hyperion, and El Paso illustrate the move from incremental servers to large, power-dependent campuses. Hyperion’s ultimate expansion ambition exceeds the specific campus scope in the initial financing. Early capacity dates and contractual rent commencement also measure different milestones. No assumption is made that planned capacity is fully energized. Meta’s engineering account identifies NVIDIA, AMD, and internal silicon; diversification helps, but software portability, cooling, grid availability, and construction execution constrain substitution.

MSL’s standalone payroll and compensation commitments are undisclosed. Company-wide SBC and R&D show rising expenses, not MSL-specific costs; unrecognized RSU expense is not a cash debt. Sensational offer reports are contested and cannot be summed into a hiring budget. Commitments, power constraints and company-wide compensation establish risk exposure, but without workload utilization and project returns they do not establish excess capacity, wasteful MSL recruitment or an ordering of proven overinvestment. Scale is an equity investment, not capex or wholly a signing bonus. Its announcement confirms Wang’s move and continued corporate independence; the investment carrying amount and reported transaction value are different figures whose reconciliation remains incomplete.

H1 grants also included options on 20 million shares at a weighted-average $2,788 exercise price, about 3.93× the October 16 close, with service and market vesting conditions. Their unrecognized expense was 0.471, small beside the 79.79 of unrecognized RSUs (claim 57). This describes incentive design, not a material MSL-specific cost total. [10-Q][S10], [price][S29]

Longer useful lives defer depreciation and improve earnings without creating cash. They may correctly reflect reusable equipment; they may also understate future replacement costs if economic obsolescence accelerates. Construction-in-progress delays expense recognition further. Cash returns and utilization should therefore accompany reported operating margins when judging payback.

**What the stock discounts.** Valuation combines the latest vendor-reported close and disclosed July share classes; market capitalization is approximate because the share count is stale, rather than an exchange-verified contemporaneous capitalization. TTM FCF equals annual FCF plus current first-half FCF less the prior first half. This is company FCF before the owner costs discussed above. The trailing multiple alone does not establish overvaluation or the growth path embedded in the price: that requires normalized FCF and a discount-rate/growth valuation, alongside the undisclosed allocation between core recommendations and frontier products. Successful ad AI can coexist with disappointing shareholder returns if investment stays excessive; conversely, today’s depressed FCF can understate earning power if capacity later produces revenue without comparable incremental spending.
