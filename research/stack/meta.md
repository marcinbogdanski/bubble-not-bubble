## Summary

- In Q2 2026, advertising supplied 97.6% of Meta’s revenue and Family of Apps earned $23.394bn in operating income; Meta had no separately reported cloud earnings base. FoA profit is not a pure-ad profit disclosure. [Results][S8]
- Against July 2026 capex guidance of $130–145bn, repeating H1 operating cash flow in H2 implies FY FCF of −$16.824bn to −$1.824bn; applying the 2025 H2/H1 cash-flow ratio instead implies +$4.664bn to +$19.664bn. These are sensitivities, not forecasts. [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8]
- On October 13, 2026, Meta reported GEM conversion and click-through improvements of 18.9% and 12%; these company measurements support ad-model benefits but do not establish profit after the full AI investment cost. [Engineering update][S13]
- At June 30, 2026, Meta disclosed $46.03bn of maximum Hyperion exposure despite not consolidating the venture; the external investor’s $27.3bn Beignet debt is supported by Meta’s rent, construction and conditional residual-value obligations. [10-Q][S10], [S&P][S16]
- Uncommenced leases reached $278.99bn in June 2026, with another $68bn signed in July; these obligations increase exposure if demand disappoints, but do not establish excess capacity or excessive MSL compensation. [10-Q][S10]
- At the October 16, 2026 close, Meta’s approximate equity value was 47.7× company FCF for the twelve months through June, using the disclosed July share count. Assessing that price requires normalized cash flow and the undisclosed investment split between core recommendations and frontier products. [Price history][S29], [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8]

## Analysis

**Scope and cash economics.** The latest financial statements cover June; September results are unavailable in the sources reviewed as of 2026-10-17. Meta reports no cloud segment, but commercialization has moved beyond the prospective categories in its July call. Meta announced Enterprise Platform on September 28, 2026, Muse for Small Business on September 29, and Muse Wonder API/code availability on October 12, including Wonder Full for Muse Code subscribers. These launches establish commercial offerings; the announcements do not disclose revenue or margins sufficient to demonstrate infrastructure payback. On September 15, Meta One reported 15 million subscriptions and trials combined, with individual bundles at $7.99/$19.99 per month. These include non-AI features: the mixed figure is not a count of paying AI users or a basis for calculating AI ARR (claims 39–40). [Enterprise][S34], [Small Business][S35], [Wonder][S36], [Meta One][S37]

This note does not establish a peer ranking for “most AI spending relative to business”: companies do not report comparable AI-only capex.

The table and [quarterly CSV](/data/meta-capex.csv) separate capital purchases from finance-lease principal. Earlier quarterly releases label property purchases net of proceeds; later releases use purchases. These are calendar-quarter cash expenditures, excluding unpaid deliveries and broader commitments. Acquisitions, equity investments, operating leases, and construction held for sale are not all captured in company capex.

Funding sensitivities give different answers. Subtracting first-half capex from annual guidance and holding H2 OCF equal to H1 gives FY FCF of −16.824 to −1.824. The historical comparison is material: FY2025 OCF 115.800 less H1 OCF 49.587 gives H2 OCF 66.213 and an H2/H1 ratio of 1.335289×. Applying that ratio to H1’26 OCF 64.088 gives illustrative H2 OCF 85.576 and FY FCF +4.664 to +19.664, at capex 145 to 130 respectively (claims 13 and 41). Neither scenario is a forecast; growth, seasonality, taxes, legal payments, working capital and payment timing can change the result. The flat-half case demonstrates funding pressure under its assumption, not inevitable negative FY FCF. [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8]

The coverage tests also differ. Breaking even on FY company FCF requires H2 OCF of 65.912–80.912 (annual capex less H1 OCF), or 2.8%–26.3% above H1. Covering H2 capex entirely within H2, without using H1 FCF, requires 79.082–94.082, or 23.4%–46.8% above H1 (claims 42–43). Positive company FCF would still precede dividends and equity-award withholding. [Inputs][S8]

First-half FCF also nearly disappears after dividends and cash withholding on employee equity. The residual in claim 15 is retained cash after selected financing outflows, not a replacement FCF definition; dividends distribute owner cash rather than incur operating expense. Stock compensation is added back in OCF but dilutes owners; withholding is a separate financing outflow. Neither should be casually treated as free economic funding. Meta has already conserved cash by stopping repurchases. Debt and project capital supplement ads. The liquidity-minus-debt figure in claim 14 excludes restricted escrow and does not net all liabilities.

April’s capex revision cited component prices and additional data-center costs. Higher spending therefore does not necessarily buy proportionately more compute. July narrowed the range upward. In its July 2026 call, management declined a numerical 2027 capex outlook while describing plans to maximize near-term capacity; this dates the absence of guidance to the statement checked. August reaffirmed capex but added the legal charge: July’s expense guidance is consequently not a complete current expense outlook. The settlement’s instalments and conditional component must not be treated as an immediate cash payment of its entire headline amount.

The August 26 settlement also creates a separate engagement and ad-inventory risk. Illinois describes teen daily time limits, nighttime feed blocks and school-time notification limits; their revenue impact is undisclosed (claim 47). Meta’s account differs on some behavioral terms. The payment totals of approximately 18 from Meta and up to 17.1 from Illinois remain unreconciled; neither a known scope explanation nor the exact reach of the restrictions should be assumed without checking the executed agreement. [Illinois AG][S32], [Meta][S27]

Q2 2026 profitability also needs a legal/severance bridge. Adding only legal charges of 2.40 and severance of 1.18 to reported operating income of 18.775 gives 22.355 and a 36.77% margin, versus 30.88% calculated from reported amounts (claims 44–45). This is a limited grade-B adjustment, not normalized earning power or cash savings. Management says operating income would have grown 9% without those charges. Approximately 8,000 affected employees were still included in June headcount, and management identified third-party AI token costs among expense-growth drivers (claim 46). Layoffs show adjustment capacity, not proven adequate savings; neither all margin deterioration nor all expense growth can be attributed to owned infrastructure or researchers. [Results][S8], [call][S11]

**Does AI pay through advertising?** The mechanism is credible: better recommendations increase engagement and inventory; better predictions improve advertiser conversion economics and willingness to bid. Growing impressions alongside growing prices is stronger evidence than volume alone. GEM engineering disclosures provide a plausible connection between additional compute and measured advertiser outcomes.

But company experiments establish narrower propositions than the investment thesis. Conversion improvements are not identical to incremental Meta revenue, and the October GEM claims do not provide a complete counterfactual, cost allocation, or audited profit bridge. Model-release gains may overlap; do not sum them. Price also reflects auction demand, currency, geography, placements, and advertiser mix. Impression growth can reflect increased ad load as well as better engagement.

Advantage+ run-rate measures revenue using the tools; some advertisers would have spent without them. It is neither a separate AI subscription business nor wholly incremental sales. Similarly, assistant monthly actives and post-rebuild daily growth use different denominators. Neither establishes paying demand or assistant margins. The strongest conclusion is that AI is improving an already monetizable ad system; the evidence does not prove that every frontier-training investment earns a satisfactory return.

**Financing and loss allocation.** Hyperion’s ownership sits in a venture whose external investor interest is financed through Beignet Investor. Meta does not consolidate the VIE because it says it lacks control over the activities most consequential to economic performance, notably remarketing. Beignet’s debt is therefore absent from Meta’s corporate debt total. That accounting judgment is not evidence that Meta has transferred all economic risk.

Meta leases the facilities, guarantees tenant obligations, and bears substantial construction risk. S&P describes minimum rent even without utilization, cost-overrun protection, and a residual-value mechanism designed to repay debt when leases end early. Its DSCR is a rating-case modeled coverage profile, not observed operating coverage or a guaranteed floor under every stress. Shareholders absorb rent, impaired equity, and applicable guarantee payments before the transaction resembles a simple abandoned building left to creditors. External equity and bondholders nevertheless retain uncovered risks, including structural and extreme-event exposures. The rating agency specifically highlights the missing direct asset pledge. The disclosed maximum venture exposure is neither expected loss nor additional corporate debt.

Do not add the maximum venture exposure, RVG threshold, lease commitment, and Beignet principal together: these overlap. Undiscounted future obligations are not present corporate debt; reconcile overlaps before summing lease and purchase commitments. They still reduce management’s ability to cut expenditure when returns disappoint. The newer El Paso partnership shows that this is becoming a repeatable financing strategy. Final closing was not independently verified.

The FT price report is verified as reporting through its syndicated article. It is not a verified transaction record. Falling long-duration bonds can reflect Treasury yields, liquidity, or credit spreads; price alone cannot demonstrate insolvency or project failure. Compare same-date spreads and duration with Meta corporate debt, and obtain actual Beignet trading evidence.

**Assets, people, and accounting.** Prometheus, Hyperion, and El Paso illustrate the move from incremental servers to large, power-dependent campuses. Hyperion’s ultimate expansion ambition exceeds the specific campus scope in the initial financing. Early capacity dates and contractual rent commencement also measure different milestones. No assumption is made that planned capacity is fully energized. Meta’s engineering account identifies NVIDIA, AMD, and internal silicon; diversification helps, but software portability, cooling, grid availability, and construction execution constrain substitution.

MSL’s standalone payroll and compensation commitments are undisclosed. Company-wide SBC and R&D show rising expenses, not MSL-specific costs; unrecognized RSU expense is not a cash debt. Sensational offer reports are contested and cannot be summed into a hiring budget. Commitments, power constraints and company-wide compensation establish risk exposure, but without workload utilization and project returns they do not establish excess capacity, wasteful MSL recruitment or an ordering of proven overinvestment. Scale is an equity investment, not capex or wholly a signing bonus. Its announcement confirms Wang’s move and continued corporate independence; the investment carrying amount and reported transaction value are different figures whose reconciliation remains incomplete.

Longer useful lives defer depreciation and improve earnings without creating cash. They may correctly reflect reusable equipment; they may also understate future replacement costs if economic obsolescence accelerates. Construction-in-progress delays expense recognition further. Cash returns and utilization should therefore accompany reported operating margins when judging payback.

**What the stock discounts.** Valuation combines the latest vendor-reported close and disclosed July share classes; market capitalization is approximate because the share count is stale, rather than an exchange-verified contemporaneous capitalization. TTM FCF equals annual FCF plus current first-half FCF less the prior first half. This is company FCF before the owner costs discussed above. The trailing multiple alone does not establish overvaluation or the growth path embedded in the price: that requires normalized FCF and a discount-rate/growth valuation, alongside the undisclosed allocation between core recommendations and frontier products. Successful ad AI can coexist with disappointing shareholder returns if investment stays excessive; conversely, today’s depressed FCF can understate earning power if capacity later produces revenue without comparable incremental spending.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Self-funding judgment:** the original note judged that current OCF did not comfortably cover the spending plan and that its flat-H2 sensitivity settled this test. **Cross-check view:** the flat-half case is valid but insufficient; its FY FCF range is −16.824 to −1.824, whereas the historical seasonal ratio gives +4.664 to +19.664 (claims 13 and 41). Both are scenarios, and neither establishes the eventual 2026 funding outcome. Actual H2 OCF, taxes, legal payments and capex will resolve it. [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8]
- **Commercialization judgment:** the original note treated enterprise/API products as prospective and emphasized assistant usage without paying-demand evidence. **Cross-check view:** the September–October launches and Meta One’s mixed subscription/trial disclosure strengthen the commercial case (claims 39–40), while still leaving paid AI adoption, contribution margins and infrastructure payback unproven. [Enterprise][S34], [Small Business][S35], [Wonder][S36], [Meta One][S37]
- **Overextension judgment:** the original Summary called commitments, researcher compensation and power/equipment competition the clearest areas of overextension. **Cross-check view:** these establish exposure, not demonstrated excess capacity or excessive MSL compensation; utilization, project returns and an MSL cost total are missing. The original concern that flexibility becomes expensive if the industry exits together remains an explicitly hypothetical stress scenario, not an observed condition. [Commitments][S10], [cost drivers][S11]
- **Valuation judgment:** the original note inferred that the multiple required durable cash-flow growth or investment normalization. **Cross-check view:** the 47.7× trailing FCF snapshot does not identify the growth path priced in or prove mispricing without normalized cash flow and a discount-rate/growth model. [Price][S29], [FY2025][S6], [H1 inputs][S8]
- **Margin interpretation:** the original note emphasized the rising investment burden. **Cross-check view:** Q2 legal/severance charges and third-party token costs also matter, while layoffs indicate adjustment capacity; the limited add-back in claim 45 is not normalized profit or evidence of sufficient savings. [Results][S8], [call][S11]
- **Bull case against the caution:** robust ad growth, model-level gains, accessible investment-grade finance, and reusable infrastructure. **Bear case against the ROI story:** weak residual cash, growing commitments, and no disclosed frontier-model return calculation. Resolve with workload-level utilization, investment, and contribution profit.
- Meta’s short lease terms imply flexibility; its guarantees and S&P’s construction-risk analysis limit the economic freedom to walk away. Obtain the full private offering documents and updated guarantee schedules.
- FT syndication confirms the reported price, but the underlying mark, timestamp, liquidity, and spread remain unverified.
- Scale’s disclosed investment value differs from its public deal description. The reported ownership percentage lacks confirmation in the primary pages reviewed. WIRED’s recruitment-package figures are explicitly disputed by Meta.
- **Settlement discrepancy:** the original claim characterized Meta’s approximately 18 and Illinois’s up to 17.1 as “differing scopes.” **Cross-check view:** both attributed amounts are supported, but their reconciliation is unresolved; the scope explanation is not established. The original discussion emphasized cash/expense; the review also flags possible lost engagement and ad inventory. Illinois describes mandatory pauses and conditional changes to time limits, while Meta describes parent-permitted overrides and prompts; their behavioral descriptions also require reconciliation to the executed agreement before modeling exact reach or revenue effects. [Meta][S27], [Illinois AG][S32]
- Current campus commissioning, a complete MSL compensation total, causal attribution of aggregate ad growth, AI-only capex, final El Paso closing, and a quantified next-year capex budget remain unverified or undisclosed.
