# The non-Nvidia accelerator layer: AMD, Broadcom, Marvell, hyperscaler silicon, Intel, Cerebras/Groq

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Cross-checked by: Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [stack-amd-data-center-quarterly.csv](/data/stack-amd-data-center-quarterly.csv), [stack-broadcom-ai-revenue-quarterly.csv](/data/stack-broadcom-ai-revenue-quarterly.csv), [stack-marvell-data-center-quarterly.csv](/data/stack-marvell-data-center-quarterly.csv)*

## Summary

- AMD supplies merchant GPUs and CPUs; Broadcom and Marvell supply custom silicon and connectivity. Broadcom's $16.7B AI semiconductor revenue includes networking; AMD's $6.718B Data Center revenue includes CPUs. These unaudited quarters demonstrate substantial sales, not isolated accelerator economics (claims 1, 11).
- Broadcom generates strong consolidated cash flow: $13.665B, a roughly 46% margin. Its ~$29B lease backstop and conditional notes up to $42B retain customer credit and equipment residual-value risk after chip sales (claims 12, 17–18).
- AMD is profitable and liquid, with $1.558B quarterly FCF and $13.111B liquidity, alongside substantial forward commitments. Customer warrants remain contingent incentives; no shares had vested and no financial-statement impact was recorded through the latest quarter (claims 2–9).
- Direct demand is concentrated. Broadcom has six XPU customers; Anthropic is forecast to be largest in 2027 and OpenAI second in 2028. Only OpenAI's initial 1 GW is explicitly binding in AMD's cited filing. Enterprise demand can still reach suppliers through intermediaries (claims 4, 13).
- Custom silicon may reduce workload-specific lifecycle costs. Modeled TCO savings do not establish universal useful-compute savings or an unchanged revenue hurdle when investment rises (claim 19).
- Marvell is GAAP-profitable; Cerebras reports a GAAP loss despite substantial liquidity. The biggest uncertainty is whether customer cash flows and funding support deployments and obligations. Anthropic's funding and reported adjusted profitability qualify a blanket insolvency narrative without proving future funding sufficiency (claims 21–23, 26–28).

## Analysis

AMD's segment includes CPUs, and Broadcom's AI business includes networking. Their revenue is evidence of a growing supplier layer, but does not isolate lab accelerator demand. Broadcom's six direct XPU buyers concentrate deployment risk without defining its whole addressable market (claims 1, 11, 13). Downstream enterprises can buy through labs and clouds.

Broadcom combines strong current cash generation with contingent credit support. Its 46% FCF margin includes software; consolidated gross margin also reflects mix and memory content, rather than a measured margin for each XPU product. The $179.2B RPO spans software and semiconductors. Its scope and termination provisions prevent treating the entire book as irrevocable lab orders (claims 12, 14–15).

Cash collection on a chip sale does not eliminate financing risk. The ~$29B backstop depends on deployment, repayments and equipment recovery values. The up-to-$42B note facility was undrawn and conditional. These exposures must not be mechanically summed or treated as debt already drawn (claims 17–18). The proposed $60B financing package also has unverified completion and possible overlap (claim 24). Customer payment schedules and financing documents determine how losses reach vendors and financiers.

AMD has positive FCF and substantial liquidity alongside purchase and lease commitments with different payment periods (claims 2–3, 8–10). Comparing their multiyear totals with quarterly FCF would exaggerate immediate cash requirements. Its OpenAI and Meta programmes are contingent; only OpenAI's first 1 GW is explicitly binding in the cited filing (claims 4–5).

The warrants transfer contingent value and can dilute shareholders. The $600 scenario produces $95.9984B intrinsic value per customer, 19.60% share-count expansion and 16.39% ownership dilution before other share changes. These are hypothetical full-exercise outcomes, not current fair value or a guaranteed GPU discount (claim 7). The absence of a recorded impact does not settle future accounting treatment or the income-statement effect of vesting. Cerebras already discloses customer-warrant revenue adjustments, without determining AMD's future treatment (claims 6, 22).

Custom silicon can lower workload-specific lifecycle costs and change who captures margin. SemiAnalysis's chip and chip-hour TCO estimates are not measured useful-token economics (claim 19). A return model also needs asset lives, utilization, throughput, operating costs and financing costs. Rising nominal investment alone establishes neither an unchanged revenue hurdle nor savings against a GPU-only alternative. Google's external system sales show that its role extends beyond rental (claim 29).

Marvell is GAAP-profitable; Cerebras's latest quarter shows losses alongside substantial liquidity. Groq's staff transfer may weaken its competitive position, but its announcement establishes continuing independent operations (claims 21–23, 25).

Anthropic's announced funding and reported adjusted profitability demonstrate capital access and operating improvement, while rigid commitments leave future funding sufficiency unresolved (claims 26–28). Adjusted profit is not cash generation. The central concern remains correlated customer credit, deployment and equipment residual-value risk. The evidence does not quantify a defensible probability of capital impairment or establish a whole-stack profitability ranking.

## Open questions

- Broadcom's semiconductor-margin transcripts conflict. Company IR or call audio is needed to resolve them (claim 15).
- Google's reported internal queues and stated frontier-model priority leave actual capacity allocation unresolved (claim 29).
- What are AMD's realized warrant economics and future accounting effects? Lack of vesting alone does not prove lack of shipment. The unreproduced tertiary near-zero GPU-cost model remains grade D (claim 7).
- Which customer facilities overlap, and when do binding payments fall due? Private prospectuses, financing documents and cash schedules are needed to assess collectability (claims 17–18, 26–28).
- Do deployed systems earn adequate returns after utilization, porting, financing and residual-value costs? Vendor outlooks and non-comparable share estimates do not settle this. Marvell's reported Trainium design loss to Alchip remains unconfirmed (claim 20).
