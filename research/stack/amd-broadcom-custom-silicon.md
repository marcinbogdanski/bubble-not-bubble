# The non-Nvidia accelerator layer: AMD, Broadcom, Marvell, hyperscaler silicon, Intel, Cerebras/Groq

*Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Status: reviewed · Reviewer: Codex (gpt-6-astra) cross-check, applied 2026-10-17*

## Summary

- Non-Nvidia suppliers have substantial, rapidly growing reported revenue. Broadcom reported $16.7bn of AI semiconductor revenue in Q3 FY26 (+221% y/y), including networking, and AMD reported $6.7bn of Data Center revenue in Q2 2026 (+107%), including CPUs. Broadcom's $21.7bn next-quarter guide and ~$115bn/~$230bn FY27/FY28 outlook, and AMD's ~$13bn company Q3 guide, are forward-looking estimates. The historical quarters are unaudited (claims 1–2, 14–16).
- Direct XPU demand is concentrated: Broadcom reports six customers, with Anthropic forecast to be largest in 2027 and OpenAI second-largest in 2028. AMD's OpenAI and Meta programmes are up to 6 GW each, with contingent incentives; only OpenAI's initial 1 GW is explicitly binding in the cited disclosure. This creates customer credit and deployment risk, but does not establish an absence of downstream enterprise demand (claims 6–7, 17, 50–51).
- AMD's customer warrants link purchase milestones and share-price performance. At a hypothetical $600 share price, full vesting and exercise imply ~$96bn intrinsic value per customer; combined exercise would expand the July 29 share count by 19.6%, diluting existing holders' ownership by 16.4%, before other changes. Neither is a present cash rebate. No financial-statement impact had been recorded through Q2; future accounting effects remain open. Nvidia also announced a deployment-linked investment intention in OpenAI, through a different instrument (claims 6–8, 13, 59).
- Custom silicon may reduce workload-specific lifecycle costs. SemiAnalysis's 30–44% TCO estimates and Amazon's and Microsoft's performance-per-dollar claims use different measures and do not establish universal useful-compute savings. Alphabet's current 2026 capex guide is $195–205bn. Continued investment alone establishes neither an unchanged revenue hurdle nor savings against a GPU-only alternative (claims 26–27, 30, 34, 52).
- Current cash generation and future exposure coexist. Broadcom reported $13.665bn consolidated FCF (46% margin), alongside ~$59bn debt, a ~$29bn maximum lease backstop and conditional customer notes up to $42bn. AMD reported $1.558bn FCF and $13.111bn liquidity, alongside $30.3bn purchase commitments, ~$14bn future lease payments and conditional investment commitments. Marvell was GAAP-profitable; Cerebras's Q2 GAAP loss supersedes its 2025 profit as the current snapshot (claims 3, 9, 14, 22, 32, 42–43, 54, 56).
- The key uncertainty is whether concentrated customer deployments and cash flows support the obligations and financing. Broadcom's $179.2bn RPO includes semiconductors and software, not just labs; its call's specified Anthropic/OpenAI amounts total 6.3 GW in 2027, with the larger >15 GW discussion in 2028. Anthropic's announced $65bn funding demonstrates capital access, while recent reporting describes large rigid commitments and Q3 adjusted profitability. The private-company reports remain C, reported, unverified; future funding sufficiency is unresolved (claims 17–18, 44–49).

## Analysis

### What this layer is

Nvidia's merchant GPUs face alternatives from AMD's Instinct GPUs, buyer-designed custom accelerators built with Broadcom or Marvell, hyperscalers' own silicon programmes, and independents including Intel, Cerebras and Groq. Broadcom's AI revenue also includes networking: the complement of its 73% XPU mix is ~27%, covering switches, NICs, optics and other connectivity, not just Ethernet switches (claims 17, 51). Its October OCP announcement illustrates how networking serves several cluster types, so six direct XPU buyers do not define the entire addressable business. Ultimate enterprise demand can reach these vendors through labs and cloud providers.

### AMD: real revenue, contingent equity incentives and balance-sheet commitments

AMD's Data Center revenue rose from $3.240bn in Q2 2025 to $6.718bn in Q2 2026, a calculated 107.35% increase (reported as 107%); current segment operating income was $2.103bn. The prior-year quarter included an $800m MI308 charge and an operating loss (claims 1, 4). EPYC CPUs and Instinct GPUs share this segment, so total Data Center revenue cannot be equated with lab accelerator spending. The October 12 Helion announcement adds CPU-led hyperscale and integrated enterprise CPU/GPU configurations, planned for November; that broadens the product plan without establishing booked enterprise revenue (claim 50).

The large announced programmes are OpenAI (6 GW), Meta (up to 6 GW, with the up-to-$60bn price a C, reported, unverified estimate) and Anthropic (up to 2 GW), alongside Oracle and Microsoft plans (claims 5–7, 10–11, 34). They are multigeneration programmes, not proof that the whole capacity is binding or installed. The OpenAI filing explicitly identifies only the initial 1 GW as binding. Anthropic's first AMD gigawatt begins deployment in 1H 2027; the release does not promise all 2 GW in that year.

OpenAI and Meta each hold warrants for up to 160m shares at $0.01. Delivery is one OpenAI condition; Meta's first-tranche language refers to shipment. Both arrangements also have stock-price/performance and additional exercise conditions (claims 6–8). These are contingent customer incentives, not evidence that either company is AMD's largest customer or that shares already paid for purchases. At a hypothetical $600, full vesting and exercise imply 160m × ($600 − $0.01) = $95.9984bn intrinsic value per customer, not current fair value or a guaranteed GPU discount. Combined exercise would increase the July 29 share count by 19.60%, corresponding to 16.39% dilution of existing holders' ownership, before other share changes (claim 13).

The KuCoin retelling of a SemiAnalysis model implies a $0.75/hour, 30% reduction from $2.50 to $1.75, and a near-zero GPU-capital-cost scenario at higher share prices. The underlying acquisition prices, chip counts, service lives, utilization and actual vesting schedule were not reproduced; retain that scenario only as D/tertiary (claim 13). The warrants transfer contingent value and can dilute shareholders. No financial-statement impact was recorded through Q2 2026, but that establishes neither future accounting treatment nor the income-statement effect of vesting or exercise. It does not explain the CFO's separate non-GAAP EPS-accretion forecast. Cerebras's disclosed customer-warrant revenue adjustments demonstrate that such instruments can affect reported revenue, without determining AMD's future treatment (claims 5, 8, 56). Nvidia's deployment-linked OpenAI investment intention uses a different instrument but shares the supplier-financing feedback mechanism (claim 59).

AMD's $30.3bn purchase commitments include cloud services and licences as well as components; $17.4bn falls in the rest of 2026. The $14.0bn lease sum combines $4.5bn unstarted at quarter-end with $9.5bn signed subsequently, with the latter commencing in 2027–28 over terms up to 16 years. Conditional investment commitments up to $5bn are subsequent events expected through FY2028; lease guarantees add potential exposure up to $4.1bn (claims 9–10). These are different categories and payment periods, not debt already drawn or one year's cash requirement. Against them AMD had $13.111bn cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments and quarterly FCF of $1.558bn, or 13.51% of $11.536bn revenue (claims 3, 54). AMD is profitable and liquid, with substantial forward exposure.

The >60% Data Center CAGR ambition over 3–5 years and ~$100bn milestone within five years are distinct management targets (claim 12). From FY2025's $16.6bn base, exactly 60% for five years gives ~$174.1bn; $100bn in five years implies ~43.2% CAGR. They can coexist as ambitions with different timing, but are not equivalent projections or evidence that lab schedules alone determine all segment growth.

### Broadcom: strong current cash generation with concentrated demand and financing exposure

Broadcom reported $16.7bn AI semiconductor revenue in Q3 FY26 and guides $21.7bn Q4 (claims 14–15). The earlier ~$3.7bn Q4 FY24 estimate is derived from ~$6.5bn Q4 FY25 / 1.74; Q4 FY24 to Q3 FY26 spans seven quarter-to-quarter intervals, not twelve months (claim 19 and the Q4 FY25 release). Q1 $8.4bn + Q2 $10.8bn + Q3 $16.7bn + Q4 guide $21.7bn = $57.6bn FY26, including a forecast quarter. The ~$115bn FY27 and ~$230bn FY28 management outlooks remain B reported forecasts from secondary transcripts, not audited results or primary-audio verification (claim 16; Sources 18–21).

RPO was $179.2bn across semiconductors and infrastructure software, not a disclosed total of lab orders. Approximately $44.8bn is expected within twelve months and $134.4bn later (claim 18). The earlier $73bn AI backlog has a different perimeter, so the comparison does not measure maturity extension. Broadcom's revenue note discusses termination rights and excludes uncommitted software contracts and other categories; consolidated RPO is neither all future revenue nor proof that every lab programme is irrevocable.

Broadcom also supports customer financing. AI XPV launched with Apollo and Blackstone and an initial $35bn Anthropic infrastructure transaction (claim 41). The filing discloses a five-year lease backstop with maximum potential liability ~$29bn upon full deployment; the default calculation uses 85% of outstanding covered obligations less rack-sale value. It separately discloses conditional customer convertible notes up to $42bn, none issued at quarter-end (claims 42–43). These are contingent exposures, not $71bn of debt already drawn, and should not be mechanically added. Collecting cash on a chip sale does not eliminate customer credit or equipment residual-value risk.

Consolidated non-GAAP FCF of $13.665bn, 46.18% of revenue, is strong and includes software. Debt carrying value was $59.4bn (claims 14, 22). Nvidia's comparable Q2 FY27 FCF was larger in dollars, $21.341bn; neither that comparison nor Broadcom's margin establishes a whole-stack profitability ranking (claim 55). Broadcom's consolidated non-GAAP GM moved from 77.9% in Q4 FY25 to 74.99% in Q3 FY26 and ~73% guided Q4, a 4.9 percentage-point change across the endpoints; Q3 GAAP GM was 69.13%. Management attributes the consolidated change to mix and memory content. It is not a measured decline in each XPU product's margin. The ~70% semiconductor comparison belongs to Q2, while the Q3 segment transcripts conflict and need IR/audio confirmation (claim 21).

Direct XPU demand is concentrated in six customers. Anthropic is forecast to be largest in 2027 and OpenAI second-largest in 2028. The call's specified 2027 quantities are 5 GW for Anthropic plus 1.3 GW for OpenAI = 6.3 GW; its incremental 10 GW Anthropic and >5 GW OpenAI discussion belongs to 2028, not 2027 (claim 17). Q3 FY26 Jalapeño chip shipments, a 2H26 rack-deployment start and a 2027 annual capacity target are different milestones. They do not establish the former note's claimed two-quarter delay (claims 17, 23).

September–October evidence makes both contractual rigidity and customer funding more concrete. Reuters's September 29 report describes at least $518bn Anthropic infrastructure commitments, ~80% non-cancelable or payable regardless of utilization, including ~$161.2bn Broadcom-related leases. Its October 1 report identifies Anthropic behind the up-to-$42bn convertible arrangement associated with a $125.2bn five-year TPU lease and describes restrictions and possible acceleration on default. Bloomberg's October 2 proposed $60bn package and October 7 preliminary ~$30bn OpenAI financing discussions remain proposals with completion unverified. These overlapping obligations and facilities must not be summed (claims 44–47; all C, reported, unverified).

Counter-evidence belongs alongside that exposure: Anthropic announced a $65bn funding round, including $15bn previously committed hyperscaler money, demonstrating substantial capital access. Reuters on October 14 reported Q3 revenue $16.8bn versus Q2 $11.6bn and adjusted profitability as described to prospective investors (claims 48–49). The latter is C, reported, unverified, not audited GAAP profit or FCF. It weakens the blanket present-tense “loss-making labs” description without proving that long-term commitments are fully financed.

### Hyperscaler silicon: the capex-per-compute question

The cost case is plausible but the measures differ. SemiAnalysis models Ironwood internal TCO/chip ~44% below a GB200 server and external TCO/hour 30–41% below GB200/GB300; those are not verified rental tariffs or useful-token economics (claim 26). JPMorgan's reported ~$13k Ironwood versus $35–40k Blackwell estimates imply 62.9–67.5% lower chip prices, conditional on those estimates, not equivalent full-system performance or savings (claim 27). Amazon and Microsoft offer performance-per-dollar claims (claims 30, 34); 30% better performance per dollar means ~23.1% lower equal-performance cost, while 40% better means ~28.6%, before workload and utilization differences. The 4× price/cost hypothesis algebraically implies 75% vendor GM, but does not verify every GPU's markup or the buyer's lifecycle cost.

Custom silicon may lower workload-specific lifecycle costs and alter who captures margin. It does not establish a universal one-third reduction in revenue needed to earn a return. Amazon's ~$200bn capex guide and Alphabet's updated $195–205bn guide demonstrate planned investment, not an unchanged aggregate revenue hurdle or a counterfactual of spending without ASICs (claims 30, 52). The former mixed-date big-four total is withdrawn. A return model needs capital cost, asset lives, financing cost, throughput, utilization, operating costs and contribution margins together. Specialized assets can present residual-value risk, but the cited evidence does not establish that external TPUs have no secondary market.

Google's role extends beyond cloud rental: the July official transcript says it first recognized TPU-system sales delivered to customer data centers in Q2 (claim 52). Outright sales therefore do not exclusively run through Broadcom. Anthropic's May announcement confirms 5 GW of next-generation TPU capacity (claim 49). Meta rentals, outright-purchase talks and a leasing JV remain reported, unverified (claim 28). Place the reported DeepMind queue story alongside Pichai's stated priority for frontier-model capacity allocation; the evidence does not resolve actual internal allocation (claims 29, 52).

Amazon reports >1.4m Trainium2 chips and Trainium3 supply nearly all committed by mid-2026, but does not disclose a customer revenue mix in the cited statement (claim 30). The Anthropic deal links conditional investment and cloud purchases: $8bn prior + $5bn now + up to $20bn later = up to $33bn total, not $33bn in addition to $8bn (claim 31). Microsoft's Maia 200 claims and simultaneous AMD Helios plans similarly show multiple procurement routes (claim 34). Funding these programmes is not limited to operating cash: Alphabet's Q2 release records $49.6bn net equity/mandatory-convertible proceeds and $20.3bn net debt issuance (claim 53).

### Marvell, Intel, Cerebras, Groq

Marvell's data center revenue rose 45.69% y/y and 18.49% q/q to $2,171.5m. Quarterly GAAP net income of ~$308m on $2,739.3m revenue implies ~11.24% net margin; “GAAP-thin” needs a comparator and is not equivalent to loss-making (claim 32). Management's custom-growth targets and the reported Trainium move to Alchip make customer design decisions material, but do not establish that Marvell is the most exposed public supplier (claim 33).

Intel's cited accelerator evidence covers Gaudi and Crescent Island plans without disclosed Gaudi revenue; it does not establish competitive market share (claim 37). The US government's $8.9bn investment is described in claim 35. Intel completed Nvidia's $5bn investment on December 26, 2025, issuing 214,776,632 shares at $23.28; closing is no longer an open question (claims 36, 57). The announced CPU collaboration supports Nvidia platforms; its contribution to Intel accelerator competitiveness remains unquantified.

Cerebras's 2025 revenue was $510m, up ~76%; G42 and MBZUAI together supplied 86% **in 2025**. Its $237.8m GAAP profit alongside a $75.7m non-GAAP loss reflects a material fair-value gain on its forward-contract liability, rather than demonstrated recurring operating profitability. Its OpenAI deal includes a customer warrant and a $1bn loan from OpenAI, with conditional interest relief (claim 38). The IPO priced at $185 with a $5.55bn base offering; the unsupported first-day close is removed (claim 39). Newer Q2 results report $180.1m GAAP revenue, $450.5m GAAP loss, −16% non-GAAP core operating margin, $25.4bn RPO and $8.6bn liquidity including restricted cash (claim 56). This strengthens the operating-loss caution while adding substantial funding capacity; the historical UAE mix should not be treated as current concentration.

Groq's primary statement describes a non-exclusive licence and transfer of key staff to Nvidia, while retaining an independent company and uninterrupted GroqCloud operations (claim 58). The ~$20bn price remains reported (claim 39). A weakened independent competitive position is a possible interpretation of the staff transfer, not evidence of market exit or a verified merger-review outcome.

### Who is comfortably profitable, who is betting

Broadcom has strong consolidated cash generation and material contingent financing risk. AMD is profitable and liquid with substantial forward commitments and conditional customer incentives. Marvell's snapshot is GAAP-profitable. Cerebras's latest quarter shows an operating loss and substantial liquidity. Intel's accelerator economics and the hyperscalers' programme-level returns are not separately established by these disclosures. Recent funding and reported adjusted profitability qualify a blanket lab-insolvency narrative. The supported concern is correlated customer credit, deployment and equipment residual-value risk across suppliers and financiers; this note does not quantify a defensible probability of capital impairment.

## Conflicts and open questions

- **Relative-risk judgment — original note versus Codex cross-check.** The original note ranked Broadcom “most comfortably profitable” and conventional/cash-paid, AMD cash-light and equity-financed, and Marvell “thin.” Codex emphasizes Broadcom's contingent financing and residual-value exposure, AMD's $13.111bn liquidity and conditional incentives, and Marvell's positive ~11.24% GAAP net margin (claims 3, 14, 32, 42–43, 54–55). Both support strong Broadcom current cash generation and material AMD commitments; the original whole-stack ranking is not established by comparable cash-flow/financing models.
- **Demand and solvency judgment — both views retained.** The original note inferred a lab bet “not on broad enterprise demand” and described the labs as loss-making with capital needs on an undemonstrated scale. Codex agrees that direct-customer concentration matters, but says enterprise demand can flow through intermediaries and cites Helion/networking breadth, Anthropic's announced funding and reported Q3 adjusted profitability (claims 17, 48–51). Neither direct-customer concentration nor those counterpoints establish the full downstream demand or funding adequacy.
- **Warrant interpretation — both views retained.** The original note called warrants stock-paid volume rebates, treated shareholder dilution as bypassing the income statement and unique relative to Nvidia, and relied on near-zero GPU cost. Codex accepts the contingent intrinsic-value arithmetic but distinguishes 19.6% share-count expansion from 16.4% ownership dilution, rejects the future-accounting inference, grades the unreproduced hourly model D, and notes Nvidia's deployment-linked investment intention (claims 6–8, 13, 59). Future AMD accounting effects and realized customer economics require filings and a reproducible model; vesting is not solely a delivery test.
- **Cost/revenue-hurdle judgment — both views retained.** The original note inferred a roughly one-third reduction in required revenue per compute unit and an unchanged aggregate hurdle because capex rose. Codex finds neither derivation established: modeled chip-hour TCO, chip prices and vendor performance-per-dollar claims have different denominators (claims 26–27, 30, 34). Consolidated mix-driven GM declines do not measure XPU product margins, and rising nominal capex does not quantify savings versus a GPU-only alternative. Workload, utilization and cash-flow modeling must resolve the return question.
- **Share-shift estimates use different denominators.** Morgan Stanley's 13% accelerator sales share, JPMorgan's 53% units, SemiAnalysis's >90% Nvidia share and New Street's 20–30% Nvidia inference scenario cannot be treated as a comparable range (claims 27, 40). Forecasts for different years, units, sales and inference workloads are not independent measurements of the same market. Vendor revenue outlooks do not alone establish future market share.
- **Timing and contract scope — both interpretations recorded.** The original note inferred two quarters of OpenAI–Broadcom slippage, ~15 GW for the labs in 2027 and a lengthening lab-only RPO book. Codex finds these unsupported: Q3 FY26 chip shipments, rack starts and energized capacity are different milestones; the specified 2027 sum is 6.3 GW, with >15 GW discussed for 2028; consolidated RPO and earlier AI backlog have different scope (claims 17–19, 23). AMD's full 12 GW programmes are not demonstrated entirely binding; only OpenAI's initial 1 GW is explicitly binding in the cited filing. Broadcom discloses termination provisions, contrary to the original blanket assertion. Track each programme's delivery, vesting, deployment and cash-payment milestones separately.
- **Broadcom Q3 segment margin conflicts.** Webull renders a correction to 56.7%; Motley Fool renders 67% after initially 76%. These are unresolved D transcript figures pending IR/audio, unlike the reproducible consolidated non-GAAP 74.99% and GAAP 69.13% (claim 21). The ~70% semiconductor figure belongs to Q2.
- **Google capacity allocation and funding.** The reported DeepMind queue story contrasts with Pichai's primary statement that frontier-model allocation has priority (claims 29, 52). The original exclusive-Broadcom-sales and operating-cash-only assertions are contradicted by Google's own external TPU sales and financing disclosures (claims 52–53). The original mixed-date big-four capex range is D and withdrawn pending a consistently defined primary-source reconstruction.
- **Customer financing: rigidity versus ability to pay.** Original analysis focused on cancellation uncertainty and future capital raising. Codex adds primary backstop/note disclosures and C reporting on largely rigid Anthropic commitments, alongside demonstrated funding and reported revenue growth (claims 41–49). Maxima, obligations, backstops and proposed packages may overlap and must not be summed. Obtain the private prospectus, financing documents and cash-payment schedules to assess who ultimately absorbs losses.
- **Independent-vendor judgment and current evidence.** The original note treated Groq as having exited while avoiding merger review; Codex cites its continuing independent company/GroqCloud operations. The staff transfer may still weaken competition, but its effect is unmeasured (claims 39, 58). Cerebras's 2025 UAE concentration is historical; its newer operating loss and liquidity both matter (claims 38, 56). Intel's Nvidia investment closing is primary-confirmed (claims 36, 57).
- **Verification limits and unverified claims.** Meta–Google rental economics, Google's reported up-to-$40bn investment, Marvell's reported Trainium loss, the Groq price and private-lab financials require further confirmation. Broadcom's multiyear outlook remains secondary-transcript evidence; no primary-audio verification is claimed. Bloomberg's October 2/7 figures and proposal status were verified in fetched Bloomberg Law article excerpts after Bloomberg/Yahoo retrieval failed; the complete reports remain unavailable. Broadcom's OCP release was fetched from its HTML page after the PDF fetch failed. The Cerebras S-1/A was verified by the cross-check but could not be re-fetched here (size limit/403); its updated Q2 release was fetched. No new numerical precision from that S-1/A is introduced.
- **What would resolve it.** AMD's next filings can report warrant status and segment results, but lack of vesting alone would not prove lack of shipment. Broadcom's Q4 release can test $21.7bn AI guidance and update RPO and financing exposure. Separate workload economics, verified customer financials, financed-versus-funded commitments and external TPU/Trainium sales are needed to assess returns.
