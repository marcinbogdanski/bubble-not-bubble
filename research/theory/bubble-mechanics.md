# Bubble mechanics, operationalised for the AI boom

*Last updated: 2026-10-21 · Cross-checked by: Claude Fable 5.1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [theory-shiller-cape.csv](/data/theory-shiller-cape.csv), [theory-finra-margin-debt.csv](/data/theory-finra-margin-debt.csv)*

## Summary

- Minsky, Kindleberger, Perez, Dalio and Shiller organise different tests of fragility. Their overlap does not independently confirm a bubble. Perez's accessible commentary places AI within the existing ICT revolution (claim 23).
- September CAPE was 40.58 against a 17.8 mean. Forward P/E of 19.0x, slightly below its ten-year mean, provides counterevidence to a simple overvaluation diagnosis (claims 1–3).
- Investment funding headroom is shrinking: the named hyperscalers spent 86.08% of OCF on cash capex in Q1 and 96.09% in Q2. Capex deficits alone cannot classify debt-service capacity (claim 8).
- Large backlogs expose suppliers to future customer cash generation. CoreWeave's cash test supports refinancing dependence, without establishing present Ponzi finance. Issuer credit stress is explicit, while S&P's opacity warning brought no new rating action (claims 9–13, 25).
- Rates and issuance can expose fragility. Concentrated IPO proceeds, bearish retail sentiment and the Fed's systemic counterevidence prevent a uniform euphoria or imminent-panic diagnosis (claims 5–7, 16–19).
- The 13-indicator composite remains withdrawn despite its arithmetic sum of 20/26. Neither it nor Yardeni's different backtest supplies calibrated crash odds. The biggest uncertainty is whether customer cash arrives before commitments fall due and what assets creditors could recover (claims 24, 26).

## Analysis

[Minsky's debt-service classifications](https://www.levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp74.pdf) concern contractual payments, not net income or discretionary growth investment. Hedge finance covers interest and principal; speculative finance needs principal refinancing; Ponzi finance needs new money even for interest. The hyperscaler funding ratio compresses room for expansion without classifying each borrower. Aggregate cash flows also conceal differences among firms. CoreWeave generated $4.469B before operating interest against $6.201B of principal and total cash interest paid; customer prepayments contributed to operating cash (claim 11). Refinancing dependence is supported. Inability to pay interest from operations is not established. Completed financing and management's demand disclosures qualify a simple collapse narrative; details belong in the [CoreWeave note](/research/stack/coreweave).

[Kindleberger's sequence](https://link.springer.com/book/9783031160073)—displacement, boom, euphoria, profit-taking and panic—supplies hypotheses to test. Rising margin debt and issuance can accompany either productive investment or speculation. Record IPO proceeds were concentrated, and the subsequent quarter weakened (claim 7). New issuance does not count new investors or prove insider profit-taking. Portfolio equity weights can rise through prices alone; the household-allocation derivation remains unverified (claim 20). Bearish survey sentiment and divergent executive views permit several stage interpretations (claims 16–17, 27).

Dalio's sustainability test asks what earnings growth would let equities outperform bonds (claim 28). High CAPE beside an ordinary forward multiple does not perform that calculation. CAPE averages historical real earnings; forward P/E uses forecast earnings. Consensus growth and forward earnings also cover different periods. At fixed prices, lower expected earnings raise P/E, but actual price changes can offset that effect. The Fed's earnings-led-rally assessment offers a competing interpretation (claim 21). Sustainable customer cash generation must distinguish an earnings boom from investment spending that temporarily supports suppliers' profits.

Yield construction also matters. The TIPS proxy is negative, while Shiller's published excess CAPE yield is positive (claims 14–15). These differently constructed and dated measures cannot share an unlabeled threshold. Partial-month inputs and estimated CPI limit precision. None of these comparisons establishes permanently unsustainable growth or a crash date.

Perez separates technological progress from investor returns. Her accessible AI commentary does not diagnose a current frenzy or predict a crash (claim 23). The financing and realised-growth tests needed for that diagnosis remain unestablished. Useful infrastructure may survive losses; utilisation, operating costs and obsolescence determine recoverable value. Physical survival alone does not repay its original investors.

Rates and issuance are possible pressure channels, not an unavoidable bust clock. The Fed minutes record multiple yield drivers and differing views of monetary restraint (claim 21). Broad high-yield spreads and issuer quotes measure different exposures and dates (claims 12–13, 29). Broad spreads cannot show that AI risk is unpriced; issuer stress cannot establish market-wide withdrawal. S&P's warning calls for a map of guarantees and off-balance-sheet claims, while its lack of rating action qualifies an immediate-downgrade inference (claim 25).

Equity ordinarily absorbs losses before creditors. Beyond equity, security, recourse, collateral and guarantees determine recoveries. Asset-price losses can spread through spending and financing, while the Fed's evidence of strong bank capital and moderate aggregate vulnerabilities limits an economy-wide fragility conclusion (claim 19). The decisive tests are contract durability, customer cash conversion, funded maturities and recoverable assets. The withdrawn composite overlaps valuation measures and uses inconsistent, uncalibrated thresholds. Yardeni's separate model and Dalio's timing caveat do not repair it (claims 22, 24, 26).

## Open questions

- Does earnings growth reflect durable end-user cash generation or spending sustained by new financing? Matched earnings periods and cohort cash returns would distinguish the competing valuation readings.
- Can borrowers service maturities without repeated customer advances or new capital? Contract cancellation rights, prepayment durability and cash available for debt service remain central.
- Is participation broadening, or do issuance totals mainly reflect concentrated deals? Direct buyer and positioning data would resolve the conflict with soft survey sentiment. The household-allocation derivation needs its originating table.
- What leverage sits in SPVs and private credit, and which guarantees transfer losses? The opacity warning supplies no hidden-debt total.
- Are falling margin balances forced deleveraging or reporting changes? FINRA warns that reporting methods can affect monthly balances.
- Which excess-yield construction fits the question? Their opposite signs remain a methodological disagreement. No current numerical Dalio aggregate was located; that absence does not settle cash economics or validate a substitute score.
