# Bubble mechanics, operationalised for the AI boom

*Last updated: 2026-11-05 · Cross-checked by: Claude Fable 5.1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [theory-shiller-cape.csv](/data/theory-shiller-cape.csv), [theory-finra-margin-debt.csv](/data/theory-finra-margin-debt.csv)*

## Summary

- Minsky, Kindleberger, Perez, Dalio and Shiller organise different tests of fragility. Their overlap does not independently confirm a bubble. Perez places AI within the existing ICT revolution (claim 23).
- Historical and forward valuation measures still give different readings; their earnings denominators and yield constructions must remain distinct (claims 1–3, 14–15).
- The named hyperscalers' Q3 cash PP&E purchases consumed 115.06% of operating cash flow, leaving a $26.425B gap before lease principal or shareholder distributions. Consolidated growth spending does not by itself identify inability to service debt (claim 8).
- Signed supplier backstops expose who absorbs customer and collateral risk. They support financing today while linking suppliers' balance sheets to customer payments and hardware resale values (claim 20).
- Funding remains selective: completed corporate issues and an equipment refinancing coexist with CoreWeave stress. Meta-linked project outlooks show counterparty transmission; Trepp's sampled loans remain current. These observations support fragility, without establishing a funding freeze or payment crisis (claims 12–13, 25, 30).
- The composite remains withdrawn. Yardeni's orderly-deflation thesis, Roman's financing warning and the bankers' productivity case are competing interpretations, not measured probabilities. The decisive uncertainty is whether customer cash and recoverable assets cover contractual payments when new financing becomes harder (claims 24, 26–27).

## Analysis

[Minsky's debt-service classifications](https://www.levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp74.pdf) concern contractual payments, not net income or discretionary growth investment. Hedge finance covers interest and principal; speculative finance needs principal refinancing; Ponzi finance needs new money even for interest. The hyperscaler aggregate now exceeds operating cash generation, but mixes firms with different cash positions and includes their non-AI businesses (claim 8). It excludes finance-lease principal and new noncash leases, so is neither total infrastructure investment nor a debt-service coverage ratio. CoreWeave's cash test supports refinancing dependence; inability to pay interest from operations remains unestablished (claim 11).

The signed backstops make the transmission mechanism concrete (claim 20). Broadcom can sell hardware while retaining contingent exposure to its buyer's lease payments and the equipment's resale value. Google support can help TeraWulf borrow against Anthropic leases. These arrangements can improve lenders' recovery prospects and near-term funding access. Our inference is that diversification is limited when customer weakness and lower hardware resale values arrive together. The lease, project financing and guarantee describe overlapping claims on the same investment; adding their face amounts would overstate spending. Anthropic's filed payment schedule belongs in the [lab note](/research/stack/anthropic); committed spend is not all due immediately.

Selective funding supplies the strongest counterevidence to an imminent freeze. Microsoft and Oracle completed issuance; IREN refinanced deployed equipment (claim 30). Different credit quality, collateral and terms prevent extrapolating those transactions to every borrower. Undrawn facilities, conditional lease commitments and Meta's forward sale cannot be counted as cash in hand. OpenAI's reported fundraising targets remain unverified and do not establish a priced down round. Financing can sustain productive expansion or postpone recognition of poor returns; cash conversion distinguishes them.

[Kindleberger's sequence](https://link.springer.com/book/9783031160073)—displacement, boom, euphoria, profit-taking and panic—supplies hypotheses to test. Margin debt and concentrated issuance do not count new investors or establish insider profit-taking (claims 4, 7). Soft survey sentiment and opposing financier views permit several stage interpretations (claims 16–17, 27). Yardeni proposes that lender selection restrains overbuilding without a crash; Roman emphasises aggregate financing pressure, while Gray and the bankers emphasise productivity. The relevant test is realised cash returns after financing costs, not the prominence of the speaker.

Dalio's sustainability test asks what earnings growth would let equities outperform bonds (claim 28). High CAPE beside an ordinary forward multiple does not perform that calculation: historical real earnings, forecast earnings and consensus growth cover different periods. The Fed's earnings-led-rally assessment offers a competing reading (claim 21). The negative TIPS proxy and positive published excess CAPE yield also use different constructions and dates (claims 14–15). Damodaran's illustrative valuation adds explicit growth and profitability assumptions, not evidence that its cash flows or assumed IPO proceeds have materialised (claim 27).

Rates remain a pressure channel, but the Fed held and nominal Treasury yields eased (claims 5–6). Credit risk can still spread through contractual dependence: S&P explicitly ties the Meta project outlooks to their counterparty. Trepp's sector-specific repricing accompanies current loans, illustrating that valuation losses precede any demonstrated payment failure (claim 25). Neither an outlook revision nor a secondary-market mark is a distressed credit event.

Perez separates technological progress from investor returns. Useful infrastructure may survive investor losses; utilisation, costs and obsolescence determine recoverable value. Strong bank capital and moderate aggregate vulnerabilities qualify a systemic-crisis conclusion (claim 19). The decisive tests remain contract durability, customer collections, funded maturities and recoveries. The withdrawn composite overlaps valuation measures and has inconsistent, uncalibrated thresholds; none of the attributed views repairs it (claims 22–24, 26–28).

## Open questions

- Does earnings growth reflect durable end-user cash generation or spending sustained by new financing? Matched earnings periods and cohort cash returns would distinguish the valuation readings.
- Can borrowers service maturities without repeated advances or capital raising? Anthropic's payment schedule improves visibility, but customer collections, cancellation remedies and cash available for debt service remain central.
- What would backstopped racks recover in a customer default? Broadcom's stated low probability of payment is management's assessment; correlated demand and collateral stress needs testing.
- Do completed financings show durable market access or dependence on stronger sponsors? Drawdowns, settlement proceeds and covenant terms would distinguish capacity from usable liquidity.
- Will Meta-linked project outlook changes remain rating pressure or lead to losses? Trepp's current loans and completed financings are concrete counterevidence to an immediate payment crisis.
- Is participation broadening beyond concentrated deals? Buyer data, comparable sentiment readings and FINRA reporting consistency matter. No current numerical Dalio aggregate or calibrated composite resolves the competing interpretations.
