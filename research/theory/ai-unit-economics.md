# AI unit economics: can the capex pay back?

*Last updated: 2026-10-25 · Cross-checked by: Claude Fable 5.1, Codex gpt-6-astra · Data: [ai-unit-economics-capex-by-year.csv](/data/ai-unit-economics-capex-by-year.csv), [ai-unit-economics-api-prices.csv](/data/ai-unit-economics-api-prices.csv), [ai-unit-economics-useful-life.csv](/data/ai-unit-economics-useful-life.csv)*

## Summary

- The illustrative $750B new cohort needs $295–468B annual owner revenue at a 50% EBITDA margin and 10% discount rate, depending on equipment life. This establishes a conditional hurdle, not an observed payback gap (claim 3).
- Required-revenue estimates differ in capital cohorts, timing and revenue layer. Gartner’s cumulative owner receipts cannot be compared directly with Patel’s annual lab revenue path (claims 10, 14).
- Profitability varies across the stack. OpenAI’s reported 33% adjusted gross margin and Anthropic’s reported adjusted profit do not establish durable free cash flow; both remain reported, unverified (claims 28–29).
- Customer advances, supplier credit and new securities fund assets and allocate risk. Highly committed labs and leveraged providers are exposed, with losses transmitted according to guarantees and collateral.
- The largest uncertainty is sustained cohort cash recovery: life, utilization, owner margin and customer funding interact. The selected margin sensitivity exceeds the life sensitivity (claim 5).
- API prices move in both directions. Growing usage alone establishes neither paid-revenue growth nor a causal Jevons effect (claims 18–21). Final released-model runs also capture only part of research compute (claim 23).

## Analysis

The capex totals mix company definitions, fiscal calendars, leases and non-AI assets. The current guidance sum and historical spending therefore cannot identify an AI-only cohort. Oracle and CoreWeave add investment on different reporting bases (claims 1–2, 26–27).

The worked model prices annual capital recovery at the infrastructure owner. Its equipment and infrastructure revenue components last for their respective assumed lives. It omits replacement assets, conventional cloud, logistics, internal AI benefits, commissioning delays, taxes, working capital, changing prices/utilization and residual values; land is not separate. A renter’s gross margin already includes rental payments funding the owner’s hardware and cannot substitute for owner EBITDA. The $1.36T extension is another fresh cohort, not a valuation of obligations remaining on previously deployed assets (claim 3).

Sequoia’s flow multiplier, Bain’s sustainable spending ratio and the bank return estimates answer different questions. Gartner accumulates receipts over a spending program. IoT models adequate returns now followed by deterioration as capital accumulates. Brookings assumes mature utilization of completed capacity. Patel’s inference revenue density requires paid demand, capability advantage and deployment to persist as capacity expands. Its lab receipts still need an owner-capture bridge. Normalizing capital makes Brookings much closer to the project scenario, though margin definitions differ (claims 6–15).

Lab receipts overlap cloud suppliers’ revenue. OpenAI’s Google Cloud distribution can draw on existing commitments; it is not independently measured final demand. Meta attributes incremental advertising revenue to AI without isolating its dollar contribution or cohort returns. Cloud profit can exclude shared AI research expenses. These boundaries prevent a sector-wide revenue gap or doubling deadline from being inferred here; the separate [demand-side note](/research/macro/demand-side) holds the end-user scenarios.

Financing changes cash timing and loss allocation. Customer advances can improve reported operating cash while adding future obligations; supplier credit and securities extend funding while adding claims on future receipts. Nvidia is profitable; CoreWeave’s adjusted EBITDA coexists with losses after depreciation and financing. Anthropic’s reported adjusted profitability is meaningful counterevidence to a blanket lab-loss claim, while SpaceX’s positive SI operating result coexists with heavy capex and a group cash deficit. Adjusted results, noncash financing remeasurements and free cash flow remain distinct. Issuer accounts and contractual exposure belong in the [stack notes](/report/stack).

Accounting lives determine earnings timing, not physical cash payback. The scenario’s depreciation sensitivity does not verify Burry’s company-specific allegations. Rental listings establish availability, not achieved utilization or lifetime profit. The single-workload benchmark cannot establish that every older GPU survives only under scarcity. Realized rents, costs and residual values through changing supply conditions would resolve more than accounting schedules alone (claims 4, 16–17, 30).

Fixed-capability price declines can coexist with premium tiers and scheduled price increases. Comparable workload, caching, tier and paid volume are needed to infer pricing power. Aggregate tokens across changing models and hardware cannot identify compute demand or price elasticity (claims 18–21, 25).

Final released-model runs exclude experiments and unreleased models that also involve training. Their small modeled shares do not make training broadly defined a minority of lab compute. Owners capitalize hardware; rented capacity, leases and prepayments have different recognition patterns. API retirement dates do not establish the economic life of research or models. OpenAI’s research-compute accounting still lacks a primary policy disclosure (claims 22–24).

## Open questions

- What cohort utilization, realized rents, operating costs and residual values support the assumed lives and margins?
- Can public lab accounts reconcile adjusted profit, cash burn, compute obligations and research accounting? Differently scoped Anthropic commitment reports remain unreconciled.
- Can the original bank models establish capital vintages and owner capture? Patel’s inference-versus-fleet allocation is also unresolved.
- Epoch’s training-cost abstract and revision note disagree on confidence-interval labeling (claim 22). The historical Gemini Pro price could not be independently recovered.
- Silicon’s dated chart and undated FAQ disagree; complete-month publication and freshness flags remain unavailable (claim 30).
