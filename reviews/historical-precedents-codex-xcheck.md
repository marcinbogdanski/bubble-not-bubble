## Omissions

1. **The last 30 days materially change the policy and credit checklist.** The window is September 17–October 17. The [September FOMC minutes][minutes], released October 7, describe hyperscaler bond spreads having widened earlier in 2026, despite some subsequent narrowing, and Treasury yields rising about 35bp over the intermeeting period. They also report earnings-supported equity gains and declining valuation multiples: relevant counterevidence to a simple euphoria story. The underlying [September 16 rate decision][fomc] is one day outside this window but essential context: +25bp to **3.75–4.00%**. A narrow rating spread and low broad HY OAS do not negate sector-specific repricing or higher all-in financing costs. Grade A for what the Fed reports; attributing yields to one cause would be an inference.

2. **A new historical financing comparison exists.** Van Nieuwerburgh's [September 23 Brookings conference draft][brookings] estimates a **$10.3tn 2025–32 scenario**, averaging **3.63% of US GDP**, against **1.10%** for the selected telecom/fiber period. These are scenario estimates, not committed or realised spending: C for the projected outcome, A for accurately reporting the author's stated assumptions. The draft separates short-lived hardware from buildings and power systems and cautions against already declaring 2008-like systemic risk. This changes the note's binary “fiber or PCs” framing and supplies a properly qualified GDP-normalised comparison; it does not validate the note's 10× assertion.

3. **Disclosed exposure is broader and more current than the note shows.** Amazon's June filing already contains the subsequent $21.3bn OpenAI payment: cumulative cash invested **$50bn**, not merely $28.7bn as an October snapshot. Nvidia also discloses **$36bn** of generally six-year AI-cloud capacity commitments, reducible as others use the capacity. That demand backstop is a distinct channel from its lease guarantees. Neither maximum commitments nor investments should be added indiscriminately to claimed financed GPU revenue. These facts predate the last-30-day window but were available in the cited sources.

4. **External funding is not identical to an operating cash shortfall.** Alphabet H1 operating cash flow **$84.859bn** exceeds its rounded $80.6bn capex by approximately **$4.26bn**; Meta's **$64.09bn** exceeds $50.92bn by **$13.17bn**. Amazon's TTM cash flow **$161.403bn** less net capex $169.007bn equals **−$7.604bn**. These different periods cannot be summed, but they demonstrate why financing issuance alone does not establish dependence on it for that period's capex. Alphabet's Q2 alone does show capex above cash generation. Sources: the three filings above; arithmetic B.

5. **The omitted Meta joint venture has a primary source.** [Meta's October 21, 2025 announcement][hyperion] describes Blue Owl-managed funds owning 80%, Meta 20%, and approximately **$27bn** of development costs for the buildings and durable infrastructure. It also discloses an initial **four-year lease** and a conditional, capped residual-value guarantee covering the first **16 operating years**. Searching one subsequent filing for the project's nickname is insufficient evidence of absence. The arrangement matters directly to ownership and loss allocation; the guarantee's precise exposure still needs contract-level treatment.

6. **The sources do not all endorse the note's bubble mechanism.** Richmond's explanation emphasizes technology and regulatory uncertainty; its discussion of incumbent resilience specifically involves the July 2000 access-pricing ruling. That is a confounder for applying the observed stock-price split to TSMC. Doms also explains that installing spare fiber can be rational because reopening trenches is expensive. Low utilisation is not automatically proof of irrational investment. These source-level disagreements belong in Conflicts, alongside the existing 1929 valuation dispute.

7. **There were credit warnings before the October 2007 equity peak.** The [Fed's 2007 Annual Report][fed2007] describes mortgage-credit repricing, securitisation disruption, leveraged-loan stress and ABCP spread widening during the summer. Comparing Baa with Aaa alone can conceal both groups repricing relative to government debt. The historical finding should be conditional on the instrument and chosen reference peak, not “credit spreads are not a timing tool.” New Century was a prominent failure, not proof that no subprime lender had failed earlier.

8. **Equity finance does not remove all financial-system links.** The [Bank of England's archival research][boe] documents the 1847 financial crisis, interconnected bank/discount-house funding and emergency liquidity operations. This does not establish that railway loans caused it; it does invalidate using the period as an unqualified “no banking crisis” control. Likewise, ending crypto at 2022 while observing Japan through 2024 is an inconsistent horizon for spillovers. The [FDIC inspector general's Signature review][signature] identifies uninsured-deposit concentration and crypto-sector ties in a bank that failed in March 2023; it does not establish that direct crypto lending caused the failure. “No banking loss of note” needs a dated, narrower claim.

9. **Recovery and reuse are different outcomes.** Surviving price indexes change constituents; returning to an old nominal price level does not restore extinct firms' equity or establish a profitable return on the original build. A housing price stabilisation date is not a vacant-stock absorption measure. Bankrupt ownership transfer is neither necessary for every useful asset nor sufficient for profitable operation: ongoing power, maintenance and replacement costs matter. The note has no data showing that every built asset was eventually used, that all ownership changed, or that every second owner profited.

10. **Some leads remain unverified.** A September 21 SB Energy prospectus was located, but the web fetch exceeded its size limit and direct SEC access returned 403; I therefore do not treat the newly reported prepayment/share-purchase details as established findings. Nortel's legacy filing also failed in this review, so its $4.1bn figure is not an independently verified sixteenth table row. Quarterly IPO timing, fiber absorption, the claim that no 2000 customer linked three vendors, and a universal 3–5-year GPU economic life remain unestablished. None is needed to sustain the narrower contingent-credit-risk argument.

## Your assessment

The strongest conclusion is that AI can create durable economic value while particular securities and projects lose money, and that concentrated customer support can transmit those losses through suppliers and financiers. Telecom is a useful mechanism comparison, but this note does not establish a historical frequency, a common absorption clock, or a base-case CLEC-style wipeout for neoclouds. I differ chiefly on timing and certainty: the October information already includes tightening and sector-credit repricing, so the correct description is mixed warning signals, not an exclusively pre-reversal stage. Conversely, external capital raising does not by itself demonstrate that every hyperscaler has exhausted internal funding, and the current Fed account includes strong earnings rather than uniformly expanding multiples. I would assess project cash returns, instrument-specific exposure, refinancing schedules and component-level recovery values before translating these analogies into impairment probabilities.
