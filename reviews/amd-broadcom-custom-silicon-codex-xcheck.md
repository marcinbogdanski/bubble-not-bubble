# Independent cross-check: AMD, Broadcom and custom silicon

*As of and sources accessed: 2026-10-17. Reviewer: Codex. Scope: the 15 load-bearing claim rows below, every derived quantity in the Analysis, and material contradictory/recent evidence. The research note was not edited.*

## Verdict

**Major revision required:** the main revenue figures check out, but the financing and risk conclusions are materially incomplete.
**Largest error:** Broadcom is presented as a conventional cash-paid supplier despite its disclosed ~$29bn lease backstop and conditional customer-note arrangements of up to $42bn.
**Direction:** real growth and concentration risk are supported; the dilution, accounting, backlog scope, deployment timing and blanket profitability assertions need correction.

## Omissions

**Broadcom's financing exposure changes the central conclusion (primary, A).** The June 9 [AI XPV announcement][xpv] names Apollo and Blackstone and a $35bn initial transaction for Anthropic infrastructure. The [10-Q's commitments note][avgoq] discloses a five-year lease backstop with maximum potential liability around $29bn upon full deployment; its default calculation depends on 85% of outstanding covered obligations less asset-sale value. It also describes conditional customer convertible notes up to $42bn, none issued at quarter-end. These are contingent arrangements, not $71bn of debt already drawn, and should not be added mechanically. The cash-generative supplier also absorbs financing and residual-value risk. This is absent despite being in the note's own cited filing.

**Last-30-day financing evidence (2026-09-17 through 2026-10-17).** These reports concern a confidential prospectus or unnamed sources; the underlying private-company documents were not available for primary verification. Grade **C, reported, unverified** throughout:

- **September 29:** Reuters reports at least $518bn of Anthropic infrastructure commitments over a decade, about 80% payable regardless of utilization, including ~$161.2bn of Broadcom-related equipment leases. This supplies concrete contractual rigidity missing from the note's generic cancellation uncertainty. Do not add those leases again to the headline total. [Reuters via Investing.com][anthleases]
- **October 1:** Reuters identifies Anthropic as the customer for up to $42bn of convertible financing associated with a $125.2bn five-year TPU lease. It reports restrictions and possible acceleration on default, and says no notes were expected before the IPO. This identifies the counterparty behind the financing risk; it does not establish cash disbursement. [Reuters via MarketScreener][anthloan]
- **October 2:** Bloomberg reports a proposed $60bn financing package for Anthropic and others; completion is unverified. [Bloomberg report][bloom60]
- **October 7:** Bloomberg reports preliminary discussions of roughly $30bn of financing for OpenAI chips, with no formal process begun. This is a watch item, not an additional signed obligation. Search-indexed original reporting was readable; full-page retrieval returned 429. [Bloomberg via Yahoo][openfin]

**Last-30-day counter-evidence on customer solvency must also appear.** Reuters's October 14 report says Anthropic told prospective investors Q3 revenue was $16.8bn, versus $11.6bn in Q2, and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis. These are **C, reported, unverified**, not audited GAAP profit or free cash flow. Nevertheless, the blanket present-tense “loss-making labs” description needs qualification. Separately, Anthropic's own May 28 announcement says it raised $65bn, including $15bn of previously committed hyperscaler funding. That is primary evidence of demonstrated capital access, without proving future commitments are fully financed. [Reuters investor-day report][anthq3], [Anthropic funding announcement][anthround]

**Last-30-day product evidence weakens the blanket “not broad enterprise demand” framing.** AMD's October 12 [Helion announcement][helion] describes CPU-led hyperscale and integrated enterprise CPU/GPU configurations, planned for November. This is a product plan, not booked enterprise revenue, but it reinforces why total AMD Data Center revenue cannot be treated as a proxy for two labs' accelerator spending. Broadcom's October 8 [OCP release][avgoocp] also covers switches, NICs and optics across AI cluster types; the networking business is broader than six XPU buyers.

**Alphabet's newer primary evidence contradicts two substantive statements.** Its July transcript reports first recognition of TPU-system sales delivered to customer data centers, so outright external sales do not exclusively run through Broadcom. Its capex guidance is now $195–205bn. Pichai's stated priority for frontier-model capacity allocation should be placed alongside the reported DeepMind queue story. The [Q2 earnings release][googq2] also reports $49.6bn net equity/mandatory-convertible proceeds and $20.3bn net debt issuance in the quarter: the statement that hyperscaler programmes are funded from operating cash flow alone is untenable. [Official transcript][googcall]

**Liquidity and profitability comparisons are asymmetric.** AMD's [Q2 release][amdq2] reports $13.111bn cash/short-term investments, which should accompany its future commitments rather than describing it simply as cash-light. Broadcom's consolidated cash generation is strong but not the largest by dollars: Nvidia's comparable quarter reports $21.341bn FCF, versus Broadcom's $13.665bn. Broadcom's FCF margin is higher in that particular comparison; neither statistic establishes “most comfortably profitable in the whole stack.” [Nvidia Q2 FY27 release][nvq2]

**Cerebras has newer public financials.** Its August 12 release reports Q2 GAAP revenue $180.1m, GAAP net loss $450.5m, core operating margin −16%, $25.4bn RPO and $8.6bn cash/restricted cash/short-term investments. This simultaneously strengthens the operating-loss caution and adds substantial funding capacity. Its 2025 UAE concentration must be dated, not presented as the current mix. The new release's revenue reconciliation also demonstrates why customer warrants can affect reported revenue; it is not proof of AMD's precise future treatment. [Cerebras Q2 results][cbrsq2]

**Two additional primary corrections:** Intel's Nvidia investment closed on December 26, 2025; that is no longer an open question. Groq's December 24 statement explicitly retains an independent company and uninterrupted GroqCloud operations. The note may argue its competitive position weakened after the talent transfer, but cannot state that it exited the market or definitively avoided regulatory review. [Intel 8-K][intelclose], [Groq announcement][groq]

## Summary-bullet assessment

1. **Scale: supported figures, unsupported epistemic label.** Claims 1, 2, 14–16 support rapid growth and management outlooks. Quarterly statements are unaudited; the forward quarters/years are projections even when furnished with an 8-K. “Marginal” lacks a market-share denominator and is not established by those absolute revenue numbers.
2. **Concentration: partly supported.** Six XPU buyers and the large AMD programmes support concentration concerns. OpenAI's forecast second-largest position is in 2028. Neither total AMD DC revenue nor all Broadcom revenue is a pure lab bet; enterprise demand can also reach silicon vendors through labs/cloud providers. “Not on broad enterprise demand” is a false inference from direct-customer concentration.
3. **Circularity: warrant facts supported, comparison and economics overstated.** The conditional ~$96bn calculation works. It is not a realised purchase rebate or present fair value, and the hourly-cost claim is tertiary. “In a way Nvidia's own deals are not” is too sweeping: Nvidia itself announced a deployment-linked investment intention in OpenAI. The instruments differ; the financing feedback mechanism is not unique. [Nvidia's announcement][nvopenai]
4. **Costs: plausible directional case, conclusion not established.** The table contains vendor claims and modeled chip-hour costs, not comparable realized useful-compute economics. An unchanged aggregate revenue hurdle does not follow from rising nominal capex. Capital cost, throughput, utilization and contribution margin must be modeled jointly.
5. **Profitability: mostly supported snapshots, misleading ranking/risk balance.** Broadcom's FCF and AMD's commitments are real. The superlative, absence of Broadcom financing guarantees, missing AMD liquidity, unexplained “thin” label and stale Cerebras snapshot prevent the comparative conclusion from being accepted as written.
6. **Honouring contracts: valid question, unsupported quantities.** Consolidated RPO is not lab-only backlog; a 12 GW multi-generation AMD programme is not demonstrated to be entirely binding. The ~15 GW/2027 sentence confuses dates. Capital access has already been demonstrated at substantial scale, although future funding sufficiency remains uncertain. This bullet should be rewritten, not merely downgraded.

## Your assessment

This layer has genuine commercial scale, expanding alternatives to Nvidia and credible opportunities to reduce selected workload costs, but revenue growth is not evidence that all installed capacity will earn an adequate return. I differ most from the note's relative-risk ranking: Broadcom combines exceptional current cash generation with meaningful contingent financing exposure, while AMD's stock incentives are conditional and its balance-sheet liquidity is stronger than the narrative suggests. Concentration in frontier labs is a real vulnerability, yet it does not disprove downstream enterprise demand; recent funding and revenue evidence belongs alongside the obligations. The strongest bubble concern is correlated customer credit, deployment and asset-residual-value risk across suppliers and financiers. The note does not establish a universal one-third reduction in required revenue, an unchanged aggregate revenue hurdle, or a defensible probability of capital impairment without a workload, cash-flow and financing model.
