Supplementary table-only arithmetic also reproduces: Groq $13bn + $4bn = $17bn; Amazon–Anthropic $8bn + $5bn + $5bn = $18bn and $18bn + $15bn = $33bn; Amazon–OpenAI $28.7bn + $21.3bn = $50bn; OpenAI–CoreWeave $11.9bn + $4bn + $6.5bn = $22.4bn; IREN 30m × $70 = $2.1bn; North Star $42bn + $18bn = $60bn. This supplementary arithmetic check is not additional source re-verification of rows 9 or 33.

**Interpretive correction:** row 38’s C grade is appropriate for scenarios, while mechanical arithmetic alone is B. Supplier equity can unlock additional debt and other equity; money can also be spent outside the supplier’s products. Accordingly, these percentages are neither identified causal contributions nor statistical bounds. The note acknowledges important exclusions, but should explicitly prohibit using these small percentages to dismiss financing dependence. Historical total contracts divided by current remaining obligations also mix different measurement dates and scopes; the note’s caveats are necessary, not optional fine print.

## Omissions

1. **A measured commercial link is available.** Microsoft’s FY2026 10-K discloses **$24.1bn revenue from OpenAI, including revenue sharing, and $6.0bn receivables** at June 30. These are A-grade observations, though not a complete cross-stack matrix and not a measure of revenue caused by Microsoft’s investment. Omitting them makes the discussion of unknown related-party revenues too sweeping. [Microsoft 10-K][MOWN]

2. **Financing is being organized at a much larger scale than the equity basket.** NVIDIA announced financing-platform MOUs on **August 10, 2026**, targeting **more than $500bn** of third-party capital. This is an announced ambition, not committed or deployed financing. The July 26 10-Q separately reports $99bn of equity investments and $25bn of equity commitments; portfolio values must not be relabeled cash invested. These disclosures strengthen the case for monitoring financing access, without furnishing an attributable-sales numerator. [Platform announcement][NPLATFORM]; [NVIDIA 10-Q][NQ]

3. **Material last-30-days evidence: October 1 Anthropic financing report.** Reuters identifies the otherwise unnamed borrower and reports **$125.2bn of five-year TPU leases**, restricted collateral, possible acceleration of lease payments after defaults, and circumstances limiting facility access. This changes the liquidity-risk analysis: the financing may be least accessible when urgently needed. Grade C, **reported, unverified**; no public prospectus was fetched. The note is right not to assert primary confirmation, but should include this evidence and the access limitation. [Reuters][R42]

4. **Recent developments require separate status labels.** October 1 reporting says NVIDIA completed its $30bn OpenAI investment; primary confirmation was not found. Grade C, reported, unverified. [The Information, indexed excerpt; full text gated][NPAID] October 8: OpenAI run rate reportedly ~$50bn versus ~$70bn grossed-up, an accounting distinction (C; reported, unverified). [Axios][ARR] These are useful updates to funding status and the gross-accounting discussion, not grounds to fabricate new A-grade rows. North Star is already included; it is not an omission. Its yield is a proposed price for a particular subordinated tranche, not an observed borrowing rate for every AI lab.

5. **Profitability and financing need coexist.** Oracle’s September 10 results disclose **negative $5bn Q1 free cash flow** and completion of a **$20bn equity sale**, alongside the operating profit cited by the note. That strengthens the distinction between earnings and funding needs. Conversely, Microsoft says nearly **90% of FY2026 Microsoft Cloud revenue** came from outside frontier-model companies, and RPO excluding OpenAI grew **25%**. This is important counterevidence to a two-lab explanation of the whole cloud business; it is not a clean measure of AI-only final demand. [Oracle results][ORESULT]; [Microsoft Q4 call][MCALL]

6. **The accounting example can be more informative.** Cerebras discloses **$74.4m H1 revenue under its OpenAI arrangement**, net of **$3.3m** OpenAI-specific warrant amortization, and **$86.3m non-cash loan credits**. Those are distinct accounting quantities; credits cannot simply be added to revenue or equated to cash receipts. CoreWeave’s prospectus also explicitly offsets revenue for the stock issued to OpenAI. These facts make the accounting discussion concrete without alleging fictitious revenue. [Cerebras 10-Q][CBQ]; [CoreWeave prospectus][CS1]

7. **Risk allocation needs two qualifications.** PORTS exposure can terminate before twenty years and grows only as phases enter service; the cap is not expected loss. Also, the statement that OpenAI/Anthropic “concentrate final demand” misclassifies intermediaries. They concentrate upstream compute purchasing and financing exposure; end users generate final demand. This correction matters when interpreting the dependency graph. [NVIDIA guarantee][NGUAR]

The recent-event search also surfaced October 7 reports of preliminary Broadcom/OpenAI financing talks. The full article could not be fetched and no executed primary agreement was found, so no proposed amount is incorporated into the re-verification or calculations. [Indexed Bloomberg/Yahoo report][BTALKS] This review does not claim an exhaustive inventory of private side letters.

## Summary-bullet assessment

All six original bullets lack both a dated observation and a numerical anchor. The conceptual caution is generally sound, but the Summary does not let a technical reader judge scale or urgency without reading the table. Concrete replacements appear in Recommended edits, item 1.

| 2 — NVIDIA guarantee confirmed; Broadcom facilities distinct; North Star unverified | **Yes.** | Correctly distinguishes direct filings from reporting. Add scale and dates. Do not imply the $29bn backstop and $42bn notes can be added as independent loss exposure. |
| 3 — Amazon completion and lower Microsoft ownership | **Yes for the disclosed updates.** | “Headline figures are stale” has no identified baseline. Name the figures. | 4 — profitable semiconductor suppliers, loss-making CoreWeave | **Supported narrowly.** | Current results support the distinction. NVIDIA OCF alone is not accounting profit; its results release separately establishes profitability. CoreWeave’s corporate loss does not establish negative lifetime returns for every project. Oracle’s funding needs should not disappear behind its operating profit. |
| 5 — repeated supply-chain revenue is normal; do not sum it as final demand | **Supported as an accounting principle, not a blanket accounting audit.** | Customer incentives may reduce recognized revenue. The graph and filings support careful separation; they do not prove every company is correctly applying gross-versus-net accounting. |
| 6 — unknown counterfactual spending and contract overlap | **Supported as an identification limitation.** | The note has no causal design or comprehensive reconciliation. That does not mean all commercially linked revenue is undisclosed. State which exact scenario cannot answer the question. |

## Your assessment

This is a substantially accurate map of financing instruments, but it is less successful at measuring their economic importance. I agree that supplier financing, ordinary intermediate sales and fraud are different propositions, and that current supplier earnings do not validate customer returns. I place more weight than the note on financing-enabled expansion and liquidity conditions: the small equity-basket percentages say little about the sales supported by guarantees, debt capacity or refinancing. I also place more weight on available counterevidence—actual revenue links, substantial cloud demand outside frontier labs, and guarantee remedies—than a narrative centered solely on two labs would suggest. The evidence supports a concentrated financing and execution risk, not a quantified conclusion that demand is fictitious, that the whole stack is insolvent, or that a particular fraction of revenue would vanish without vendor support.
