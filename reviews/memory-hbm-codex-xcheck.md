## Omissions

1. **September 30 contract protection is stronger—and less uniform—than the synthesis conveys.** Micron reports $32bn of customer financial commitments, mostly deposits, and expects floor-priced SCA margins above previous cycle peaks. Commitments are not all cash already collected. Its 10-K also explicitly warns that contractual purchases can build customer inventory and that breached agreements can expose industry overcapacity. These disclosures support both a better-protected supplier and a potentially delayed inventory cycle. Neither should be omitted. [Micron deck][MD], slide 11; [10-K][MK], Strategic Customer Agreements and customer-risk discussion.

2. **September 30 inventory explanation is already available.** Management attributes the increase in DIO to node-end-of-life build-ahead and incentive compensation capitalized in inventory, and expects DIO to decline. The signal table says rising inventory “merits explanation” without supplying that explanation. It is reasonable to monitor whether management's forecast comes true; it is not reasonable to imply that no explanation was disclosed. [Prepared remarks][MR], p.8. **A for management's explanation, not independent proof of its completeness.**

3. **Consumer demand destruction and price-led growth are observable, not just hypothetical.** Micron's September 30 deck allows for double-digit PC/mobile unit declines and trims expected server memory-content growth. Samsung's Q2 slides show DX losing KRW0.8tn and identify component inflation as a pressure. This is evidence that supplier pricing power can coexist with weaker downstream economics, even within one conglomerate. It does not establish an AI spending collapse. [Micron deck][MD], slides 12/14; [Samsung slides][S2], p.6.

4. **October 14 equipment bottlenecks weaken a simple near-term glut calendar.** ASML's official video transcript says planned 30% capacity growth in 2027 and an aspiration for another 30% in 2028 would still not meet customer demand. Installed-base upgrades can also increase customer output. Consequently, fab dates alone can overstate or understate supply growth. Its official prepared remarks directly verify the note's 75% memory-system-sales growth forecast, allowing claim 34 to be upgraded to A as a management forecast. [ASML video transcript][AV], pp.3–4; [official remarks][AR], p.2.

5. **October 16 architecture developments complicate a fixed HBM-per-accelerator demand model.** SK hynix describes HBF, rack-level memory pooling and KV-cache reuse at OCP. These are demonstrations/internal simulations, not quantified commercial displacement. They nevertheless identify mechanisms through which AI workloads can redistribute demand across HBM, conventional DRAM and NAND. The implication is a mix/efficiency sensitivity, not evidence that HBM demand is already falling. [SK hynix OCP report][OCP].

6. **Older but material: Samsung's contract program is more concrete than claim 26 suggests.** Its July Q&A describes five finalized major data-center accounts, five further negotiations, five-year rolling terms and required advances. It envisages 60–70% of planned DRAM/NAND capacity under multi-year agreements after pending deals close. This is not current achieved coverage or a disclosed price floor. It broadens the risk-transfer comparison beyond Micron. [Samsung Q2 management transcript][ST]. **B; hosted speech, not independently inspected contracts.**

7. **The investment conclusion lacks a valuation and impairment bridge.** The note supplies no dated share price, enterprise value, normalized earnings/ROIC case, or project cash-flow stress test. Therefore “main vulnerability” is a qualitative ranking, not a demonstrated probability of equity drawdown or debt loss. This is an analytical omission, not a claim that stocks are expensive. A price decline in a consumer module also cannot by itself resolve impairment of contracted HBM capacity.

The search covered company releases/IR materials and recent industry research, with social and press reports treated as leads. I did not establish a newer actual Korean memory-segment result by the cutoff, or primary confirmation of recent reported Samsung 2027 blanket sellout/price-tripling stories. Those are not adopted as facts. The omissions above change the balance and specificity of the argument; they do not overturn the verified current-profitability conclusion.

## Your assessment

Memory producers currently have unusually strong pricing power and financial resources, and the note correctly distinguishes HBM from broader memory earnings, contracts from realized end demand, and construction from qualified supply. My difference is in the weighting: the retrieved evidence supports near-term scarcity more strongly than near-term overbuild, while contractual floors and staged equipment installation deserve more weight as defenses against a repeat of the old cycle. At the same time, customer inventory accumulation, consumer affordability and architectural substitution offer more concrete routes to a later reversal than the note's generic “customer retrenchment.” I would retain its caution about returns after normalization, but would not present it as a quantified bubble verdict or assume that the historical margin trough applies uniformly to the newly contracted revenue base. Equity valuation remains a separate unanswered question.
