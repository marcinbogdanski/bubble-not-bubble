## Omissions

1. **JPM's forward P/E split and 30-year baseline, in the deck the note already cites (data 2026-09-30).** The top ten trade at 20.9x forward, 100% of their own average of 20.8x. The other 490 trade at 17.9x, 112% of their 16.0x average. The index is 19.0x against a 30-year average of 17.2x (+1 SD = 20.5x). This cuts both ways. Bull: the leaders' multiples are not stretched against their own history, and the forward split implies ~36.9% of forward earnings for 40.6% of cap. Bear: on a 30-year baseline 19.0x is about +0.9 SD, which the note's 5-/10-year FactSet averages (19.8x/19.1x) hide. Both vendors accepted the shorter averages without comment. The implication is that the concentration risk is an earnings/margin risk, which matters given Micron's 86.8% gross margin. A for the published figures, B for the implied share.
2. **CoreWeave secondary bond prices and project-bond marks, 15 October, already logged in signals/2026-10-17.md rows 1–2.** CNBC gives the 9.75% 2031 notes a yield of 15.78%. WSJ has the 9.625% 2032 notes recovering from 76 to almost 81 cents (14.6%). FT/Folha has Hyperion/Beignet project bonds just under 92 cents and CoreWeave below 80. The note's credit perimeter (claims 37/48) is only broad OAS plus one CDS print. Traded bond prices are stronger evidence of selective distress than an intraday CDS high, and they show stress has reached investment-grade project paper, not only the neocloud equity. These come from the project's own log, graded C to B there.
3. **CoreWeave CEO and co-founder sales (16 October).** Reuters reports Intrator and affiliates sold nearly $1bn (more than 10m shares) since the IPO, mostly under preset plans. MT Newswires relays Bloomberg's $3.3bn co-founder tally (signals/2026-10-17.md row 7). The note's CoreWeave insider sample is one CFO sale of $1.81m (claim 31), which understates insider supply at the most stressed issuer. B as a filing-based report.
4. **Margin debt is a record, and up 31.4% year on year (FINRA workbook, 1997–2026).** The note says only "below June". theory.md's "record $1.50T in June" is correct but sourced nowhere in the note. A balances, B arithmetic.
5. **The week's moves look like credit, not rates.** From 10-09 to 10-15 the 10-year fell 14bp (5.24% to 5.10%) while HY OAS widened 12bp (3.11% to 3.23%). The CPI release on 10-14 coincided with a lower 10-year (5.21% to 5.19%). The note's inference that September CPI "supplies a contemporaneous discount-rate/cost risk" (Analysis, claim 46 paragraph) is not supported by Treasury moves that week. M1b falling 16.5% against M1a's 10.5% fits a financing/credit channel better. Still descriptive, not causal. A data, inference mine.
6. **Fresher supplier and earnings data.** Micron guides FQ1 FY2027 revenue to $61.5bn ± $1.5bn at ~85.95% gross margin, up from $54.23bn. FactSet's 10-16 Q3 blended growth is 29.0% (9% reported, 84% beating), fresher than Citadel's 10-12 estimate. A.
7. **The basket's own drawdown.** The register's M1 basket is already −23.8% from its 2026 peak and M1b −43.4%. Saying so in the note or register lets readers see that the M1 trigger means roughly −54% from peak, which explains why M1b's probabilities sit far above M1a's. B arithmetic.

## Your assessment

The supplier tier is comfortably profitable now. Micron's $33.2bn quarterly adjusted FCF, guidance pointing higher, and TSMC's 51% net margin are cash, not marks. The S&P's concentration rests on earnings rather than unusual leader multiples: JPM puts the top ten at their own historical average P/E. What is overextended is the financing-dependent purchaser tier and the equity priced off it. CoreWeave trades at 15–16% bond yields with large insider supply. Oracle's equity is down 48% from its 2026 high. M1b, the three financing-sensitive names, is already −43% from its June peak. Leverage sits on top: record margin debt, +31% YoY, and $34bn in TQQQ alone. Losses land first on the equity and unsecured/convertible holders of CoreWeave-type issuers and on margin-financed retail holders of the momentum names. Then come project-bond holders, whose paper is already at 80–92 cents. Mega-cap IG holders come last, and their losses would be mark-to-market rather than realised. I differ from the note in three places. (1) The market-wide risk is margin normalisation feeding forward earnings, at a 19x multiple that is about 0.9 SD rich on 30 years. It is not leader-multiple compression, so claim 39's earnings leg matters more than its price leg, and its 36.1% input is near the JPM-implied 36.9%. (2) This week's evidence points to a credit/financing channel rather than a CPI rates shock. (3) The credit perimeter should include traded bond prices, which make "selective distress" an observed fact rather than an inference from one CDS print.
