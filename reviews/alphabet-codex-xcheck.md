## Verdict

The 15 selected load-bearing claims are corroborated; the calculations hold, apart from an immaterial rounding ambiguity in claim 8.
The note's central distinction—financeable investment versus unproven investment returns—is supported, but its funding and demand evidence is incomplete.
Revise before publication: quantify the bridge lease and depreciation pipeline, add paid-demand and October evidence, and replace the abstract final Summary bullet.

## Omissions

**Recent developments, September 17–October 17, 2026.** The following change the balance or specificity of the argument, without establishing a new consolidated financial result:

- **October 6 Finnish construction intervention — execution risk, primary A.** LVV's own release calls on Tuike Finland to suspend environmentally significant preparatory work at Muhos and Kajaani immediately, no later than October 23, until environmental assessment is complete. It seeks voluntary correction first and reserves coercive enforcement; calling this already a final binding shutdown would overstate it. These locations are part of Google's September 9 announcement of at least **€13bn for 2027–2028**, not $13bn of incremental 2026 capex. This is concrete evidence that cash commitments do not ensure timely usable capacity. The release does not quantify delay, loss or effect on group guidance. [LVV release, Oct 6][LVV]; [Google Finland investment, Sept 9][Finland].
- **October 8 enterprise adoption and distribution — positive counterevidence, primary A as company claims.** Google says nearly **80% of Cloud customers use its AI products**, and announces a universal Gemini agent with third-party model choice, routing and project spending caps. This provides a more current enterprise distribution story than consumer MAU alone. It is not an AI revenue-share figure, a disclosed paid-seat count, or proof of profitable general deployment. Cost controls also make it less defensible to extrapolate revenue proportionally from tokens. [Google Cloud CEO keynote, Oct 8][Work].
- **October 13 Search implementation — already mentioned, but too vague.** The new filing is a **plaintiffs' proposed syndication template**, with Google's objections and financial terms still partly redacted; it is not a new final court order or a disclosed profit impact. That procedural distinction would make the existing regulatory paragraph more informative. [DOJ filing, Oct 13][Syndication].

**Older disclosures with material analytical consequences:**

- **Bridge lease, compensation cash and working capital.** The 5.8 short-term lease beginning Q3 is absent from the note. It belongs beside management's warning about costly temporary capacity, although the filing does not identify its counterparty or prove it is the specific deal discussed in Q&A. H1 net stock-award payments are **12.056**, classified as financing and therefore outside reported FCF; H1 FCF less those payments is **−7.795**, before dividends. This makes the ATM discussion more economically useful. Conversely, Q2 inventory absorbed **6.696** of operating cash, with management explaining the TPU inventory build. That helps explain the cash/margin divergence and means one negative FCF quarter alone is not proof of deteriorating unit economics. Do not add the inventory cash use to the existing FCF deficit again. [June 10-Q][10Q], Note 4; [Q2 cash-flow statement][Q2]; [Q2 call][Q2call].
- **Depreciation pipeline.** Assets not yet in service grew from **78.592 at December 31 to 122.814 at June 30**. These have not begun the depreciation that starts when ready for use. The note discusses six-year lives but omits this large lag between cash deployment and expense. It cuts both ways: more future depreciation, but also more capacity still to enter revenue-producing service. This is a mixed asset category; dividing the whole balance by six would be wrong. [June 10-Q][10Q], Note 7; [10-K][10K], property-and-equipment policy.
- **Paid demand and backlog composition.** In July, management explicitly described paid-token growth, with nearly **500 customers each above 1tn tokens over the past year**, and more than **2,000 enterprises above 100bn**. These groups overlap and must not be added. The CFO says the vast majority of backlog is GCP agreements. These disclosures strengthen the case against wholly speculative demand, though they do not disclose realized revenue per token, customer self-funding, concentration, or margins. The Q1 short-duration reporting change was only **7.3** of **467.6** total RPO; it is a comparability adjustment, not an explanation for most of the backlog. [CEO Q2 remarks][CEOQ2]; [Q2 call][Q2call]; [March 10-Q][Q1Q].
- **Actual external-capital mechanism and updated silicon.** Blackstone's May 18 announcement commits **5bn from its funds** to a Google TPU cloud venture targeting **500MW in 2027**; Google supplies hardware, software and services. This is a concrete financing route missing from the generic discussion of selling systems externally. Neither release establishes that Google bears no residual risk. Also, April 22's **TPU 8t/8i** announcement makes an Ironwood-only technical discussion stale: vendor claims are up to **2.7× training performance/dollar** and **80% better inference performance/dollar** versus Ironwood. These are not audited cost savings or proof that deployed older chips are obsolete. [Blackstone announcement][Blackstone]; [Google confirmation][GoogleBX]; [TPU architecture announcement][TPU8].

The latest-period search found both positive adoption evidence and negative delivery evidence. I did not establish a post-June ATM balance, revised group capex guidance after July, a reconciled counterparty concentration figure, or product-level AI profitability. The verified October 28 event remains prospective. Those are unresolved questions, not evidence that no subsequent transactions occurred.

## Your assessment

Alphabet's disclosed operating performance makes a thesis of entirely fictitious AI demand untenable, while its financing transition makes a thesis of effortlessly self-funded growth equally unpersuasive. I agree that disappointing returns would initially fall mainly on equity through cash compression, dilution and eventual asset losses, rather than imply an imminent parent-level refinancing crisis. I differ in emphasis: the note understates both the positive evidence of paid enterprise usage and the negative timing risks from temporary capacity, assets awaiting service and permitting. The Blackstone venture also makes external financing of Google's silicon ecosystem a present mechanism rather than a hypothetical one. My conclusion is therefore stronger about the reality of demand, more conditional about near-term cash coverage, and unchanged about the lack of proof that the expanding capital base will earn an adequate return. The trailing FCF multiple is a useful warning signal, but does not by itself settle whether the equity is mispriced. [Q2 results][Q2], [Q2 call][Q2call], [paid usage][CEOQ2], [Blackstone][Blackstone], [LVV][LVV].
