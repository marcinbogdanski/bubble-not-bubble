## Verdict

The figures are accurate. All 12 re-verified rows match their primary sources, every derived figure recomputes, and the CSV agrees with FRED to the second decimal.
Shared blind spot: neither Codex pass looks at the direction in which rates and oil moved, only their levels. In the week of 9-16 October the 10-year fell 15bp, real yields fell, hike odds fell and physical Brent fell, while the AI basket dropped 12.7%. So the theory page's "not all of that repricing is AI-specific" reading of that week is not supported.
Other gaps: the "highest since 2002" claim was removed although FRED supports it; the minutes' AI-specific credit findings and real-rate attribution are left out; a September staff PCE estimate (3.8%) is circulating that BEA has since superseded (3.4%); and the "direct sovereign lab floor" leaves out MGX's reported OpenAI participation.

## Omissions

1. **Direction of the macro variables during the 10-17 October repricing (most important; affects report/theory.md).** Close-to-close, 9 to 16 October (data/ai-basket-performance.csv; FRED; Treasury par table):
   - **Equities:** equal-weight M1 basket −12.7%; CRWV −25.2%, VRT −15.0%, MU −14.6%, AMD −13.0%, ORCL −9.3%, NVDA −6.6%; SOX −11.4%; NDX −5.5%.
   - **Rates and oil (FRED through Oct 15 unless noted):** 10-year −15bp (5.24→5.09); 30-year −17bp (5.60→5.43); real 10-year (DFII10) −13bp (2.91→2.78); 2-year −3bp (4.80→4.77); physical Brent −2.7% (130.03→126.55, through Oct 14); CME October hike odds fell from about 33% (Reuters, Oct 14, per signals) to 15.5% (Oct 17).
   - **Credit:** ICE HY OAS +12bp (3.11→3.23). Goldman, via a TipRanks wrap (secondary, C), reports AI-related HY spreads +25bp to 360bp on Oct 13-14 against **+2bp for non-AI HY**. Friday Oct 16: Dow −0.34%, S&P −1.09%, NDX −2.50%.
   - **Reading:** every discount-rate and oil variable moved the way that should have *helped* long-duration equities. Falling yields alongside a tech sell-off look like a flight to quality. The note has no market-reaction section, and the theory page relies on the note for this inference.
2. **The cycle high is verifiable.** 5.31% on Oct 5 is the highest DGS10 close since 5.32% on 14 May 2002, and above the 2007 peak of 5.26% (FRED CSV scan; B for the comparison). The bubble-mechanics note and its Codex review already record it. Signals P11 flagged it "not verified" only because Treasury's table was used. The note itself cites only 5.10%. The 30-year reached 5.67% on Oct 7.
3. **Real yields.** The minutes attribute "most of the net increase in longer-maturity Treasury yields" to real rates. DFII10 was 2.95% on Oct 5. For equity duration, the real yield is the right discount-rate variable, and the note never cites it.
4. **The minutes' AI-specific financial findings.** Hyperscaler AI debt spreads "remained wide", "heavy private debt issuance" to fund AI infrastructure, and some participants warning that AI demand could "outpace aggregate supply". These matter more to the bubble question than the generic "supportive conditions" line the note leads with. The Codex correction made the note lean toward benign conditions, and these quotes balance it.
5. **PCE vintage conflict.** The minutes' staff estimate of 3.8% headline / 3.4% core (old basis) is superseded by BEA's 3.4% / 3.0%. reviews/2026-10-18-claude-opus-review.md quotes "August PCE inflation of 3.8%". The note should record the vintage gap so the 3.8% does not propagate.
6. **EIA's forecast is stale.** It was completed Oct 1. Brent jumped to $135.51 on Oct 2 and has averaged $126.98 in October to date. Reaching the $105 Q4 average would need roughly $101 for the rest of the quarter (B: about 64 trading days in Q4, 10 observed at $126.98).
7. **Electricity CPI +4.4% y/y.** This partly offsets the note's use of commercial electric power PPI (−0.6% m/m) as a counterexample to energy pass-through.
8. **MGX in OpenAI's rounds (reported).** Secondary reports say MGX participated in OpenAI's $122bn March 2026 round ([MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=34969)) and in the October 2024 round ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/ai/2024/10/uaes-mgx-part-of-openais-6-6bn-fund-raising/)); amounts are undisclosed (C, reported, unverified). Presenting HUMAIN's $3bn as "the selected historical sovereign lab investment" therefore understates sovereign-linked lab exposure in kind. The October UAE talks are still unclosed per the latest reports ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/10/06/openai-in-talks-for-us30-billion-round-with-uae-funds-blackrock/)).
9. **What China's registration covers.** It applies to outbound transfer and licensing of Chinese AI technology and to foreign takeovers of its owners. It is not a hardware or chip measure. Its direct channel to US AI capex is small. It affects Chinese labs' offshore licensing and foreign acquisitions of Chinese AI firms. Summary bullet 7 groups it with Taiwan as a deployment constraint, which overstates it.
10. **SoftBank's funding cost is the sharpest fragility fact in the note, and it is underplayed.** The final OpenAI tranche was funded with notes at a 9.41% weighted USD coupon. Its OpenAI-linked loan has valuation-linked collateral and prepayment terms. Equity in a loss-making lab is being bought with high-yield debt, which is the leverage-financed-purchase indicator in its plainest form. Yet the stack page's lab-financing column does not name SoftBank, the largest disclosed OpenAI investor at $64.6bn.
11. **Not checked here:** "first since 2023" (theory page); CoreWeave debt rows; CBO and Treasury financing rows.

## Your assessment

Rates and oil are a real level headwind. The 10-year is at 5.1-5.3% (highest since 2002), the real 10-year near 2.8-2.95%, and physical Brent near $127. They raise every refinancing hurdle in the stack, and the Fed's own minutes say the boom is partly causing the inflation it is leaning against. That is the classic late-cycle feedback: on the policy indicator I score the boom more "overextended" than the note's post-Codex framing does. But macro was not the marginal driver of the 10-17 October repricing. Every rate and oil variable eased that week, while AI names fell two to five times as much as the NDX and AI high-yield spreads widened about 12 times as much as non-AI ones (C). The comfortably profitable parties are the cash-generating incumbents and TSMC. The overextended ones borrow at high-single to mid-teens yields against long-dated, unproven AI cash flows: CoreWeave (SOFR+4.5-5.5%, bonds near 15%) and SoftBank (9.4% notes to buy OpenAI equity, with value-linked collateral). Their equity holders lose first, then SoftBank's and the neoclouds' unsecured creditors. Sovereigns are the least likely forced sellers and the hardest to count. I differ from the note in three ways. It weighs the minutes too benignly. It is silent on market-reaction evidence. And its sovereign section is mostly bookkeeping that cannot change a conclusion.

**On the theory page's claim** ("the week of 10-17 October coincided with a 5.1-5.3% 10-year and Brent above $100, so not all of that repricing is AI-specific information"): the levels are right (the 10-year range that week was 5.09-5.24%), but the inference is wrong. Information is in changes, and the changes ran the other way. The one day with a plausible macro catalyst is **14 October**: CPI came in at 3.7% against a 3.6% consensus, with core +0.3% against +0.2%. That day the basket fell 6.2% (CRWV −15.5%, ORCL −8.8%), the 2-year rose 3bp and HY OAS widened 9bp, while the 10-year fell 2bp and the real 10-year fell 4bp. Even then the move concentrated in levered AI names (NDX −2.0%), which points to financing-sensitivity rather than broad discounting. A defensible wording: rates and oil set the backdrop (refinancing at 5%+ Treasury yields, a September hike, energy-led inflation); the week's repricing itself was mostly AI- and AI-financing-specific, and Treasuries rallied during it.

**Evidence that would separate the two:**
- A daily regression of basket returns in excess of the S&P equal-weight on Δ real 10-year, Δ 2-year, Δ Brent front-month and CPI/PPI-day dummies, over September-October.
- A non-AI duration control: unprofitable non-AI growth stocks, biotech (XBI), REITs, utilities. If they did not fall alike, the move is AI-specific.
- Credit pairs at the same rating: AI-linked versus non-AI HY and IG spreads (Goldman's +25bp versus +2bp is one such reading; get the primary note), and CoreWeave CDS against CDX HY.
- Intraday timing around the 8:30 CPI and PPI releases versus company-specific headlines.
- Hike odds and the 2-year on each down day.
- Breakeven versus real-yield decomposition.

The index row "AI spreads against the index as the 10-year and Brent move" should name this specification.
