## Verdict

All 12 load-bearing figures I re-fetched match the primary filings. The arithmetic holds, and every front-page figure from this note still holds as a dated June 30 figure. One wording fix: the CFO said "the majority" of backlog is GCP contracts, not "the vast majority".
Both Codex passes miss three things. The note's "undisclosed" $5.8bn bridge lease is identifiable as Google's $920m-a-month compute contract with SpaceX, whose shares Alphabet holds ($94.1bn). Q2's $99.0bn equity gain splits $77.4bn private company (reported as Anthropic) and $21.4bn marketable (mainly SpaceX). Alphabet also issued $25bn more of bonds on August 10, at coupons up to 6.50%.
The conclusion survives: very profitable core, financeable build, no solvency question. But Alphabet's AI exposure is more concentrated on two counterparties, Anthropic and SpaceX, than the note conveys, and H1 2026 was a ~$67bn all-in cash deficit before financing.

## Omissions

**Not in the note, and missed by both Codex passes:**

1. **The bridge lease counterparty is SpaceX, and SpaceX is also a $94.1bn holding (primary, A amounts / B linkage).** SpaceX's June 5 FWP describes a Google compute contract: $920m a month, Oct 2026–Jun 2029, cancellable on 90 days after Dec 31, 2026. It matches the 10-Q's $5.8bn short-term non-cancellable lease, entered in June and starting Q3. At the Q2 call, an analyst referred to "the third‑party compute deal with SpaceX"; management did not comment. Two consequences. (a) A cross-holding loop: Alphabet pays up to ~$11bn a year to a company whose shares it marks at $94.1bn and which generated most of Q2's $21.4bn marketable-equity gain. (b) The bridge spend is short-dated and cancellable. That limits overextension: the bridge is a ~$5.8bn floor, not $30bn. [SpaceX FWP][SPXFWP]; [10-Q][10Q] Notes 3–4; [Q2 call][Q2call].
2. **Earnings quality and the Anthropic nexus (A for filing amounts; C for identity and commitment).** The note says only "largely unrealized gains". The filing splits Q2's $99.0bn into $77.4bn on measurement-alternative private holdings and $21.4bn on marketable equities, attributing both to "SpaceX and a private company". It adds $77.1bn to net income (69%), and creates $20.6bn of deferred tax that becomes cash tax on realization. The private holdings' carrying value is $124.3bn, "primarily … a private company"; cumulative upward adjustments are $85.7bn on $47.6bn of cost. A Bloomberg headline of July 23 (secondary; seen in search results, not fetched) identifies the company as Anthropic, stake ~ $124bn. Taken together, Alphabet is Anthropic's investor (~$124bn carrying), conditional funder ($20bn equity derivative), and supplier. That last role covers TPUs via Broadcom (claims 27–28) and a reported **$111.1bn** minimum spend commitment, April 2026–July 2033, from Anthropic's confidential draft S-1 (reported, unverified, C; see [anthropic note](/research/stack/anthropic) claim 27). The note's Conflicts section says counterparty concentration is "not quantified sufficiently". The project already has an indicative figure: if the commitment is in RPO, it would be ~22% of Cloud backlog. The front page says "backlog is concentrated" only for Microsoft/OpenAI; this is the Google analogue. [10-Q][10Q] Note 3, MD&A; [Q2 release][Q2]; [Bloomberg, Jul 23][BBG].
3. **$25bn of US-dollar notes closed August 10, 2026 (primary, A).** There were ten tranches, 2028–2066, including $1.25bn of floating-rate notes, with fixed coupons 4.500–6.500% (weighted 5.61%). The 2066 tranche priced at 6.500%, against a 5.75% top coupon on the H1 2026 USD notes (2029–2066). Part of that is maturity mix and rates, so this is not a pure credit-spread reading. Pro forma debt face is ~$126bn, and the note's debt figures are only valid at June 30. On August 6 the ATM was widened to 16 managers, which is consistent with preparing to use it; no sales are disclosed. [August 8-K][AUG8K]; [ATM supplement][ATM].
4. **Securities class action filed October 2, 2026 (plaintiff-firm releases, C).** The case is in N.D. Cal., reportedly 3:26-cv-11290, against Alphabet, Pichai and Ashkenazi. The class period is May 19–July 16, 2026, and it alleges undisclosed Gemini 3.5 Pro training problems. The stock fell $16.40 (4.4%) to $353.81 on July 16 after a Bloomberg report that the model was months behind schedule. These are allegations only. Two points matter here: the June common offering at $355.20 priced inside the alleged class period, and the October 16 close of $340.59 is 4.1% below that price. The case is a model-competitiveness signal, not a financial exposure. Sources are tertiary leads (law-firm notices via search); trace to the docket before citing.
5. **Smaller primary details.** ~60% of technical infrastructure is servers and network (10-Q Note 7), which sharpens the depreciation point. Notes fair value was $94.9bn against $101.1bn face at June 30. Interest expense was $1.278bn in Q2 against $0.261bn a year earlier, an annualised ~$5.1bn before the August issue.

**Last 30 days, checked and not changing conclusions:** the Q3 call is confirmed for October 28. Gemini model and product launches were reported on October 13–16 (search leads only, not traced). The LVV October 23 deadline has not passed. I found no post-July change to capex guidance and no disclosed ATM sales.

## Your assessment

Alphabet is comfortably profitable where it matters for solvency. In Q2 2026, Services earned $39.5bn of operating income and Cloud $8.8bn at a 35.6% margin. Liquidity was $155bn ex equities against ~$126bn of debt after August. It is not overextended as a credit. It is extended as an equity story, in a specific way the note underplays. H1 2026 ran a ~$67bn all-in cash deficit before financing. That deficit was covered by ~$100bn of equity and bonds, then $25bn more in August. Meanwhile 69% of Q2 net income, and a growing slice of book equity, came from marks on two counterparties, Anthropic and SpaceX. Alphabet's commercial relationships with both are large: a reported $111bn Anthropic commitment and TPU supply, and a $920m-a-month SpaceX compute contract. Who loses first: common shareholders, through reversal of those marks (non-cash, but visible in EPS and equity) and dilution, before any lender. Bondholders are protected by the Search franchise. Where I differ from the note: it treats the backstops ($43.8bn notional) as the main lab-linked exposure. I think the larger correlated exposure is Anthropic, across equity, conditional funding, backlog and silicon. A disappointment there would hit Alphabet's other income, backlog and TPU volume at the same time. On the other side, the note is too pessimistic about bridge capacity: the SpaceX terms make it cancellable from early 2027, so it is a timing cost, not a lock-in. Net: same "profitable core, unproven return on the increment" verdict, with more concentration risk and less bridge risk.
