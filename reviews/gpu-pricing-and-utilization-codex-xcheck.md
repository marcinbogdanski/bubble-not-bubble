## Omissions

The recent-event search covered **2026-09-17 through 2026-10-17**, supplemented with older primary documents where the missing mechanism is material. New evidence strengthens near-term pricing but also strengthens the case for separating supplier-supported cash flows from independent demand.

1. **October Nebius price changes are a direct, recent comparator.** The search engine's approximately three-week-old capture of [Nebius's own price page](https://nebius.com/prices) retains old/new columns effective October 1: H100 3.85→4.50 (+16.9%); H200 4.50→5.40 (+20.0%); B200 7.15→8.50 (+18.9%); B300 7.85→9.50 (+21.0%). Direct fetch confirms the new prices but no longer displays the old columns. Treat the effective date and historical comparison as B because the old primary page was recovered through the search cache; current posted prices are A. The same page advertises preemptible floors of 0.79 for H100/H200 and 0.99 for B200/B300, with dynamic actual prices. This strengthens segmented pricing, not a transaction-price or occupancy claim.

2. **Rubin is already a production competitor, not just an April shipment-delay forecast.** On September 30, CoreWeave described limited Rubin availability, hundreds of GPUs across multiple regions and production use by Cognition. It reports up to 4.8× GB200 token throughput per GPU at matched interactivity for inference; this is a vendor/customer result, not a universal independently replicated comparison. The omission matters to residual values and the conversion from physical GPU counts to effective supply. Neither an old-card rent increase nor an April Rubin-delay forecast settles the next renewal's economics. [CoreWeave's technical deployment account](https://www.coreweave.com/blog/cognition-becomes-first-customer-for-nvidia-vera-rubin-nvl72-on-coreweave-cloud), September 30; A announcement, C generalized performance/economic implications.

3. **Recent financing makes the loss-allocation paragraph concrete.** On September 18, CoreWeave priced 3.7bn of 2.875% senior unsecured convertible notes due 2033, with a further 0.5bn option. Stated net proceeds before other offering expenses were 3.6445bn, with 0.4988bn intended for capped calls: about 3.1457bn remaining. The announcement demonstrates access to finance, not an independently verified closing or a low all-in capital cost. Conversion rights and capped-call expenditure matter alongside the coupon. [CoreWeave financing release](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Prices-Upsized-3-7-Billion-Convertible-Senior-Notes-Offering/default.aspx), A terms/B subtraction.

4. **Supplier-backed demand is the largest missing economic mechanism (older than 30 days).** CoreWeave's September 2025 agreement obligates NVIDIA to buy residual unsold capacity within the agreement through April 13, 2032, subject to service/delivery and termination provisions; its initial value was 6.3bn. NVIDIA's July 26, 2026 10-Q additionally reports 36bn of AI-cloud commitments, generally six years, decreasing as third parties or NVIDIA use capacity. Do not add these figures: their overlap is not resolved here. These arrangements can protect a provider's receipts while leaving productive end-user demand uncertain and transferring some risk to the supplier. They are not guarantees of the whole fleet, collateral or debt. [CoreWeave 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962825000047/crwv-20250909.htm), [NVIDIA 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm), A disclosure; interpretation is my inference.

5. **H200 scarcity is geographically qualified.** The same NVIDIA filing records a 0.4bn H200 excess-inventory/purchase-obligation charge in H1 FY2027 amid China sales restrictions. Rising OCPI H200 rents can coexist with legally inaccessible demand and impaired inventory elsewhere. This is a concrete counterexample to treating “newer cards strengthened” as universal inventory health. A for the charge; no inference that these units are freely transferable to the sampled rental market. [NVIDIA 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).

6. **Some missing qualifications are in sources the note already cites.** The SemiAnalysis dashboard includes April's one-year H100 range and a prepayment assumption. The CoreWeave lease disclosures concern scheduled future delivery, not necessarily delays. Its adjusted EBITDA is not operating cash flow or capital return. Nebius's price range describes a subset of deals and mainly future revenue. These are interpretation fixes, not arithmetic failures.

7. **Benchmark construction matters even after refusing to splice indices.** OCPI methodology uses a rolling one-hour calculation window; the market page supplies daily 4 p.m. ET observations. An equal-weight mean of these observations is not a full-month transaction average. Its market-disruption rules also permit carrying forward a previous print. FastGPU's published composite uses three-day endpoint averaging and a seven-day trailing plotted average. The note omits both details. [OCPI methodology, §§3.1, 3.7, 4.3](https://data.ornn.com/methodology), [FastGPU methodology](https://fastgpu.co/price-index), A descriptions. Require publication/quality flags before treating every dated observation as fresh trading in a successor forecast rule.

## Summary-bullet assessment

1. **Local scarcity / no market-wide glut:** Supported as a bounded interpretation by row 20's short-term contracts, row 16's historical survey and the price dispersion. It is not a directly measured cluster-availability result: inventory was not ordered, and row 23 alone does not establish a bottleneck or delay. The summary has no date, price or scale. Use replacement 1 below, anchored on actual disclosed contract terms rather than a generic scarcity assertion.

2. **Different indices cannot be spliced:** Supported. [Silicon's August 27 methodology](https://www.silicondata.com/documents/silicon-data-index-methodology.pdf) standardizes rental type, country, CPU platform and GPU variant, then weights providers; OCPI uses active on-demand rentals; FastGPU uses advertised floors. The sentence lists Silicon/Ornn/FastGPU but then describes transaction/provider/floor weighting in a different order, inviting misattribution. A numbered figure would be artificial here; explicit name-to-method mapping and observation dates solve the abstraction. Use replacement 1.

3. **Newer GPUs firmer, H100 flat, A100 softer:** Supported by the independently reproduced monthly means, within OCPI. “Brief H100 reversal” is true for the specified dates, not a demonstrated ongoing downtrend. No figure or period in the summary makes the current wording unnecessarily vague. Use replacement 1's precise month-to-date comparison.

4. **Margins versus replacement and financing:** Directionally supported by row 18 and financing disclosures, but adjusted EBITDA cannot establish cash margins after replacement or capital destruction. The note has no cohort-level replacement capex, lifetime cash flows or hurdle rate. Its generic allocation of risk also omits supplier backstops. Use replacement 1 and add recommendation 4.

5. **Old accelerators retain utility; depreciation/resale unvalidated:** Narrowly supported: the H100 term survey demonstrates a priced rental market for an older generation, and advertised A100 offers show some continuing demand opportunity, but neither validates the whole fleet's recoverable value. Strong and appropriately cautious on modeled resale. The existing summary lacks a number/date. Use replacement 1; add the Rubin and accounting qualifications below.

6. **Paid occupancy and comparable renewals are the largest uncertainty:** Supported as the reviewer's judgment about the supplied evidence. SF Compute's [September 9 resale explanation](https://sfcompute.com/news/realized-discount) confirms its 25% figure is a financial rebate, not an occupancy measurement. The current summary is conceptually useful but needs an example and a dated denominator. Use replacement 1. This review has not proven that no provider anywhere publishes productive utilization.

## Your assessment

I agree that the retrieved evidence does not demonstrate a market-wide GPU glut: new contract premiums and rising prices for some models coexist with cheap offers and weakness in older hardware. I differ in how much economic comfort I take from those prices. Supplier capacity commitments can sustain provider receipts without establishing independent final demand; scheduled-but-undelivered MW do not by themselves prove scarcity; and advancing workload throughput can undermine future rents even while physical GPU-hour prices rise. Conversely, the omitted October Nebius rate card is firmer direct evidence of current asking-price increases than the old survey alone. I would describe this as a segmented market with demonstrated pricing power in selected contracts and unproven lifetime capital returns. The evidence supports neither an aggregate shortage forecast nor a finding that accounting lives are generally fraudulent or generally sound.
