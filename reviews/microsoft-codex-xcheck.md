*Review date and information cutoff: 2026-10-17. Reviewed: `research/stack/microsoft.md` (2026-10-17 draft). All external sources below were accessed on 2026-10-17. No changes made to the research note.*

## Verdict

The headline operating results are substantially verified; Microsoft is comfortably profitable, but its infrastructure commitments deserve more scrutiny than the note gives them.
Material corrections are required: the omitted contractual-obligations table, the cash-flow bridge, mixed-basis lease totals, and unsupported OpenAI exposure estimates.
Retain the concentration/overbuild thesis, but do not publish the June 41% OpenAI share, $20bn carrying value, or inevitable FY2027 borrowing as established findings.

## Omissions

**The biggest omission is already in the cited filing.** K26's MD&A contractual-obligations table reports **743.821bn total**, of which **241.922bn** is due in FY27. It includes **194.060bn purchase commitments** (169.008bn in FY27), **34.566bn construction commitments** (29.848bn in FY27), and **443.506bn lease payments** (32.411bn in FY27). Purchase commitments mainly concern datacenters, including purchase orders and take-or-pay contracts. Thus the sentence saying there is no table is false. Supplier-specific allocations are not given; their absence does not mean commitments are absent from aggregate disclosure. These figures overlap future operating costs, investment and leases: do not add them wholesale to capex guidance or interpret the FY27 total as a financing deficit. **A.** [K26][K26], Material Cash Requirements and Other Obligations.

**Cash intensity is understated in a different way from the note's explanation.** Other investing used 19.861bn; management attributes the year-over-year increase mainly to facilitating component purchases. PP&E purchases remaining in accounts payable increased from **6.9bn to 26.7bn**, implying substantially more supplier financing/payment timing exposure. Neither is captured by simply subtracting the cash PP&E line from OCF. An illustrative residual after cash PP&E, all other investing, lease principal and gross cash shareholder distributions is **−4.691bn**, rather than +15.170bn. This is a sensitivity, not a new standardized FCF definition or proof all other investing is AI capex. **A inputs/B arithmetic.** [K26][K26], MD&A and Note 6.

**Supplier funding is partly Microsoft's cash, and recourse changes after acceptance.** IREN's contract requires 20% prepayments by tranche, approximately **1.94bn** over the whole 9.7bn contract; this is not proof the whole amount has already been paid. Its FY26 filing shows only **0.938bn** of the 3.645bn GPU facility funded/issued at June 30 (0.413+0.525), not 3.6bn already borrowed. Its remarketing guarantee is conditional and released as tranches are accepted, rather than a perpetual blanket parent guarantee. This changes both Microsoft's pre-delivery exposure and the order in which IREN/lender losses can occur. **A/B.** [IREN December 2025 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1878848/000187884826000015/iren-20251231.htm), strategic agreement; [IREN FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1878848/000187884826000052/iren-20260630.htm), Note 23.

**The strongest counter-evidence to a single-customer demand story deserves prominence.** Microsoft says nearly 90% of FY26 Microsoft Cloud revenue came from customers outside frontier model companies. This is a revenue mix disclosure, not a backlog mix and not evidence that 90% is non-AI. It materially qualifies the statement that the entire capacity-constraint narrative rests on OpenAI and Anthropic. **A for management disclosure.** [Q4 call][C426]

**OpenAI's financing capacity cannot be assessed solely from its Azure commitment.** OpenAI announced **122bn of committed capital** on March 31, plus a **4.7bn** revolving facility undrawn at closing. This is relevant support for payment capacity but not evidence that all commitments became unrestricted cash, or that its obligations to all suppliers are funded. **A as company disclosure; current liquidity unverified.** [OpenAI funding announcement](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/)

## Your assessment

Microsoft is comfortably profitable and is not demonstrated to be financially overextended at the corporate level, but the marginal infrastructure commitments are exposed to customer concentration, rapid hardware replacement and uncertain returns. Its software franchise and broad cloud revenue base are real buffers; they do not make capital allocation risk-free. I differ from the note in both directions: the purchase-commitment schedule, component-related investing and unpaid PP&E make funding pressure more tangible than its selective capex discussion suggests, while its FY27 borrowing prediction, June OpenAI backlog precision and full-default loss framing overstate what can be inferred. Microsoft's equity holders bear lower cash generation and impaired returns first; an OpenAI failure would also hit its receivable and investment. Thinly capitalized suppliers and their financiers can suffer earlier market-value or liquidity losses, but Microsoft remains obliged under valid accepted capacity/lease contracts: an OpenAI default does not automatically transfer Microsoft's losses to its suppliers. Supplier construction delays, acceptance clauses and post-acceptance guarantees determine the actual loss order. This is an assessment of return and financing risk, not proof of imminent insolvency or of what the share price already discounts.
