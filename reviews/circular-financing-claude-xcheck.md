# Circular financing: independent Claude cross-check

*Review date: 2026-10-18. Reviewer: Claude (fresh context, no subagents). Scope: `research/macro/circular-financing.md` as of 2026-10-18 and `data/circular-financing-deals.csv` (CF001–CF051). 13 of the 15 allowed fetches were used: seven SEC filings pulled directly (NVIDIA 8-K and 10-Q, CoreWeave 8-K, AMD 10-Q, Broadcom 10-Q, Microsoft 10-K, Amazon 10-Q), plus the Alphabet, Oracle and TeraWulf 10-Qs, the SoftBank release, the OpenAI Stargate page (HTTP 403) and one secondary report. Searches were used to find leads. No project file other than this one was changed.*

## Verdict

The figures in all 12 load-bearing rows reproduce from primary filings. One linkage is under-graded: Broadcom's 10-Q ties the $29bn backstop to the $35bn AI XPV tranche. Two rows are stale: the NVIDIA $100bn LOI (CF001) and TeraWulf's Abernathy JV (CF043).
The register is materially incomplete on the support side. It misses Alphabet's $43.8bn of data-centre credit backstops (plus $7.6bn of guarantees and $24.1bn agreed), AMD's $4.1bn of lease guarantees, NVIDIA's $20bn of leases taken for third parties and its extended payment terms, and roughly $190bn of mark-up gains that suppliers booked on lab equity in 2026.
The loops do not all fail together. Equity and offtake links are wrong-way, but investment-grade guarantees turn a lab default into a loss for the supplier rather than a project default, and NVIDIA's CoreWeave support ends on insolvency. Even so, one OpenAI or CoreWeave failure can produce two of the register's "distinct" obligor events, so the headline overstates how independent those events are.

**What this means for "two distinct obligor groups".** Three root clusters dominate.

- **OpenAI → Oracle → Oracle-tenanted vehicles, plus → CoreWeave.** One root can produce two or more register events.
- **CoreWeave → Core Scientific and Applied Digital.** One root, two or three events.
- **Anthropic → XPV and North Star, plus Nscale's conditional $44.6bn and TeraWulf's direct lease.** One root, two events possible.

Investment-grade backstops (Google, NVIDIA from 2028, Meta) break most other paths. So "two distinct obligor groups" does not imply two independent failures. The 24-month conditional of 0.55 for two-or-more events may well be right, but it is right **because** of these common roots, and the register should say so.

The phrase "each loop fails together" does not appear in `report/index.md` as of this review; the front page only cites "(51 mapped links)". On the evidence, the phrase would be right for equity stakes and offtake, which are wrong-way together. It would be wrong for guarantees, which pay out precisely when the lab defaults unless the guarantor is itself stressed. It would be half right for NVIDIA's CoreWeave support, which ends at insolvency.

## Your assessment

**Is "circular financing" the right name?** Only partly. True round-trips are a minority of the instruments: money returning to the firm that supplied it, as in NVIDIA→OpenAI→NVIDIA, Amazon→Anthropic→AWS, Google→Anthropic→TPU/Cloud, CoreWeave stock issued to OpenAI, and the Cerebras loan repaid in service credits. The largest dollar items are **contingent credit support**: NVIDIA, Alphabet, Broadcom and AMD guarantees of about $193bn. These move no cash until a default and push tail risk upstream to investment-grade balance sheets. A third family runs the other way, with **customer prepayments** funding the supplier (Microsoft's 20% to IREN, Oracle's $11.4bn of advances, Nebius's >$9bn). A better title is "Vendor, investor and customer financing in the AI stack", with three sub-registers: round-trip equity and loans, contingent credit support, and prepayment or offtake. "Circular" should be kept for the round-trips.

**Share of 2026 accelerator demand that is supplier-supported (grade D; scenario, not measurement).**
- **Denominator (assumption, not re-fetched).** About $450–550bn of 2026 accelerator and AI-system purchases: NVIDIA's $384.8bn annualized revenue, mostly data centre, plus AMD, Broadcom AI, TPU and Trainium.
- **Narrow numerator** (supplier money returning to that supplier's chips in 2026): NVIDIA private-equity outflow of about $62bn annualized, at 50–100% returning, gives $31–62bn. Add about half of the $35bn XPV tranche delivered in 2026 ($17.5bn) and $0–10bn of capacity support not already counted. That is about $50–90bn, or **roughly 10–20%**.
- **Broad numerator** (adding hyperscaler and SoftBank equity into lab customers, about $110–120bn in 2026, of which 40–60% ends up as accelerator spend): an extra $45–70bn, giving **roughly 17–35%**.
- **Hard-evidenced core** (filed, paid in, attributable to a specific chip purchase): under 5%.
- Almost all of it routes through OpenAI and Anthropic. Both bounds rest on assumed pass-through rates, so the note should publish the method, not a point estimate.

**Single point of failure.** For the mechanism, it is **OpenAI's ability to keep raising equity at or above its last round price**, with Anthropic close behind. Three things all reference that price: the roughly $190bn of supplier marks; the lab burn that pays the offtake contracts; and the termination condition built into NVIDIA's PORTS guarantee ("OpenAI achieving a satisfactory credit rating" assumes the lab's credit improves). A flat or down round would at once mark down supplier earnings, slow orders and raise the odds of a guarantee trigger. On the credit side, the single point of failure is **Oracle**. It is the one large conduit with **no investment-grade guarantor** between OpenAI and tens of billions of project loans (Abilene, Vantage, Jupiter, Michigan), and its debt already trades below par.
