Reviewed 2026-10-17 by Codex. Scope: `research/stack/neoclouds-other.md`, using `report/methodology.md` and `tasks/RESEARCH_BRIEF.md`. All external sources below accessed 2026-10-17. No changes to the note or its CSV.

## Verdict

**Major revision required:** the financing-risk thesis is credible, but the headline debt total and profitability ranking are not established on comparable evidence.
**Material errors:** duplicated Nebius financing, wrong segment margin, misclassified warrants, understated Google support, and incorrect claims about undisclosed financials and contract terms.
**Important counterweight:** demand and financing remain substantial; neither current rental-price collapse nor a universal sequence of creditor losses is demonstrated.

## Omissions

1. **September 18: Nscale published financials and consequential contract conditions.** H1 revenue was $140.6m, operating loss $492.0m, adjusted EBITDA loss $199.2m and net loss $1,020.1m; the net loss includes $457.1m of fair-value adjustments. This is evidence of operating losses beyond that noncash charge. Its S-1 says financing required for the $44.6bn Anthropic agreements had **no binding commitments as of the prospectus**, and describes delivery/service obligations, termination rights and remedies. This directly changes the profitability and contract-firmness discussion. Do not assume the financing remained uncommitted on October 17 without a later update. [S-1][ns-s1].

2. **September 25: Cipher's larger Barber Lake contract also disclosed delivery and overrun risk.** The same release cited for the extra $5.2bn describes phased delivery in Q4 2026–Q1 2027 and makes Cipher responsible for the first **$359.3m** above budget; the tenant reimburses half of further overruns through additional rent over twenty years. Larger nominal TCV therefore came with funding obligations and timing risk. Separately, Black Pearl had begun delivering capacity and earning rent in August: describing Cipher as currently HPC-pre-revenue is stale. [Amendment][cifr-amend], [business update][cifr-deck].

3. **September 24: Hut 8's parent revolver changes the recourse story.** The $1.07bn facility was undrawn at closing, so it is not $1.07bn of additional outstanding debt. But it has the parent as borrower and a minimum-liquidity covenant beginning March 2027, at 40% of commitments before stabilization and 25% thereafter. The note should analyze this alongside nonrecourse project bonds. [8-K][hut-rcf].

4. **September 25/October 1: announced funding is not all cash received.** Nvidia's $1bn Nscale investment was expected in mid-November; Lambda's $1.008bn delayed-draw financing funds against commissioned deployments. Both support continued lender appetite, but neither announcement justifies treating the full amount as current balance-sheet debt/cash. [Nscale][ns-convert], [Lambda][lambda-loan].

5. **October 5: TeraWulf's Muskie expansion is prospective power, not delivered lease revenue.** The release raises planned utility capacity from 500 MW to 1 GW, with the second 500 MW targeted for 2029, subject to approvals and scheduling. It does not establish an additional 1 GW of executed revenue contracts. [Muskie release][wulf-muskie].

6. **Older but decisive omissions: contract remedies, recourse and wider customers.** IREN's Microsoft 8-K publicly describes delivery deadlines, cure provisions, termination rights and delay credits. Its 10-K describes a parent guarantee if a funded tranche is validly terminated and not successfully remarketed, net of GPU disposal proceeds, with potential prepayment-refund exposure. IREN also announced a $3.4bn Nvidia cloud contract, and Core Scientific's AMD agreement is in the note itself. Thus concentration is real, but “almost all” tied to four buyers is unquantified and misses material counterparties. [IREN Microsoft][iren-msft], [10-K][iren-10k], [Nvidia agreement][iren-nvidia], [AMD][corz-amd].

7. **Google support was disclosed more fully than the note suggests.** Beyond the TeraWulf correction in row 23, Cipher's initial Barber Lake announcement disclosed a **$1.4bn Google backstop** and warrants issued to Google. This does not prove the later extended term is fully guaranteed; it does invalidate saying no amount was stated. The extension needs its own guarantee-scope check. [Cipher original agreement][cifr-google].

8. **Accounting undermines the “comfortable” ranking and the proposed contagion mechanism.** CORZ's relevant warrants are liabilities on its own shares; TeraWulf issued warrants to Google. They are not portfolios of CoreWeave/Google stock warrants owned by the landlords. Also, Nebius extended server/network useful lives from four to five years in 2026; adjusted EBITDA excludes depreciation entirely. Positive segment EBITDA/NOI therefore does not establish an adequate return after replacement capex, interest and equity dilution. [CORZ warrant note][corz], [TeraWulf][wulf-q2], [Nebius accounting discussion][nb-q1].

The searched period was September 17–October 17. The recent primary disclosures above supply both adverse evidence and evidence that demand/financing persist. This is not a claim to have exhaustively searched every subsidiary filing or private financing.

## Summary-bullet assessment

1. **Debt size — not supported as written.** The rows support a large financing footprint. They do not support a June 30 $45bn balance-sheet total, an accurate October roll-forward, or >$110bn of comparable outstanding debt. Mixed dates, principal/carrying values, undrawn commitments, missing retirements and unverified JV debt matter.

2. **Four counterparties — partially supported.** Several large exposures ultimately depend on Microsoft, Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic. “Almost all” has no measured denominator; AMD, Nvidia and other customers are visible. Google is a support provider, not interchangeable with the operating lessee. Nscale's current primary Microsoft disclosure materially strengthens the large-commitments observation while making the old $23bn example stale.

3. **Pricing not showing stress — cautiously supported for sampled offers, overstated for the market.** The evidence contains healthy management-reported new contracts and firm advertised prices. It does not prove market-wide sell-outs, realised fleet yields or a growing time-series share of capex funded by prepayments. The payback is a forward estimate and the price series are heterogeneous.

4. **Profitability split — directionally plausible, unreliable ranking.** The note itself contradicts “41% AI-cloud margin” and HPC-pre-revenue TeraWulf. APLD NOI and CORZ gross margin measure different cost layers; Hut's EBITDA needs the asset-mark qualifier and its major AI deliveries were still scheduled for 2027. Nscale's disclosed operating losses belong in the comparison. Debt versus ARR alone cannot establish insolvency or comfort.

5. **Equity repricing — partly supported by the note's quotations, causality unsupported.** Arithmetic supports severe drawdowns and the four cited out-of-money examples. “At lows” and “50–75%” need correction. The bond/Gartner news may be contemporaneous explanations; no event study or primary evidence makes them established proximate causes. Gartner forecasts consolidation, not 80% defaults. The equity quotes were outside the selected primary re-verification sample.

6. **Contract firmness — important question, false blanket statement.** Public filings already disclose material termination, delivery, financing and guarantee conditions. The unresolved question is the completeness and deal-specific effect of those terms, not whether any terms are public.

## Your assessment

The evidence supports a concentrated, heavily financed construction and GPU-deployment boom whose realised cash returns lag its commitments; it does not yet establish broad rental-price collapse or inevitable defaults. My principal concern is the combination of completion obligations, conditional financing, restricted liquidity, customer concentration and replacement costs: those mechanisms can hurt equity even while buyers still want compute. I am less comfortable than the note with calling selected operators financially comfortable on segment margins, and more concerned about Nscale after its public filing. Conversely, I assign more weight to disclosed Google support, Meta's residual-capacity commitment, existing HPC revenue and continuing rated financing than the note's sequential crash narrative permits. Loss allocation must be modeled by obligor, security, guarantees and maturity, rather than imposed as one layer-wide waterfall; a guarantee may pay before a project bondholder loses money, and convertible creditors do not become equity merely because conversion once looked likely.
