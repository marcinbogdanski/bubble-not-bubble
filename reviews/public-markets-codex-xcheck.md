## Omissions

1. **September 18: CoreWeave could still price substantial new financing.** Its issuer announcement specifies a $3.7bn, 2.875% convertible issue due 2033, upsized from $3bn; expected net proceeds before expenses were $3.6445bn, with $498.8m earmarked for capped calls. That leaves approximately $3.1457bn before other offering expenses if the option is not exercised. Pricing does not prove settlement, and September access does not establish October access. Nevertheless, omitting this makes the financing picture too one-sided. The coupon cannot be compared directly with straight-debt yields because investors also receive a conversion option. **A** terms, **B** subtraction. [Issuer, September 18](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Prices-Upsized-3-7-Billion-Convertible-Senior-Notes-Offering/default.aspx).

2. **September 30–October 15: supplier fundamentals have much fresher primary evidence than claim 10.** Micron's September 30 release reports $54.23bn fiscal-Q4 revenue, $37.70bn GAAP net income and $33.20bn adjusted free cash flow; ASML's October 14 release reports €11.662bn Q3 sales and €4.960bn net income; TSMC's October 15 report shows $46.72bn Q3 revenue and a 51.1% net margin. These strengthen the profitable-supplier case while leaving purchasers' returns unresolved. Micron's 86.8% GAAP quarterly gross margin also makes profit normalization an important scenario assumption; extraordinary margins should not silently become perpetuities. **A** reported figures; normalization concern is my inference. [Micron](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx), [ASML](https://investor.asml.com/news-releases/news-release-details/asml-reports-eu11.7-billion-total-net-sales-and-eu5.0-billion-net-income-q3-2026), [TSMC](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/f83780000ddc0c3b48e57a87eab6814c4ab35058/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf).

6. **The note overgeneralizes the observable credit perimeter.** Broad US corporate OAS does not measure private-credit marks, construction finance, collateral haircuts or all overseas funding. A single CDS quote and two broad index levels establish dispersion, not the extent or absence of contagion. Likewise, equity's contractual subordination does not mean equity prices must fall before debt prices or that creditors must wait for equity to reach zero before recognizing losses. These are analytical corrections, not newly discovered events.

## Your assessment

The evidence supports a concentrated, earnings-sensitive market with selective funding fragility, rather than an established marketwide financing collapse or a conclusion that every AI-linked equity is overpriced. I agree with the note's distinction between profitable suppliers and potentially overextended purchasers, and with its rejection of automatic selling by capitalization-weighted funds when prices fall. I differ in giving greater weight to observed earnings quality: profits linked to private investment revaluations and negative cash after capex are already visible, so “earnings durability” needs a specified denominator rather than an abstract warning. In the other direction, recent supplier cash generation and CoreWeave's September convertible pricing strengthen the countercase that real economics and financing access persist. The right next test is normalized operating cash generation against financing obligations and valuation; neither a high CAPE, a low forward multiple, nor a large drawdown alone resolves that test. The stress arithmetic is useful only with explicit, consistent earnings assumptions and should not acquire the appearance of a forecast through numerical precision.
