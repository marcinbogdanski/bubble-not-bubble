# Semiconductor equipment — independent Codex cross-check

*Review date: 2026-10-17. Reviewed: `research/stack/semiconductor-equipment.md`, draft dated 2026-10-17. Evidence cutoff: 2026-10-17; all linked sources accessed during this review on that date.*

## Verdict

Major revision required: the headline earnings and demand figures largely verify, but several conclusions exceed the evidence.
Material problems include misdated contract liabilities, an obsolete backlog definition, assumed deposit forfeiture, overlapping AI categories, and an unsupported downturn-margin calculation.
Equipment makers appear financially resilient and demand is strong; neither inevitable shareholder losses nor an unavoidable hard landing is established.

## Omissions

1. **Customer confirmation within the last 30 days strengthens the near-term demand case.** On **October 15**, TSMC reiterated its **$60–64bn CY2026 capex budget**, described tight tool supply, and retained a much higher 2026–2028 investment trajectory than the prior three years. It also flagged weakness in consumer/price-sensitive markets. This is buyer evidence the equipment note should summarize rather than simply delegate elsewhere. TSMC reported **58.7% Q3 operating margin**, directly undermining the equipment layer's unqualified profitability superlative. [TSMC Q3 transcript][TSMC-call]; [results][TSMC-results]

2. **Micron's latest annual filing adds both demand and financing evidence.** The FY2026 10-K available in October projects net-of-incentive PP&E capex of **~$25bn in H1 FY2027, with more in H2**: thus more than $50bn for the year is a derived implication, not an exact full-year guide. It discloses **$12.75bn customer deposits** and **$7bn issued/committed letters of credit** supporting strategic customer agreements. These arrangements improve commitment visibility but also demonstrate that prepayment exposure propagates across layers. Do not add the two measures together as cash received. [Micron 10-K, liquidity/capital resources][MU-AR]

3. **SEMI published a newer forecast on October 13.** Its 300mm front-end fab-equipment series projects **$149bn in 2026 (+31%)**, reaching **$227bn in 2029**, with double-digit annual growth through that horizon. It expects 13 new fabs/lines to commence operation or first silicon in 2026 and 23 in 2027. This supports the physical expansion story and supersedes reliance on an old December-2025 fab-spending forecast. It is a different series from July's OEM WFE forecast; retain both with their definitions rather than splice them. [SEMI October release][SEMI-Oct]

4. **Deposit and backlog protection is materially weaker than claimed.** ASML Note 2 records refunded down payments in 2025. It reports **€46.5bn remaining performance obligations, 65% expected within twelve months**, a useful timing disclosure distinct from system backlog. KLA expressly warns that customers can generally cancel or delay with limited or no penalty and that export-license failures can require deposit returns. These facts are inconsistent with treating deposits as a universal non-refundable guarantee. [ASML Note 2][ASML-AR]; [KLA risk factors][KLA-AR]

5. **There is financial intermediation at the tool layer.** ASML sold **€6.2125bn receivables** in 2025, including **€3.5077bn down-payment receivables**, and recorded the proceeds in OCF. This is a flow during the year, not a year-end outstanding stock. It also had **€626.8m sales-type lease receivables** at year-end. Factoring is not automatically debt, but the absolute claim that the layer has no vendor financing is untenable; nor can all OCF be interpreted as cash paid directly by customers during the period. [ASML Notes 5–6][ASML-AR]

6. **The recurring-business comparison is historically wrong and economically too strong.** ASML had **€253.639m service sales in 2001**, about 16% of revenue. Its current upgrades benefit from tight capacity: the October remarks attribute elevated gross margin to high-margin IBM components. Lam's CSBG includes mature-node equipment as well as spares, service and upgrades. The defensible conclusion is that aftermarket revenue may cushion cycles, with an unquantified recurring share; a counter-cyclical floor has not been demonstrated. [2003 filing][ASML-2003]; [October remarks][ASML-remarks]; [Lam release][LAM-Q4]

7. **Concentration and export-control uncertainty need more precise treatment.** Add ASML's 2025 top-four concentration of 61.2%, and Lam's latest-quarter China share of 26% alongside its fiscal-year 34%. H.R. 8170's official introduced text confirms the April 2 date, and S. 4281 has a separate April 13 Senate introduction. The note's geopolitical claim can be sourced to legislation rather than a technology-news relay, but the introduced texts do not establish the latest amended text or enactment status on October 17. I did not obtain a usable current action-history page, so I do **not** independently certify “not enacted” or a fresh near-total Dutch ban. Keep the August pressure report explicitly reported and separate from law. [House text][MATCH-House]; [Senate text][MATCH-Senate]

8. **The gentler historical outcome is already in the evidence.** ASML's 2024 revenue rose 2.6%, gross margin held at 51.3%, and net income was €7.572bn. Its 2023 IBM revenue had actually declined from €5.743bn to €5.620bn while total sales grew. These facts contradict both an inevitable hard landing and any assumption that all installed-base revenue mechanically rises during a tool downturn. [2024 results][ASML-2024]; [2023 results][ASML-2023]

## Summary-bullet assessment

1. **Profitability — partially supported.** The cited margins are substantially correct, but mixing gross, operating, net and non-GAAP margins does not establish a cross-stack ranking. TSMC provides a direct counterexample. All five show strong cash generation on the cited periods; the note itself does not supply TEL's cash-flow bridge. TEL's Q1 presentation reports ¥80.5bn company-defined FCF against ¥39.9bn capex, which supports that narrower statement. “Every one” should specify the periods and FCF definitions. [TEL slides, pp. 4, 10][TEL-slides]

2. **Financing — partially supported, materially overstated.** Strong liquidity, modest net borrowing and buybacks are supported. “Not by debt” contradicts the acknowledged bonds. The contract-liability year is wrong, H1 OCF was negative, and the almost-entirely-prepayments claim is unquantified. Replace the all-or-nothing financing description with dominant funding sources and acknowledge factoring, leases and deposits' contractual limits.

3. **AI-led demand ahead of supply — mostly supported as management expectations.** Primary forecasts and buyer capex evidence support the direction. Capacity expansion is scoped to specified tool families and 2028 plans remain under investigation. 2029–2030 interest is not accepted orders. TEL's AI share is an estimate. No cited figure proves final-user AI economics or eliminates over-ordering.

4. **Concentration — supported with qualification.** ASML/customer and reported geographic shares check out. Add the current top-four measure, label each fiscal/quarterly period and distinguish geography from prohibited products. China revenue is exposure, not the size of an automatic total loss. The specific current legal status remains less well verified than the financial figures.

5. **Historical hard landing — numerical support, unsupported universal conclusion.** 2001 and 2009 establish severe cyclical loss risk. They cannot establish that this layer never lands softly; the same bullet's 2024 example is a profitable plateau. Be explicit that cancellations and push-outs are combined and that the 260→70 unit comparison spans two years.

6. **Loss of early signal — substantially supported; proposed replacements are incomplete.** Quarterly bookings are absent from the 2026 releases checked. But contract liabilities are affected by deliveries/revenue recognition, refunds, payment terms, reclassification and unconditional receivables: a fall need not mean fewer new orders. The cited old backlog definition cannot identify a present twelve-month portion. Annual backlogs and deferred revenue are complementary indicators, not direct replacements for bookings.

7. **Order durability versus valuation — a reasonable risk question, not an established pricing conclusion.** A downturn can hurt equity more than solvency, but the quoted valuation is tertiary and internally inconsistent. The note has no reverse valuation, scenario probabilities or stress model supporting “priced for them to hold” or “shareholders' valuations would not” survive. State these conditionally, and separate business survival, profitability and equity drawdown.

## Your assessment

The evidence supports a highly profitable, cash-generative equipment sector responding to strong AI-related orders, with recent buyer capex commitments reinforcing rather than contradicting the suppliers' demand narrative. I agree that concentration, memory cyclicality and export policy can transmit a downstream investment reversal into sharp tool-order declines, and that equity prices can be more vulnerable than balance sheets. I differ on the strength of the claimed protections and inevitabilities: deposits are not universally forfeitable, system backlog is neither a twelve-month delivery guarantee nor directly comparable with total sales, upgrade revenue is not all recurring, and current profit does not establish the return on the next capacity increment. Conversely, the note overstates bearish certainty by turning two severe historical episodes into a rule and reading a specific future path out of an unreconciled valuation multiple. My assessment is strong current operating fundamentals with material cyclical and valuation risk; the evidence does not yet establish either equipment overbuild or unavoidable shareholder impairment.
