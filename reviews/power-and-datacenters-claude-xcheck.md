## Verdict

The figures are reliable: 12 of 12 re-verified rows match their sources. The defect is that the Jupiter permit row is stale: the main hearing has reportedly moved to 7 December, so the 23 November deadline is moot.
The Codex reviewer and the note share one blind spot. Both read PJM's 6,831 MW RTO-wide capacity shortfall, cleared at the price cap, as evidence of a campus-energisation constraint. Better local evidence exists and is missing: Dominion's queue, Jupiter's unpowered buildings, and the new Virginia moratorium bills.
Keep the note's thesis: incumbents are profitable and financed campuses are unproven. Fix three things: the Jupiter timeline, the stack page's unqualified sentence "energisation is the regional constraint", and its misdated bond range. Then add Nvidia's wider lease exposure and a counterweight to the reassuring Hut 8 example.

## Omissions

1. **Jupiter permit slip (17 Oct, C).** The main air-permit hearing moved to 7 December, which makes the 23 November deadline moot. The buildings are unpowered and microgrid work has not started. This is the clearest live case of power gating a financed campus. It touches about $18B of reported loans quoted at 89–91 (debt-markets note, C), Oracle lease timing and the force-majeure correspondence. A permit decision now looks likely to land after Oracle's mid-December Q2 results.
2. **Virginia statewide moratorium bills (16 Oct, C).** SB 843 (Perry/Roem) would bar datacenter permits statewide from July 2027 to July 2030, including power lines and on-site power that solely serve datacenters. A separate Roem bill ties approvals to Dominion's queue being energised. The article cites Dominion at ~25,000 MW with energised dates, a further ~75,000 MW without, and a ~7-year wait. Debate begins 13 January 2027. This is the direct local energisation evidence that the PJM capacity figure is being used to stand in for. [Virginia Mercury via WYDaily](https://wydaily.com/latest/2026/10/16/virginia-lawmakers-propose-moratoriums-on-new-data-centers)
3. **Nvidia's wider lease-of-last-resort role (A, 10-Q already cited).** The 3.8 GW option, $25bn of uncommenced leases ($20bn for third parties, to be reassigned) and the $3.5bn shell guarantees. The supplier sits in the real-estate loss chain as a lessee, not only as a guarantor.
4. **Equipment incumbents are partly customer-funded (A).** GEV's $5.1bn Q2 FCF and its FCF guide raised by $5bn coincide with slot-reservation growth. 54% of the 116 GW is reservations. In a downturn the first loss is soft backlog and forfeited deposits on the customer side, and the reservations are where GEV's own exposure lies. Vertiv's Q2 release gave no orders or backlog, which matters given VRT is −45% from peak.
5. **The December 2026 PJM auction (2029/30) is the next observable test** and is missing from Signals. So is the Texas TCEQ compliance update due 19 October.
6. **Equinix tripled its medium-term capex plan to $5–7bn a year**, showing that even established colocation is moving into a larger build phase.
7. **A third PJM 2027/28 cleared figure (134,585 MW)** in PJM's own July 2026 release. This is minor, but it belongs in the existing conflict row.
8. **The financing examples are unbalanced.** The note's main financing example is Hut 8's River Bend, which is fully amortizing and backstopped by Google: the best-protected structure in the sample. The worst-placed one gets only a passing mention: Jupiter's construction loans, unsyndicated with the tenant's power unpermitted. The Codex review pushed in the reassuring direction here.

## Your assessment

Comfortably profitable today: equipment incumbents (Vertiv 19.5% operating margin; GE Vernova's Power segment at 18.8%, with FCF swelled by customer deposits) and established colocation (Equinix ~50% EBITDA margin even ex-fees). Their protection comes partly from customers prepaying, so the incumbent equity risk is backlog conversion and multiple compression, not credit. VRT's −45% shows the market already pricing that. Overextended: single-tenant campuses whose tenant is a cash-burning lab or a stretched intermediary, and whose power is not yet energised. The leading case is Jupiter: about $18B of reported loans, unpowered buildings, a permit hearing pushed to December and a delayed pipeline. Who loses first: construction lenders and underwriters left holding unsyndicated loans, and developer equity (APLD-style, with construction pass-through revenue and $6.4bn of debt). Next come project bondholders where tenant credit is weak; where the tenant is Meta, Microsoft or Google, the bonds trade on rates and liquidity more than on default risk. I differ from the note in two ways. First, I read power scarcity mainly as a **timing-mismatch** risk, not a ranking question against chips. Lease commencements, chip deliveries and debt service run on fixed schedules while energisation slips, so whoever holds fixed obligations without rent bears the cost. Second, the note's "regional constraint" evidence should be local queues and permits (Dominion, Jupiter, Texas), not PJM's capacity auction. On that evidence, power is a real regional constraint at grade C, not a demonstrated system-wide one.

Power-layer forecast (VRT, GEV, EQIX, DLR plus $1B+ project debt, 20/38/52): I would hold it. It sits correctly above F4 (10/25/40). Its credit branch is largely the same events as the Oracle row (Oracle-tenanted project loans such as Jupiter's count in both) and the neocloud row (CoreWeave-tenanted landlords such as APLD). The near-identical Oracle row (22/38/52) reflects that overlap, so the rows should not be read as independent layers.
