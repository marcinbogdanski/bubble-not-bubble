## The short answer

**1. Are the signals that past bubbles showed present? Several are, and the financing ones are AI-specific.** Forward purchases are at records, and the build is now funded in markets. Within ten days Oracle sold $10B of stock and $10B of notes, Meta sold $14.4B of preferred stock, $25B of shares forward and $15B of notes, Microsoft issued $25B of bonds, and Broadcom backstopped up to $43B of a $63B financing vehicle for Anthropic (filings, A). The Fed held at 3.75-4.00% on 28 October with two votes for a hike (A). CAPE was 40.6 in September against December 1999's 44.2, a market-wide signal ([theory](/report/theory), A). These signals do not establish overpricing.

**2. Is capital being deployed faster than returns can service it? Unproven; an illustrative payback scenario sets a high bar.** At a 50% owner EBITDA margin and 10% discount rate, a $750B cohort needs about $295B annual owner revenue at a six-year equipment life, $468B at three years. Applied to a 60-100% AI share of Goldman's $3.4T capex forecast, the 2026-28 cohorts need $0.8-1.3T annually once operating. At 50% owner capture, that implies $1.6-2.7T annual end-user revenue. Reaching it by 2029-30 requires 78-157% annual growth (arithmetic B; inputs and sector application D). Estimated 2026 end-user revenue is ~$160B (range $125-200B; a fifth now rests on filed figures, A; the rest is C and D). Revenue roughly tripled in 2026 and exits near a $250B run-rate; our base path assumes +88% then +60%. Replacement capex, advertising, internal savings, commissioning and residual values could change this [scenario](/research/macro/demand-side). Capex cuts (C1, C2) offer a self-correcting path; none is guided, though the 3 November votes against data-centre building could slow the build from outside.

**3. Where is the fragility, and who bears it? Failure risk sits in the middle of the stack; the largest mark-to-market exposure sits at the top, and part of it has now been taken.** Oracle, neoclouds and both frontier labs face funding risk. No AI-related debt has defaulted. On 5 November, after midterm results that favour data-centre moratoria in Texas, Ohio and Georgia, the Nasdaq-100 fell 6.4% and this report's AI basket 12% in one session; the basket is 18% below its 16 October level (B). The fall ran through equities: CoreWeave's CDS reached 1,165bp and its 2032 notes 74.75 cents (reported marks, C), but Oracle, Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet all raised money in the window. They paid for it: Oracle's notes priced 240-335bp over Treasuries, between the BB and single-B indices, though it is rated BBB− (term sheet, A). Losses without defaults could still be largest in Nvidia and hyperscaler equities, and on $330B of AI-related investment-grade bonds issued through Q2 2026 (KC Fed, A): a 100-200bp spread widening implies roughly $30-60B of paper losses (arithmetic B, ranking C). First realised credit losses are likelier in construction and sub-investment-grade GPU-backed loans. [Stack](/report/stack)

## The four facts that carry the answer

- **The incumbents generate cash but now fund the build in markets.** In Q3 Microsoft's free cash flow fell 27% to $18.6B; Alphabet's was −$9.1B, and it has raised $61.9B of equity this year; Amazon's trailing twelve-month figure is −$42.4B after $115B of debt proceeds in nine months, and it has a new supplier loan facility of up to about $80B; Meta's was −$2.1B before its raises, and S&P moved its outlook to negative (filings, A; S&P as reported, B). Amazon carries its Anthropic stake at $238.7B, 39% of its book equity (10-Q, A).
- **The intermediaries are cash-strained now.** Oracle: FY2026 FCF −$23.7B. It capped new market funding at $20B through FY2030 and used all of it within a week; the rest of a roughly $220B build is to come from vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware and prepayments, about $110B on our reading of its slides (filings, A; inference, C). CoreWeave: net interest consumed 25% of Q2 revenue; H1 operating cash of $3.7B against $5.2B of principal repaid; disclosure controls ineffective at 30 June (10-Q, A); shares 29% below 16 October; Q3 results on 10 November. Microsoft trimmed its 2026 capex guide by about $5B because third-party capacity arrived late (call, A).
- **Demand is growing fast and is broad, while backlog is concentrated.** Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103% (including chip-system sales), AWS +39%, with backlogs of $677B, $561B and $578B (filings, A). OpenAI paid Microsoft $10.0B in the quarter, 10.8% of its revenue (10-Q, A). Anthropic's S-1 gives the first filed accounts from a frontier lab: revenue of $4.6B in 2025 and $33.2B in nine months of 2026; Q3 revenue $17.0B, with a GAAP operating loss of $567M and adjusted operating income of $2.5B; operating cash flow of $6.6B in nine months; 44% of revenue sold through cloud marketplaces ([S-1/A](/research/stack/anthropic), A; interim figures unaudited). OpenAI's roughly $50B annualised revenue is still reported, unverified, and it has reportedly cut its funding target from $30B to $20B (C).
- **Investment returns are unknown, and commitments are larger than reported.** No company discloses cohort returns. Anthropic's filed commitments are $895.4B, against $518B reported before: $69.8B falls due within twelve months and $447.5B before the end of 2030; $213.8B is terminable or conditional (S-1/A, A). OpenAI's 2026-30 cash-flow forecasts drifted from +$14B to −$179B across three plans, and its funding covers February's burn plan only into 2028 (reported, unverified, C).

[Markets](/signals/2026-10-18-market-views): Polymarket's “AI bubble burst” market (three of six triggers) stood at 6.9% by end-2026 and 16.5% by June 2027 on 17 October, closest to M1a. CoreWeave's CDS implies risk-neutral default probabilities of about 9% at six months and 32% at two years, bracketing F1. No market prices an Oracle downgrade or a lab commitment cut.

## Strongest arguments each way

| Topic | Bull (source, grade) | Bear (source, grade) |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation | S&P technology ~21x earnings versus 70x in 2000 (Timmer; attribution B, estimates C) | GPU buyers' capex is suppliers' revenue; earnings can be the bubble despite ordinary P/Es (Druckenmiller via FT, B) |
| Demand path | OpenAI plus Anthropic revenue $600-700B by December 2027 (SemiAnalysis, C) | Scenario: $8.5T cumulative owner revenue needed through 2030 at a 12% return; at most $3.6T attributable to AI datacenters (Gartner; attribution A, outcomes C) |
| Supply | SK Hynix has over half of 2027-28 output under long-term agreements at an 80% operating margin; AMD guides Q4 revenue to $17B; OCPI B200 rents averaged $8.50 in October against $7.25 in September (A) | About 600,000 H200s reportedly wait in Hong Kong for import approval (C); Nvidia guides gross margin to 71-73% (A) |
| Credit | Markets are open: Microsoft borrowed $25B at 25-105bp over Treasuries (A) | IMF: ~$300B equity into weaker firms, ~$650B additional debt across investors/investees, ~$1.4T future orders returning spending to backers (staff estimates, A) |
| Politics | A slower build would remove excess without a crash (Yardeni, B attribution); existing capacity gains scarcity value | 40 of 41 local ballot contests went against development and three governors-elect favour moratoria; none is law yet (B) |
| Demand quality | 19.8% of employer businesses use AI (Census, May 2026, A) | Token spending roughly flat August-September despite rising usage (Ramp sample, A) |

## What we do not know, and what would tell us

| Unknown | The observable test |
|---|---|
| GPU economic life | H100 monthly index (P1, age test by 2028); used prices; depreciation changes |
| OpenAI's audited numbers; cohort returns at either lab | An OpenAI filing; Anthropic's first 10-Q; comparable run-rates (D1, D2); the IPO price against the $965B private mark (F5) |
| Contract firmness | Deferral or cancellation (F3, F3b); whether Anthropic uses its termination rights; CoreWeave's Q3 report, 10 November |
| Project-bond and GPU-loan holders and marks | $1B+ project/SPV distress (F4); waivers; November lender filings |
| Demand without supplier, investor or customer financing | Use of backstops (Broadcom's $43B, Alphabet's $48B); warrant vesting; Nvidia receivables |
| Hyperscaler cuts (C1, C2) | Calendar-2027 capex guidance with Q4 2026 results |
| Oracle customer funding | Q2 FY2027 vendor and customer offsets (mid-December) against the roughly $110B the plan needs; Jupiter permit hearing, 7 December |
| Whether politics binds the build | What Texas does after 19 January; state legislation; the Senate permitting vote on 9 November |
| Whether the fall is political or financial | AI spreads against same-rated non-AI spreads; on 5 November Treasury yields fell while the basket dropped, as in mid-October |

## Glossary

RPO: contracted revenue not yet recognised. FCF: operating cash flow less capex. CDS: cost in basis points (bp, 0.01%) of insuring debt. DDTL: delayed-draw term loan. SPV: special-purpose vehicle. CAPE: cyclically adjusted P/E. IG: investment grade.
