# Is AI a bubble? A live, evidence-backed assessment

*Version 0.1, 2026-10-17. First full pass over the stack. Not yet reviewed
by an independent agent; six research notes still in progress. Built by AI
agents from primary sources, steered by a human. See
[Methodology](/report/methodology).*

## The short answer

**Yes, by the mechanism, in the stage where financing escalates.** Every
indicator on the classic checklist except sentiment is lit: CAPE at 40.6,
record margin debt, record forward purchases, capex migrating from cash flow
to bonds, equity, SPVs and vendor credit, new classes of buyer entering, and
a central bank hiking into 3.7% inflation. The week of 10-17 October 2026
brought the first credit repricing in the weakest layer.

**But the bubble is in the financing of the build-out, not in the
technology, and not evenly across the stack.** Demand is real and growing
very fast: deduplicated end-user AI revenue is roughly $180B in 2026 from
$55B in 2025, Azure grew 43%, Google Cloud 82%. The equipment makers, TSMC
and Nvidia earn record margins and are paid first. The risk sits in the
middle: Oracle, the neoclouds and the frontier labs, which have taken on
over $1T of commitments and roughly $600-700B of identifiable debt and
project financing against a revenue base an order of magnitude smaller, and
in the hyperscalers' earnings once depreciation and lease costs land.

Our headline forecast: a 2000-style unwind in AI-linked assets (a 40% basket
drawdown with at least two credit events) at **15% within 6 months, 30%
within 12, 45% within 24**. The full register, by question and by layer, is
in [Forecasts](/forecasts/register).

## What we know

- **Capex**: the four largest hyperscalers spent $231B (2024), $381B (2025)
  and have guided ~$730B for 2026; Oracle and CoreWeave add $100B+. Nvidia's
  supply commitments went from $119B to $279B in one quarter.
- **Required return**: on the sector's own assumptions (six-year GPU life,
  50% margin, 10% cost of capital) the 2026 cohort alone needs ~$295B of
  incremental annual revenue; at a three-year life, $468B. End-user AI
  revenue is ~$180B. [Theory](/report/theory)
- **Financing has moved off cash flow**: Amazon's trailing FCF is negative,
  Meta's was $0.8B last quarter, Alphabet raised $49.6B of equity, Microsoft
  has $329B of signed leases not yet started, Oracle borrowed $125B and sold
  $20B of stock with FCF of −$24B a year. The neocloud layer carries ~$100B+
  of debt against contracts with four counterparties.
- **Vendor financing is explicit**: Nvidia guarantees up to $105B of OpenAI's
  leases, holds $99B of equity stakes and says labs it backs will be "a
  quarter of our business next year"; AMD pays OpenAI and Meta in warrants.
  In 2000 Lucent and Nortel carried about $5B each. [Stack](/report/stack)
- **One counterparty**: OpenAI, with ~$50B of net run-rate revenue, sits
  behind Nvidia's guarantee, AMD's warrants, ~41% of Microsoft's RPO, most of
  Oracle's $664B RPO, $22B of CoreWeave's backlog and Amazon's $50B
  investment.
- **Credit has started to move**: CoreWeave 5-year CDS above 1,000bp, its
  2032 notes near 78 cents, Oracle CDS at a record ~290bp one notch above
  junk, single-tenant datacenter bonds at 87-93 cents. Aggregate spreads are
  still tight, which in 1929, 2000 and 2007 was also true at the top.
- **Prices for compute are not falling**: H100 rental indices are up ~50%
  from their early-2025 low. Overcapacity is not yet visible in the one
  market that would show it first.

## What we do not know

- The economic life of a GPU under 2026 conditions. It moves required
  revenue by 1.6x and decides whether reported hyperscaler earnings are
  overstated by 20% or more.
- The labs' real numbers: no audited accounts, run-rates on different
  accounting bases (OpenAI net, Anthropic gross), and undisclosed terms on
  every large commitment.
- Contract firmness: the deferral and termination rights in the Microsoft,
  Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic take-or-pay agreements that back ~$100B of
  neocloud debt.
- Who ultimately holds the project bonds and GPU-backed loans (insurers,
  pensions, private-credit funds, bank warehouses) and at what marks.

## What would change the answer

Toward "not a bubble": lab revenue growth sustained at current rates through
2027 on net accounting; GPU rental prices holding through the 2027 Blackwell
glut; hyperscaler FCF recovering while capex still grows; OpenAI and
Anthropic publishing audited numbers that close the margin gap.

Toward "bursting": a 2027 capex guidance cut by any hyperscaler; an OpenAI
commitment deferral; a GPU-backed loan default; the H100 index below $1.50;
a lab down round; the Fed continuing to hike.
