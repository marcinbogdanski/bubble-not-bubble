## The short answer

**1. Are the signals that past bubbles showed present? Several are, and the financing ones are AI-specific.** Forward purchase commitments are large, and the build is now funded in markets. From 26 October to 5 November Oracle sold $10B of stock and $10B of notes; Meta issued $14.4B of preferred stock, arranged a $25B forward share sale that brings no cash yet, and priced $15B of notes; Microsoft issued $25B of bonds; and banks committed a $63B structure to buy equipment leased to Anthropic, with Broadcom's residual-value support capped near $43B (filings and bank announcement, A). The Fed held at 3.75-4.00% on 28 October with two votes for a hike (A). CAPE was 40.6 in September against December 1999's 44.2, a market-wide signal ([theory](/report/theory), A). These signals do not establish overpricing.

**2. Is capital being deployed faster than returns can service it? Unproven; an illustrative payback scenario sets a high bar.** At a 50% owner EBITDA margin and 10% discount rate, a $750B cohort needs about $295B annual owner revenue at a six-year equipment life, $468B at three years. Applied to a 60-100% AI share of Goldman's $3.4T capex forecast, the 2026-28 cohorts need $0.8-1.3T annually once operating. At 50% owner capture, that implies $1.6-2.7T annual end-user revenue. Reaching it by 2029-30 requires 78-157% annual growth (arithmetic B; inputs and sector application D). Our illustrative estimate of 2026 end-user revenue is ~$160B (range $125-200B; a fifth rests on filed figures, A; the rest is C and D). Our model puts 2026 revenue at about three times 2025 and a December exit run-rate near $250B; the base scenario then grows 88% and 60% (D). Other inputs could change this [scenario](/research/macro/demand-side). Capex cuts (C1, C2) offer a self-correcting path; none is established, and Microsoft's small trim reflects late deliveries. Restrictions and proposals against data-centre building could slow spending while leaving fixed obligations in place.

**3. Where is the fragility, and who bears it? Failure risk sits in the middle of the stack; the largest mark-to-market exposure sits at the top, and part of it has now been taken.** Oracle, neoclouds and both frontier labs face funding risk. No distressed credit event has been established. On 5 November the Nasdaq-100 fell 6.4% and this report's AI basket 12% in one session, leaving the basket 18% below 16 October (B). Reporting linked the fall to midterm results that favour data-centre moratoria in Texas, Ohio and Georgia; the causal weights are unmeasured. After the close IREN delayed its revenue target and said $6B of its FY2027 funding depends on markets (A). Credit took part: CoreWeave's CDS was reported as high as 1,268bp and its 2032 notes at 74.75 cents, and broad corporate risk gauges reportedly had their worst day in nearly eight months (C); index spread data for the day are not yet out. Still, Oracle, Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet raised money in the window. They paid for it: Oracle's notes priced 240-335bp over Treasuries, between the BB and single-B index spreads, though it is rated BBB− (term sheet, A; comparison approximate). Losses without defaults could still be largest in Nvidia and hyperscaler equities, and on $330B of AI-related investment-grade bonds issued in H1 2026 (KC Fed, A): a 100-200bp spread widening implies roughly $30-60B of paper losses (arithmetic B, ranking C). ## The four facts that carry the answer

- **The incumbents generate cash but now fund the build in markets.** In Q3 Microsoft's free cash flow fell 27% to $18.6B; Alphabet's was −$9.1B, and it has raised $61.9B of equity this year; Amazon's trailing twelve-month figure is −$42.4B alongside $115B of debt proceeds in nine months, and it has a new supplier loan facility of up to about $80B; Meta's was −$2.1B before its raises; S&P moved its outlook to negative, and a New Mexico court imposed a $25B penalty that Meta will appeal (filings A; derived changes B; S&P as reported B). Amazon carries its Anthropic stake at $238.7B, 39% of its book equity (10-Q inputs A; ratio B).
- **The intermediaries are cash-strained now.** Oracle: FY2026 FCF −$23.7B. It capped further market funding for FY2027-30 at $20B, excluding refinancing, and raised all of it a week after announcing the cap; of a roughly $220B build, about $110B is to come from vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware and prepayments and about $90B from other cash flows, on our reading of its slides (plan A; inference C). CoreWeave: net interest consumed 25% of Q2 revenue; disclosure controls ineffective at 30 June (10-Q, A; ratio B); shares 29% below 16 October; Q3 results on 10 November. Microsoft trimmed its 2026 capex guide by about $5B because third-party capacity arrived late (call, A).
- **Demand is growing fast and is broad, while backlog is concentrated.** Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103% (including chip-system sales), AWS +39%, with backlogs of $677B, $561B and $578B (filings, A). Microsoft recognised $10.0B of revenue from OpenAI in the quarter, 10.8% of its total; recognition is not collection (10-Q, A). Anthropic's [S-1/A](/research/stack/anthropic) gives the first filed accounts from a frontier lab: revenue of $4.6B in 2025 and $33.2B in nine months of 2026. Q3 revenue was $17.0B, with a GAAP operating loss of $567M; adjusted operating income of $2.5B excludes $1.07B of stock pay and $1.96B of donations. Nine-month operating cash flow was $6.6B, but management expects significant cash use ahead. It estimates that 5-7% of nine-month revenue came from customers since restricted for policy breaches. The 44% of revenue sold through cloud marketplaces is booked gross (filed, A; 2026 figures unaudited). OpenAI's roughly $50B net annualised revenue, and its funding target cut from $30B to $20B, remain reported, unverified (C).
- **Investment returns are unknown, and commitments are large.** No company discloses cohort returns. Anthropic's filed commitments are $895.4B: $69.8B is expected within twelve months of 30 September and $447.5B before the end of 2030; $213.8B is terminable or conditional. Variable arrangements estimated at $150-175B are excluded, and some rent starts before capacity is usable (S-1/A, A). OpenAI's reported February plan implies $179B of net cash outflow in 2026-30 and needs more funding during 2028; its actual liquidity is undisclosed (reported, unverified, C).

| Supply | SK Hynix has over half of 2027-28 capacity under long-term agreements and earned an 80% operating margin in Q3; AMD guides Q4 revenue to $17B (A); OCPI B200 rents averaged $8.50 in October against $7.25 in September (B) | About 600,000 H200s reportedly await import approval in Hong Kong (C); Nvidia guides gross margin down to 71-73% from 74% (A) |
| Credit | Markets are open: Microsoft borrowed $25B at 25-105bp over Treasuries (A) | IMF: ~$300B equity into weaker firms, ~$650B additional debt across investors/investees, ~$1.4T future orders returning spending to backers (staff estimates, A) |
| Politics | A slower build would remove excess without a crash (Yardeni, B attribution) | 40 of 41 local ballot contests went against development and three governors-elect favour moratoria; none is law yet (B) |

## Glossary

RPO: contracted revenue not yet recognised. FCF: operating cash flow less capex. CDS: cost in basis points (bp, 0.01%) of insuring debt. DDTL: delayed-draw term loan. SPV: special-purpose vehicle. CAPE: cyclically adjusted P/E. IG: investment grade.
